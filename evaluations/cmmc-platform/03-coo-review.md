COO REVIEW — Rampart (AI-first CMMC compliance platform for the Defense Industrial Base)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is operationally one of the cleaner shapes I evaluate: a remote-first, software-led SaaS with no trucks, no installs, no field crew, no inventory, and no physical supply chain — the entire unit of work is digital document ingestion, AI-drafted artifacts, a guided interview, and an ongoing tracking dashboard. Two founders who can build the software and sell it can absolutely operate the v1; the real operational question is not "can two people do the work" (they can host the software and let it run) but "how much human compliance review does each customer's output actually require before it is safe to put in front of a C3PAO assessor, and does that review load scale linearly and bury the founders." My honest read is that delivery is highly repeatable and the tooling is buildable, but the AI-drafted-policy product carries a hidden manual-QA tail (a wrong control mapping or a hallucinated policy clause that fails an assessment is a reputation-killing event in a tight, referral-driven 76K-org market), and that tail is where the operational risk concentrates.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Picture month 12. Say Rampart has roughly 80–120 paying accounts at a ~$12K midpoint ACV (the math below justifies this band), plus 2–3 MSP/consultancy partner accounts each carrying a small book of DIB clients. It is a Tuesday in March 2027. The two founders are not driving anywhere; both are at their desks. Founder A (sales/marketing-led) is running the top of funnel: three discovery calls with machine shops and one with an aerospace parts supplier who just saw a DFARS clause appear in a prime's solicitation and is now genuinely scared — that fear is the product's tailwind. Founder A spends the morning on demos and the afternoon writing positioning copy and answering inbound from a webinar. Founder B (technical) is split three ways: (1) babysitting the ingestion-and-drafting pipeline — a new electronics manufacturer uploaded 40 PDFs of messy existing IT procedures last night and the AI generated a draft SSP plus 22 policies that need a human eyeball before the customer relies on them; (2) handling roughly 6–10 support tickets that came in overnight ("the platform mapped our MFA evidence to the wrong control," "how do I document our incident-response capability," "our C3PAO asked for X, where is it in the export"); and (3) shipping a small product fix. Critically, somewhere in this Tuesday a human with CMMC fluency — the named Registered Practitioner advisor, or a fractional compliance reviewer — has to do a sanity pass on each new customer's generated artifact set, because the founders' own stated CMMC depth is "scaffolded through an advisor," not held on day one. That review is the operational pinch point. Where it breaks: if 4–5 new accounts close in the same week (a deadline-cluster as a DFARS phase hits), the founders have 4–5 full artifact sets needing expert review simultaneously, the advisor is part-time, and there is no second reviewer — so either onboarding slips (a churn/refund risk because the customer bought "certified in weeks") or an unreviewed set goes out and risks an assessment failure. Nothing about this Tuesday requires a truck; everything about it requires that the human compliance-review function be staffed ahead of the deadline waves, not behind them.

Human delivery-load math (the binding constraint):
- Phase-1 work per NEW account = AI generates the artifacts, but a CMMC-fluent human must review the generated SSP + ~20 policies + 110-control gap scorecard + starter POA&M before the customer relies on it for an assessment. Estimate human expert review at [ASSUMED] 4–8 hours per new account (read/correct AI output, fix control mappings, sit one interview-clarification call). Verify by timing the first 10 onboardings.
- New logos/month to hit month-12 ladder: ~50 accounts over the year ≈ 4–5 new accounts/month steady, but lumpy around DFARS deadline waves (could spike to 8–12 in a hot month).
- Review hours/month at steady state: 5 accounts × 6 hrs = 30 hrs/month. One part-time Registered Practitioner advisor (say 20–40 hrs/month available) covers steady state. In a 10-account spike month: 10 × 6 = 60 hrs — that EXCEEDS a single part-time advisor and forces the first real hire.
- First hire (named): a fractional/contract CMMC Registered Practitioner or compliance reviewer, ~$60–$120/hr [ASSUMED for RP-level contract labor; verify on RP directories/Upwork-style sourcing], scaling from part-time to ~half-time (~80 hrs/month, ~$6.4K–$9.6K/month) as accounts cross ~12–15 new/month. This is a known, available, remote labor pool — RPs exist by the thousand in the CMMC ecosystem — not a scarce field-crew problem.
- Phase-2 ongoing load per account = mostly self-serve dashboard + automated reminders; human support estimated at [ASSUMED] 0.5–1.5 hrs/account/month (renewal questions, evidence-collection help, "my assessor asked for X"). At 100 accounts: 100 × 1 hr = 100 support hrs/month ≈ 0.6 FTE of support. Manageable; this is the part that genuinely scales with software.
- Bottleneck location: it lands FIRST on expert review of Phase-1 artifacts during deadline-cluster spikes — not on infrastructure, not on sales-call volume, not on support. Two founders + one part-time RP advisor hold ~50–80 accounts; crossing into the several-hundred-account range requires 1–2 fractional reviewers, which the unit economics ($12K ACV vs. ~$300–$700 of review labor per onboarding) easily fund.

Net: the income ladder is reachable with a tiny logo count; the operational governor is the human compliance-review function on AI output, which is hireable from a real remote labor pool and is well-funded by the ACV.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.5 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Effectively zero logistics and zero physical supply chain: remote-first SaaS, no installs/trucks/site visits, delivered identically to all 50 states — the founders' explicit "no field ops" constraint is structurally honored, scoring a 10 on logistics tractability.
- The deliverable is anchored to a FIXED, externally-defined spec — the 110 NIST 800-171 controls — which makes the artifact set template-able, trainable, and repeatable rather than bespoke per customer, and lets AI carry the heavy first draft.
- Reachable income ladder on a tiny logo count: only ~50 accounts at $12K ACV clears the month-12 ~$300K/founder rung and ~85–90 (or fewer with MSP partner books) clears month-24 ~$500K/founder, against a ~76K-org TAM — throughput is not the binding constraint on income.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Quality control on AI-generated compliance artifacts (scored 5): a wrong control mapping or hallucinated policy clause that contributes to a failed C3PAO assessment is a severe, referral-poisoning failure in a small niche; consistency at volume is unproven and partly depends on an external reviewer.
- Deadline-cluster lumpiness in Phase-1 onboarding: when a DFARS phase hits, multiple artifact sets need expert human review in the same week (8–12 new logos × 4–8 hrs each can exceed a single part-time advisor), creating either onboarding slip (churn/refund risk against a "certified in weeks" promise) or pressure to ship unreviewed work.
- Single-advisor domain dependency: the CMMC authority the founders lack on day one sits with one named practitioner/advisor; until there is a bench of 2+ Registered Practitioners, that person is a single point of failure for the entire review-and-credibility function.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to hurt this is the human-review tail on AI-generated compliance artifacts colliding with deadline-driven demand spikes. The product's whole promise is "certified in weeks, not months," and the artifacts it ships are not advice but the company's actual SSP, policies, POA&M, and 110-control scorecard that go straight to a third-party C3PAO assessor. That means quality is not a nice-to-have — it is the product, and the failure mode is binary and public: a customer fails or stumbles in an assessment and tells the next machine shop at the same industry association. AI drafting compresses the labor beautifully on average, but it has variance, and the only thing standing between variance and a reputation event is a human-in-the-loop expert review step that (a) is not yet proven to be fast and reliable, and (b) currently rests on a single external advisor rather than in-house depth. The reason I'm not fully comfortable is timing: demand here is not smooth — it arrives in deadline clusters as DFARS phases hit solicitations, so the exact moment the most new customers need their artifacts reviewed simultaneously is the moment a two-founder team plus one part-time advisor is most likely to be overwhelmed, and the pressure to ship unreviewed sets to honor the "weeks" promise is highest. This does not make the business un-operable — it makes "staff the compliance-review bench ahead of the deadline waves, and never let an unreviewed artifact set reach an assessor" the single most important operating discipline, and it is the thing I'd want proven before scaling marketing spend.

- How will you smooth deadline-cluster lumpiness — do you have a pipeline of 2+ contract Registered Practitioners you can surge to, a defined onboarding-queue/SLA, and a hard rule that no artifact set reaches an assessor without expert sign-off even in a spike month?
- What is the actual Phase-2 ongoing support load per account per month (renewal/affirmation help, evidence-collection questions, assessor requests), measured on the first cohort, so the support FTE math at 100–300 accounts is real rather than [ASSUMED]?
- How interchangeable is the underlying LLM in practice — has the ingestion/control-mapping pipeline been tested across at least two model providers so a single vendor's price hike, deprecation, or terms change can't stall artifact generation?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Operationally this is a strong fit for a two-founder bootstrapped, remote-first venture: no logistics, no physical supply chain, no field ops, a fixed and template-able deliverable spec (the 110 controls), a tiny logo count needed to clear the income ladder (~50 accounts at $12K ACV for the month-12 ~$300K/founder rung), and the one role it must hire — a CMMC Registered Practitioner reviewer — is drawn from a deep, available, geography-independent remote labor pool that the ACV comfortably funds. The single thing that keeps this from a higher score is the quality-control tail on AI-generated compliance artifacts (scored 5) and its collision with deadline-cluster demand: the work the human must still do is high-stakes review, the failure mode is reputation-damaging in a referral-driven niche, and on day one that review depth lives with one external advisor. I am recommending GO rather than REFINE because none of that is structural — it is an operating-discipline and staffing-sequencing problem, not a trucks-and-installs problem — but I would condition scaling marketing on the founders proving (1) measured per-account review time on the first 10 onboardings, (2) a defined "no unreviewed set reaches an assessor" QC gate with a named second reviewer, and (3) a surge bench of 2+ contract RPs to absorb deadline spikes. Get those three in place and a lean team operates this comfortably into the several-hundred-account range.
