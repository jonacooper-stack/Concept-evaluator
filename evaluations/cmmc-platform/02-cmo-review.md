CMO REVIEW — Rampart

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ

This is one of the cleaner must-have stories I have evaluated: the pain is a contractual gate, not a preference — a small machine shop that wants to keep its DoD work has no choice but to get to CMMC Level 2, and the deadline is arriving inside actual solicitations under the 48 CFR/DFARS rule. That is the good news, and it is genuinely good. The bad news is that the CMMC software market is NOT a sleepy, unclaimed niche — it is already contested by funded, CMMC-specific players (Vanta, Drata both launched CMMC products; Hyperproof, Cyturus, Kuiper/Steel Root, FutureFeed, Ignyte, Totem, ControlMap-by-CyberSaint) plus a thick layer of MSPs and C3PAOs who own the customer relationship and the trust. The wedge — "AI drafts your SSP and 110-control gap analysis from your own messy docs" — is real and currently underbuilt, but it is a feature a funded incumbent can ship in two to three quarters, and the founders are entering a market where the buyer's deepest fear (will this pass my C3PAO assessment?) is answered by trust and assessor relationships, not by software cleverness. Sellable yes; defensible and uncrowded, much less so.

POSITIONING DRAFT

"The AI compliance platform that gets your defense shop CMMC Level 2 certified in weeks, not months — built only for the DIB, priced for a 40-person machine shop, not an enterprise security team." That is a competent, sharp sentence and I can write it without it sounding like every horizontal GRC tool — the "only for the DIB / priced for a small shop" clause does real work and is the most honest edge here. But note what it is NOT claiming: it does not (and cannot honestly) claim "guaranteed to pass your assessment," because Rampart is not the C3PAO and does not control the assessor's judgment. The positioning is strong on speed and specialization; it is silent on the thing the buyer is most afraid of, and competitors with assessor partnerships will attack exactly that gap.

THE WEDGE

The first 100 customers are reachable and I can name the specific vectors, which is more than most concepts offer:

1. MSP/MSSP channel (the highest-leverage wedge). A few hundred MSPs in the U.S. specialize in DIB clients and each manages a book of 10–50 small contractors who all face the same mandate. Landing 10–15 of these partners via the multi-tenant tier could put Rampart in front of hundreds of end customers without per-logo CAC. Tactic: founder-led outbound + presence in the MSP-for-DIB communities (e.g., the CompTIA / MSP-focused groups, and CMMC-specific Slack/LinkedIn circles). Cost: founder time plus ~$2K–$5K/mo in targeted LinkedIn/outbound tooling. This is the strongest distribution insight in the one-pager and the founders flag it correctly.

2. C3PAO and Registered Practitioner Organization (RPO) referrals. Assessors and RP firms see every unprepared contractor and are structurally motivated to send clients to a tool that makes their assessment go faster. Co-marketing with 3–5 named C3PAOs is a credible early channel. Cost: revenue share or referral fee, near-zero cash.

3. SEO/content against high-intent, low-competition long-tail. Queries like "CMMC Level 2 SSP template," "NIST 800-171 POA&M example," "CMMC consultant cost" are searched by exactly the buyer, and the horizontal players rank thinly here. A library of control-by-control content plus a free gap-scorecard tool is a classic, cheap, founder-fit inbound engine. [ASSUMED] blended content CAC $400–$1,200 for a $12K ACV — verify with a 60-day SEO pilot tracking demo requests per published asset.

4. Prime-contractor supply-chain push-down. Primes (Lockheed, RTX, Northrop, GD) are pressuring their subs to get certified; a "preferred tool" relationship with even one prime's supplier-development program would be a firehose. Harder to land early, but worth a named-target list.

The wedge is real. The risk is not "no entry path" — it is that the two best channels (MSPs, C3PAOs) are exactly where Vanta, Drata, and the CMMC-native players are also fishing, with bigger budgets and existing brand.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE

Naming real competitors, because this market is more crowded than the one-pager implies:

- Vanta — best at brand, velocity, and the "automated compliance" category they largely created; launched explicit CMMC / NIST 800-171 support and has the marketing machine to own the search term. The single most dangerous entity if this works.
- Drata — best at integrations, continuous-monitoring polish, and enterprise-grade trust; also offers NIST 800-171 / CMMC frameworks. Same threat profile as Vanta, slightly more upmarket.
- Hyperproof / Cyturus / Ignyte — best at GRC depth and FedRAMP/CMMC-adjacent government compliance; Ignyte in particular is FedRAMP-authorized and DIB-credible.
- FutureFeed, Totem Technologies, Kuiper (Steel Root), ControlMap (CyberSaint), Cyber Sierra — best at being CMMC-NATIVE and small-contractor-priced today. These, not Vanta, are Rampart's true head-to-head competitors: they already speak DIB, already map the 110 controls, already sell to machine shops. Totem and FutureFeed in particular are built for exactly this buyer.
- The MSP / consultancy layer — Steel Root, Summit 7, Redspin (a C3PAO), CMMC Center of Excellence partners. They own the trust and the relationship and many resell or wrap a tool. They are channel AND competitor.

Where is the air? Two pockets. (1) AI-first ingestion: most CMMC-native tools still make the buyer fill in templates; genuinely drafting the SSP and policy set FROM the customer's uploaded docs is a real, currently-underbuilt experience — that is the wedge and it is legitimate for ~12–24 months. (2) Design and onboarding taste: the CMMC-native tools are, frankly, ugly and clunky, and the founders' positioning/marketing edge is a real advantage against Totem/FutureFeed (though NOT against Vanta/Drata, who out-market everyone). Where is there NOT air: the category itself is claimed, the search terms are contested, and the buyer's trust gravitates to whoever the assessor and the prime already endorse.

SCORES (1–10)

1. Positioning clarity: 8 — "Only for the DIB, priced for a 40-person shop, certified in weeks not months" is a genuinely sharp, edged sentence that differentiates against the named horizontal players Vanta and Drata; it earns the 8 on specificity, not adjectives.

2. Real differentiation: 6 — AI-drafting the SSP/policy set from the customer's own uploads is a real and currently-underbuilt experience, but it is a feature, not a structural moat; Vanta or Totem can ship comparable AI ingestion within 2–3 quarters, which caps this at 6.

3. Wedge to first 1,000 customers: 8 — three concrete, low-cash channels (MSP multi-tenant resale to books of 10–50 DIB clients, C3PAO/RPO referral co-marketing, and high-intent long-tail SEO like "CMMC Level 2 SSP template") that fit founder-led GTM; named and costed, so it earns the 8.

4. GTM motion fit: 8 — marketing-led inbound plus founder-led close into a fragmented small-contractor segment is exactly the founders' stated strength (top-tier at positioning and founder-led sales), and the partner/MSP motion multiplies reach without per-logo CAC.

5. Distribution channel realism: 7 — multiple working channels exist (SEO, MSP partnerships, C3PAO referrals, prime push-down), but the two best of them are the same channels Vanta and Drata are funding harder, so realism is good-not-great.

6. CAC realism: 6 — [ASSUMED] content CAC $400–$1,200 and MSP-channel CAC far lower look plausible against a $12K ACV, but this is a considered, multi-month, trust-gated B2B sale and the founders almost certainly underestimate the sales-cycle length and the cost of competing for attention against Vanta's brand; verify with a 90-day pipeline-velocity test.

7. Brand / story compounding: 7 — "the CMMC people, built only for the DIB" is a story that compounds through assessor and MSP word-of-mouth and a content library, rather than every sale being a fresh fight; not a 9 because the buyer's ultimate trust anchor is the assessor, not the vendor brand.

8. Competitive air: 5 — this is the honest weak point: the category is already contested by funded, CMMC-specific entrants (Vanta, Drata) AND CMMC-native incumbents (Totem, FutureFeed, ControlMap, Ignyte) AND the MSP/C3PAO trust layer; it is fragmented but NOT sleepy, so it scores mediocre.

9. Defensibility: 5 — the durable moat (becoming the system of record with switching costs once a company's living SSP and evidence live in Rampart) is real but only accrues AFTER traction; on day one the product is largely copyable, and data/network effects are weak because each contractor's compliance data is siloed, so 5 is honest.

10. Customer truth: 6 — the founders have genuine adjacent operator credibility (ran SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, GDPR programs) which is real and relevant, but they explicitly do NOT yet hold CMMC/assessor-specific knowledge (scaffolded via an advisor) and there is no evidence in the one-pager that they have interviewed actual DIB machine-shop buyers; the pain is well-documented from rulemaking, but the specific customer is still partly inferred, not known cold.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.6 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS

- The pain is a true painkiller, not a vitamin: CMMC Level 2 is a contractual gate appearing in DoD solicitations now under the 48 CFR/DFARS rollout, and losing eligibility means losing the core of the business — this is a forced, deadline-driven, budgeted buy across a large estimated population ([ASSUMED] ~76,000–80,000 orgs per DoD rulemaking figures).
- A genuinely sharp, defensible-for-now positioning wedge: "AI drafts your actual SSP and 110-control gap analysis from your own uploaded documents — built only for the DIB, priced for a small shop" differentiates cleanly against horizontal Vanta/Drata and against clunky CMMC-native tools, and fits the founders' marketing/positioning edge.
- High-leverage, low-cash distribution: the MSP multi-tenant channel (each MSP carries 10–50 DIB clients) plus C3PAO/RPO referrals plus high-intent long-tail SEO give a credible founder-led path to the first hundreds of customers without enterprise sales-team spend.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS

- The category is already claimed and funded: Vanta and Drata both ship CMMC/NIST 800-171 support and out-market everyone, while Totem, FutureFeed, ControlMap, Ignyte, and Cyturus are CMMC-native and small-contractor-priced today — this is not the sleepy, unclaimed niche the founders' framing implies.
- The wedge is copyable: AI ingestion of source docs into draft SSP/policy is a 2–3-quarter feature for a funded incumbent, so the differentiation window is real but time-boxed, and the durable moat (system-of-record switching cost) only arrives after meaningful traction.
- Trust, not software, may govern the buy: the buyer's deepest fear is "will this pass my C3PAO assessment?" — a question answered by assessor relationships and references, not by product polish, and the MSP/C3PAO layer that owns that trust can choose to back a competitor (or build/wrap their own).

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The single most likely way this dies on the marketing/competitive axis is that Rampart wins the demo and loses the trust war. In a forced-compliance purchase, the small contractor is not buying software features — they are buying confidence that they will not fail a C3PAO assessment and lose their DoD revenue. That confidence is currently brokered by assessors (Redspin, Summit 7), by the MSP who already runs their IT, and increasingly by the prime that is pushing them to certify. Rampart's AI-drafting wedge is the kind of thing that wins a head-to-head feature comparison and a Product Hunt thread, but it does not, by itself, answer the buyer's actual question. Meanwhile the two best acquisition channels — MSPs and C3PAOs — are the very gatekeepers most able to back a different horse: Vanta and Drata are courting the same MSPs with bigger co-marketing budgets and a category-defining brand, and the CMMC-native tools (Totem, FutureFeed) already have the assessor relationships and DIB references Rampart lacks on day one. The concept can absolutely sell — but if the founders treat this as a marketing/positioning fight (their instinct) rather than a trust-and-channel fight, they will generate demos and stall at the close, because the person the buyer trusts most has not endorsed them. The defensible version of this business is won by capturing the MSP and assessor channel FAST and becoming the embedded system of record before the funded players notice the DIB-small-shop segment is worth their full attention.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE

- How many actual DIB small-contractor buyers (machine-shop owners, MSP principals serving the DIB) have you interviewed, and what did they say their single biggest fear is when choosing a CMMC tool — speed, cost, or assessment-pass confidence? (If it is the third, your positioning has a hole.)
- What is your concrete plan to win the MSP and C3PAO channel before Vanta and Drata fund their own DIB-channel push — which named MSPs and assessors are you targeting first, and what is the offer that makes them choose you over a Vanta partnership?
- How do you handle the "will this pass my assessment?" question honestly, given that you are not the C3PAO — do you partner with named assessors for a co-endorsed "assessment-ready" guarantee, build a references program, or something else? What is the structural answer, not the marketing line?
- When (not if) Totem, FutureFeed, or Vanta ships comparable AI document ingestion, what is the second layer of differentiation that you will have built by then (proprietary DIB control-mapping data, depth of MSP lock-in, evidence-automation integrations)?
- What is your realistic sales-cycle length and the true blended CAC once you account for a trust-gated, multi-stakeholder (owner + MSP + sometimes prime) purchase — and have you pressure-tested the $12K midpoint ACV against what Totem and FutureFeed actually charge a 40-person shop today?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE

The must-have demand is excellent and the wedge is real, which is why this is worth refining rather than killing — but the one-pager materially understates the competition (it frames a contested, funded category as a sleepy underserved niche), and the differentiation, defensibility, and competitive-air scores reflect that. To get to a confident GO I need three changes. First, reframe the GTM from a positioning fight to a CHANNEL fight: make landing 10–15 named MSP/MSSP partners and 3–5 named C3PAOs the explicit month-one objective, because that is both the cheapest acquisition and the only durable moat (embedded system of record across a partner's whole book). Second, close the trust gap the positioning currently ignores: pair the speed claim with a concrete, assessor-co-endorsed "assessment-ready" mechanism so the buyer's real fear is answered by something other than your marketing voice. Third, replace inferred customer truth with evidence: run 15–20 buyer interviews across machine shops AND DIB-focused MSPs and confirm the fear hierarchy and the real prevailing price before committing to the $12K ACV. Do those three and the differentiation moves from "good feature" toward "owned channel," and the competitive-air problem becomes survivable.
