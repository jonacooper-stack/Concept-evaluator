LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Member Engagement & Dues-Collection Overlay for Labor Unions

(Standard disclaimer, stated once: I am not a licensed attorney and this is not formal legal advice. I am an experienced operator flagging where the regulatory and liability landmines sit so the founders can brief real counsel.)

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
This looks like a "SaaS overlay" but its monetizing core is a money-movement business: a take-rate on ACH and card dues collected on behalf of unions, plus markup on outbound SMS. That single design choice drags the concept into the two most aggressively enforced consumer-protection regimes in the country at once — the TCPA/FCC for the texting layer and the FTC/Nacha/card-network/state-money-transmission stack for the payments layer — wrapped around recurring billing of individual consumers (their own union members), which triggers federal and state auto-renewal/negative-option rules. Layered on top is the politically charged substance: dues collection in right-to-work and "paycheck-protection" states is governed by statutes that frequently require signed, member-specific written authorization to deduct or collect dues, and getting that authorization mechanic wrong is not a paperwork problem — it is unfair-labor-practice and consumer-finance exposure. The good news: every regime here is knowable, B2B-contracted at the union level, and squarely in a domain where the founders' SOC 2 / GDPR / CCPA background is directly relevant; the bad news is the surface area is broad and the consumer-facing payment and texting pieces are where serious money and serious enforcement live.

REGULATORY MAP
- TCPA (Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. 227) / FCC — automated/marketing SMS to cell phones requires prior express consent (prior express WRITTEN consent for marketing content). Statutory damages are $500–$1,500 per message and class actions are routine. Because the product's whole value prop is mass texting members whose "personal cell" was just captured, consent provenance is the central control. The 2021 Facebook v. Duguid narrowing of "autodialer" helps, but informational vs. marketing line-drawing and per-message liability remain high-burden. HIGH burden, must be built in day one.
- CAN-SPAM Act (15 U.S.C. 7701) / FTC — commercial email needs accurate headers, opt-out, physical address. LOW burden, easily built in.
- State money transmission laws / state banking departments (e.g., NY DFS MT statutes, CA DFPI, TX Dept. of Banking) — collecting member dues and remitting to the union is a classic "money transmission / money services business" pattern UNLESS the company never takes possession/control of funds and instead sits on top of a licensed processor under that processor's agent/PayFac model. This is the single most important structural decision. If they route through Stripe Connect / Adyen / a sponsor bank as a facilitator and never hold funds, MT licensing is largely avoided; if they hold/settle funds themselves, it is 40+ state licenses, surety bonds, and FinCEN MSB registration (31 CFR 1022). MODERATE-to-FATAL depending on architecture.
- FinCEN / BSA-AML (31 U.S.C. 5311 et seq., 31 CFR 1022) — if deemed an MSB, AML program, registration, and SAR obligations attach. Avoidable via the facilitator model. MODERATE.
- PCI-DSS (card network / PCI-SSC contractual standard, not a statute) — card acceptance requires PCI compliance; using a tokenizing processor (Stripe, Braintree) reduces scope to SAQ-A. LOW-to-MODERATE, founders have adjacent SOC 2 experience.
- Nacha Operating Rules (ACH) — direct debit of member accounts requires proper written-authorization, return-rate monitoring, and Nacha's WEB-debit account-validation rule. Unauthorized-debit returns above thresholds get a TPPP/originator shut down by the ODFI. MODERATE, and tightly coupled to the dues-authorization question below.
- FTC ROSCA (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, 15 U.S.C. 8403) + FTC negative-option/"click-to-cancel" rulemaking — recurring dues billing of consumers needs clear disclosure, informed consent, and simple cancellation. The FTC's 2024 click-to-cancel rule has had a turbulent litigation/enforcement history, so treat the federal floor as ROSCA-stable and watch the rule. MODERATE.
- State automatic-renewal laws (California ARL Bus. & Prof. Code 17600 et seq.; also NY, IL, VA, and ~20 others) — recurring consumer charges across 50 states each impose disclosure/cancel/reminder mechanics. Real multi-state surface area because members are individuals nationwide. MODERATE-to-HIGH burden in aggregate.
- National Labor Relations Act (29 U.S.C. 151 et seq.) / NLRB — dues authorization and checkoff are governed by NLRA Sec. 302 and state law; in right-to-work states (Taft-Hartley Sec. 14(b)) and "paycheck protection" states, the form, voluntariness, and revocability of dues authorizations are litigated. Beck rights (Communications Workers v. Beck) require objectors be able to limit dues to representational costs. A botched authorization flow exposes the UNION to ULP charges and the vendor to contributory exposure. HIGH substantive risk, but the legal burden sits primarily on the customer (the union); the product must not engineer non-compliant consent.
- LMRDA (Labor-Management Reporting and Disclosure Act, 29 U.S.C. 401 et seq.) / DOL OLMS — union financial reporting (LM-2/LM-3) and members' bill-of-rights touch dues handling and member data; relevant context, not a direct vendor license. LOW-to-MODERATE.
- State data-privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA, plus the ~19 state comprehensive privacy acts: VA CDPA, CO, CT, TX TDPSA, etc.) — the product aggregates personal cell numbers, emails, and payment data on millions of individuals; "union membership" is treated as SENSITIVE data under several of these and under GDPR Art. 9. As a service provider/processor the company is largely under DPA-governed obligations, but the sensitive-category classification raises the stakes. MODERATE, and founders have direct CCPA/GDPR experience.
- TCR / 10DLC and carrier A2P registration (CTIA + carrier rules, contractual) — application-to-person SMS now requires brand/campaign registration with The Campaign Registry; political/advocacy content gets extra carrier scrutiny and filtering. MODERATE operational, LOW legal.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No occupational or professional license is required to build or sell this software, and that is a genuine strength — the founders need no specialized credential. The license question lives entirely in the payments layer and is binary on architecture:
- Facilitator / payment-services model (recommended): Route every dollar through a licensed processor (Stripe Connect, Adyen, or a sponsor-bank PayFac arrangement) so the company NEVER takes possession or control of dues funds. Under this model the company is generally NOT a money transmitter, NOT an MSB, and needs only (a) standard business entity registration, (b) PCI-DSS SAQ-A attestation via the processor, and (c) the processor's master services/sub-merchant agreement. Cost: low four figures plus per-transaction fees the take-rate is built on top of.
- Principal / settle-funds model (avoid): If the company itself holds, pools, or settles member dues before remitting to the union, it likely becomes a money transmitter requiring licensure in 40+ states (each with application fees, surety bonds of $25K–$1M+, net-worth requirements, and exams), FinCEN MSB registration with an AML program, and ongoing state exams. Realistic all-in cost: hundreds of thousands of dollars and 12–24 months — effectively fatal for a bootstrapped two-person team.
SMS markup requires no license but requires 10DLC/TCR campaign registration. Bottom line: keep the company out of the flow of funds and the licensing burden collapses to near zero; touch the funds and the business is unviable.

LIABILITY POSTURE
The founders' direct entity exposure is concentrated in three buckets. First, TCPA: if members are texted on consent the union supplied but cannot document, the platform is the visible sender and a natural co-defendant in $500–$1,500-per-text class actions; this is the highest-frequency, highest-severity exposure and is managed by (a) a consent-capture mechanic that records source, timestamp, and content type, (b) contractual reps-and-warranties + indemnification from the union that supplied/authorized the contacts, and (c) a tight informational-vs-marketing content posture. Second, payments: chargebacks, unauthorized-ACH returns, and the union disputing reconciliation — managed by the facilitator architecture (processor carries settlement/MT risk), Nacha-compliant written authorization, and clear allocation-of-loss terms in the union MSA. Third, data: a breach of a database of millions of identified union members (sensitive-category PII) triggers state breach-notification statutes in all 50 states and contractual DPA liability — managed by SOC 2-grade controls (which these founders have built before), cyber/tech-E&O insurance, and processor liability caps. Insurance package: tech E&O / professional liability, cyber, and general liability — all readily available at startup premiums (low five figures annually) given a software-only, no-funds-held posture. The residual that no contract or insurance fully erases is the substantive NLRA/dues-authorization risk: if the product's onboarding flow generates dues authorizations that a labor board later deems involuntary or non-Beck-compliant, the union bears ULP liability and will look hard at the vendor whose UX produced the defective consent — this must be designed with union counsel, not the vendor's TOS.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most dangerous issue is the collision of TCPA per-message liability with the product's core mechanic of texting newly-captured personal cell numbers at scale. The whole reason the product exists is that the union LOST its clean channel to members and is scrambling to rebuild contact data fast; that urgency is exactly the condition under which consent gets captured sloppily or assumed from a stale employer relationship. Under the TCPA (47 U.S.C. 227) and FCC rules, sending automated or marketing texts to a cell without the proper prior express (written, for marketing) consent carries statutory damages of $500–$1,500 per message, with no cap and a thriving plaintiffs' bar that aggregates these into class actions; a single 100,000-member text blast on defective consent is nominal exposure in the tens of millions. The platform is the visible originator and the deep-pocketed software defendant of choice, even when the union supplied the list. This is not theoretical and not curable after the fact — it must be engineered in from day one (per-contact consent source/timestamp/content-type logging, hard separation of informational vs. marketing sends, 10DLC/TCR campaign registration, and strong reps-and-warranties + indemnification from the union for any contact it authorizes), and it interacts with the dues-authorization problem because the same onboarding flow is capturing both the phone number AND the payment authorization. Get the consent architecture wrong and the product's flagship feature becomes its largest liability.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
1. Payments structure: Can we structure dues collection so the company NEVER takes possession or control of funds (Stripe Connect / Adyen / sponsor-bank PayFac), and will a fintech/regulatory lawyer confirm that posture keeps us out of state money-transmitter licensure and FinCEN MSB registration (31 CFR 1022) in every state our members reside in?
2. TCPA consent: What exact consent-capture mechanic (language, timestamp, source-of-record, marketing-vs-informational separation) does TCPA/FCC doctrine require for us to text member cell numbers, and how do we contractually shift residual liability to the union for any contact it supplies or authorizes?
3. Dues authorization under labor law: In right-to-work and paycheck-protection states, what must the digital dues-authorization workflow contain to be voluntary, revocable, and Beck-compliant under NLRA Sec. 302 / Taft-Hartley 14(b), and how do we design the UX so we don't create ULP exposure for our union customers?
4. Auto-renewal compliance: Which state automatic-renewal statutes (CA ARL, NY, IL, etc.) and the FTC ROSCA/negative-option rule apply to recurring dues we bill on the union's behalf, and is the union or the platform the "seller" responsible for the disclosure/cancel mechanics?
5. Data classification: Is "union membership" treated as sensitive personal data under CPRA, the state comprehensive privacy acts, and GDPR Art. 9 for our specific role (service provider/processor), and what DPA terms, data-residency, and breach-notification obligations follow across all 50 states?
6. ACH/Nacha: What written-authorization and account-validation (WEB-debit) controls and return-rate monitoring do Nacha rules require, and how do we contract with the ODFI/processor to avoid origination shut-off?
7. Legacy-system integration: Do our integrations with UnionWare / Union Link rely on sanctioned APIs or on access that could raise CFAA or terms-of-service/contract claims, and do we need data-access agreements with those vendors or only with the union?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
This is a real must-have problem with a clean B2B buyer and a founder-skill fit on the compliance side, but it is not a low-regulation SaaS play — it is a consumer-payments-plus-mass-messaging business wearing a SaaS overlay, and the average reflects that genuinely mixed posture (one fatal-if-wrong axis, several manageable-but-broad axes, a couple of clean strengths). It is not NO-GO because every serious risk is structurally avoidable with the right architecture. The structural changes that would move this toward GO: (a) lock in the no-funds-held facilitator model in writing before building anything, so money-transmission and MSB licensing are off the table entirely; (b) treat TCPA consent provenance and 10DLC registration as a day-one core feature, not a setting, with contractual indemnification from the union for supplied contacts; (c) push the legal "seller of record" for recurring dues and the dues-authorization compliance onto the union by design, with the platform acting as documented processor/service provider; and (d) draft the union MSA + DPA with reps-and-warranties and loss-allocation that match this posture. Make those four changes and the consumer-protection, payments, and privacy axes drop from landmines to managed-paperwork; leave them unresolved and the texting and dues-authorization layers can produce existential liability. Refine the architecture and contracting motion first, then proceed.
