COO REVIEW — Member Engagement & Dues-Collection Overlay for Labor Unions

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is an operationally favorable shape: a SaaS-plus-payments overlay with no trucks, no installs, no field crew, and no physical supply chain. The unit of work is software integration plus configuration plus a digital data-collection campaign, all deliverable remotely. The real operational risk is not labor intensity but two specific friction points: (1) integration with idiosyncratic legacy databases of record (UnionWare, Union Link, and a long tail of homegrown systems), which can turn a "lightweight overlay" into bespoke per-customer engineering, and (2) becoming a money-movement business — operating a dues-collection rail across cards and ACH means chargebacks, disputes, KYC/onboarding of each union as a merchant, and payment-ops support that two founders will own personally. Both are tractable for a software-strong remote-first team, but neither is the zero-touch self-serve story the brief implies.

It is Tuesday in March, month 12. Assume the founders have landed 3 national/state parent bodies who have mandated rollout, and the product is live across roughly 60 locals representing ~120,000 members, with ~$40K MRR coming mostly from payment take-rate plus a thin SMS markup. Founder A starts the morning in the payment-ops and support queue. Overnight: 9 ACH return notifications (R01 insufficient funds, R02 account closed, R10 unauthorized) from members whose dues debits failed, 2 card chargebacks filed by members who forgot they authorized recurring dues and disputed them, and 4 support tickets from local secretary-treasurers asking why their reconciliation report doesn't match what hit their bank account. Each ACH return needs a member re-contact and re-collection workflow; each chargeback needs evidence submission (the signed digital authorization) within the processor's deadline or the union loses the money plus a $15 dispute fee. This is recurring, not one-time: at 120,000 members paying monthly, even a 0.5% ACH return rate is ~600 returns/month, ~30/business day. That is real daily labor.

Founder B is mid-integration on a new district that runs a 2009-era UnionWare instance with custom fields and no documented API. The "lightweight overlay" promised hierarchical sync (national → state → district → local), but this instance exports member records as a quarterly CSV with inconsistent column names. B spends the day writing a one-off mapping and a deduplication pass, then runs a test renewal campaign to 800 members to validate that personal cell numbers are actually being captured (the core value prop). Conversion on the digital data-capture link is ~22% on first send — meaning ~78% of members still have no good personal contact data, so B sets up SMS-then-email reminder sequences. Meanwhile a state organizing director calls wanting a custom dashboard view for an upcoming mobilization, which is not in the product yet.

By end of day: payment ops cleared, one integration half-done, one enterprise feature request logged, and a renewal at a national parent body that wants a security questionnaire and proof of PCI-DSS scope answered before they'll route real money through the rail. The founders' SOC 2 background helps here — they can answer it — but it is a half-day of work per large buyer. Nothing here requires a 50-person ops team; it requires two technically strong founders absorbing payment-ops toil and integration variance until they can productize/automate both.

CAPACITY MATH (show the work)
Target: ~$300K/founder by month 12 = ~$600K combined annual income. To fund two founders at $600K plus modest infrastructure/tooling/support cost, the business needs roughly $750K–$900K gross revenue (payments businesses run thin margins on the processing pass-through but the take-rate spread and SMS markup are high-margin). [ASSUMED cost structure; verify processor interchange-plus pricing and the realized spread.]

Revenue mechanics. Two streams:
- Payment take-rate on dues. Suppose average dues are $40/month/member and the founders net a take-rate spread of ~50 bps (0.5%) above processor cost on card/ACH. That is $0.20/member/month = $2.40/member/year. To reach $750K from take-rate alone would need ~312,000 paying members. At a more aggressive 1.0% net spread, $0.40/member/month = $4.80/year, needing ~156,000 members.
- SMS markup. If a union sends ~4 texts/member/month and the founders mark up ~$0.005/text, that is $0.02/member/month = $0.24/member/year. Across 156,000 members that adds ~$37K/year — meaningful but secondary.

So the realistic target at ~1% net payment spread + SMS is roughly 150,000–200,000 paying members actively running dues through the rail. [ASSUMED spread and dues amount; verify against a real processor quote and 2–3 union dues schedules.]

Delivery/labor capacity. The work is NOT per-member; it is per-onboarded-organization plus ongoing payment ops. Estimate:
- Onboarding a parent body + initial locals: integration + config + first data-capture campaign ≈ 40–80 hours of founder eng/config time per parent body (heavy if legacy DB is custom). To reach ~180,000 members you might need ~5–10 parent bodies and ~80–150 active locals. At an average 60 hrs each across, say, 8 parent bodies = ~480 hours of integration in year one ≈ 12 founder-weeks. Tractable for one founder over 12 months if integrations are templatized after the first few.
- Ongoing payment ops: at 180,000 members, ACH returns at 0.5% = ~900/month (~45/business day), chargebacks at ~0.1% of card txns, plus reconciliation tickets. At ~10 min handling per exception, 900 returns × 10 min = 150 hours/month = ~0.9 FTE of pure payment-ops toil. This is the bottleneck that lands first, and it grows linearly with members. It must be automated (self-serve retry flows, member-facing failed-payment portals) or it eats a founder.
- Support: at 180,000 members across ~150 locals, the support surface is the ~150 local admins, not the members directly (member self-service for payment). ~150 admins at low-touch ≈ 1–2 tickets/admin/month = ~200–300 tickets/month = ~50 hours/month. Manageable by one founder part-time, or a single offshore support hire (~$3–5K/month) by the time MRR justifies it.

Verdict on capacity: Two founders can reach the income ladder IF (a) integrations templatize after the first 3–4, and (b) payment-ops exceptions are automated down to <0.3 FTE. If either fails — if every union DB is a snowflake, or if return/chargeback handling stays manual — the model still works but needs 1–2 support/ops hires earlier, which the take-rate margin can fund.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.8 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- No field ops, no physical supply chain, no installs — fully remote software-and-payments delivery, the single easiest operational profile for two bootstrapped founders (logistics 9, geography 8).
- Top-down mandate distribution: one national/state buyer can push adoption to dozens of locals, so customer acquisition operationally collapses into a small number of enterprise relationships rather than thousands of individual sales — support surface is ~150 admins, not 180K members.
- Margin-funded scaling: the payment take-rate generates high-margin recurring cash that can fund the first ops/support hire (~$3–5K/month) before the founders are overwhelmed, so the org grows in step with revenue.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Legacy-database integration variance — UnionWare, Union Link, and homegrown systems with undocumented APIs and dirty data can turn the "lightweight overlay" into per-customer bespoke engineering, blowing the templatization assumption.
- Payment-ops exception load — ACH returns (~900/month at 0.5% on 180K members), chargebacks, and reconciliation disputes are recurring daily toil that grows linearly with members and must be automated or it consumes a founder.
- Single-processor dependency and merchant-risk classification — the business is a money-movement layer riding one processor; a risk-team reclassification of "union dues" as high-risk, an account hold, or a terms change can halt core revenue overnight.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to kill this is the collision of two things that both land on the founders personally: becoming a regulated money-movement operation while simultaneously fighting integration variance across legacy union databases. Standing up a dues-collection rail means the founders are not just shipping software — they are onboarding each union as a merchant (KYC/underwriting), handling NACHA ACH-return codes, managing chargeback evidence within processor deadlines, reconciling money to the penny for each local's bank account, and answering PCI-DSS scope questions from every large buyer before money moves. That payment-ops surface grows linearly with members (~45 ACH returns per business day at 180K members) and never goes away. Layer on top of it that every parent body's database of record is potentially a snowflake requiring custom mapping, and the "fast-to-deploy overlay" promise can quietly invert into a high-touch integration-plus-payment-ops services business — exactly the manual-toil shape the founders want to avoid. Two people can do this only if both the integration layer and the exception-handling layer are aggressively productized into self-serve flows early; if either stays manual, the model survives financially (the margin can fund hires) but stops being the lean two-founder business it is pitched as.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Integration shape: Of the target unions, what fraction run UnionWare vs. Union Link vs. homegrown/no-API systems, and have you built and timed an integration against at least one real legacy instance? Cheap verification: get read access to two live union databases and measure hours-to-sync.
- Payment risk posture: Have you obtained a written processor quote and risk classification for "labor union dues collection," and do you know whether you operate as merchant-of-record, payfac, or a referral/ISO model? This determines who owns chargeback liability, KYC, and the regulatory burden. Verify with one underwriting conversation with Stripe/Adyen/a payfac platform.
- Payment-ops automation plan: At 0.5% ACH returns on 100K+ members, what is the concrete self-serve flow that lets a member fix a failed payment without a human, and what is the residual FTE load after automation? Verify by mapping the exception workflow before scaling past the first 10K members.
- Data-capture conversion reality: The entire value prop hinges on actually collecting missing personal cell/email — what is the real first-pass conversion on a renewal/data-capture link, and how many touches to reach usable coverage? Verify with one live campaign on a single local before promising parent bodies a contact-data outcome.

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally this is one of the cleaner shapes I evaluate — no field ops, no physical logistics, national year-round recurring demand, and top-down mandate distribution that minimizes the acquisition surface. It is not a NO-GO. But two distinct things keep it from a clean GO and must be de-risked before scaling: (1) prove that legacy-DB integration templatizes after the first few — pilot against a real UnionWare/Union Link instance and measure hours, so the overlay doesn't become bespoke services; and (2) make a deliberate, contractual decision about the payment rail — choose a merchant-of-record/payfac structure that pushes chargeback liability and KYC onto a platform partner rather than onto the two founders, and design member-facing self-serve failed-payment recovery before you exceed ~10K paying members. Do those two things and the capacity math closes for two founders plus one ops hire at the income ladder; skip them and the business quietly becomes the manual integration-and-payment-ops grind the founders explicitly do not want.
