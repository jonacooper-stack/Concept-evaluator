## Who they are
Two **versatile, adaptive generalist** founders who will step into an acquired business
**full-time as owner-operators / general managers**. They learn fast and operate across
functions. They are NOT specialists clinging to one trade — and for an acquisition they
do not need to be, because they will **manage** the operation and its existing workforce
rather than personally perform the trade.

## Core strengths (where they win)
- **Marketing, positioning, and messaging** — top-tier. The #1 lever they pull on a
  sleepy, un-marketed acquired business.
- **Reaching and persuading people** — outbound, partnerships, founder-led sales, key-
  account relationships; useful for retaining and growing an inherited customer base.
- **Business strategy and finance** — they can read financials, model a deal, scrutinize
  add-backs, structure an SBA purchase, and manage to KPIs.
- **Software development & technical modernization** — they can deploy modern off-the-
  shelf systems, automation, and AI on top of a business that runs on paper and tribal
  knowledge. They build only the thin wedge; they buy the rest.
- **Compliance-operator instinct** — real program experience (SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA/
  HITECH, GDPR/CCPA). Helps them inherit and clean up a regulated or messy back office.
- **Regulated-enterprise deal-closing** — multi-million-dollar enterprise sales into
  regulated industries (insurance). Translates to credibility with sellers, lenders,
  and B2B customers.

## The acquisition edge (why buying + modernizing fits them)
Their single biggest edge is taking a **sound but un-modernized business** — strong
customers, working crew, real cash flow, owned by a retiring operator who never built a
website, never ran an ad, never bought a CRM, never raised prices — and **running the
same business better**: modern marketing to grow leads, modern systems to streamline
ops and margin, modern tech/AI to cut busywork. They are not turnaround artists rescuing
a failing business; they are professionalizers of a healthy, sleepy one.

## Operator role (decided)
- **Full-time owner-operators.** One or both run the business day-to-day, leading sales,
  marketing, finance, and the modernization program.
- **They manage an inherited workforce, including blue-collar field crews.** Inheriting
  and managing a field crew is acceptable and often the point.
- **They do NOT personally perform a licensed trade.** Where a trade/professional license
  is legally required, it must transfer, be hireable, or be held by a qualifying
  employee — the founders will not be the person holding the wrench and the credential.

## Where they win competitively
- Buying from **retiring Baby Boomer owners** in **fragmented, sleepy, local/regional**
  industries where no competitor has modernized marketing, operations, or technology.
- **Essential, durable, recurring/repeat-revenue** businesses (service contracts, route
  density, sticky B2B books) bought at sane Main-Street multiples.
- Situations where the value comes from **professionalizing and modernizing**, not from
  outbidding strategic/PE buyers.

## Where they do NOT win (avoid)
- **Industries being actively rolled up by well-funded PE consolidators** who bid entry
  multiples up and compete margins down (e.g., parts of HVAC, plumbing, dental,
  veterinary, auto repair). A bidding war is not their game.
- **Non-transferable, owner-is-the-business** targets where the goodwill and the key
  relationships walk out with the seller and nothing conveys.
- **Targets requiring a non-bridgeable personal credential** to operate, where no
  licensed employee or transfer can fill the gap.
- **Distressed turnarounds** of failing businesses — higher-risk, different game.

## Capital posture

## How the acquisition council should use this (two-stage model)
Remember the split (see `01-objectives.md`): **Stage 1** scores the INDUSTRY/TYPE; **Stage 2**
(`10-due-diligence.md`) checks the specific company. Use this profile accordingly.

- **Operator fit (Stage-1 dim 7):** score HIGH when the founders can run this TYPE of
  business as GMs and the field work is done by a managed crew; score LOW only when the
  *model* structurally requires the owner to personally hold a non-bridgeable credential or
  do sustained manual labor. Do NOT penalize merely for a blue-collar workforce — managing
  one is in scope.
- **Modernization upside (Stage-1 dim 4):** score HIGH when the INDUSTRY is broadly stale
  (un-marketed, manual, old tech) so the founders' edge applies across most operators; score
  LOW when the industry is already modern and professionalized.
- **Competitive structure (Stage-1 dim 6):** score HIGH for fragmented sleepy fields; score
  LOW when a well-funded PE consolidator is actively bidding the market up.
- **Owner-dependency, transferability, this company's systems, and earnings verification are
  NOT Stage-1 industry factors** — they are universal across retiring-owner businesses and
  are checked in Stage-2 due diligence on a real listing. Do not let them drag the industry
  score.
- Treat a managed field workforce, a bridgeable licensed-employee requirement, and a levered
  SBA structure as **normal and acceptable** — they are the model here, not red flags.
