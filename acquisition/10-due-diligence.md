# Stage 2 — Target Due Diligence (one real company)

Stage 1 (the Industry Screen, docs `01`–`07`) decides **which TYPES of business to hunt in**.
This doc is **Stage 2**: you now have a **specific company for sale**, with real numbers and a
real owner. Here you check the things you could NOT know at the industry stage — the ones
that are **universal** across every sleepy, retiring-owner business and therefore can only be
judged on the actual business, never on the industry.

These checks were deliberately **kept out of the Stage-1 score** so they wouldn't drag every
industry down equally. They do the real work HERE.

## Plain-language verdict (no jargon)
For a specific company, the call is:
- **GO** — buy it at (or near) the asking terms; the diligence checks out.
- **PASS** — walk away; a check failed in a way money can't fix.
- **OFFER A LOWER PRICE** — the business is fine but worth less than the asking price (or
  needs different terms) given what diligence found. *("Offer a lower price" is what M&A pros
  call a "re-trade": you agreed a price, diligence changed what the business is worth, so you
  go back and ask the seller to accept less — or to take part of the price as a standby seller
  note, an earn-out, or escrow. We just say "offer a lower price.")*

## How to use this
Work the six check areas below on the real company. Each has **green flags** (good),
**red flags** (bad / kill or discount), and the **document or test** that settles it. You do
NOT score these 1–10 like Stage 1 — you decide, per area, whether it's **Clear**, **Fixable
with a lower price / better terms**, or a **Deal-killer**. Then make the overall call.

## 1. Earnings quality — are the numbers real? (CFO)
The seller will show "adjusted SDE/EBITDA." Prove it.
- **Green flags:** 3–5 years of tax returns that tie to the P&L; few, defensible add-backs;
  stable or growing earnings; bank statements that match revenue.
- **Red flags:** earnings that exist only after large or vague add-backs; cash-heavy revenue
  that can't be tied to deposits; a recent revenue spike right before sale; declining trend
  the seller waves away.
- **The test:** a **Quality-of-Earnings (QoE)** review by a CPA — tie add-backs to tax
  returns and bank statements; separate recurring/contracted revenue from one-off and from
  any commodity/price-variable revenue.
- **Effect on the call:** unverifiable earnings → PASS. Earnings real but lower than asked →
  OFFER A LOWER PRICE (at the verified number × a sane multiple).

## 2. Owner-dependency & transferability — does it survive the owner leaving? (COO)
Is the business a *company*, or is it the *owner*?
- **Green flags:** a #2 / GM runs day-to-day; customers are tied to the company (contracts,
  routes), not the owner's friendships; the owner already took a step back; documented
  processes.
- **Red flags:** the owner is the top salesperson AND the scheduler AND holds the biggest
  relationships; everything is in their head; quotes/judgment only the owner can do; revenue
  likely to follow the owner out the door.
- **The test:** ask the owner to map who does what; interview the #2; check what share of
  revenue touches the owner personally; require a **seller transition period** (e.g., 3–12
  months) and **stay agreements** for key staff.
- **Effect on the call:** severe, un-mitigable owner-dependency → PASS. Mitigable with a long
  transition, a seller note on standby, an earn-out tied to retention, and stay bonuses →
  GO with those terms (often OFFER A LOWER PRICE plus structure).

## 3. Key staff & workforce — will the people stay? (COO)
- **Green flags:** tenured crew, low turnover, a credible #2, licenses held by employees who
  will stay; non-competes/non-solicits where relevant.
- **Red flags:** one irreplaceable employee; the only license-holder is the departing owner;
  high turnover; staff who'll leave on a sale; pay below market (a raise you'll have to fund).
- **The test:** review the roster, tenure, pay vs market, and who holds any required license;
  secure **stay agreements** for the key people before close.
- **Effect:** the one license-holder or rainmaker walking with no replacement → PASS or OFFER
  A LOWER PRICE plus retention structure.

## 4. Customer concentration & retention — is the revenue safe? (CFO/CMO)
- **Green flags:** no single customer > ~10%; many small recurring accounts; long tenure;
  contracts that assign to a new owner.
- **Red flags:** one customer = 20–40%+ of revenue; big accounts are the owner's personal
  relationships; contracts with change-of-control clauses; recent churn.
- **The test:** pull the customer list with revenue by account, tenure, and contract terms;
  read the top contracts for assignment/change-of-control language; call (or have the seller
  reassure) the top accounts during transition.
- **Effect:** fatal concentration with no path to diversify → PASS. Meaningful but manageable
  concentration → OFFER A LOWER PRICE and/or an earn-out tied to those accounts staying.

## 5. Systems, data & cybersecurity — what are you inheriting? (CTO)
- **Green flags:** customer/job/financial data is exportable; reasonable existing tools; basic
  security hygiene; backups exist.
- **Red flags:** everything on paper or one old PC; data trapped in a dead/proprietary system;
  no backups; shared passwords/no MFA; a custom system only the owner understands.
- **The test:** ask to see the actual systems; confirm you can **export the customer/job data**
  in a usable format; check who administers it; scope the migration before close.
- **Effect:** data you can't extract (so the customer history doesn't really convey) → PASS or
  OFFER A LOWER PRICE. Otherwise budget the migration as a cost and proceed.

## 6. Liabilities, contracts, licenses & legal tail — what follows you? (Legal)
- **Green flags:** clean litigation/tax history; an asset deal with escrow/holdback; licenses
  that transfer or pass to a qualifying employee; assignable lease at a fair rate; no
  environmental exposure.
- **Red flags:** pending litigation, tax liens, unpaid payroll/sales tax; a lease that dies on
  sale; a license only the owner can hold; environmental contamination (auto/industrial/fuel);
  worker-misclassification exposure.
- **The test:** lien/litigation/tax searches; **Phase I environmental** where relevant; lease
  and top-contract review for assignment; confirm the license path; structure as an **asset
  purchase** with **reps & warranties, indemnification, escrow/holdback, and tax clearances**.
- **Effect:** uninsurable tail or non-transferable license → PASS. Containable tail → GO with
  escrow/holdback (and OFFER A LOWER PRICE if the fix costs real money).

## Deal-structure tools that turn an "OFFER A LOWER PRICE" into a GO
When diligence finds a fixable problem, these protect the buyer without killing the deal:
- **Lower headline price** — pay the QoE-verified earnings × a sane multiple, not the asking.
- **Standby seller note** — the seller finances part of the price and gets paid *after* the
  SBA loan; it can count toward the equity injection and keeps the seller invested in a clean
  handover.
- **Earn-out** — part of the price is paid later, only if revenue/customers/earnings hold
  (great against concentration and owner-dependency risk).
- **Escrow / holdback** — part of the price is held back to cover undisclosed liabilities.
- **Seller transition period + stay agreements** — contracted owner training and key-staff
  retention.

## The overall Stage-2 call
After the six areas:
- **GO** — all areas Clear (or fixed by structure), the QoE-verified DSCR clears comfortably,
  and the owner take-home hits the ladder.
- **OFFER A LOWER PRICE** — the business is good but the asking price/terms don't reflect what
  diligence found; counter with a lower price and/or the structure tools above.
- **PASS** — any deal-killer that money and structure can't fix (fake earnings, fatal
  concentration, non-transferable license, uninsurable tail, data you can't extract).

## Re-running the council on a real listing
When you have a real company's actual financials, you can also re-run the Stage-1 council
docs against the real numbers (now scoring on verified data instead of industry assumptions)
as extra rigor — but the buy/pass decision is made here, in Stage 2, on the checks above.
