# CFO REVIEW — Niche Industrial Distribution (Fasteners / Industrial Supply with VMI)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The economics of niche industrial distribution are durable on the revenue side but structurally constrained on the balance sheet. Gross margins for fastener/MRO distributors are healthy for distribution (typically ~25–35% gross [ASSUMED]), and VMI integration creates genuine reorder stickiness, which is financially attractive. But this is the one model in most acquisition sets where the working-capital and inventory profile materially erodes free cash flow: distribution structurally ties up cash in inventory (thousands of low-unit-cost SKUs) and in accounts receivable to B2B customers on net-30/45 terms. The deal can finance — there is hard-asset collateral in inventory — but a buyer must fund both debt service AND a growing working-capital line, and net margins after that drag are thinner than the gross headline suggests. This is a hunt-worthy type only if the buyer goes in clear-eyed about cash conversion, not gross margin.

## THE TYPICAL-DEAL MATH
Representative business in this industry (NAICS 423840 — Industrial Supplies Merchant Wholesalers):

- **Typical revenue:** ~$3M–$6M [ASSUMED — pull 5 BizBuySell / BizQuest comps for NAICS 423840]. Use **$4.5M** as the representative.
- **Typical SDE band:** ~$500K–$900K [ASSUMED]. Use **$650K** representative. Note SDE margin (~14% of revenue) is healthy on paper but is BEFORE the working-capital cash drag below.
- **Normal multiple:** ~3.0–3.5x SDE [ASSUMED — verify against actual closed-deal comps for this NAICS; distribution often trades at the upper main-street range because of recurring revenue, but inventory-heavy deals can be discounted by buyers]. Use **3.25x**.
- **Enterprise price:** $650K × 3.25 = **~$2.11M** (asset/SDE basis; real deals layer in inventory at cost separately — see below).

**Inventory working capital — addressed explicitly (the structural item):**
- A $4.5M-revenue distributor at ~70% COGS carries ~$3.15M of annual product cost. At a typical distribution inventory turn of **~3–5x/year [ASSUMED — verify with industry benchmarks / NAICS financial ratios from RMA or IBISWorld]**, that implies **~$630K–$1.05M of inventory sitting on the floor** at any time. This is structural, not a one-company quirk.
- SBA deals frequently require the buyer to **purchase inventory at cost on top of the goodwill/SDE-multiple price**, or fund it via a working-capital tranche. That can add **$600K–$1M+** to "uses."
- Plus **AR**: B2B net-30/45 terms on $4.5M revenue tie up roughly **$370K–$560K [ASSUMED]** (30–45 days of sales). Partially offset by AP (the distributor also buys on terms), but the model is net cash-absorptive as it grows.

**Sources & Uses (representative, SBA 7(a)):**
- Uses: ~$2.11M enterprise + ~$0.8M inventory at cost (representative midpoint) ≈ **$2.91M**, plus closing/WC cushion ~$0.15M ≈ **~$3.06M total**.
- Sources: SBA 7(a) loan ~90% and ~10% injection. On ~$3.06M: **~$2.75M loan**, **~$306K injection** (part can be a standby seller note). Within the $5M 7(a) cap.

**Annual debt service:** $2.75M, 10-year term, ~10.5% [ASSUMED — Prime + 2.75%, verify 2026 Prime]. Annual P&I ≈ **~$445K/year** (10yr amortization at 10.5%).

**DSCR (base):** SDE $650K − market owner salary (~$110K [ASSUMED] for a GM running a $4.5M distributor) = ~$540K available for debt service. $540K / $445K = **~1.21x base DSCR** — BELOW the 1.25x lender floor at the representative midpoint, and well below the ≥1.5x ideal. This is the warning. It clears only at the favorable end (higher SDE ~$800K, or a multiple at 3.0x, or a larger standby seller note reducing serviced debt).
- **DSCR (stressed):** apply a 10% gross-margin/volume compression → SDE ~$540K, available ~$430K → DSCR **~0.97x**. The model does NOT absorb a downturn comfortably at the representative midpoint once inventory financing is in the stack.

**Owner take-home vs ladder:** $110K salary + (~$540K − $445K) ~$95K residual profit = **~$205K/founder year 1** [ASSUMED, single founder]. Below the ~$300K year-1 ladder at the representative midpoint; reaches it only at the larger/higher-SDE end or after modernization lifts margin and turns. The income ladder is reachable but not automatic, and the inventory cash drag is the thing standing in the way.

## WHAT I AM DEFERRING TO DUE DILIGENCE
- **Quality-of-earnings / add-back tie-out** on a specific seller's SDE — distribution SDE is often inflated by personal vehicle, owner's family on payroll, and inventory accounting choices (LIFO/FIFO, write-down discipline); must be tied out on real books.
- **The specific company's true working-capital gap and inventory health** — dead/obsolete/slow-moving stock, actual turns by SKU, and how much "inventory at cost" is really salable. This is the single biggest Stage-2 cash item and CANNOT be judged from outside.
- **Real customer concentration and AR aging** — whether a few accounts drive the VMI revenue, and how clean the receivables are.

## AVERAGE SCORE: 6.0 / 10

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Genuinely recurring, embedded revenue — VMI bin replenishment is consumable reorder revenue with real switching costs, the durable backbone of the cash flow.
2. Hard-asset collateral exists (inventory + AR), so the model is bankable and supports SBA financing rather than being a thin-air goodwill deal.
3. Asset-light on FIXED capital — no plant, no heavy replacement-reserve CapEx; the "heavy" part is working capital, which is at least partly financeable and improvable via better turns.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS (of the type)
1. Structural working-capital absorption — inventory (~$630K–$1.05M) plus AR ties up cash and competes with debt service for the same dollars.
2. Thin debt-service headroom at the representative midpoint (~1.21x base, ~0.97x stressed), leaving little room for a downturn or an inventory write-down.
3. Pass-through pricing on commodity input costs and competition from national MRO platforms cap margin resilience.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The defining structural financial problem of distribution is that **cash is trapped in inventory, and that trapped cash competes directly with SBA debt service for the same operating dollars.** A buyer underwrites against SDE (~14% of revenue here), but a meaningful slice of that SDE never converts to free cash because it is permanently reinvested in stock and receivables — and worse, it gets WORSE as the business grows, because growth requires more inventory and more AR before the cash comes back. Layer on that SBA lenders typically require the buyer to fund inventory at cost on top of the goodwill price (inflating "uses" by $600K–$1M) and to carry a working-capital line, and the representative-midpoint deal lands at ~1.21x DSCR — under the 1.25x lender floor and far from the 1.5x comfort line — with a stressed case under 1.0x. This is not a one-company artifact; it is true of the TYPE. The industry only works financially at the higher-SDE end of the band, with a sizable standby seller note to thin the serviced debt, or where modernization can credibly lift inventory turns and net margin fast enough to widen the DSCR cushion.

## RECOMMENDATION: MAYBE
The recurring VMI revenue and bankable collateral are real and attractive, but the structural working-capital absorption pulls the representative-midpoint deal below the DSCR floor and the year-1 income ladder, which is why this is not a clean PURSUE on economics alone. It becomes worth hunting only in a **narrower sub-segment**: target the **higher-SDE end (~$800K–$900K SDE), higher-turn, higher-margin specialty/VMI-embedded** distributors (specialty or critical-fastener niches with pricing power, not commodity steel pass-through), structured with a **substantial standby seller note** to thin serviced debt, and underwritten with modernization (e-commerce reorder + dynamic pricing + inventory-turn analytics) as the explicit lever to widen DSCR. At the commodity, lower-SDE, low-turn end, the economics do not clear at any sane price.
