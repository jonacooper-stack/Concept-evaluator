# CFO REVIEW — Industrial Water-Treatment Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The economics of this TYPE are among the most attractive in the Main-Street acquisition
universe: recurring multi-year service contracts plus consumable chemical reorders produce
sticky, predictable revenue at healthy blended margins, with low capital intensity (light
service fleet and test/dosing kit rather than plants or heavy CapEx). The recurring base
gives this industry a financeability profile most one-off trades cannot match, and at sane
Main-Street SDE multiples the typical deal services SBA debt with real headroom after a
market owner salary. The main financial caution is that the recurring service-contract
character can draw strategic and PE interest, and chemical input costs are a pass-through to
watch — but at the mid-market/regional size band the model is fragmented and SBA-priced. On
the numbers I can see from the outside, this is a PURSUE-grade industry to hunt.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Recurring, contractual, compliance-driven revenue base — predictable cash flow that banks
   well and supports DSCR.
2. Low capital intensity — asset-light service model means free cash flow isn't eaten by
   replacement reserves.
3. Clear multiple-arbitrage path — named strategics and consolidators want recurring
   route-based service books at a premium to Main-Street entry.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS (of the type)
1. Multiple creep — the recurring-contract profile is attractive enough that entry pricing
   can push past the sane SBA band, eroding day-one DSCR.
2. Chemical input-cost pass-through — margins depend on the ability to re-price chemical
   costs to customers; lag compresses the SDE margin.
3. Strategic/PE encroachment at the top of the size band — majors and consolidators can cap
   pricing power on larger accounts and bid up the better assets.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely way this becomes a bad TYPE to hunt is **multiple inflation driven by
the very recurring-revenue quality that makes it attractive.** Recurring, contractual,
compliance-mandated service revenue is precisely what strategic acquirers and PE
roll-ups pay up for; if that bid filters down into the SBA-tier size band, entry multiples
drift from ~3.0–3.5x toward 4x+, and the typical-deal math that comfortably clears 1.62x
DSCR at 3.25x compresses toward the 1.25x floor. The structural economics of the model are
sound; the risk is paying a price that hands the future cash flow to the lender. This is a
discipline-on-entry problem, not a margin problem — solvable by walking from over-priced
deals, but real, and it is the thing that would turn a good industry into a bad set of
deals.

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE
The structural economics of industrial water-treatment service are attractive to hunt: sticky
recurring contractual revenue, healthy SDE margins, low capital intensity, a typical-deal
DSCR (~1.62x base) that clears the lender floor with cushion, an income ladder that is
reachable at the mid-point and clearly scalable with price capture plus a bolt-on, and a
genuine step-up resale path to named strategics and consolidators. The discipline this
industry demands is on **entry price** — its recurring quality invites premium multiples, so
the hunt must walk from anything north of the sane ~3.0–3.5x band. Target the mid-market /
regional independent size band ($1.5M–$3.5M revenue) that sits below the national majors'
account threshold. PURSUE.
