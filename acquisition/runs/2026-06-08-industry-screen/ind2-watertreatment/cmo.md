# CMO REVIEW — Industrial Water-Treatment Service (boiler / cooling-tower / closed-loop chemical treatment & testing)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is an unusually attractive customer/demand shape: the model runs on multi-year recurring
service contracts plus reorder of consumable chemicals, serving essential, non-discretionary
needs (corrosion/scale control, Legionella prevention, equipment uptime) at facilities that
cannot rationally switch on price because switching risks expensive equipment damage and
compliance lapses. The end-market — hospitals, universities, food/bev plants, manufacturers,
large buildings — is sticky, recession-resilient, and growing with the Legionella/ASHRAE-188
regulatory tailwind. The founders' marketing-modernization edge applies broadly: the typical
regional independent has no website worth the name, no CRM, no customer compliance portal, no
SEO, and rarely raises prices — and crucially, the national majors (Veolia/Nalco, ChemTreat,
Solenis, Kurita) deliberately ignore the mid-market and regional accounts these independents
own, so there is no consolidator bidding war pricing the segment up. The chief caution is that
this is a relationship-and-technical-trust sale, not a transactional one, so marketing
modernizes lead-gen and retention/expansion rather than driving impulse purchases.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT (industry-level)
A modern operator re-positions the typical sleepy independent away from "the chemical guy who
shows up" toward **"your facility's water-compliance and uptime partner — proof on a dashboard,
not a clipboard."** The headline GTM upgrade: turn the legally-required service report into a
visible, real-time **customer compliance portal** (Legionella/ASHRAE-188 logs, corrosion
trends, chemical usage, audit-ready exports) that the facility manager, their boss, and the
risk/insurance reviewer can all see. That single move converts an invisible commodity into a
documented risk-reduction service, justifies price increases, hardens retention at renewal,
and becomes the proof asset in every new sales pitch. Secondary positioning line: **"Switch
risk is our problem, not yours — we inherit your logs and run continuity from day one,"**
which directly neutralizes the buyer's biggest objection in a sticky market.

## THE MODERNIZATION WEDGE (typical)
The upgrades that work across most operators in this industry, with cost ranges and expected
lift:
- **Customer compliance/reporting portal + digitized service reports.** Replace paper logs
  with a tablet-based field-capture and a customer-facing dashboard. Build/configure ~$15K–$60K
  on a low-code stack or vertical SaaS, plus a few hundred/month [ASSUMED]. Expected lift: this
  is primarily a **retention + price-increase + win-rate** lever, not a volume one. Plausibly
  supports 3–7% annual price increases the category historically leaves on the table and lifts
  competitive win-rate at renewal/RFP. Verify: ask 3–5 operators whether they hand customers
  any digital report today; check if any RFPs in the segment require electronic compliance
  records.
- **A real B2B website + SEO + Google Business Profile** for terms like "cooling tower water
  treatment [metro]," "Legionella compliance service," "boiler chemical treatment." Cost
  ~$5K–$20K build + ~$1.5K–$5K/mo [ASSUMED]. Expected lift: most independents rank nowhere; even
  modest local SEO can become a meaningful inbound channel because competitors aren't bidding.
  Verify: search 3 metros for these terms and count how many independents (vs Veolia/Nalco) have
  any first-page presence — likely near zero.
- **CRM + contract-renewal and reorder automation.** ~$50–$150/seat/mo [ASSUMED]. Lift: recovers
  silent churn at renewal and captures chemical-reorder revenue that currently leaks. Verify:
  ask operators how renewals are tracked today (answer is usually "the owner remembers").
- **LinkedIn / facilities-association targeted outbound + referral systems** to facility
  managers and building engineers. Low cash cost, mostly labor. Lift: opens an account-based
  channel the category essentially does not use.
- **Reviews/reputation engine.** Trivial cost; B2B services still benefit from credibility
  signals. Verify: pull 3 operators' Google review counts vs each other — almost all are in
  single digits.
The honest framing: the wedge here is **higher pricing + better retention + a working inbound
channel in an empty channel**, not viral demand creation.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
The typical competitor is a **regional independent** founded by a water-treatment chemist or
engineer, 1–10 trucks, a handful of long-tenured technicians, sticky multi-year accounts, and
marketing that stops at word-of-mouth and the owner's relationships. The named consolidators —
**Veolia Water Technologies / Nalco (Ecolab), ChemTreat (Danaher), Solenis, Kurita, and
SUEZ/Veolia** — are real and large, but they compete for **large national / multi-site
industrial accounts** and structurally under-serve the regional mid-market and single-site
commercial accounts (hospitals, universities, mid-size plants, large buildings) that the
independents own; their cost-to-serve a $30K/year account is uneconomic. There are some
buy-and-build players and distributors rolling up regional treaters, but this is **not** the
saturated SBA-tier PE bidding war seen in residential HVAC/plumbing — multiples have not been
broadly bid out of SBA range [ASSUMED — verify with NAICS 562910/325998/541380 broker comps].
**Where the air is:** local/regional digital presence (essentially vacant), the mid-market
account band the majors skip, and the compliance-reporting experience nobody delivers well.

## WHAT I AM DEFERRING TO DUE DILIGENCE
- **This seller's top-customer concentration** — a route built on one giant hospital system or
  food plant is a different risk than 60 small accounts; decides the deal, not the industry.
- **Brand-vs-owner goodwill** — whether accounts stay because of the company or because the
  retiring chemist personally signs every report and holds the technical trust; verify on the
  real listing.
- **Actual retention/churn, real review counts, and the current website/CRM state** of the
  specific target — the industry is sleepy on average, but I score the type, not one firm.
- **Whether the target's technicians/licenses transfer** with the business (a Stage-2 + legal
  item that affects continuity but not the industry's marketing attractiveness).

## AVERAGE SCORE: 8.2 / 10

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Recurring contracted revenue with very high natural switching costs — the demand shape is near-ideal for buy-and-hold.
2. Essential, regulation-backed, recession-resilient end-market with a genuine Legionella/ASHRAE-188 tailwind.
3. A clean white space: the national majors deliberately skip the mid-market, and independents have left local digital channels and the compliance-reporting experience completely unmodernized.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS (of the type)
1. It's a trust-and-technical sale — marketing modernizes lead-gen/retention/pricing but won't manufacture explosive top-line growth, so the income-ladder story leans on price + bolt-ons.
2. Goodwill in the typical firm can sit with the retiring chemist personally; if technical trust is the moat, transfer risk is real (Stage-2, but it shadows the type).
3. Distributors and buy-and-build platforms are starting to roll up regional treaters; the segment could professionalize over time, compressing the modernization edge.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The demand and competitive picture are genuinely strong, so the biggest marketing risk is not
"a PE consolidator is eating this market" (the majors structurally avoid the mid-market) nor
"demand is declining" (it's essential and regulation-tailwinded) — it is that **the moat and
the goodwill in this type of business may be lodged in a specific person's technical
credibility rather than in a transferable brand.** Accounts in this industry stay because the
chemist who has signed their compliance reports for 15 years is trusted to keep their boiler
from failing and their cooling tower from growing Legionella. That stickiness is an asset when
you own it and a landmine at the handoff: if customers were really buying the owner, a new
owner-operator with marketing skill but no water-chemistry track record can see churn at the
first renewal cycle before the modernization wedge has time to lock accounts in. The mitigant
is structural — the technical knowledge is a **bridgeable** moat held by trained/licensed
technicians who can be retained, and the compliance-portal upgrade actively converts
person-based trust into company-based, documented value — but whether that conversion happens
fast enough is the question that separates a good deal from a bad one in this otherwise
attractive type.

## QUESTIONS TO ANSWER WHILE SOURCING IN THIS INDUSTRY
1. In the target's metros, do any independents (vs Veolia/Nalco/ChemTreat) rank at all for
   "cooling tower water treatment [metro]" / "Legionella compliance service," and what do their
   Google review counts look like? (Sizes the empty-channel opportunity.)
2. Are buy-and-build platforms or distributors actively acquiring regional treaters in the
   target geography, and what multiples are they paying? (Confirms no bidding war has reached
   SBA tier.)
3. For listings in scope, how concentrated are accounts (one anchor hospital/plant vs many
   mid-size), and are technicians/licenses staying through transition? (Decides whether the
   industry's stickiness is real for that deal.)
4. How long are typical contract terms and what's the historical renewal rate and price-increase
   cadence? (Validates the pricing-power and retention thesis at the segment level.)
5. Are RFPs in the segment beginning to require electronic/audit-ready compliance records? (If
   yes, the portal wedge is not just nice-to-have — it's becoming table stakes the founders can
   own first.)

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE
This is a strong industry to hunt in. The customer shape (recurring, contracted, very sticky),
the demand (essential, regulated, recession-resilient), the competitive structure (fragmented
independents the majors deliberately skip, no SBA-tier bidding war), and the modernization gap
(no portals, CRM, SEO, or pricing discipline) all line up with the founders' edge, and the
founders' compliance/reporting background is an unusually clean fit for the single
highest-leverage upgrade. The honest caveat that keeps this from being a 9: growth here is
pricing-, retention-, and bolt-on-driven rather than explosive, and the moat can be
person-lodged, so the income ladder depends on disciplined price increases and a roll-up of
nearby routes. Source in the mid-market/regional band (single-site and multi-site commercial/
industrial accounts the majors ignore), and prioritize targets where technical staff transfer
and accounts are not anchored on one giant customer.
