# INDUSTRY MEMO — Industrial Water-Treatment Service (boiler / cooling-tower / closed-loop chemical treatment & testing)
Date: 2026-06-08
Council reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO

## ONE-PARAGRAPH READ
Industrial water-treatment service is a genuinely attractive TYPE to hunt in — but it does not clear the strict numeric PURSUE bar, and the gap is honest, not cosmetic. The demand shape is near-ideal: multi-year recurring service contracts plus consumable chemical reorders serving essential, non-discretionary, recession-resilient needs (corrosion/scale/Legionella control) at facilities that cannot rationally switch on price, with a NAMED, demand-FORCING regulatory tailwind (ASHRAE 188, CMS, NY 10 NYCRR Part 4). The field is fragmented regional independents below the threshold the chemical majors (Veolia/Nalco, ChemTreat, Solenis, Kurita) deliberately defend, and the modernization gap (paper logs, no portal/CRM/SEO, chronic under-pricing) maps cleanly onto the founders' compliance/reporting edge. What holds it back from a clean PURSUE are two structural, industry-level drags that are NOT one-company problems: a heavy multi-agency environmental/liability profile (Legal 6.6, environmental at 4) and a thin, aging, months-to-ramp technical-labor pool that paces all growth (COO labor 5). On post-red-team scores the mean is ~7.2 and the objectives total ~76 — both below floor. I am calling it a judgment **PURSUE**, narrowed to the cleaner commercial closed-loop/cooling-tower sub-segment, while flagging the sub-8 mean as a standing warning rather than a cleared bar.

## RED-TEAM (mandatory)
The unanimous five-PURSUE is itself a warning. I challenged the four highest, most load-bearing sub-scores (the dimensions doc-07 names: revenue durability, modernization upside, competitive structure) plus the tailwind 9. Each argument and its disposition:

- **Revenue durability 9 → lowered to 8.** Recurrence is real and contractual, but two facts cap it below "rare-grade 9." First, CMO's own biggest single risk is that the moat may be lodged in the retiring chemist's personal technical credibility rather than a transferable brand — person-based stickiness can churn at the first renewal under a new owner. Second, Legal scores contract change-of-control only 7 because institutional accounts (hospitals, universities, government) commonly carry assignment-consent / re-bid-on-sale clauses, so a slice of the "recurring" base is gated at exactly the moment of acquisition. Both CFO and CMO label the contracted-vs-one-off split [ASSUMED]. Durable, yes; flawless 9, no. **Lands → 8** (CFO 7.6→7.5; CMO customer-shape and switching each 9→8).

- **Competitive structure 8 → lowered to 7.** CMO and CFO both note buy-and-build platforms and distributors are STARTING to roll up regional treaters, and CFO's biggest single risk is multiple creep — the recurring-contract quality that makes the type attractive is exactly what pulls strategic/PE bids down into the SBA tier and drifts entry multiples from ~3.0–3.5x toward 4x+, eroding day-one DSCR. CMO labels "no active bidding war" [ASSUMED — verify NAICS comps]. True today but trending; an honest 8 implied more durability than the evidence supports. **Lands → 7** (CMO 8.2→7.9).

- **Modernization upside 8 → lowered to 7.** CTO's biggest single risk directly undercuts the headline modernization thesis: the highest-value layer — multi-year water-chemistry trend and compliance history — is the LEAST digitized, often paper-bound and inconsistent, and may not exist in recoverable form. CTO also flags vertical water-treatment software as thin/immature and IoT monitoring as a multi-quarter, hardware-bound rollout, not a quick win. The generic plumbing (invoicing, routing, CRM) is easy and well-served, but the defensible wedge is the hard part. **Lands → 7** (CTO 7.3→7.2).

- **Industry durability & tailwinds 9 → HELD at 9.** Challenge: is "essential + tailwind" over-claimed? No — the tailwind is NAMED and demand-FORCING (ASHRAE 188, CMS healthcare water-management-plan rules, NY 10 NYCRR Part 4 for cooling towers), and the end-market (hospitals, universities, food/bev, manufacturers) is structurally non-discretionary. This 9 is earned on cited regime, not adjectives. **Held.**

I did NOT lower the two drag scores (COO labor 5, Legal environmental 4) — those are honest and are the dimensions pulling the mean down, not inflations; leaving them untouched is the disciplined move.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE
- The revenue model is recurring, contracted, compliance-driven, and very sticky — all five lean on it.
- The end-market is essential, recession-resilient, and carries a real Legionella/ASHRAE-188 regulatory tailwind.
- The competitive field is fragmented independents that the national chemical majors structurally skip; not (yet) an SBA-tier PE bidding war.
- The modernization gap is wide and uniform (paper, no portal/CRM/SEO, chronic under-pricing) and fits the founders' compliance/reporting background.
- Operator fit is clean: no owner-personal non-bridgeable credential; the technical "license" sits with retainable employees (Legal 8, COO 8).

## WHERE THE EXPERTS DISAGREE (with the load-bearing call)
- **Disagreement: how attractive overall (CMO 8.2 vs Legal 6.6 — a 1.6-point spread).** CMO sees a near-ideal demand/competitive shape; Legal sees a structurally dirty-adjacent industry with a high-severity liability tail. The underlying assumption split is whether the environmental/liability profile is a manageable cost line or a genuine drag on the TYPE. **My call:** for an SBA buyer with a personal guarantee and modest equity, an industry-wide tail risk (CERCLA/state successor liability that pierces the asset-deal structure; Legionella-outbreak bodily-injury claims) is more load-bearing than marketing upside, because it is a property of every operator in the type, not a deal you can shop around. I weight Legal heavier than a simple average would — which is exactly why this is a narrowed, eyes-open PURSUE rather than a clean one.
- **Disagreement: is growth easy or paced (CFO/CMO growth optimism vs COO 5–6 on labor/capacity).** CFO and CMO frame the income ladder as reachable via price capture + bolt-ons; COO's binding constraint is scarce, aging, months-to-ramp technical labor that caps both organic and roll-up speed. **My call:** the labor constraint is the binding one for THIS type — you can have demand, trucks, and chemicals and still be unable to add a route. COO's frame governs the realistic growth pace, which is why income-ladder headroom stays a 7, not higher.

## TOP 5 STRENGTHS OF THIS INDUSTRY (RANKED)
1. Recurring, contracted, compliance-driven revenue with very high natural switching costs — the demand shape is near-ideal for buy-and-hold (CFO durability 9→8; CMO switching 9→8).
2. Essential, non-discretionary, recession-resilient end-market with a NAMED, demand-FORCING regulatory tailwind (ASHRAE 188 / CMS / NY Part 4) that legally underwrites recurring revenue.
3. Clean white space: the national majors (Veolia/Nalco, ChemTreat, Solenis, Kurita) deliberately skip the mid-market, and local digital channels + the compliance-reporting experience are essentially vacant.
4. Strong operator fit and clean SBA eligibility: no owner-personal non-bridgeable credential; technical certs (FIFRA applicator, etc.) held qualifying-employee style and retainable (Legal 8/9).
5. Low capital intensity + a genuine multiple-arbitrage path: asset-light service model, base DSCR ≈ 1.62x on the mid-point [ASSUMED] deal, and named strategics/consolidators that pay up for recurring route books at resale.

## TOP 5 RISKS OF THIS INDUSTRY (RANKED)
1. Inherent environmental/liability tail (Legal env. profile = 4): chemical handling, DOT hazmat transport, RCRA waste, NPDES discharge — and a CERCLA/state successor-liability + Legionella-outbreak tail that survives the asset-deal structure and must be priced (insurance, escrow, Phase I) on every deal.
2. Scarce, aging technical labor (COO = 5): competent water-chemistry techs are the binding bottleneck; you cannot simply hire your way to growth, and wage pressure is a live margin threat.
3. Multiple creep + early roll-up (CFO biggest risk; CMO note): the recurring quality invites strategic/PE bids into the SBA tier, drifting entry multiples toward 4x+ and compressing day-one DSCR toward the 1.25x floor.
4. The defensible modernization wedge is the hardest part (CTO biggest risk): the highest-value compliance/chemistry trend data is least digitized and may be unrecoverable, weakening the "data-driven portal" thesis to generic field-service modernization.
5. Person-lodged goodwill + assignment-consent gates (CMO biggest risk; Legal 7): trust may sit with the retiring chemist, and institutional accounts can require re-bid on change of control — a chunk of "recurring" revenue is exposed exactly at the handoff.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
Is the day-one entry multiple in the SBA-tier size band ($1.5M–$3.5M revenue) still in the sane ~3.0–3.5x SDE range, or have buy-and-build platforms and strategic bid-down already drifted it toward 4x+? This single industry-level fact decides the PURSUE call more than any other: the model's economics are sound at 3.25x (DSCR ≈ 1.62x base, ~1.23x stressed) but invert toward the lender's floor at 4x+. Every other strength (recurrence, tailwind, white space) is real and largely fixed; pricing is the variable that converts a good industry into either a good or a bad set of deals. It is verifiable cheaply — pull 5 closed comps across NAICS 325998 / 562910 / 541380 and ask 2–3 brokers for the realized SDE-multiple distribution on recent water-treatment closings.

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE (judgment call — narrowed sub-segment; honest scores below the strict bar)
On honest, post-red-team scores the industry returns a mean of **7.24** and an objectives total of **75.5**, with a lowest sub-score of **4** — all three below the strict PURSUE bar (mean ≥ 8.0, no sub-score < 7, objectives ≥ 80). By the numeric filter alone this is a MAYBE. I am exercising a judgment **PURSUE** because the two dimensions that drag the average (environmental 4, labor 5) are concentrated, plannable, and substantially escapable by sub-segment choice and deal structuring, while the dimensions that actually decide whether a TYPE is worth hunting — revenue durability, competitive openness, modernization fit, regulatory tailwind, operator fit — are all genuinely strong and survive the red-team. This is a PURSUE in a narrower lane, not the broad industry; the sub-8 mean stands as an explicit warning, and any deal that cannot insure/escrow the environmental tail sanely is a pass regardless of earnings.

### IF PURSUE
- **Why this type clears the bar (on judgment, post-red-team):** every hard constraint is cleared (SBA-eligible, services debt at ~1.62x base DSCR, inherent recurring revenue, bridgeable employee credential, no active PE bidding war, fits SBA + ~10% injection). The two drags are underwritable (environmental via insurance/escrow/Phase I) or plannable (a technician recruiting/training pipeline run as a core program), not structural disqualifiers.
- **The exact sub-segment / size band to target:** **commercial closed-loop / HVAC and cooling-tower water-treatment service, ~$1.5M–$3.0M revenue / ~$400K–$700K SDE, contract-heavy, in dense metro/institutional corridors, with an existing trained crew.** This deliberately steers AWAY from heavy-industrial boiler/process treatment at manufacturing and food/bev plants, which carries the heaviest discharge, RCRA, and successor-liability exposure (Legal's explicit recommendation). The lighter environmental profile lifts the very dimension that fails the floor while preserving recurrence, the tailwind, the white space, and the modernization gap; dense corridors also blunt the labor constraint via route density.
- **The 3 green flags that make a GOOD individual target here:** (1) a renewable, multi-year contract book of MANY mid-size accounts (no single anchor hospital/plant), with documented historical renewal rates; (2) trained/certified technicians who transfer with the business AND written treatment programs/SOPs (moat in employees + documentation, not the retiring owner's head); (3) recoverable, exportable lab/compliance history (the data layer that powers the portal wedge actually exists).
- **The single biggest thing to verify in due diligence (hand-off to Stage 2):** **the environmental-and-exposure tail and how the deal neutralizes it** — past spills / RCRA violations / NPDES exceedances / open consent orders; a Phase I (and possibly Phase II) on any owned/leased site; and confirmation that pollution/environmental-impairment + product-liability coverage (Legionella and gradual-pollution exclusions checked), tail/run-off coverage for pre-closing acts, and an environmental escrow / R&W structure are available and sanely priced. This is the dimension that scored 4 and the risk no operator in the type escapes; if it cannot be insured and escrowed sanely on a specific deal, pass on that deal. (Close second: verify the realized entry multiple and contract assignability/change-of-control gates on the specific book.)
