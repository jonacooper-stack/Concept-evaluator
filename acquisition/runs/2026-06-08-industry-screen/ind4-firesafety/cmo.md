# CMO REVIEW — Commercial Fire & Life-Safety Inspection / Testing (ITM)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is one of the most attractive customer/demand shapes I see in the SBA-acquisition field: the typical operator runs a **recurring book of buildings on code-mandated inspection schedules** — annual/quarterly/monthly ITM that re-occurs on a deadline whether or not the customer "wants" it, because the fire marshal and the building's insurer require documented compliance under NFPA codes (NFPA 10 extinguishers, NFPA 25 sprinklers/standpipes, NFPA 72 alarms, NFPA 96 kitchen hoods). Demand is non-discretionary and recession-resilient — a strip mall, hospital, or warehouse still has to pass inspection in a downturn. The founders' marketing-modernization edge applies broadly here because the typical small/mid independent runs paper tickets, has no automated renewal pipeline, and no digital lead capture, while the licensing moat (state fire licenses, NICET) keeps national platforms out of the SBA tier — meaning the edge can be applied without walking into a PE bidding war.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT (industry-level)
**Positioning line:** *"Never fail an inspection, never miss a deadline — your building's fire-safety compliance, handled and documented automatically."* Reframe the typical sleepy operator from "the guy who swings by once a year with a clipboard" to a **compliance-assurance partner** that guarantees the building owner/property manager is always inspection-ready and audit-ready. The headline GTM upgrade is converting a reactive, word-of-mouth, paper-ticket operation into a **renewal-driven recurring-revenue machine with a deficiency-to-repair conversion engine** on top: every mandated inspection date is a scheduled, auto-reminded touch; every found deficiency becomes a quoted repair within 24 hours; every report is a branded digital PDF the property manager can hand to their insurer and AHJ. Target the multi-site property manager / facilities buyer who values "one vendor, all my buildings, all my deadlines, one portal" — that buyer rewards organization, not the lowest extinguisher price.

## THE MODERNIZATION WEDGE (typical)
The upgrades that work across most operators in this industry, with cost ranges and expected lift:

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
The typical competitor is a **fragmented local/regional independent** behind a state-licensing/NICET moat — a retiring owner with a recurring book, paper systems, and no marketing. The active **consolidators are real and named**, but they fish the LARGE tier above the SBA buy-box: **Pye-Barker Fire & Safety** (the most aggressive national roll-up — has acquired well over 200 fire/life-safety businesses), **Summit Fire & Security / SFP Holding** (backed by CI Capital/Leonard Green-type capital), and **Cintas Fire Protection** (the uniform/facilities giant's fire division). These platforms have bid up the large, multi-branch, $5M+ revenue operators. **The air for us is the sub-$3M-revenue independent** below their typical buy-box: too small to move the needle for a national platform, still licensed-moated locally, and not yet bid up. The risk to watch is the consolidators **drifting down-market** over time as the large supply thins — but as of now the small tier is sleepy and not in a bidding war.

## WHAT I AM DEFERRING TO DUE DILIGENCE
- A *specific* seller's **top-customer concentration** (one big property-management contract vs many small accounts) and its renewal terms — decides the deal, not the industry score.
- Whether a *specific* company's goodwill sits in the **brand/recurring book vs the owner's personal relationships** with local fire marshals and property managers.
- A *specific* company's **actual renewal/retention rate, real reviews, and current website/CRM state** — the real modernization starting point.

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. **Legally-mandated, deadline-driven recurring demand** — the single most durable customer shape available in this field; renewals re-occur by code, not by goodwill.
2. **Licensing moat shields the SBA tier from the PE bidding war** — Pye-Barker/Summit/Cintas concentrate above the buy-box, leaving sub-$3M independents un-bid-up.
3. **Broad, concrete modernization gap** — paper-to-software renewal automation plus deficiency-to-repair conversion is a repeatable, high-margin lever across most operators.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS (of the type)
1. **Consolidator down-market drift** — as large supply thins, Pye-Barker et al. may push into the small tier and bid it up.
2. **Commodity-floor on basic inspection pricing** — extinguisher/basic ITM is price-shoppable enough to cap pricing power at "good," not "great."
3. **Licensing/qualifying-employee dependency** — the moat that protects you also means a departing licensed/NICET qualifier can disrupt operations (mitigable, but a real GTM/retention concern).

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The demand/competition issue most likely to make this a bad TYPE to hunt is **consolidator drift down-market**. Today the thesis rests on national platforms — Pye-Barker (200+ acquisitions and counting), Summit Fire/SFP, and Cintas Fire — confining themselves to the large, multi-branch tier and leaving the SBA-sized independent sleepy and reasonably priced. That gap is the whole reason this industry scores well on competitive structure and deal economics. But these platforms are capital-rich roll-up machines, and as the supply of large targets thins, the economically rational next move is to push acquisition criteria downward and start tucking in smaller independents — which would bid up entry multiples and compress the very margins and modernization arbitrage we're counting on. The licensing moat slows but does not stop this, because a national platform can simply hire or acquire the local qualifying credential. The mitigant is timing and geography: hunt in metros and sub-segments (e.g., specialty hood/suppression or backflow niches) where the named platforms have not yet built density, and move while the small tier is still below their stated buy-box.

## QUESTIONS TO ANSWER WHILE SOURCING IN THIS INDUSTRY
1. In the target metro, **how close are Pye-Barker / Summit-SFP / Cintas branches**, and have they done any sub-$3M tuck-ins locally — i.e., is the buy-box drifting down here yet?
2. For 3–5 representative local operators, **how do they track which buildings are due** (software vs spreadsheet/memory) and **what is their Google review-count gap** vs the metro leader — sizing the real modernization wedge?
3. What share of typical revenue is **recurring mandated ITM vs lumpy new-install/repair**, and **what is the typical deficiency-to-repair capture rate** the paper operators are leaving on the table?
4. Which **sub-segment** (sprinkler/NFPA 25, alarm/NFPA 72, hood-suppression/NFPA 96, backflow) has the **highest mandated-recurring share and lowest consolidator presence** in the target geography?
