# CFO REVIEW — Commercial Fire & Life-Safety Inspection / Testing (ITM)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This is one of the financially cleanest Main-Street TYPES I see for an SBA buy. The economics rest on **legally-mandated, deadline-driven recurring inspection revenue** (NFPA-code ITM cycles) with a high-margin, sticky inspection book, plus a "inspect → find deficiency → fix" pull-through that adds lumpier but higher-dollar repair work. Margins are solid, capital intensity is low (trucks + test gear, mostly a goodwill/recurring-book buy), and working capital is favorable on the inspection side (small, recurring B2B invoices that collect reasonably fast). The main financial tension is that valuation multiples for the genuinely recurring-revenue, software-enabled tier of this space have crept up as buyers recognize its quality — so the discipline is to buy a *sleepy, paper-based* operator at a sane SDE multiple, not a professionalized book already priced for its recurrence.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Legally-mandated recurring inspection revenue makes the base cash flow unusually durable, so DSCR holds even under stress.
2. Asset-light, low-CapEx model — most of the price is goodwill/recurring book, and little cash is tied up in inventory or reserves.
3. A real step-up exit: consolidators buy professionalized recurring fire books at higher multiples, creating genuine multiple-arbitrage on resale.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS (of the type)
1. Multiple creep — buyers increasingly price the recurrence in, so the sane-multiple deal requires finding a genuinely un-modernized seller.
2. Repair/install revenue is lumpier and more cyclical/competitive than inspections; a book skewed to install carries thinner durability.
3. A single typical deal lands just under the year-1 income rung, so the ladder depends on modernization/bolt-ons actually executing.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The structural financial risk for this TYPE is **valuation multiple creep on the recurring tier**. The very quality that makes fire ITM attractive — code-mandated, renewing, sticky revenue — is increasingly understood by brokers and buyers, which pushes asking multiples toward and past the top of the sane SDE band for any operator that has already professionalized its renewal book. If you overpay for that recurrence, the DSCR cushion that makes the type financeable erodes fast (a move from 3.0x to 3.75x at this size adds ~$50K+/yr of debt service and pulls base DSCR toward the floor). The whole thesis depends on buying a *paper-based, un-marketed* operator at a Main-Street SDE multiple and adding the recurrence-capture systems yourself — not paying a premium for a book that already runs on software.
