# CMO REVIEW — Commercial Refrigeration Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is an attractive customer/demand shape for a marketing-modernization play. The typical
business runs on recurring preventive-maintenance contracts anchoring a base of urgent,
non-discretionary emergency break-fix — grocery, c-store, restaurant, and cold-storage
customers cannot let a cooler stay down because spoiled inventory is an immediate dollar loss,
which removes price sensitivity from the emergency call. The founders' edge — modern B2B
go-to-market, FSM software, online presence — applies broadly because the industry is owned by
relationship-built, never-marketed retiring technician-owners. The one real demand-side
limitation is that this is a B2B field-service category where most growth comes from account
penetration, route density, and outbound/referral capture rather than high-volume consumer
search, so the marketing wedge is real but narrower than in a consumer home-services category.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT (industry-level)
A modern operator re-positions the typical sleepy shop from "the guy you call when the cooler
dies" to **"guaranteed-uptime cold-chain partner: monitored, contracted, food-safe, response
SLA in writing."** The headline GTM upgrade: convert one-off break-fix relationships into
contracted PM + remote-monitoring service plans with a documented response-time guarantee, then
market that uptime/food-safety guarantee to multi-location operators (regional grocery chains,
restaurant groups, 3PL cold warehouses) who care about HACCP compliance and inventory loss far
more than about the lowest hourly rate. The positioning line writes itself around protecting the
customer's most expensive perishable asset, not around selling refrigeration hours.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
The typical competitor is a fragmented local/regional independent: a former lead tech who owns
the trucks, holds the customer relationships personally, and competes on responsiveness and
loyalty rather than marketing. Above them sit national mechanical/HVAC-R players —
**EMCOR**, **Comfort Systems USA**, and refrigeration-specialist nationals like **Hussmann
(Panasonic)**, **Hill Phoenix (Dover)** on the equipment/contract side, and large facility
managers like **CoolSys** (PE-backed, Ares Management — the notable consolidator that DOES focus
on commercial refrigeration/HVAC-R) — but these concentrate on national-account and large-format
work, leaving the sub-$5M local tier largely alone. The residential-HVAC PE roll-ups
(Wrench Group, Apex Service Partners, Authority Brands) explicitly target residential, not
commercial refrigeration. The air is in the sub-$5M local/regional independent serving
multi-location regional customers — that band is consolidator-light. The one caveat to flag:
CoolSys exists and is actively rolling up, so this is not a market with zero consolidator
interest; it is one where the consolidator is fishing in a larger pond than our target tier.

## SCORES (1–10)
1.  Typical customer-base shape:        8  — PM contracts + repeat emergency break-fix across many B2B accounts; recurring/route-like by model, not one-off project work.
2.  Demand durability of end-market:    9  — Keeping food cold is essential, mandated by health/HACCP rules, and recession-resilient; grocery/c-store/cold-storage demand is non-discretionary.
3.  Pricing-power norms:                8  — Emergency calls on a failing cold asset are price-insensitive (spoiled inventory >> repair cost); retiring owners "never raised prices systematically" per the one-pager, leaving capture room.
4.  Modernization-marketing upside:     7  — Industry-wide gap is real (no FSM, no CRM, no digital per one-pager), but it's B2B field service so the marketing lift is account-penetration-driven, not consumer-search-driven.
5.  Competitive structure:              7  — Fragmented local field at the sub-$5M tier and residential roll-ups skip it, but named consolidator CoolSys (Ares-backed) IS active in commercial refrigeration, so not zero PE interest.
6.  Lead-gen channel headroom:          7  — GBP, website, and B2B outbound to facility managers are broadly open, but B2B volume is lower than consumer home-services, so headroom is good not exceptional.
7.  Brand / local-moat potential:       8  — Route density, written response-SLAs, PM contracts, and remote-monitoring plans build genuine switching costs and a defensible local moat.
8.  Demand cyclicality/seasonality:     8  — Cold assets run year-round and fail year-round; some summer load skew but not construction- or weather-gated like new-build trades.

## AVERAGE SCORE: 7.7 / 10

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Non-discretionary, price-insensitive emergency demand on assets customers cannot let stay
   down — durable, recession-resilient end-market.
2. Recurring PM + remote-monitoring service-plan model gives real switching costs and a
   contracted revenue anchor.
3. Genuinely sleepy go-to-market across the industry (no FSM/CRM/digital), so the founders' edge
   applies broadly, and residential PE roll-ups skip the category.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS (of the type)
1. CoolSys (Ares-backed) is an active commercial-refrigeration consolidator — the market is not
   consolidator-free, even if it concentrates above our tier.
2. B2B field-service marketing has lower volume leverage than consumer home-services; the
   modernization wedge is operational/account-penetration-led, not a paid-search firehose.
3. The binding growth constraint is EPA-608 technician recruiting/retention, which marketing
   cannot fix — demand capture can outrun the crew's capacity to serve it.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The demand/competition issue most likely to make this a worse TYPE than it looks is the presence
of an active, well-capitalized consolidator specifically in commercial refrigeration: CoolSys,
backed by Ares Management, has been rolling up commercial refrigeration and HVAC-R service for
years. Unlike the residential trades — where the roll-ups (Apex, Wrench, Authority Brands) leave
commercial refrigeration alone — here there IS a national buyer who understands and wants this
exact category. The mitigant is that CoolSys and the national mechanicals (EMCOR, Comfort
Systems) chase national-account and large-format contracts, leaving the sub-$5M local/regional
independent tier comparatively untouched and not yet bid up across the board. But it means the
industry is not the pristine consolidator-vacuum the one-pager implies, and a buyer must verify,
market by market, that local entry multiples have not been pulled up by CoolSys tuck-in activity
in that geography.

## QUESTIONS TO ANSWER WHILE SOURCING IN THIS INDUSTRY
1. In the specific target metro, is CoolSys (or any regional commercial-refrigeration roll-up)
   actively acquiring, and has it bid up local entry multiples above the assumed ~3.0–3.5x SDE?
2. What share of typical local operators in this segment currently sell ANY PM/service agreement
   or remote-monitoring plan vs. running pure break-fix — i.e., how much contract-conversion
   upside actually exists?
3. What is the realistic local supply of EPA-608-certified technicians, since marketing-driven
   demand growth is worthless if the crew cannot be staffed to serve it?
4. Do typical customers in this segment buy on lowest hourly rate or on response-time/uptime
   guarantee, which determines whether the uptime-partner positioning will command a premium?

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE
This is the right kind of industry to hunt in: essential non-discretionary demand, a recurring
contract + price-insensitive emergency model, genuine switching costs, and a broadly un-marketed
field where the founders' modernization edge applies. The one disciplined caveat is that this is
NOT a consolidator-free market — CoolSys (Ares-backed) is an active commercial-refrigeration
roll-up — so the narrower sub-segment to favor is the **sub-$5M-revenue local/regional
independent serving multi-location regional grocery/c-store/restaurant/cold-storage accounts**,
in metros where CoolSys is NOT actively buying. Hunt there.
