# CFO REVIEW — Commercial Refrigeration Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The economics of this TYPE are genuinely attractive to hunt. Field service of essential cold
assets pairs a contracted preventive-maintenance base with high-margin, non-discretionary
emergency repair — a durable revenue shape that banks cleanly because the collateral
(trucks, tools, parts) is hard-asset and the labor is the cost, not capital. Typical
Main-Street multiples sit in a sane SBA-financeable band, and the model is asset-light
relative to its earnings, so a representative deal services debt with comfortable headroom
after a market owner salary. The two real financial watch-items are technician wage
inflation compressing margins and the fact that earnings are people-bound rather than
asset-bound — but neither is a structural disqualifier at the industry level.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Durable two-layer revenue — contracted PM base under high-margin, non-discretionary
   emergency repair — gives stable, recession-resilient cash flow.
2. Strong, stress-tested debt-service headroom (base DSCR ≈1.83x, ≥1.37x under −20%) at a
   sane ~3.0x SDE entry multiple the consolidators haven't bid up.
3. Asset-light economics with bankable hard-asset collateral — finances cleanly and frees
   cash for modernization rather than heavy replacement reserves.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS (of the type)
1. Technician wage inflation — the central industry constraint — directly compresses the
   margin that the whole DSCR rests on.
2. Earnings are people-bound, not asset-bound; the value walks out the door each night,
   raising the practical risk behind otherwise-comfortable coverage.
3. Two-founder $500K+ ladder and resale step-up both depend on growth/bolt-on execution,
   not on the base deal economics alone.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The structural financial vulnerability of this TYPE is **technician labor cost**. The model
earns a ~24% SDE margin because skilled, EPA-608-certified refrigeration techs are the
engine — and they are scarce industry-wide. If wage inflation outruns the operator's ability
to pass through emergency/T&M pricing, the SDE margin compresses, and because DSCR is
computed off that same SDE, coverage erodes from both directions at once (lower cash, same
or rising debt service). The stress cases still hold at a −20% earnings haircut, so this is
a margin-resilience watch-item rather than a disqualifier — but it is the one structural
lever most likely to turn a comfortable typical deal into a tight one, and it is a property
of the industry, not of any single seller.
