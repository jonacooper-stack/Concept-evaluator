# Industry One-Pager (Stage-1 Industry Screen)

## The industry / business type
**Commercial refrigeration service** — independent companies that service the refrigeration
and cold-storage systems of grocery stores, convenience stores, restaurants, food
processors, cold-storage warehouses, and similar food-related customers. The work is
**dispatch field service**: recurring **preventive-maintenance (PM) contracts** on walk-in
coolers/freezers, display cases, compressor racks, and ice machines, plus **emergency
break-fix** when an essential cold asset fails (spoiled inventory makes response urgent and
non-discretionary). Technicians hold **EPA Section 608** refrigerant certification.

## Why this is a buy-and-modernize target type
A large population of these companies are owned by retiring technicians-turned-owners who
built a loyal book on relationships and never marketed, never adopted field-service software,
and never raised prices systematically. The end-market (keeping food cold) is essential and
recession-resilient, and residential-HVAC-focused PE consolidators largely **skip commercial
refrigeration**, leaving the field fragmented and sleepy.

## Revenue mechanism (why it's durable)
Recurring PM/service-agreement contracts (scheduled, contracted) anchoring a larger base of
**urgent, high-margin emergency repair** on assets customers cannot let stay down.

## Operating model (typical)
Teams of EPA-608-certified refrigeration technicians (certification held by employees,
transferable/hireable), dispatch/office staff, and an owner who typically runs sales and
scheduling. Field work done by the crew; the owner-operator manages. The central operating
constraint industry-wide is **skilled-technician recruiting and retention**.

## The "sleepy" picture (modernization upside across the industry)
Operators in this space typically have **no field-service-management software, no remote
monitoring upsell, no CRM, no digital marketing**; scheduling and invoicing are manual.
Modern FSM (e.g., ServiceTitan/BuildOps class), IoT remote-monitoring service plans, online
booking, and B2B marketing are broadly open upside.

## Competitive structure
Fragmented local/regional independents; national mechanical players and residential-HVAC PE
roll-ups concentrate elsewhere, leaving commercial refrigeration consolidator-light at the
sub-$5M tier.
