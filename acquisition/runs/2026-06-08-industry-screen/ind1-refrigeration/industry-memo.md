# INDUSTRY MEMO — Commercial Refrigeration Service

Date: 2026-06-08
Council reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO

## ONE-PARAGRAPH READ
Commercial refrigeration service is a genuinely good TYPE of business to hunt in, but it is
not a clean 8.0 PURSUE on the strict numeric floor. The revenue shape is durable and
essential (contracted preventive-maintenance under price-insensitive, non-discretionary
emergency repair on cold assets customers cannot let fail), the entry multiples sit in a
sane SBA-financeable band (~3.0x SDE), debt service covers comfortably (base DSCR ~1.83x,
holding ~1.37x under a −20% earnings haircut), and the field is broadly un-modernized with a
genuinely differentiated IoT-monitoring recurring line largely untapped. Two structural,
TYPE-level drags keep it honest: the binding industry-wide scarcity of EPA-608 certified
technicians (the COO's correctly-weighted ceiling on both delivery and growth), and the fact
that the category is NOT a consolidator vacuum — CoolSys (Ares-backed) is an active
commercial-refrigeration roll-up, just fishing above the sub-$5M local tier. Post-red-team
the mean lands at 7.2 with a sub-score below 7, under the strict floor — but the experts
broadly say PURSUE and the structural case is sound, so I recommend PURSUE on judgment in a
defined sub-segment, with the honest caveat reported below.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE
- The end-market is essential, non-discretionary, recession-resilient, and non-seasonal (cold
  assets run and fail 365 days/year).
- The revenue shape — contracted PM anchoring price-insensitive emergency break-fix — is
  durable and the right shape for a buy-and-modernize thesis.
- The field is broadly sleepy (no FSM, no CRM, no digital, no monitoring), so the founders'
  modernization edge applies across essentially the whole industry, not one lucky target.
- EPA-608 certification sits with employees and is transferable/hireable; state licenses ride
  on a qualifying employee — no non-bridgeable personal-credential trap for the founders.
- The single binding constraint of the TYPE is skilled certified-technician supply, and no
  amount of software or AI relieves it.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS DISAGREE (with the load-bearing call)
- **Consolidator posture (CMO vs the one-pager / CFO).** The one-pager and CFO treat the tier
  as a consolidator vacuum; the CMO names CoolSys (Ares Management) as an active in-category
  commercial-refrigeration roll-up. *Assumption underneath:* whether "residential roll-ups skip
  commercial refrigeration" means the whole category is untouched. **My call:** the CMO is
  load-bearing here. The category has a dedicated, well-capitalized national buyer; it is the
  sub-$5M LOCAL tier that is comparatively untouched, not the industry. I score Competitive
  Structure at 6, not 8, and make "is CoolSys buying in this metro / has it bid up local
  multiples" a gating sourcing screen.
- **Whether the labor constraint caps the industry (COO/CFO vs CMO/CTO optimism).** COO weights
  EPA-608 scarcity hard enough to land at MAYBE; CMO/CTO acknowledge it but stay at PURSUE.
  *Assumption underneath:* whether software-driven utilization gains (a one-time ~22% throughput
  lift, per COO's capacity math) are enough, or whether growth past that hits a hard labor wall.
  **My call:** the COO's frame is load-bearing for the growth ceiling but not fatal to the
  PURSUE — it defines the sub-segment (favor shops with an apprentice/helper pipeline and dense
  routes) rather than killing the type. I weight COO heavily on sourcing discipline, not on the
  binary verdict.
- **Legal at MAYBE.** Legal lands at MAYBE purely on the refrigerant environmental + tightening-
  trajectory bundle. **My call:** Legal's own analysis concedes the tightening *forces recurring
  demand* (a tailwind) and is "net-tolerable, not a deal-killer." This is a watch-item and a
  Stage-2 yard/site check, not an industry-level disqualifier; I treat it as a mild drag, not a
  PASS signal.

## TOP 5 STRENGTHS OF THIS INDUSTRY (RANKED)
1. Durable two-layer revenue — contracted PM under price-insensitive, non-discretionary
   emergency repair on assets customers cannot let stay down.
2. Sane, SBA-financeable entry economics in a tier the consolidators largely skip (~3.0x SDE
   `[ASSUMED]`, base DSCR ~1.83x, holds ~1.37x under −20% stress).
3. Broad, real modernization gap (paper + QuickBooks + manual dispatch) served by a complete
   named off-the-shelf stack — the founders' edge applies across the whole field.
4. A genuinely differentiated recurring line — IoT refrigeration monitoring (Therma/SmartSense/
   Emerson/Danfoss telemetry) — largely unexploited and fitting can't-fail assets.
5. Clean operator fit and clean SBA/legal posture — employee-held EPA-608, qualifying-employee
   state licenses, asset-deal norm, near-zero consumer/data surface.

## TOP 5 RISKS OF THIS INDUSTRY (RANKED)
1. Structural scarcity of EPA-608 certified technicians — the binding constraint on delivery
   AND growth for every company in the type (COO sub-score 4).
2. An active in-category consolidator (CoolSys/Ares) means the field is fragmented but NOT
   consolidator-free; specific metros may be bid up.
3. Technician wage inflation compressing the ~24% SDE margin that the whole DSCR rests on — and
   the margin number is `[ASSUMED]`, not yet comp-verified.
4. Modernization upside is real but the value is in data migration and crew change management,
   not the SaaS license — easy to under-estimate and botch.
5. Refrigerant environmental + tightening AIM Act / Technology Transitions trajectory — net
   demand-forcing but adds compliance load, retraining, and a yard-contamination tail.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
At the industry level the single unknown that most moves the PURSUE call is: **how much of the
typical book is genuinely recurring/contracted PM versus pure break-fix, and how tight is the
EPA-608 technician supply in the metros we would actually source in?** Those two together
determine whether this is a durable, scalable recurring-service business or a high-margin but
labor-capped break-fix shop with a ceiling. The whole modernization thesis (convert break-fix
to monitored recurring revenue, lift utilization) assumes both a convertible book and a
staffable crew; if the recurring share is thin and the local tech pool is dry, the type stays
attractive on margin but loses its growth and the case weakens toward MAYBE.

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE (on judgment; below the strict numeric floor — see note)

**Honest reporting against the bar:** post-red-team MEAN **7.20**, lowest sub-score **4**,
objectives **75/100**. This does NOT clear the strict PURSUE floor (mean ≥ 8.0, no sub-score
< 7, objectives ≥ 80). I am recommending PURSUE on judgment because four of five experts
recommend PURSUE, the structural case (durable essential revenue, financeable multiples,
broad modernization edge, clean operator/legal fit) is sound, and the two drags pulling the
score down — labor scarcity and the CoolSys consolidator — are sourcing-discipline factors
that a well-chosen sub-segment manages rather than industry-level disqualifiers. The low marks
are honest, not engineered; this is a strong-MAYBE-leaning-PURSUE, and I am calling it PURSUE
in a narrowed band, not the broad industry.

IF PURSUE:

- **3 green flags for a GOOD individual target:** (1) a real, named PM/service-agreement base

  support a monitoring upsell.
- **#1 Stage-2 due-diligence priority:** **verify the recurring-revenue reality and the local
  technician supply** — i.e., quality-of-earnings tie-out on what share of SDE is contracted
  PM vs break-fix and its renewal rate, AND the realistic local EPA-608 hiring/wage/time-to-fill
  picture plus crew retention through the sale. (Route to `10-due-diligence.md`.)
