# Industry One-Pager (Stage-1 Industry Screen)

## The industry / business type
**Calibration & test-equipment service labs** — independent **ISO/IEC 17025-accredited** labs
that calibrate measurement and test instruments (gauges, scales, torque tools, electrical/
dimensional/temperature/pressure instruments) for manufacturers, aerospace/defense suppliers,
medical-device makers, labs, and industrial customers. Quality systems (ISO 9001, AS9100,
FDA, etc.) **require instruments to be calibrated on a fixed recall cycle**, so customers must
re-calibrate on schedule. Work is done in-lab and on-site. Revenue is per-instrument
calibration fees across a large recurring **recall book**, plus repair and accreditation work.

## Why this is a buy-and-modernize target type
Owners are often metrologists who built an accredited lab and a recall book over decades and
are now retiring. Demand is **mandated by customers' own quality systems** (not discretionary)
and very sticky. The **ISO 17025 accreditation is a real barrier** that keeps casual entrants
out, and large national labs (e.g., Trescal, SIMCO/Tektronix, Transcat) concentrate on big
accounts, leaving regional labs fragmented and sleepy.

## Revenue mechanism (why it's durable)
A recurring **recall book**: each instrument must be re-calibrated every cycle (e.g.,
annually). Customers' audits force it. High retention; revenue compounds as the instrument
base grows.

## Operating model (typical)
Accredited metrology technicians, a quality manager (maintains the 17025 scope), and an owner.
The accreditation scope is tied to the lab's documented quality system and qualified staff —
so **staff/quality-system continuity** is the central operating consideration (a Stage-2
diligence item). Owner-operator manages; technicians do the bench/field work.

## The "sleepy" picture (modernization upside across the industry)
Typical labs run on **manual asset/recall spreadsheets, paper certificates, no customer recall
portal, no automated reminders, no marketing**. Recall-reminder automation, a customer asset/
certificate portal, scheduling software, and B2B marketing are broadly open upside; the
founders' compliance background fits the quality-system side.

## Competitive structure
Fragmented regional accredited labs below the national TIC platforms' threshold; consolidators
target larger labs, leaving the SBA tier sleepy.
