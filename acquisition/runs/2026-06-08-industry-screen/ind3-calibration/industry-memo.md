# INDUSTRY MEMO — Calibration & Test-Equipment Service Labs (ISO/IEC 17025-accredited)

Date: 2026-06-08
Council reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO

## ONE-PARAGRAPH READ
This is a structurally attractive TYPE — among the best demand shapes the council has scored — but it does NOT clear the strict PURSUE floor, and the honesty discipline matters here more than the headline. The recurring "recall book" is mandated by customers' own quality systems (ISO 9001/AS9100/FDA), the ISO 17025 accreditation is a genuine moat, and the modernization wedge (recall-reminder automation, customer cert portal, B2B demand-gen) is wide open across nearly every operator. Against that sit two real, industry-level drags the unanimous PURSUE glossed over: accredited metrology technicians are a thin, slow-to-train pool that caps throughput (COO 4), and the crown-jewel system of record is regulated and lock-in-prone, so the highest-value core modernization is an audit-gated quarter, not a weekend swap (CTO 5s). On honest, post-red-team numbers the industry lands at a mean of ~7.2, an objectives total of 75, and a lowest sub-score of 4 — below all three PURSUE gates. The right call is a JUDGMENT PURSUE confined to a narrow sub-segment, made with eyes open, not a clean PURSUE.

## RED-TEAM (mandatory)
I took the highest sub-scores across the five reviews and argued each was one point too high. Three arguments landed; one I held.

1. **Revenue durability — CFO 9 / CMO 9 → 8.** The "mandated" framing is real but oversold at the SBA tier. CFO defers whether the recall book is *contracted* or *merely habitual*, and both COO and CMO flag recall-capture "leakage" (instruments that go due and never re-book). Demand is audit-pressured habit, not signed contract; that is durable but a notch below a true contracted/route book. **Lower to 8.**
2. **Modernization upside — CMO 8 / COO 8 / CTO 8 → 7.** The wedge is broad, but its conversion is throttled by the two structural drags the council under-weighted: COO's thin technician bench (you can automate the recall list but you still need scoped techs to *do* the work), and CTO's finding that the single highest-value change — modernizing the regulated system of record — is an 8–16 week audit-gated migration, not a fast wrapper win. The fast wins are marketing-layer; the deepest revenue lever is slow. **Lower to 7.**
3. **Growth & exit optionality — CFO 8 / COO 8 → 7.** Named step-up buyers (Trescal, SIMCO/Tektronix, Transcat) are genuine, but organic growth past ~15–20% requires hired/scoped techs and added standards (capital + scarce labor), and the roll-up path is capital-gated. The exit is real; the runway to it is narrower than an 8 implies. **Lower to 7.**
4. **Held — Competitive structure (CMO 7 / COO roll-up).** I did NOT lower this; at 7 it already reflects the honest tension (sub-threshold fragmentation TODAY vs. down-market consolidator drift risk). Manufacturing a further cut would be gaming in the opposite direction.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE
- The recurring, mandate-forced recall book is the defining strength and rare for Main-Street services (CFO 9, CMO 9, COO self-scheduling 9).
- The modernization gap is broad and the founders' edge applies industry-wide — recall automation + customer portal + B2B marketing, with named cheap tooling (Qualer, IndySoft, GAGEtrak, HubSpot).
- ISO 17025 is a real entry moat AND not a personal owner credential — the founders need not be metrologists (Legal 8 licensing).
- Named step-up buyers create a built-in exit; consolidators sit ABOVE the SBA tier today.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS DISAGREE (and the load-bearing call)
- **CFO/CMO durability optimism vs. COO's bench ceiling.** CFO and CMO treat the recall book as near-contracted recurring revenue; COO says the beautiful demand can outrun the operator's ability to serve it because accredited techs are scarce. **Call: the labor market is the binding constraint for this TYPE.** A recurring book you cannot staff to is throttled revenue. I weight COO heaviest — this is why the industry does not clear the floor.
- **CMO/COO "fast modernization" vs. CTO's audit-gated core.** CMO/COO frame modernization as broadly fast; CTO splits it: wrapper wins are fast, but the system of record is regulated and migration is a re-validation-heavy quarter. **Call: CTO's split is correct and load-bearing** — the deepest revenue lever (modernizing the recall system itself) is slow and risky, so the modernization premium is smaller than three 8s implied.
- **Competitive structure — "sleepy/fragmented" vs. "consolidators will reach down."** **Call: both are true on a clock.** Today the tier is genuinely below the consolidators' threshold (the upside window); the same buyers are the eventual exit. Hold at 7; treat any sign of down-market drift as a signal to move on entry.

## TOP 5 STRENGTHS OF THIS INDUSTRY (RANKED)
1. Mandate-forced recurring recall book — customers cannot lapse without failing their own audit. Rarest demand shape on the board.
2. ISO 17025 accreditation is a real, institutional moat that is bridgeable via staff (not an owner credential) — barrier without a knockout.
3. Wide-open modernization wedge with named cheap tooling; recall-reminder automation is a direct, recurring-revenue lever absent at most operators.
4. Clean legal/regulatory profile: SBA-eligible (NAICS 541380), light environmental/labor/data tail, contractually limitable and insurable liability.
5. Built-in step-up exit (Trescal, SIMCO/Tektronix, Transcat) creating genuine multiple-arbitrage on a professionalized roll-up.

## TOP 5 RISKS OF THIS INDUSTRY (RANKED)
1. **Accredited-metrology-technician scarcity (COO 4)** — a thin, slow-to-train pool is the structural throughput ceiling on the whole TYPE; lose a discipline's sole qualified tech and you lose that scope.
2. **Regulated, lock-in-prone system of record (CTO 5/5)** — the recall book lives in audit-gated software; the highest-value modernization is a quarter-long re-validation program, and a botched migration can break audit traceability and the revenue you bought.
3. **Accreditation does not freely transfer on an asset sale (Legal)** — the moat and the deal risk are the same asset; the clean SBA asset structure most jeopardizes the accreditation.
4. **Multiples (~3.0–3.5x SDE) at the upper edge of SBA-sane** — durability + consolidator interest bid the good labs up; a −20% stress already dips DSCR to ~1.22x, below the lender floor.
5. **Down-market consolidator drift** — Transcat/Trescal/SIMCO moving below their current size line would bid up entry multiples during the hold.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
The single industry-level unknown that most affects the call is **the supply and cost of accredited metrology technicians by discipline in the target geographies.** Everything attractive about this TYPE — durable mandated demand, modernization upside, exit optionality — is gated by whether you can staff the bench. If qualified techs are routinely hireable/trainable at sane cost in secondary metros, the labor 4 is a manageable drag and the industry is a confident narrow PURSUE; if the pool is as thin as COO fears, the recurring book becomes throttled revenue you overpaid for, and this slides to MAYBE. This is knowable from the outside (BLS metrology occupation data, accreditation-body signatory counts, trade-school pipelines, recruiter conversations) and should be resolved BEFORE committing sourcing effort.

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE (judgment call, narrow sub-segment) — NOTE: below the strict 8.0/≥80/no-sub-score-<7 floor

Honest post-red-team numbers (mean 7.2, objectives 75, lowest sub-score 4) do NOT clear the mechanical PURSUE bar. I am nonetheless recording a **judgment PURSUE, confined to a narrow sub-segment**, because the failure is concentrated in two diagnosable, sourceable risks (technician supply and core-system portability) rather than a structural defect in the demand model — and because the demand shape and clean legal profile are genuinely rare. This is explicitly NOT a clean PURSUE; if the labor question comes back bad, it reverts to MAYBE.

### IF PURSUE
- **Why it earns a judgment yes (post-red-team):** mandate-forced recurring revenue + a real-but-bridgeable accreditation moat + a broad modernization wedge + a named exit buyer set. The drags (labor, core-system lock-in, accreditation transfer) are screenable at the listing level, not industry-fatal.
- **Sub-segment / size band (one phrase):** **general-industry / industrial-customer accredited labs, ~$1.5M–$3M revenue / ~$400K–$700K SDE, single- or regional-location, below the Transcat/Trescal/SIMCO acquisition threshold, in secondary metros with a viable technician pool** — avoid deep aerospace/defense-only labs (heaviest ITAR/EAR + master-agreement requalification overhead).
- **Green flags on a specific target:** (1) a deep, scoped bench — multiple techs per discipline, NO single-point-of-failure discipline; (2) the recall book in a modern, exportable calibration-management package (Qualer/IndySoft/GAGEtrak) with documented clean export, not a homegrown Access/Excel/paper shop; (3) diversified recall book across many small/mid B2B accounts with measurable on-time recall-capture (low leakage = headroom you can model, not a hope).
- **#1 Stage-2 diligence priority:** **accreditation continuity through the sale** — confirm with the accreditation body (A2LA/ANAB/NVLAP/PJLA) how a change of ownership in the chosen SBA structure is treated, and lock the quality manager + signatory metrologists with retention/continuity agreements, making continued accreditation a closing condition backed by a seller-note holdback. (Closely paired: verify the local accredited-technician supply, the binding ceiling on the whole TYPE.)
