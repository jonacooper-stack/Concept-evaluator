# CMO REVIEW — Calibration & Test-Equipment Service Labs (ISO/IEC 17025-accredited)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is one of the most structurally attractive customer/demand shapes I evaluate: the typical lab runs on a **recurring recall book** where customers' OWN quality systems (ISO 9001, AS9100, IATF 16949, FDA QSR) legally compel re-calibration on a fixed cycle — demand is mandated, not sold, which is the rare case where the buyer cannot lapse without failing their own audit. The customer base is normally **many small-to-mid B2B accounts** (manufacturers, aerospace/defense suppliers, medical-device makers), not a handful of whales, and ISO 17025 accreditation is a genuine entry barrier that keeps the field thin. The founders' marketing-modernization edge applies broadly because the typical lab markets itself essentially not at all — no recall portal, no automated reminders, no SEO, paper certificates — so the wedge (turn a compliance obligation into a retention/upsell engine) is wide open across most of the industry. The one caution that keeps this from a clean rave is that demand is so mandate-driven and sticky that "marketing" here is less about net-new logo acquisition and more about recall capture, certificate-portal stickiness, and B2B account expansion — real upside, but a different shape than a classic "sleepy local services" land-grab.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT (industry-level)
**Positioning line:** *"Never fail an audit again — every instrument tracked, every recall scheduled before it's due, every certificate one click away."* The typical sleepy lab sells "calibration services" as a transactional bench task; a modern operator re-positions the lab as the customer's **outsourced calibration-compliance department** — owning the asset register, the recall calendar, and the audit-ready certificate trail. **Headline GTM upgrade:** replace the paper-certificate-and-spreadsheet relationship with a **customer asset/certificate portal plus automated recall reminders**, which simultaneously (a) closes the leak of customers who forget to re-book and drift to a competitor, (b) creates a switching cost (their whole asset history lives in your portal), and (c) generates inbound expansion as customers add instruments. Secondary upgrade: a real B2B web presence targeting the specific quality-standard pain (e.g., "AS9100 calibration in [metro]") where the category currently shows almost no digital footprint.

## THE MODERNIZATION WEDGE (typical)
The wedge that works across most labs in this industry, with cost ranges and expected lift:
- **Automated recall-reminder system + booking** — instead of a tech remembering to call, the recall book drives automated email/SMS reminders 30/60/90 days out with a booking link. Cost: low — built on existing calibration-management software (e.g., **IndySoft, GAGEtrak, Qualer, ProCalV5**) or a CRM layer; ~$5K–$30K/yr [ASSUMED]. Expected lift: recall-capture/retention improvement of **3–8 points** [ASSUMED]; verify by asking 3 labs what % of due instruments actually re-book on time (industry "leakage" is the cheap tell).
- **Customer asset/certificate portal** — self-serve access to every instrument's status and downloadable certs. Cost: moderate, often a module of the platforms above or a light custom build; ~$10K–$50K [ASSUMED]. Lift: switching-cost increase + reduced admin labor; verify by checking whether 3–5 regional labs offer ANY customer login (most do not).
- **B2B demand-gen: SEO + targeted paid + LinkedIn/quality-manager outreach** — the category is nearly empty online. Cost: ~$2K–$8K/mo managed [ASSUMED]. Lift: net-new accounts in a market where competitors don't show up; verify by Googling "ISO 17025 calibration [3 metros]" and counting how many regional labs run any paid or content presence (typically near zero).
- **On-site/route optimization marketing** — package recurring on-site calibration days for clustered accounts, raising density and stickiness. Cost: low. Lift: higher revenue per route + harder to dislodge.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
The field splits cleanly. At the top are **national/global TIC (testing-inspection-certification) consolidators**: **Trescal** (PE-backed, has rolled up dozens of calibration labs globally and in the US), **SIMCO Electronics** (now under the **Tektronix/Fortive** umbrella), and **Transcat (NASDAQ: TRNS)**, a publicly traded serial acquirer that explicitly grows its calibration-services segment by buying regional labs. **Keysight, Endress+Hauser**, and large in-house OEM labs round out the big end. **Where the air is:** these consolidators concentrate on large enterprise accounts and **larger labs** as acquisition targets — they are buying $5M+ revenue platforms, not the **$1.5M–$3M SBA-tier regional lab**, which leaves a fragmented field of single-location, metrologist-owned, retiring-owner labs **below the consolidators' acquisition threshold**. So the market IS being bid up — but at a size tier above ours. The relevant risk is not that PE outbids us today; it's that an aggressive acquirer like Transcat or Trescal eventually moves down-market, or that they are the natural eventual buyer at exit (which is also the upside — a built-in step-up buyer).

## WHAT I AM DEFERRING TO DUE DILIGENCE
- **This seller's top-customer concentration** — whether the recall book is healthily diversified across many accounts or leans on one or two big OEM contracts (a deal-killer at the company level, not an industry trait).
- **Brand-vs-owner goodwill** — whether accounts re-book because of the accredited lab/quality system or because of the retiring metrologist's personal relationships; central to transferability, but a Stage-2 question.
- **Real retention/churn and actual recall-capture rate** of the specific lab, plus the true state of its asset register/CRM data quality (which determines how fast the portal/reminder wedge can be deployed).

## AVERAGE SCORE: 7.9 / 10

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. **Mandated, audit-forced recurring demand** — customers re-calibrate because their own ISO/AS9100/FDA quality systems require it, the rare case where the buyer cannot lapse without self-inflicted audit failure.
2. **A genuine entry barrier (ISO 17025 accreditation)** that thins the field and protects pricing/retention in a way most sleepy local-service categories lack.
3. **A wide-open modernization wedge** — the recall book is the ideal substrate for portal + automated reminders + B2B demand-gen, none of which the typical lab runs today, with cheap named tooling.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS (of the type)
1. **Down-market consolidator drift** — Transcat, Trescal, and SIMCO/Tektronix could move below their current size threshold and bid up the SBA tier.
2. **Demand is so mandate-locked that "marketing" is more retention/expansion than acquisition** — net-new logo growth is slower than a classic underserved-consumer market.
3. **Scaling is accreditation- and metrologist-talent-gated** — growth requires qualified technicians and quality-system continuity, not just more leads, which throttles how fast the marketing edge converts to revenue.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single demand/competition issue most likely to make this a bad TYPE to hunt is **consolidator encroachment moving down-market**. Today the SBA-tier $1.5M–$3M regional lab sits below the acquisition threshold of the active rollers — **Transcat (a public serial acquirer of regional calibration labs), Trescal (PE-backed global rollup), and SIMCO under Fortive/Tektronix)** — which is precisely why the field is still fragmented and sleepy. But these are exactly the buyers whose stated growth model is acquiring calibration labs, and as the larger targets get expensive or scarce, the rational move is to reach down. If that happens during our hold, entry multiples at the SBA tier get bid up and the "sleepy fragmented" thesis erodes. The mitigant — and it's a genuine one — is that the same dynamic makes these consolidators a **built-in step-up exit buyer**, so the downside on entry is partly the upside on exit; but a buyer must source NOW while the tier is still cheap, because this window is a function of where the consolidators draw their size line.

## QUESTIONS TO ANSWER WHILE SOURCING IN THIS INDUSTRY
1. **At what revenue/scope size do Transcat and Trescal actually start acquiring** — i.e., where exactly is the down-market threshold, and how stable is it? (Pull their last 8–12 announced US acquisitions and the target sizes.)
2. **What is the typical recall-capture leakage** — what fraction of due instruments fail to re-book on time across regional labs, since that leakage IS the size of the modernization prize? (Ask 3–5 labs / brokers.)
3. **How broad is the accreditation scope of typical SBA-tier targets**, and how transferable/expandable is it — does the scope (electrical/dimensional/temperature/pressure) match the densest local demand, and what does scope expansion cost in time and qualified staff?
4. **What multiples are SBA-tier calibration labs (NAICS 541380) actually trading at** in broker comps, and are they already creeping up from consolidator interest?

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE
This is a strong industry to hunt in. The customer/demand shape — mandated, audit-forced, recurring B2B recall revenue behind a real accreditation barrier — is rare and durable, and the founders' marketing-modernization edge applies broadly through the recall-portal/automated-reminder/B2B-demand-gen wedge that almost no SBA-tier lab runs today. The principal caution is consolidator behavior: hunt the **sub-threshold tier specifically ($1.5M–$3M revenue, single/regional accredited labs below where Transcat/Trescal/SIMCO are currently buying)**, and treat any sign that those acquirers are moving down-market as a signal to move fast on entry — while keeping in mind that the same consolidators are the natural step-up exit buyer. Score reflects honest absolute merit, not a target.
