# CFO REVIEW — Calibration & Test-Equipment Service Labs (ISO/IEC 17025-accredited)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The economics of this type are genuinely attractive on a buy-side basis: a labor- and bench-leveraged service model with a *mandated* recurring recall book (customers' own ISO 9001 / AS9100 / FDA quality systems force re-calibration on a fixed cycle), which produces durable, contracted-feeling revenue rather than one-off project lumps. Margins are healthy because the input is technician hours against fee-per-instrument, consumables are modest, and the ISO 17025 accreditation is a real entry barrier that supports pricing. The main financial cautions are at the structural level — accreditation standards/equipment are real CapEx (precision reference standards need periodic replacement and their own up-the-chain calibration), and the national TIC consolidators (Trescal, SIMCO/Tektronix, Transcat) prove a step-up buyer exists but also mean the *good* labs can attract competitive bidding. At the SBA tier this is a financeable, debt-serviceable model with a clear ladder path, and it earns a hunt.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Mandated recurring recall book — durability rare among Main-Street service businesses.
2. Healthy bench-leverage margins (~22–27% SDE) that service SBA debt comfortably (base DSCR ≈ 1.64x).
3. A real, named step-up buyer set (Trescal, SIMCO, Transcat) creating genuine multiple-arbitrage on exit.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS (of the type)
1. Multiples (~3.0–3.5x) at the upper edge of SBA-sane; consolidator interest can bid good labs up and compress DSCR.
2. Precision-standards replacement is a real recurring CapEx reserve that erodes "true" cash flow if under-funded.
3. Much of SDE may be the retiring owner's own accredited bench time — a re-hire cost that shrinks day-one cash (deferred, but structurally common).

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The structural financial risk is **valuation creep meeting CapEx reality**: because the recurring recall book and 17025 accreditation are genuinely attractive and the national TIC platforms are visibly acquisitive, the *better* labs in this segment can be priced toward or above 3.5x SDE — and at the same time the model carries a real, easy-to-overlook reserve for replacing precision reference standards and paying for their own up-the-chain calibration. If a buyer pays the high multiple and under-reserves CapEx, the base DSCR cushion that looks like 1.6x compresses fast under a modest downturn (a −20% SDE stress already dips to ~1.22x, below the lender floor). This is a *type-level* tension — durability and accreditation are exactly what bid the multiple up — not a single-company quirk, which is why it caps the multiples (6) and CapEx (6) dimensions despite an otherwise strong sheet.

## RECOMMENDATION: PURSUE
The structural economics of this type are attractive to hunt: mandated, sticky recurring revenue; healthy bench-leverage margins; clean SBA financeability with a comfortable base DSCR (~1.64x at a 3.25x / 10.5% / 10-yr representative deal); and a named, active step-up buyer set that creates real multiple-arbitrage on a professionalized roll-up. The honest cautions — multiples sitting at the high end of SBA-sane and a genuine standards-replacement CapEx reserve — keep this out of "rare/9+" territory but do not break the model. Hunt this segment with discipline on price (do not chase past ~3.5x SDE) and demand a verified CapEx reserve; the $1.5–3M-revenue, ~$400–700K-SDE band is the sweet spot, and the ladder to $500K/founder runs through recall-book growth plus a bolt-on.
