CFO REVIEW — Portable-Sanitation Rental Company (~$2.5M rev / ~$700K SDE)

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
At a ~$2.25M asking price on ~$700K SDE (~3.2x), this deal pencils comfortably on paper — DSCR runs ~1.9–2.0x after a market owner salary, and the all-in price is well inside SBA 7(a) capacity. The catch is that this is an ASSET-HEAVY business where the headline SDE is flattered by depreciation/financing add-backs and by deferred unit-replacement CapEx; "real" owner cash flow after a true fleet-replacement reserve is materially lower than $700K, and that reserve — not the debt service — is what compresses the founder take-home. The deal is a GO at this multiple, but only after a quality-of-earnings tie-out confirms the SDE survives a CapEx-normalization haircut and the rolling stock isn't separately re-priced on top of the $2.25M.

THE DEAL MATH
Revenue and normalized SDE. Revenue ~$2.5M/yr [ASSUMED — verify with 3 years of tax returns + P&L], majority recurring construction rental + scheduled servicing, with a peaky higher-margin event layer. Seller-stated SDE ~$700K (28% of revenue), which is plausible for a well-run porta-john operator (industry SDE margins commonly 20–35%; verify against 3–5 BizBuySell / broker comps). Add-back scrutiny — the ones I would pressure-test hard:
- Depreciation add-back: in an asset-heavy rental business, D&A is a REAL economic cost (units and trucks wear out and must be replaced). SDE conventionally adds back depreciation, but a buyer must subtract a true replacement reserve to get to honest distributable cash. I treat ~$80–130K/yr of the "SDE" as economically spoken-for by CapEx [ASSUMED — verify with the depreciation schedule + fleet age/condition].
- Owner salary normalization: the owner runs quoting + dispatch + contractor relationships — a real ~$90–120K GM role. If SDE is pre-salary, a market owner salary must come out before DSCR. I model cash-flow-after-market-salary, not raw SDE.
- Interest add-back on existing equipment loans: legitimate add-back only if that debt is retired at close; if rolling stock is financed and assumed, it isn't.
- Watch for personal-vehicle, fuel, phone, and related-party yard rent run through the business — common in owner-operated route businesses. [VERIFY via QoE.]

Defensible normalized cash flow. Start $700K SDE; subtract ~$100K market owner salary and ~$100K true CapEx reserve → ~$500K of honest, distributable, debt-serviceable cash flow [ASSUMED range $450–550K]. I run DSCR on this conservative figure, not on the $700K headline.

Implied multiple. $2.25M / $700K SDE = 3.2x SDE (full price). On my haircut cash-flow basis (~$500K after CapEx reserve + salary), the effective multiple is ~4.5x — still defensible because a large share of price is hard-asset collateral (units + vacuum trucks) with independent resale value, but it is NOT the bargain 3.2x implies. Main-Street rental-service deals trade ~3.0–4.0x SDE; this is mid-range. [Verify with 3–5 comps.]

SOURCES & USES (at $2.25M).
- Uses: $2.25M purchase + ~$60K closing/SBA fees + ~$100K working-capital cushion ≈ $2.41M.
- Sources: SBA 7(a) loan ~90% of price = ~$2.025M; equity injection ~10% = ~$225K (founders' cash and/or standby seller note — a seller note on full standby can count toward injection and is worth negotiating given a retiring owner); WC line ~$100K. Confirm whether rolling stock is inside the $2.25M or separately consideration — if trucks are priced separately on top, the deal silently re-trades upward.

Annual debt service. ~$2.025M at ~10.5% (Prime ~7.5% + 2.75%) [ASSUMED — verify current Prime + lender spread], 10-yr amortization → annual payment ≈ $328K/yr (~$27.3K/mo).

DSCR.
- Base: $500K normalized cash flow (after salary + CapEx reserve) ÷ $328K = ~1.52x. On raw $700K SDE ÷ $328K it's 2.13x, but that ignores CapEx and is not how I underwrite.
- Stressed (+200 bps to 12.5% → ~$356K/yr debt service; −15% revenue collapsing cash flow from $500K to ~$350K, since rental margins are operating-leverage sensitive): ~$350K ÷ $356K = ~0.98x — BELOW the 1.25x floor. The combined construction-downturn-plus-rate-spike scenario is the real fragility: a construction-cycle pullback hits the recurring base directly.
- Rate-only stress (12.5%, revenue flat at $500K): ~1.40x — fine. Revenue-only stress (−15%, rate flat): ~1.07x — thin. The business carries the debt well in normal conditions; a construction recession is what breaks it.

Owner take-home vs the income ladder. Two founders. After $328K debt service, the ~$500K normalized cash flow leaves ~$172K distributable PLUS the ~$100K market salary they pay themselves = ~$272K combined owner earnings in year 1 — roughly $136K/founder, BELOW the ~$300K/founder yr1–2 rung for two founders. This is comfortably a $300K+ single-owner-operator business but does NOT clear the two-founder ladder on day-one math; it gets there only via modernization (pricing, utilization, route density, a tuck-in) or if one founder runs it. Honest read: yr1–2 ~$136K/founder for two; the $500K/founder yr3 target requires real growth, not just inheritance.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.2 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Hard-asset collateral: a large share of the $2.25M is owned units + vacuum trucks with independent resale value, which de-risks the SBA loan and gives downside protection the cash flow alone wouldn't.
- Recurring route revenue: months-long construction placements with scheduled weekly servicing create a sticky, repeat base — far better than one-time project lumpiness, and the event layer adds high-margin upside.
- Real pricing/utilization upside: a sleepy seller who hasn't raised prices and routes by hand leaves visible margin (price increases flow ~100% to the bottom line) and fleet-utilization gains for the founders to capture.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Construction-cycle sensitivity: the recurring base is tied to construction activity; a regional building slowdown hits utilization and revenue directly, and stressed DSCR falls below 1.0x in a downturn-plus-rate scenario.
- CapEx masquerading as earnings: the depreciation add-back inflates SDE; a true unit/truck replacement reserve (~$80–130K/yr) is a hard cost that lowers real distributable cash to ~$500K.
- Two-founder income gap: at ~$136K/founder in yr1, the day-one math does not clear the ~$300K/founder ladder for two principals without growth or single-operator structure.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The deal-killer is the interaction of asset-heavy CapEx and construction-cycle revenue, and whether the headline SDE survives both. The $700K SDE leans on a depreciation add-back that, in a fleet-rental business, is not free cash — units crack and yellow, vacuum trucks need engines and pumps, and a buyer who doesn't fund ~$100K/yr of replacement is simply liquidating the asset base that secures the loan. Subtract that reserve and a market owner salary and you're underwriting ~$500K, where DSCR is a healthy 1.52x in normal times but slips to ~0.98x if a construction pullback shaves 15% off revenue while rates tick up 200 bps. Because the recurring base is cyclical demand (not contractually locked maintenance plans), that downturn scenario is not far-fetched — it's the normal construction cycle. The number that breaks this deal is the replacement-CapEx reserve the seller's SDE quietly omits, magnified by a building slowdown.

QUESTIONS / DUE-DILIGENCE ITEMS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Quality-of-earnings + CapEx normalization: tie the ~$700K SDE to 3 years of tax returns, list every add-back, and pull the depreciation schedule — what is the true annual unit-refurb + truck-replacement reserve, and what is SDE AFTER it?
- Fleet age and condition: how many units and vacuum trucks, what age/hours, what's the deferred-maintenance backlog, and is the rolling stock inside the $2.25M or separately priced on top (which silently re-trades the deal up)?
- Customer concentration and contract terms: top-1 / top-5 / top-10 contractor accounts as % of revenue, and are placements on signed terms or at-will phone orders? (Concentration >35% or all at-will materially changes durability.)
- Revenue mix and seasonality: recurring construction rental vs event vs delivery/pickup fees, and the trough-month cash-flow profile — does the WC cushion cover the seasonal low?
- AR aging on large contractor accounts: what's the net-60+ exposure, and what working capital (if any) conveys with the sale vs must be funded day one?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
At ~$2.25M / 3.2x SDE this pencils: clean SBA structure with strong fleet collateral, base DSCR ~1.52x on conservative CapEx-haircut cash flow, and real pricing/utilization upside for sleepy-operator modernization. It is a GO conditioned on QoE confirming the SDE survives a true replacement-reserve haircut and on the rolling stock NOT being separately priced above $2.25M. If diligence shows fleet age is high (large near-term CapEx), concentration above ~35% in one contractor, or the trucks add ~$300–500K on top of the asking price, this becomes a RE-TRADE to ~$1.9–2.0M to restore stressed DSCR above the floor and protect the founders' downside. For two founders the income ladder is met only via modernization/growth or single-operator structure — that's a strategy caveat, not a deal-killer.
