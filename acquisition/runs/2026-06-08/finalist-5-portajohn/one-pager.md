# Acquisition Target One-Pager

## The business
A **portable-sanitation rental company** (portable toilets / restroom trailers / temporary
fencing and related site services). It **owns a fleet** of portable restroom units and
rents them to **construction sites** (the recurring base — units stay on a job for months
with a scheduled weekly service) and to **special events** (higher-margin, peaky). Each
unit on rent is serviced on a recurring route — pumped, cleaned, restocked — by vacuum
service trucks. Revenue is the monthly/weekly rental charge per unit-in-service plus
scheduled servicing, delivery/pickup fees, and event premiums.

## Why it's available
The owner is **retiring** and is selling the operating business and its fleet of units and
service trucks.

## Representative financials [ASSUMED — verify with QoE / tax returns / 3–5 comps]
- Revenue: **~$2.5M/yr**, the majority recurring construction-rental + servicing, with an
  event-driven seasonal layer.
- SDE: **~$700K/yr**, asset-supported.
- Asking price: **~$2.25M (~3.2× SDE)** [ASSUMED]; note a meaningful share of value is the
  hard-asset fleet (units + trucks).
- Indicative SBA 7(a) structure: ~10% equity injection, 10-yr term, ~10.5% rate, personal
  guarantee; the unit fleet and vacuum trucks are strong collateral; possible separate
  consideration for rolling stock.
- Customer concentration: moderate — large contractor accounts can be sizeable. [VERIFY.]

## Revenue mechanism
Recurring **rental + scheduled-service** revenue per unit-in-service (asset-backed, route-
serviced), anchored by months-long construction placements, plus higher-margin event
rentals. Utilization of the owned fleet drives the economics.

## Operating shape
Vacuum-truck service drivers running daily routes, delivery/pickup drivers, yard staff
(cleaning/staging units), and office/dispatch. The owner handles contractor relationships,
quoting, and dispatch. **Capital-intensive**: the value and the earnings rest on the owned
fleet, its utilization, and ongoing unit replacement/refurb CapEx.

## Current go-to-market & systems (the "sleepy" picture)
Business comes from **established contractor relationships and phone orders**; **no online
quoting/ordering, no GPS dispatch/route optimization, manual billing**, no CRM, little
digital marketing. Servicing is routed by hand. The work is unglamorous and capital-heavy,
which keeps national players regional and the local field fragmented and sleepy. Digital
quoting, route/dispatch optimization, and construction-account CRM are open upside.

## What a buyer would be taking on
Inheriting and managing the service-driver crew and contractor accounts through transition;
managing fleet utilization, unit-replacement CapEx, and construction-cycle / seasonal
demand swings; replacing the owner's quoting/dispatch role; and modernizing quoting,
dispatch, billing, and marketing.
