ONE-PARAGRAPH DEAL READ
This is a fundamentally sound, asset-backed, recurring-revenue route business in a locally-fragmented field — a good acquisition profile, not a spectacular one. The construction base (months-long placements on weekly scheduled service) is genuinely sticky, the modernization thesis (ServiceCore-class route platform, GPS, digital billing, pricing discipline) is concrete and off-the-shelf, and there is no personal-credential gate. But three things keep it short of the shortlist bar: (1) the headline ~$700K SDE is depreciation/CapEx-flattered — honest distributable cash is ~$500K after a true ~$100K replacement reserve; (2) the recurring base is construction-cycle demand, not contractually locked, and stressed DSCR slips below 1.0x; and (3) the durable-looking goodwill is partly the retiring owner's personal contractor relationships and in-his-head dispatch/quoting, a real transition-walk risk. Two of five experts already flag RE-TRADE. This is a RE-TRADE candidate, not a clean GO, and it does not clear the shortlist bar.

WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE (high signal)
- The recurring construction base is genuinely sticky and survives the owner's exit at the WORK level — units stay on jobs, drivers run routes by muscle memory (CFO, CMO, COO all credit this).
- The modernization thesis is real, concrete, and off-the-shelf: route/dispatch optimization, digital billing/DSO cut, CRM/bid-chasing, pricing discipline (COO 8, CTO 8, CMO 6).
- The asset-heavy SDE is flattered by a depreciation add-back that hides ~$100K–200K/yr real replacement CapEx — honest distributable cash is materially below $700K (CFO, COO both independently land here).
- No personal-credential gate; clean SBA eligibility with hard-asset collateral (CFO, Legal).

WHERE THE EXPERTS DISAGREE (and my call)
- GO (CFO/CMO/CTO) vs RE-TRADE (COO/Legal). The split traces to ONE hidden assumption: whether the seller-tracked numbers (utilization, true CapEx, concentration, contracted-vs-at-will mix) hold up in diligence. The GO camp underwrites the headline and conditions on QoE; the RE-TRADE camp says the price/score literally cannot be set until those unmeasured numbers are verified. The RE-TRADE frame is load-bearing here: in an asset-heavy rental business, the multiple IS a function of utilization and replacement CapEx, and the seller measures neither. You cannot honestly price what isn't measured — so RE-TRADE-pending-verification is the correct posture, not GO.
- Transferability: COO/CMO score owner-dependence ~5 (relationships + quoting walk); CTO frames the same fact as a tech knowledge-transfer problem. My call: it is primarily a transition/retention risk (COO/CMO frame), only partly solvable by software. The dispatcher/office-manager single-point-of-failure (COO) compounds it. Load-bearing for THIS route business: contractor re-papering + key-staff stay agreements, not just a platform migration.

TOP 5 RISKS (RANKED)
1. CapEx masquerading as earnings (CFO, COO). The depreciation add-back hides ~$100K–200K/yr of real unit/truck replacement; subtract it and honest cash is ~$500K, making the effective multiple ~4.5x not 3.2x. Reduced by: QoE tie-out of SDE net of a true replacement reserve + the equipment schedule with fleet age/condition.
2. Construction-cycle + rate stress breaks DSCR (CFO). Stressed scenario (−15% revenue + 200bps) drops DSCR to ~0.98x, below the 1.25x floor — the recurring base is cyclical demand, not locked contracts. Reduced by: re-trade to ~$1.9–2.0M to restore stressed cushion; confirm trough-month utilization.
3. Owner-relationship + in-head dispatch transfer (CMO, COO, CTO). Contractor goodwill, quoting, and the unit-location/route map live with the seller; thin handover = accounts and routes walk. Reduced by: contracted 90-day-min (6-mo preferred) paid transition, warm intros to top-10 accounts re-papered in 30 days, route/account dataset INTO the platform as a closing condition.
4. Unmeasured fleet utilization (COO, CTO). The metric the whole P&L turns on isn't tracked; idle units could be sunk cost dragging the asset story. Reduced by: 24-month units-on-rent vs units-owned log; re-trade if soft.
5. Environmental / wastewater-disposal tail (Legal). Septage transport + on-yard staging carry CERCLA/successor exposure an asset deal doesn't fully escape; loss of disposal access is an operational kill-switch. Reduced by: Phase I ESA (Phase II if flagged), confirmed assignable/replaceable POTW disposal access, septage-hauler permit + new USDOT sequencing, tax-clearance certs, 12–18mo escrow.

TOP 3 STRENGTHS
- Hard-asset collateral (owned units + vacuum trucks) with independent resale value de-risks the SBA loan and gives downside protection the cash flow alone wouldn't.
- Recurring, route-dense, OSHA-mandated construction base with mid-job switching friction — a durable repeat book, not project lumps, plus a high-margin event layer.
- A near-textbook sleepy-ops target with off-the-shelf modernization (ServiceCore + Samsara + QBO/Stripe), real pricing/fee leakage to fix near-zero-cost, and latent fleet/route capacity that buys growth before CapEx.

THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
What is the TRUE distributable cash flow after a real replacement-CapEx reserve, and does the recurring base hold its utilization through a construction trough? Everything pivots here. If QoE shows SDE survives a ~$100K reserve haircut, the fleet is genuinely deployed (not aged-out idle inventory), top-contractor concentration is under ~35% with a real contracted share, and the seller will commit to a 6-month transition with warm account intros — then ~$500K honest cash at a re-traded price clears DSCR with cushion and this is a financeable GO. If utilization is soft, the fleet is aging into a near-term CapEx wall, or the base is all at-will phone orders glued to a departing owner, the asset story is hollow and the price has to come down or the deal dies.
