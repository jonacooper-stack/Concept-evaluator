# CMO REVIEW — Portable-Sanitation Rental Company (~$2.5M rev / ~$700K SDE)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is a genuinely durable, asset-backed book: the construction base is recurring rental + weekly scheduled service, which is contracted-ish, route-dense, and stickier than most field-service customer bases — units sit on a job for months and switching mid-project is a real hassle for the contractor. The risk is that "established contractor relationships and phone orders" can mean the goodwill is partly the retiring owner's rolodex and handshake quoting, and the event layer is fickle, low-loyalty, and price-shopped. The modernization upside is real but more operational than top-of-funnel: this category sells on availability, reliability, price, and being easy to order from, not on a glossy brand — so the founders' edge is online quoting/ordering, account-level CRM and proactive bid-chasing, reviews, and pricing discipline, not a Facebook-ads blitz. Honestly mid-to-good customer quality with a solid operational-marketing wedge, not a glaring digital-marketing goldmine.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT
Reposition from "the local porta-john guy you phone" to **"the portable-sanitation partner that quotes in minutes, shows up on schedule, and never makes a foreman chase a service truck."** Customer-facing positioning sentence: *"Clean units, on time, every week — quote and book your jobsite restrooms online in two minutes, with a service guarantee you can hold us to."* The headline of the new GTM is **reliability-as-brand for contractors plus frictionless online ordering for events**: win construction accounts on documented service reliability and an account portal (auto-reorder, add-units mid-job, one invoice), and win events on being the easiest, fastest, best-reviewed local company to book online. The brand promise is operational, not aesthetic — and that is exactly what these founders can systematize.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
The portable-sanitation field is **nationally consolidated at the top but fragmented locally**, which is the nuance that matters for this deal:

- **United Site Services (USS)** — the dominant national, PE-backed (Platinum Equity), built by aggressive roll-up. Strong in large-metro construction and big events; bureaucratic, often middling on local reviews and responsiveness. *They are the consolidator to watch.*
- **United Rentals** — added portable sanitation/site services to its national equipment-rental footprint; competes for large construction accounts.
- **Satellite Industries / PolyJohn** — these are primarily **manufacturers** of the units (relevant to CapEx/fleet replacement), not direct service competitors, but worth naming.
- **Regional/local independents** — the bulk of the field: family-owned operators (every metro has 3–8 of them) competing on price, availability, and relationship. This is the sleepy fragmented layer the thesis likes.

Demand mix matters: the **construction base is the durable, recurring, defensible revenue**; the **event layer is higher-margin but peaky, seasonal, and far more price-/review-shopped.** The competitive warning: **USS and United Rentals ARE actively acquiring** in this space, which can both (a) bid up multiples for larger/metro targets and (b) give the founders a natural strategic exit. At ~$2.5M revenue this target is likely below USS's direct acquisition appetite (they hunt larger platforms), so entry multiple pressure is moderate — but verify whether USS/United Rentals have recently bought in this specific metro, because that compresses both pricing power and the local talent pool.

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS

- Genuine sleepy under-pricing and fee leakage the founders can fix near-zero-cost for a fast 4–8% revenue lift [ASSUMED].

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- The owner personally owns contractor relationships and quoting/dispatch — goodwill-walk risk if handover is thin.
- Active national consolidators (USS/Platinum Equity, United Rentals) competing for large construction accounts and bidding up larger targets.
- Growth is fleet-gated: marketing can fill trucks but new units require CapEx, so "marketing-only" upside is real but capped.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely customer/marketing killer is **owner-relationship dependence on the construction side**. The base revenue — the very thing that makes this durable — comes from "established contractor relationships and phone orders," with the owner personally handling contractor relationships, quoting, and dispatch. If those contractor GMs/PMs do business with *him* and not with a company brand, a portfolio of accounts can quietly migrate to a competitor at the next project once he's gone, and there are no glossy contracts to stop it because much of it is at-will, project-by-project ordering. This is compounded by competition: USS and United Rentals are exactly the kind of well-resourced vendors a wavering contractor would test. The mitigant is structural — a documented 6–12 month seller transition with warm introductions to every top account, retention of the key dispatcher/route drivers, and rapidly substituting *system-based reliability and an account portal* for the owner's personal touch — but until the contracted-vs-at-will split and a named handover plan are in hand, I treat the durable-looking base as partly fragile.
