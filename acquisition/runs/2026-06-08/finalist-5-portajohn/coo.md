COO REVIEW — Portable-Sanitation Rental Company (~$2.5M rev / ~$700K SDE)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is a route-density, asset-utilization business, and route businesses largely survive the owner's exit because the *work* lives in the trucks, the drivers, and the placement schedule, not in the owner's charisma — the units stay on the jobsite whether the seller shows up Monday or not, and the weekly service tickets keep generating. The real owner-dependency here is narrower but sharp: contractor relationship-holding, custom quoting, and same-day dispatch routing done in the seller's head and on a whiteboard. The modernization upside is genuinely strong on the ops side — this is a near-textbook "no GPS dispatch, manual billing, hand-routed service, no CRM" sleepy operator where route-optimization software and digital billing convert directly into more stops per truck-day and faster cash. The binding constraints to watch are fleet utilization (is the inventory of units actually deployed and earning, or sitting in the yard?), unit-replacement CapEx, and the construction-cycle/seasonal swing that can leave trucks and units idle in a downturn.

THE TRANSITION WALKTHROUGH (first 90 days post-close)
Day one, the seller must be present and contractually retained — a route business with hand-routing and head-held contractor relationships is a cliff-edge handover otherwise. The non-negotiable is a paid seller-transition period of at least 90 days, ideally 6 months part-time, because the single most fragile asset is the seller's knowledge of *which contractor calls which week, what the negotiated rate is on the big accounts, and how the service routes are sequenced*. Week one: the founders ride routes with the service drivers and ride sales calls / phone-order intake with the seller. The work itself — pumping, cleaning, restocking units on the existing weekly cadence — does NOT break, because the drivers know their routes by muscle memory; this is the operation's saving grace and why it scores better on transition than a relationship-only services business. What breaks if mishandled: (1) the office/dispatch person — the one who actually knows the manual billing and the route sheet — is a key-person risk equal to or greater than the seller; if they walk, billing and routing seize. (2) A top contractor account tests the new owner by shopping a competitor; the founders must personally call the top 10 accounts in week one to re-paper relationships onto the company. (3) Vendors — the chemical/deodorizer supplier, the unit manufacturer (PolyJohn / Satellite / Five Peaks), the truck/pump service vendor — may have informal terms tied to the owner; confirm these convey. Month one: founders take over quoting with the seller reviewing every quote over a threshold; begin documenting the route book and contractor pricing into a CRM before that knowledge can evaporate. Month two–three: founders run dispatch with the seller on call, pilot route-optimization software against the manual routes, and stand up digital invoicing. The catch mechanism through all of this is the seller-training clause plus a stay agreement for the dispatcher/office manager and the lead driver.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The deal's earnings rest on **fleet utilization that no one is measuring and CapEx that no one is reserving for.** A portable-sanitation business is only worth its multiple if a high share of its owned units are actually on rent and earning, and if the SDE is net of the real, recurring cost of replacing units (~7–10 yr life) and vacuum trucks (~10 yr life). A sleepy seller who tracks nothing on a dashboard may be (a) carrying hundreds of idle units that are sunk cost dragging the asset story, and/or (b) reporting an SDE that has not been charged the true maintenance CapEx, making the ~3.2× look cheaper than it is. Combined with the construction-cycle exposure — where a local building slowdown idles both units and trucks while fixed costs continue — the failure mode is buying a fleet that is less utilized and more CapEx-hungry than the headline financials imply, and discovering it in the first soft quarter. This is a diligence-killable risk (the equipment schedule, unit-utilization log, and AR aging tell the truth), which is why it's a RE-TRADE lever, not an automatic NO-GO.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the **actual fleet utilization** — units-on-rent vs. units-owned, by month for the last 24 months — and what is the average age and remaining life of the unit fleet and the vacuum/delivery trucks (pull the equipment schedule)?
- Who runs **dispatch and billing** day-to-day, will that person (and the lead drivers) sign a stay agreement, and what is the seller's contracted, paid transition/training period (target 90 days minimum, 6 months part-time preferred)?
- What is the **customer concentration** on the construction side (top-5 and top-10 accounts as a share of revenue), are those relationships contracted or handshake, and which renew/recur — i.e., what walks if a contractor tests the new owner?
- What is **true maintenance CapEx** (units + trucks replaced per year, at what cost) and has SDE been charged for it, or is replacement CapEx an undisclosed add-back inflating earnings?
- Is the **yard/site leased or owner-owned**, is the lease assignable at market, and what is trough-month (off-season / construction-slowdown) utilization versus the annual average?

RECOMMENDATION: RE-TRADE
Operationally this is a fundamentally sound route business with a strong, concrete modernization thesis — but the score and the price hinge on numbers the sleepy seller doesn't track. Make it a GO conditioned on: (1) a verified equipment schedule and 24-month unit-utilization log confirming the fleet is genuinely deployed and not aged-out; (2) SDE restated net of true unit/truck replacement CapEx (~$100K–200K/yr [ASSUMED]); (3) a contracted seller-transition period of 90 days minimum (6 months part-time preferred) plus stay agreements for the dispatcher/office manager and lead drivers; and (4) the top-10 contractor accounts confirmed and personally re-papered to the company in the first 30 days. If utilization and CapEx come in soft, re-trade the price down from ~3.2× before walking.
