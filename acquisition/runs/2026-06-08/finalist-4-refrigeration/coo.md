# COO REVIEW — Commercial Refrigeration Service Company (~$3.2M rev / ~$720K SDE)

## ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
The operation survives the owner's exit IF the EPA-608-certified techs and the anchor
accounts stay — that is the whole ballgame. The actual refrigeration work is done by a
licensed, hireable crew (good: the founders never need to hold a wrench or a credential),
but the owner today personally carries sales, key-account relationships, and scheduling/
dispatch — three jobs that must be replaced on day one or the wheels wobble. The
modernization upside is genuine and large: this is a paper-and-whiteboard shop in a niche
PE consolidators skip, so field-service software, IoT remote-monitoring service plans,
and disciplined after-hours dispatch are real, capturable efficiency and margin gains —
not hand-waving. Net: a transferable, essential-service operation with a concentrated
day-one dependency on (a) the owner's replaceable-but-undocumented coordinating role and
(b) retaining a scarce skilled crew through the handover.

## THE TRANSITION WALKTHROUGH (first 90 days post-close)

**Pre-close (the deal term that makes or breaks this):** Negotiate a **6-month paid
seller-transition/consulting period** plus a tapering on-call tail to month 12. The
owner's value on day one is not the trade — it is the rolodex (which anchor grocery/
cold-storage buyer to call, which decision-maker signs the PM renewal) and the scheduling
logic in their head. Without contracted transition this is a cliff-edge handover.

**Day one:** Seller present, physically, doing introductions. The single most fragile
asset is the certified crew — if the lead tech walks, capacity drops ~25-33% overnight on
a small team. Before close I want **stay agreements** signed by the lead tech and any
senior techs (target: retention bonus ~$10-20K each, paid at 6 and 12 months [ASSUMED —
size against current comp; verify headcount and tenure]). Founders shadow the owner on
dispatch and ride along on emergency calls to learn job-type and customer cadence.

**Week one:** Founders meet — in person, owner-accompanied — the top 8-10 accounts that
drive the PM contract base and the anchor emergency relationships. The risk here is a top
customer "testing the new owner": a grocery chain facilities manager who renews PM only
because they trust the seller. Every anchor account gets a face-to-face and a written
reassurance that the crew and response time are unchanged. Office/dispatch staff retained;
no process changes yet — stability first.

**Month one:** Owner still present. Founders take over inbound sales calls and quoting
with owner backstop. Begin documenting the undocumented: build the customer/contract
list, the PM schedule, the parts-vendor list, and the after-hours on-call rotation into
written form (today it's tribal). Do NOT deploy new software yet — learn the manual flow
first so the new system mirrors reality.

**Months two-three:** Founders running sales and dispatch with owner on-call only. Begin
the field-service software rollout in parallel with the existing whiteboard (dual-run, no
cutover risk). What breaks if mishandled: a missed after-hours emergency call (a walk-in
freezer down at a grocery store = spoiled inventory + a furious anchor account), or a tech
quitting because the new owners changed pay or on-call rules. Both are caught by holding
comp/on-call terms steady through transition and over-staffing the on-call rotation early.

## STREAMLINE & MODERNIZE PLAN (specific upgrades + expected gain)

1. **Field-service management software** (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, FieldEdge, or
   commercial-refrigeration-tuned BuildOps) — replaces whiteboard scheduling, paper work
   orders, and manual invoicing. Expected gain: **+10-20% tech-hours billed** by killing
   drive-time waste, double-bookings, and un-invoiced work, plus **invoice-to-cash from
   ~30-45 days to ~7-14 days** via mobile invoicing/payment at job completion. [ASSUMED —
   range; verify by sampling current job tickets vs. invoiced hours and current DSO.]
   Cheap verify: ask the seller for 20 recent work orders and reconcile to invoices.

2. **Digital dispatch + after-hours on-call discipline** — structured on-call rotation,
   automated emergency-call routing, SLA timestamps. Expected gain: faster emergency
   response (the high-margin revenue) and the data to *prove* response time to anchor
   accounts at renewal. [ASSUMED — emergency calls are the margin engine; verify the
   T&M-vs-PM revenue split from the P&L.]

3. **IoT remote-monitoring service plans** — sensors on walk-ins/compressor racks that
   alert on temperature drift before a failure. Converts reactive break-fix into a
   premium **recurring monitoring subscription** AND catches failures early (less spoiled
   inventory = stickier customer). Expected gain: new recurring revenue line at high
   margin; **[ASSUMED] +5-15% of PM-base customers attach at $50-150/site/month** — verify
   by piloting on 3-5 friendly anchor accounts before pricing it broadly.

4. **CRM + B2B marketing** — the seller has *zero* paid acquisition and no CRM; a simple
   pipeline + targeted outreach to grocery/c-store/cold-storage chains in the territory
   opens organic PM-contract growth (covered more by CMO, but ops-relevant for capacity
   planning).

5. **Pricing/contract review** — owner likely hasn't raised PM rates in years; a
   disciplined annual escalator (3-5%) on renewals is near-pure margin. [ASSUMED — verify
   last PM-rate increase from contract dates.]

Sequencing: stabilize crew + accounts (months 0-3), then software (3-6), then monitoring
plans + marketing + pricing (6-12). Do not modernize before you stabilize.

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- **Essential, non-discretionary, year-round demand** — a downed walk-in freezer at a
  grocery store is an emergency, not a deferrable purchase; that underpins both PM renewals
  and high-margin emergency T&M, with no meaningful seasonality.
- **Large, concrete modernization wedge on a scarce resource** — FSM software adds
  ~10-20% billable capacity from the *existing* certified crew (≈1-1.5 techs of output
  with zero hiring), and IoT monitoring opens a brand-new recurring revenue line, in a
  niche consolidators ignore.
- **The credential is held by employees and is hireable/transferable** — founders manage,
  techs hold the EPA-608; no owner-credential trap, which clears the founders' hard
  constraint on operating tractability.

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- **Skilled-tech retention through transition** — the certified crew IS the capacity;
  with an [ASSUMED] ~8-tech base and no named #2, one or two departures on close cut
  throughput 12-33% and there is no quick replacement in a scarce labor market.
- **Triple owner-role replacement on day one** — sales, anchor-account relationships, and
  dispatch/scheduling all live with the departing owner and are undocumented.
- **Capacity ceiling is labor, not capital** — at ~78% [ASSUMED] utilization, growth past
  ~20% is gated on recruiting aging-out EPA-608 techs, the hardest input to source.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operation's entire deliverable capacity sits in a small group of EPA-608-certified
refrigeration technicians who are scarce, aging, and hard to replace — and the person who
currently coordinates their work, holds the anchor-account relationships, and wins the
sales is the retiring owner who leaves on close. The failure mode is not exotic: the lead
tech, sensing change-of-control uncertainty, takes a competitor's offer in month two; the
founders, still learning dispatch, can't backfill the certified hours; an anchor grocery
account's PM contract comes up for renewal and the new facilities manager — never having
met the founders — quietly shops it. Capacity drops, response times slip on the very
emergency calls that carry the margin, and the spoiled-inventory reputation risk compounds
the churn. Every dollar of the SDE assumes this crew shows up Monday. The mitigations are
known and affordable (stay-bonuses, a 6-month paid seller transition, holding comp/on-call
terms steady, over-staffing on-call early), but they are not optional — they are the deal.

## RECOMMENDATION: RE-TRADE
This is an operationally sound, essential-service business with a genuinely large
modernization wedge — but it is NOT a "GO" as a clean handoff, because day-one execution
depends on retaining a scarce certified crew and replacing three owner roles at once. Make
it a GO by contracting the transition risk into the deal: (1) a **6-month paid seller
transition/consulting agreement** with a tapering on-call tail to month 12; (2) **signed
stay agreements with the lead tech and senior techs before close**, funded with a defined
retention pool (~$10-20K each [ASSUMED]); (3) **written handover** of the account list,
dispatch logic, vendor relationships, and after-hours on-call process during the
transition; and (4) a **closing condition** that the verified EPA-608 headcount, top-
customer concentration, and PM/T&M split match the representations. With those terms it's
a strong GO; without the seller-transition and crew stay agreements, the day-one
key-person risk is too concentrated to underwrite at this price.
