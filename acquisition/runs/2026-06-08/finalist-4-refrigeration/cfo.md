CFO REVIEW — Commercial Refrigeration Service Company (~$3.2M rev / ~$720K SDE)

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
At ~$2.5M for ~$720K SDE (3.5x), this pencils with real cushion on paper because the price is low relative to earnings — but the 3.5x multiple is doing a lot of work and the SDE almost certainly overstates transferable cash flow. The owner personally runs sales, key accounts, and dispatch, so a market GM/owner salary must come out of SDE before debt service, and the recurring/PM share of revenue (the part that justifies any multiple) is unverified. The deal is financeable and the headline DSCR is comfortable, but the quality of the $720K and the top-customer concentration are the two numbers that decide whether this is a genuine 8-figure-return acquisition or a re-trade. I lean GO subject to QoE, but the score is gated by unverified earnings quality and concentration.

THE DEAL MATH

Revenue & normalized SDE. Seller-reported SDE ~$720K on ~$3.2M revenue (22.5% SDE margin — plausible for a contract-heavy commercial refrigeration shop with high-margin emergency T&M; commercial refrigeration service typically runs 15–25% SDE margin [ASSUMED — verify against 3–5 BizBuySell/industry comps]). The critical adjustment: this owner is not absentee — he runs sales, key accounts, and scheduling. SDE includes the owner's full economic contribution, so I must subtract a market replacement cost for the role the founders will actually fill. A working owner-operator/GM in commercial field service costs ~$120K–$150K fully loaded [ASSUMED — verify with comps]. Because the founders ARE the owner-operators, that salary is take-home, not a leak — but for DSCR the lender underwrites cash flow after a market owner salary, so:
- Normalized cash flow for DSCR = SDE $720K − market owner salary ~$135K = ~$585K.
Add-backs I would NOT accept until tied out: any "one-time" emergency-repair surge that is actually recurring break-fix demand dressed as non-recurring; personal vehicle/insurance/phone run through the business beyond one normalized owner truck; related-party rent if the shop/yard is owner-owned and below market (would raise true occupancy cost post-close). Add-back scrutiny is the whole ballgame here — if even $80K of the $720K is soft, normalized cash flow drops to ~$505K and every number below moves.

Implied multiple. $2.5M ÷ $720K SDE = 3.47x SDE. That is at the top of the Main-Street band (2.0–3.5x SDE) but not above it; for a contract-heavy essential-service business with EPA-608 techs in place, 3.5x is defensible, arguably fair-to-slightly-rich [ASSUMED — pull comps; refrigeration/HVAC-service deals at this size commonly clear 3.0–4.0x SDE]. On post-salary cash flow ($585K), the implied multiple is ~4.3x — the more honest lens, and it is no longer a screaming bargain.

DSCR.
- Base: $585K normalized cash flow ÷ $364K = 1.61x. Comfortable, above the 1.5x cushion target.
- Stress 1 (+200 bps to 12.5%): payment ≈ $32,930/mo, annual ≈ $395K; DSCR = 585/395 = 1.48x. Still above floor.
- Stress 2 (−15% revenue, margins hold): cash flow ≈ $585K − (15% × $720K contribution ≈ $108K) ≈ $477K; DSCR at base rate = 477/364 = 1.31x. Above 1.25x but thin.
- Stress 3 (combined +200 bps AND −15% revenue): 477/395 = 1.21x — BELOW the 1.25x floor. The deal breaks under a simultaneous rate-and-revenue shock, which a transition with anchor-account churn could trigger.
- Add-back haircut sensitivity: if $80K of SDE is disallowed, base normalized cash flow ≈ $505K, base DSCR = 505/364 = 1.39x — still fine, but the cushion erodes fast.

Owner take-home vs the ladder. With both founders as owner-operators of one business: market owner salary (~$135K, split or to one) PLUS distributable profit after debt service. Distributable ≈ normalized cash flow $585K − debt service $364K = ~$221K, on top of the $135K salary already paid = ~$356K total owner economics in year 1 [ASSUMED, base case]. For TWO founders that is ~$178K/founder year 1 — BELOW the ~$300K/founder yr1–2 target. The ladder only clears if (a) one founder runs it solo, or (b) modernization (IoT monitoring plans, raised PM pricing, recovered emergency-call margin) lifts SDE meaningfully by yr2–3. The ~$500K/founder yr3 target requires real growth, not just the inherited book. As a single-operator business it clears yr1 comfortably (~$356K to one founder); as a two-founder business it does not without growth.

- Genuinely comfortable base DSCR (1.61x) at a defensible 3.5x multiple and bankable SBA structure, with real pricing/modernization upside (under-priced PM book, IoT-monitoring service plans) the sleepy seller left untapped.
- Clean financeability and exit optionality: certified techs and contracts are the asset, the niche is fragmented and PE-skipped, and a modernized version re-sells at a step-up multiple.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Earnings quality: SDE includes the owner's hands-on sales/dispatch role; after a market salary and an honest add-back haircut, transferable cash flow could be $505K–$585K, not $720K — directly compressing DSCR and take-home.
- Customer concentration: "a few anchor accounts may be meaningful" is unquantified; if one grocery/cold-storage account is >25–35% and walks in transition, both DSCR and the price collapse.
- Combined-stress fragility: a simultaneous +200 bps rate move and −15% revenue (very possible if anchor accounts churn during owner handoff) drops DSCR to 1.21x, below the lender floor.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The deal lives or dies on the quality and transferability of the $720K SDE, and the single sharpest threat inside it is anchor-customer concentration colliding with owner-relationship dependence. The seller personally owns the key-account relationships and the dispatch function; the one-pager admits "a few anchor grocery/cold-storage accounts may be meaningful" without a number. Commercial refrigeration books are frequently carried by 2–4 large grocery or cold-storage chains. If the top account is 30–40% of revenue and that relationship is the departing owner's — not contracted with auto-renewal and assignment language — then a single account loss in year one of transition removes ~$1M+ of revenue and roughly $200K+ of contribution. That alone swings base DSCR from 1.61x toward ~1.2x and erases the founder take-home margin, turning a comfortable deal into a covenant problem. Concentration plus owner-relationship dependence is the number that breaks this deal, and it is currently a [VERIFY], not a known.

QUESTIONS / DUE-DILIGENCE ITEMS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Quality-of-earnings: which add-backs survive tax-return tie-out? Specifically, what is normalized SDE after a true market owner-operator salary, removal of personal expenses, and a market-rate occupancy cost if the building/yard is owner-related?
- Top-customer concentration: pull the customer-level revenue schedule — what % of revenue is the top 1 / top 5 / top 10 customers, are those relationships under written PM contracts with assignment and auto-renewal clauses, and what is the contract-vs-T&M revenue split (the recurring base that justifies the multiple)?
- Revenue mix and retention: what share of the $3.2M is contracted PM vs emergency T&M, and what is historical PM-contract renewal/churn over 3–5 years (to validate "stable to growing" and to model transition churn)?
- Equipment/fleet schedule: age, mileage, and condition of every service truck and major tool set, and the deferred-maintenance/replacement reserve required in years 1–2 (the hidden CapEx that wrecks DSCR).
- Working capital: what AR aging, deposits, and WC are conveyed in the sale, and what revolver size is needed to cover the T&M billing lag and after-hours payroll on day one?
- Tech retention: how many EPA-608 techs, their tenure/comp, and what stay/retention package is needed — because if the crew walks, the contracts are unservable and the earnings vanish regardless of the math.

RECOMMENDATION: GO (conditional / lean RE-TRADE)
At $2.5M / 3.5x SDE this pencils: base DSCR 1.61x, bankable SBA structure, essential non-discretionary demand, and real modernization upside. I recommend advancing to full council and QoE, but the GO is conditional on two diligence findings. If QoE confirms normalized cash flow at or above ~$560K AND top-customer concentration is under ~25% with assignable contracts, it is a clean GO at $2.5M. If concentration is >30% on owner-held relationships, or add-backs haircut SDE below ~$580K, re-trade to ~$2.1M–$2.25M (≈3.0x) and/or push a larger portion of the price into a standby/earn-out seller note tied to anchor-account retention through month 18 — which both lowers debt service and aligns the seller to the handoff.
