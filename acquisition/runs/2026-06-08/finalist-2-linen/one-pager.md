# Acquisition Target One-Pager

## The business
An independent **uniform and linen rental-route business**. It rents and launders
reusable textiles to local B2B customers on a **weekly delivery route**: work uniforms
(mechanics, food service, industrial), floor mats, shop towels, aprons, restroom linens,
and similar. The company **owns the textile inventory** and rents it; soiled items are
picked up, laundered (in-house plant or outsourced wash), and clean items redelivered on a
fixed weekly cadence under multi-year service agreements. Revenue is the weekly rental
charge per item-in-service plus replacement/loss charges and ancillary add-ons.

## Why it's available
The owner is **retiring** after building deep route density over ~30 years. It is one of
the remaining independents in a market where the national majors have consolidated.

## Representative financials [ASSUMED — verify with QoE / tax returns / 3–5 comps]
- Revenue: **~$2.8M/yr**, ~90%+ recurring weekly-route revenue under contract.
- SDE: **~$650K/yr**, stable, with deep route density (high stops-per-mile).
- Asking price: **~$2.3M (~3.5× SDE)** [ASSUMED].
- Indicative SBA 7(a) structure: ~10% equity injection, 10-yr term, ~10.5% rate, personal
  guarantee; laundry equipment, trucks, and rental textile inventory are collateral.
- Customer concentration: low; hundreds of small/mid accounts across the route.

## Revenue mechanism
Multi-year auto-renewing rental-service contracts billed weekly per item-in-service —
sticky due to switching costs, contract terms, and route convenience — plus
replacement/prep/ancillary charges.

## Operating shape
Route sales representatives (drivers who also retain/grow accounts), plant/laundry staff
(or an outsourced wash arrangement), and office/billing. Significant **owned assets**:
textile inventory in service, a laundry plant or wash relationship, and a delivery fleet.
Working capital includes the textile inventory cycle. The owner historically handles big
accounts and pricing.

## Current go-to-market & systems (the "sleepy" picture)
Growth has been **referral and route-knock** based; **paper route sheets**, no customer
portal, no online ordering, and **pricing that hasn't been raised** in years. No CRM, no
digital marketing. The "big three" (Cintas, Aramark, UniFirst) pursue larger accounts and
**ignore the small-account independents**, which remain un-modernized and do not bid each
other up aggressively.

## What a buyer would be taking on
Retaining the route reps (who hold the customer relationships) and the contract base
through transition; managing the textile-inventory working-capital cycle and laundry-plant
operations/CapEx; modernizing routing, customer portal, pricing, and upsell; and competing
for new stops against the majors' shadow.
