CFO REVIEW — Independent Uniform & Linen Rental-Route Business

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
At ~$2.3M for ~$650K of SDE (3.5×), this deal pencils on paper at SBA terms — base DSCR lands around 1.7x after a market owner salary — and the recurring, contracted, low-concentration revenue is exactly the cash-flow shape this mandate wants. But two things keep me from a clean GO: the SDE is seller-asserted and laundry-route businesses carry a real, easy-to-hide capital tail (textile inventory replacement, plant/boiler/water-treatment CapEx, and aging delivery fleet) that lives BELOW the SDE line and quietly eats DSCR; and 3.5× is at the top of the defensible Main-Street band for a business this size, not a margin of safety. The math works at 3.0× with verified earnings; at 3.5× with unverified add-backs and deferred plant CapEx it gets tight. This is a RE-TRADE, not a NO-GO.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Genuinely recurring, contracted, low-concentration revenue (~90%+ weekly route, hundreds of accounts, none >~10%) — the durable cash-flow shape the mandate targets.
- Bankable SBA structure with hard collateral (plant equipment, fleet, textile inventory) and a standby-seller-note path to ease the equity injection.
- Real, un-exploited pricing power: prices not raised in years on sticky contracts means modernization upside flows almost straight to SDE — a 3–5% price increase on $2.8M is ~$84K–$140K of near-pure margin.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Capital tail below the SDE line: plant (boiler/washers/water treatment), delivery fleet, and rental-textile replacement are three concurrent CapEx/reserve calls that a seller can defer to inflate the number being sold.
- Valuation at/above the defensible band (3.5× asserted, ~4.0× normalized) leaves no margin of safety; the deal needs a re-trade to ~3.0× to be comfortable.
- DSCR fragility under a transition-churn + rate shock (drops to ~1.02x), and the route reps — who personally hold the customer relationships — are the single point of revenue failure during handover.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The under-reserved rental-textile and deferred-plant-maintenance problem is the number most likely to evaporate the earnings and break the deal. In a uniform/linen rental business, the textiles ARE the inventory and they wear out continuously; a normal replacement reserve runs into six figures annually. A retiring owner preparing to sell has a strong incentive to under-buy replacement linen and defer boiler/washer/water-treatment maintenance for 12–24 months, which directly pumps reported SDE — the very $650K the price is built on. If true reserve-adjusted SDE is closer to $550K–$575K, the implied multiple jumps from 3.5× to ~4.0×, conservative DSCR falls to ~1.42x, and the buyer inherits a deferred capital call (re-stock textiles + plant repairs, easily $150K–$300K in year one) that lands on top of the day-one working-capital line and pushes stressed DSCR below 1.0x. The entire deal's safety margin lives in this one line item, and it is invisible without a quality-of-earnings tie-out and a physical textile/equipment inspection.

QUESTIONS / DUE-DILIGENCE ITEMS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Quality-of-earnings: tie the asserted $650K SDE to 3 years of tax returns line-by-line — which add-backs survive, and specifically what was the annual rental-textile replacement spend each of the last 3 years (is it trending DOWN into the sale)?
- Plant & fleet condition: independent inspection of boiler, washers/extractors, dryers, water-treatment, and the delivery fleet — what is the deferred-maintenance and replacement-reserve number, and is the laundry in-house or outsourced (if in-house, the CapEx exposure is far larger)?
- Real estate & rent: does the seller own the plant building, and is current rent at, above, or below market? Normalize SDE to a market lease (or price in the building separately, which would shift this to 25-yr SBA terms).
- Route-rep retention: how many route reps, what is their tenure and comp, are they under non-competes, and what % of revenue rides on the 2–3 reps with the deepest account relationships — what is the realistic transition churn if one leaves?
- Contract terms & pricing: pull the actual service agreements — auto-renew language, term length, price-escalation clauses, and how far below market current per-item pricing sits (sizing the pricing-modernization upside).
- Working capital: confirm what WC (textiles in service, AR, AP) conveys in the sale vs. what the buyer must fund day one.

RECOMMENDATION: RE-TRADE
The business is real and the revenue shape is excellent, but the price carries no margin of safety and the earnings/CapEx picture is unverified in exactly the line that matters most. This is a GO at ~$1.95M–$2.0M (≈3.0× a QoE-verified, reserve-adjusted SDE) WITH a ~$115K standby seller note inside the injection and a confirmed plant/fleet inspection showing no major near-term CapEx — at which point base DSCR sits comfortably ~1.7x+ and the deal clears the ladder for the founders with modernization upside on top. At the $2.3M ask on unverified $650K SDE with three concurrent capital tails, I will not approve it: re-trade the price down, or get a QoE that genuinely defends $650K AND an inspection that rules out deferred plant/textile CapEx. It becomes a NO-GO only if QoE shows the textile reserve was starved and true SDE is sub-$550K.
