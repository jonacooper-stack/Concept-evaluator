CFO REVIEW — Backflow-Prevention Testing & Cross-Connection-Control Compliance Company

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This is a clean, legally-mandated recurring-revenue book at a fair ~3.0x SDE multiple, and the deal services its debt with comfortable cushion — but it is SMALL. At ~$650K revenue / ~$240K SDE, the debt is trivially serviced (DSCR well above 2x), yet the entire honest financial problem is the income ceiling: after a market owner salary and debt service, a single founder clears the ~$300K yr1-2 ladder ONLY if you treat nearly all of SDE as take-home, and TWO founders cannot both clear it from this business as-is. The cash flow is real and durable; the catch is that at this size the deal is a platform/foothold, not a destination, and the whole investment thesis rests on bolt-ons or aggressive organic growth to lift the ceiling.

DSCR (cash flow after a market owner salary ÷ debt service):
- A market GM/owner salary for a sub-$1M service business: ~$80K [ASSUMED].
- Base case, stated SDE $240K − $80K salary = $160K available for debt service. DSCR = $160K ÷ $105K = **~1.52x.**
- Conservative case (normalized SDE $160K, tester already hired): $160K − $80K = $80K ÷ $105K = **~0.76x — FAILS the 1.25x floor.** This is the case that kills the deal.
- Realistic middle (normalized SDE ~$200K): $200K − $80K = $120K ÷ $105K = **~1.14x — still under floor.**
- Stressed (−15% revenue AND +200 bps rate, on the BASE case): revenue −15% ≈ knocks ~$60–75K off SDE recurring/repair; debt service at 12.5% ≈ ~$114K/yr. SDE ~$170K − $80K = $90K ÷ $114K = **~0.79x — FAILS.**
- Read: the deal pencils comfortably (1.5x) ONLY if the seller's $240K SDE is genuinely transferable with the owner's field labor cheaply replaced. The moment you (a) add a real tester cost, or (b) lose ~15% of the route in transition, DSCR drops below the 1.25x floor. The cushion is thinner than the headline 3x multiple suggests.

Owner take-home vs the income ladder (income-ceiling honesty):
- Best case: $80K market salary + distributable profit after $105K debt service. From stated SDE $240K: $240K − $105K debt = $135K total to ownership (salary + distribution), i.e., ~$135K for ONE founder — **below the ~$300K yr1-2 target, and nowhere near it for two.**
- To even approach $300K for one founder, you must grow SDE toward ~$400K+ (debt service + $300K), i.e., nearly DOUBLE the business. For two founders at $300K each you need SDE ~$700K — a ~3x lift.
- Honest conclusion: this business does NOT clear the founder-income ladder as-is for even one founder, and is structurally incapable of carrying two. It is viable ONLY as a modernization-and-bolt-on platform where the renewal-engine automation + tuck-in acquisitions of other solo testers lift the book 2–3x. The recurring, mandated nature of the revenue makes that growth plausible, but it is a thesis to be proven, not cash in hand.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single number that breaks this deal is the owner's tester labor masquerading as transferable SDE. SDE by definition adds back ONE owner's total compensation, but this owner is simultaneously the general manager AND the lead certified tester who built and runs the route. To the extent his $240K SDE reflects field-testing labor the buyer must now PAY a hired certified tester to perform (~$70–95K/yr), that portion of SDE is not discretionary earnings — it is an operating cost in disguise. If even half the owner's time was billable testing, normalized SDE drops to roughly $200K, distributable cash after a market salary and debt service falls to ~$15–20K, DSCR sinks to ~1.14x (under the lender floor), and the income ladder becomes unreachable without first doubling the book. Quality-of-earnings must tie out, hour by hour, how much of the owner's time was billable field testing versus management — that ratio, not the multiple, decides whether this deal is a 1.5x DSCR GO or a sub-floor NO-GO.

QUESTIONS / DUE-DILIGENCE ITEMS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Quality-of-earnings: tie the ~$240K SDE to 3 years of tax returns. Specifically, what share of the owner's time was BILLABLE field testing vs. management, and does the SDE add-back double-count a tester salary the buyer must now pay?
- Renewal-book composition: pull the actual renewal list — how many assemblies, at what per-test fee, with what historical renewal/retention rate? What % of revenue is recurring testing vs. one-time repair/replacement?
- Tester continuity: is there an employed certified tester besides the owner, and will they sign on? If not, what is the cost and lead time to hire/certify one, and is the owner willing to do a 6–12 month standby + seller note tied to retention?
- Water-authority & referral dependency: how much new and renewal business flows from authority referrals tied to the OWNER personally vs. the company, and what % of the route is contractually committed vs. goodwill?
- AR aging + working-capital gap: confirm billing cadence, AR days, and what working capital (if any) conveys in the sale.

I would proceed only at a RE-TRADE: price to ~2.5x on a QoE-normalized SDE (if QoE confirms ~$200K normalized, that is ~$500K, not $720K), with a meaningful standby seller note tied to 12-month route retention and a tester-continuity contingency. As a standalone income vehicle it fails the ladder for even one founder; it earns a place only as a bolt-on PLATFORM where mandated-recurring revenue + automation + tuck-ins can lift the book 2–3x. GO only if (a) QoE confirms the SDE survives a hired tester, and (b) the founders are explicitly buying a growth platform, not a finished income stream.
