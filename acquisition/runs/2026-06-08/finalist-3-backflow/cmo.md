# CMO REVIEW — Backflow-Prevention Testing & Cross-Connection-Control Compliance Co.

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is one of the best customer bases a marketing-strong buyer can inherit: a book of
legally-mandated, annually-recurring compliance tests where the customer has no choice
but to renew (the water authority requires it) and the work is too small and too
unglamorous for anyone to shop hard. Demand is non-discretionary and recession-proof —
nobody cancels a code-required test because the economy softened. The danger is that the
"book" is currently a filing cabinet in the owner's head: if the renewal list and the
authority-filing relationships don't convey cleanly, you're buying a fading memory, not a
recurring engine. The modernization upside is real but mostly OPERATIONAL (turning the
paper renewal list into an automated reminder-and-rebook machine) rather than classic
top-of-funnel demand-gen — this market doesn't need more leads, it needs to never lose a
renewal and to systematically capture the assemblies it already knows exist.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT
**Positioning sentence (to property managers):** "We keep every backflow assembly on your
properties tested, certified, and filed with the water authority — on time, automatically,
with proof — so you never get a shut-off notice or a fine." **GTM headline:** Shift from
"the guy who shows up when the city sends a postcard" to **a managed annual compliance
service** sold to multi-property owners and property-management firms as a portfolio
program (one vendor, every site, one dashboard of certificates and due dates). The whole
re-brand is *reliability and proof-of-compliance*, not price — in a mandated category the
buyer fears the fine and the liability, not the $75 test fee.

## THE MODERNIZATION WEDGE
First-90-day upgrades, in priority order. Note: in this niche the wedge is
**retention/density** first, demand-gen second — losing a renewal is the cardinal sin.

1. **Automated renewal-reminder + rebooking engine (weeks 1–4).** The single highest-ROI
   move. Get the entire renewal list off paper into a CRM/field-service tool
   (ServiceTitan, Jobber, or a backflow-specific tool like SwiftComply/Aegis) keyed to
   each assembly's annual due date, with automated email/SMS reminders to the property
   manager and auto-scheduling. Cost: **~$150–400/mo SaaS + 40–80 hrs data entry [ASSUMED
   range; verify by counting assemblies on the actual route list].** Expected lift: even a
   **3–5 point reduction in silent renewal leakage** on a recurring base is pure margin —
   if the book quietly loses 8–10%/yr to forgotten devices and we cut that in half, that's
   real recovered revenue with near-zero acquisition cost.
2. **Online booking + a property-manager portal (weeks 3–8).** Let a property manager see
   every assembly, its due date, and download certificates without phoning the owner.
   Cost: bundled in the SaaS above. Lift: stickiness — converts a transactional vendor
   into infrastructure the PM won't switch away from.
3. **Google Business Profile + reviews engine (weeks 2–6).** Competitors here are solo
   testers with no marketing — almost certainly **<10 Google reviews each [ASSUMED; verify
   by searching "backflow testing [metro]" and reading the map pack].** A simple
   post-service review-request automation gets us to 50+ reviews and ownership of the map
   pack cheaply (**~$0–200/mo**), making us the obvious call for any NEW property or PM
   who searches.
4. **Targeted outbound to property managers + water-authority "non-compliant" lists
   (weeks 6–12).** Many water authorities publish or will share lists of assemblies that
   are overdue/non-compliant — that's a pre-qualified, legally-pressured prospect list.
   Pair with direct outreach to the top regional property-management firms (CBRE, Cushman
   & Wakefield local offices, Greystar for multifamily). Cost: founder time + **~$500–1K/mo
   list/CRM tooling.** Lift: this is the organic growth lever — each PM relationship can be
   dozens of assemblies at once.
5. **Light paid search / Local Service Ads (optional, weeks 8–12).** "[Metro] backflow
   testing" is low-volume but high-intent; **~$500–1,500/mo [ASSUMED; verify CPC in Google
   Keyword Planner]** likely captures the handful of new-construction and one-off inbound
   searches competitors ignore.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
The competitive field is exactly the "sleepy fragmented" archetype the thesis wants.
- **Direct competitors: solo certified backflow testers** (independent licensed
  tradespeople, often one-truck operations) and small plumbing companies that test as a
  sideline. Each is un-marketed, paper-driven, and capacity-constrained by being a single
  certified person — none runs a renewal-automation engine or a review presence. That's
  the air: density and reliability at scale.
- **Adjacent: regional plumbing/mechanical contractors** (e.g., local Roto-Rooter
  franchises, mid-size commercial plumbers) that *can* test but treat it as low-priority
  filler — they don't chase the renewal book systematically.
- **Software, not roll-ups, is the "consolidator" here:** **SwiftComply** and **Aegis
  Backflow / TrackMyBackflow** are compliance-software platforms used by water authorities
  and testers — they're potential TOOLS to adopt, and a signal the niche is real, not
  PE-rolled. **There is no national PE consolidator bidding up backflow-testing companies**
  the way they are HVAC and plumbing platforms — the **certified-tester barrier and tiny
  per-deal size keep institutional buyers out**, which is good for entry multiple and bad
  for exit multiple (fewer step-up buyers). Verify by searching BizBuySell for "backflow"
  listings and checking whether any are broker-marketed to platforms.

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- **Demand is written into law.** Annual backflow testing is mandated and enforced by the
  water authority — the customer must renew, the only question is whether they renew with
  *us*. That is about as durable and recession-proof as small-business demand gets.
- **A genuinely fragmented, un-marketed field with no PE consolidator.** Competitors are
  solo, paper-driven testers; the certification barrier and tiny deal size keep
  institutional buyers out, so we're not bidding against a roll-up at entry.
- **The flagship modernization move is cheap and obvious.** Turning a paper renewal list
  into an automated reminder/rebook engine plus a PM portal is a few hundred dollars a
  month of SaaS and recovers leakage immediately — high-ROI, founder-executable, and it
  also builds the switching-cost moat.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- **The book may be in the owner's head, not on paper that conveys.** If the renewal list,
  the per-assembly due dates, and the water-authority filing relationships are informal,
  the asset you're paying ~3× SDE for can erode the moment he leaves.
- **Certified-tester capacity is a single point of failure.** The whole business depends on
  a state/authority-certified tester; if the owner IS that certification and a replacement
  tester can't be retained or hired quickly, revenue stops regardless of marketing.
- **Small absolute size caps the income ceiling.** At ~$240K SDE, even excellent marketing
  on the same operation won't reach the founder-income ladder alone — growth depends on PM
  portfolio wins, pricing, and bolt-ons, all of which take time.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The goodwill-and-credential walking out the door together. This business's value is the
**recurring renewal book + the water-authority filing relationships + an active certified
tester**, and in a "runs out of his head and a filing cabinet" operation all three may be
embodied in the departing owner. If the renewal list isn't a clean, exportable,
assembly-level dataset with due dates, you can't run the automated renewal engine that is
the entire modernization thesis — you'd be reconstructing the book from memory and lapsed
postcards while it churns. And because the owner is also the lead certified tester, his
exit can simultaneously remove the *ability to do the work* and the *relationships that
file it*. The deal lives or dies on (a) extracting the full renewal list into software
*before* close and (b) a transition that retains or replaces certified-tester capacity with
overlap. Get both and this is a durable annuity; miss either and the recurring revenue is a
story, not an asset.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- **What exactly is the "renewal book," and does it convey as data?** How many assemblies,
  at how many properties, owned by how many distinct customers — and is there an
  exportable list with per-assembly addresses, device types, and annual due dates, or is
  it literally a filing cabinet plus the owner's memory?
- **What is the certified-tester transition plan?** Is there a second certified tester
  already employed who stays, or is the owner the sole credential-holder? What's the
  seller's overlap/transition commitment, and how long to hire/certify a replacement in
  this state?
- **What is the real annual renewal-retention (and leakage) rate, and the current per-test
  fee vs. neighboring purveyors' going rates?** This sizes both the retention risk and the
  immediate pricing/leakage-recovery upside that drive the modernization ROI.
- **How are water-authority filing relationships structured** — is the company an approved/
  registered tester with each authority, and does that registration transfer with the
  business or attach to the individual?

## RECOMMENDATION: GO
This is a structurally attractive customer base — legally-mandated, recurring,
recession-proof, in a fragmented field with no PE roll-up bidding it up — and the
modernization thesis (paper renewal list → automated renewal/rebook engine + PM portal +
reviews) is cheap, obvious, and founder-executable. I recommend GO, conditioned on
diligence: the entire investment case rests on the renewal book conveying as clean,
exportable data and on certified-tester capacity transferring. If diligence reveals the
book is unrecoverable from the owner's head, or the owner is the sole non-transferable
credential with no path to replacement, this flips to RE-TRADE (lower price / heavy seller
note on standby / earnout tied to retained renewals) or NO-GO. Score reflects a strong,
durable book held back from higher marks by owner-dependence and a small absolute size that
caps near-term income.
