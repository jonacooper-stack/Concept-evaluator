# COO REVIEW — Backflow-Prevention Testing & Cross-Connection-Control Compliance Company

## ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is a route-based, legally-mandated annual-renewal service business whose operational
engine — the renewal list, scheduling, certificate generation, and authority filings — lives
entirely in the retiring owner's head and a filing cabinet, while the owner is also the lead
certified tester actually performing the billable work. The business itself is genuinely
sticky (every assembly must be re-tested every year by law, and the incumbent tester owns the
relationship), so the *demand* survives the owner's exit cleanly; what does NOT automatically
survive is the *capacity to deliver* it, because on the Monday after close someone with a valid
backflow-tester certification has to be in the truck running the route, and right now that
person is the seller. The modernization upside on the ops side is unusually clean and concrete:
a paper renewal list is the single easiest thing in field services to convert into an automated
recurring-revenue engine, and doing so directly lifts capture rate and capacity. The deal lives
or dies on one question — does certified-tester capacity transfer — and that is answerable with
the right structure, not a fatal flaw.

## THE TRANSITION WALKTHROUGH (first 90 days post-close)

**Day one (seller present).** The seller must be present and ideally still holding a valid
tester certification on day one. The single most important artifact to physically take control
of at close is the **renewal list** — the master record of every assembly, its serial/location,
the property owner/manager contact, the water authority it files to, and the test-due (anniversary)
date. If this lives only in the owner's spreadsheet plus his memory of "I always do the
Riverside apartments in March," month-one revenue is at risk because nobody knows what's due
when. Day one is: take the list, take the test-kit calibration records, take the login/portal
credentials for each water authority's filing system, and ride along on the route.

**Week one.** The founders shadow the tester (whether that's the seller under a transition
agreement or a retained/hired certified employee) on actual route stops to learn the physical
work cadence — drive to property, locate assembly, run the test gauge sequence, pass/fail,
paperwork, file with authority. The founders are NOT performing the test (it requires
certification), but they must learn it cold to schedule it, price it, and manage the tester.
The first re-trade risk surfaces here: if there is only ONE certified tester and it is the
departing owner, week one must already have a credentialed replacement either hired or
identified, or the route stalls the moment the seller's transition period ends.

**Month one.** Reconcile the renewal list against the actual filing calendar for the water
authorities served — confirm which months carry the heaviest test-due volume (compliance work
is often anniversary-clustered, creating seasonality inside the year). Begin standing up the
automated renewal pipeline in parallel so the manual "I'll remember to call them" process is
replaced before the founders are relying on the seller's memory. Begin contacting the top
property-management accounts to introduce the new ownership and reassure on continuity — these
PMs control many assemblies each and are the closest thing to concentration in an otherwise
diffuse book.

**The seller-training / transition period.** Because the owner is the lead tester AND the keeper
of all tribal knowledge AND the certification, a short handover is dangerous. The founders need
a **contracted seller transition of at least 6 months** [ASSUMED appropriate range: 6–12 months],
covering (a) tester ride-alongs until a retained/new certified tester is fully productive,
(b) a complete walk of the renewal list and filing calendar, and (c) warm introductions to every
water authority and top PM. Verify by writing the transition scope and tester-certification
contingency directly into the LOI/APA.

## STREAMLINE & MODERNIZE PLAN
In priority order, with expected gain:

1. **Automated recurring-renewal engine (highest ROI).** Convert the paper/spreadsheet renewal
   list into a database with each assembly's anniversary date, and fire automated
   reminder/booking outreach (email/SMS) to property managers ahead of each test-due date, with
   online scheduling. Expected gain: lift the annual **renewal capture rate** — the share of
   due assemblies that actually get re-booked and tested — by **[ASSUMED 5–15 points]**, because
   manual incumbents routinely lose tests they simply forgot to chase. On a ~$650K book this is
   the single largest dollar lever. Verify cheaply by asking the seller for last year's
   tested-vs-due count (if he can't produce it, that's itself the opportunity).

2. **Field-service / compliance software** (e.g., a backflow-specific platform — there are niche
   tools that auto-format and e-file certifications to water authorities). Expected gain: cut
   per-test administrative/filing time from **[ASSUMED 15–25 min of paperwork per device]** to
   **[ASSUMED <5 min]**, freeing tester windshield-and-wrench time, i.e., more tests/day on the
   same headcount. Verify by timing a few of the seller's current filings.

3. **Digital invoicing + card/ACH payments.** Replace paper invoices; compress days-sales-
   outstanding and cut the office labor of chasing checks. Expected gain: faster cash conversion
   and fewer admin hours [ASSUMED 0.25–0.5 FTE of admin time freed].

4. **CRM + property-manager portal.** Treat PMs as accounts (each controls many assemblies);
   makes the book defensible and enables upsell of repair/rebuild work at the point of a failed
   test. Expected gain: higher repair-conversion and stickier multi-assembly accounts.

5. **Review presence / SEO / water-authority referral cultivation.** The book grows by being the
   default tester; capturing reviews and local search converts the "who do I call" moment.

6. **Tester-capacity scaling.** Because the binding constraint is *certified labor*, the ops play
   that unlocks growth is sponsoring apprentices through certification (a weeks-to-months,
   bridgeable credential) to add route capacity — this is both a risk mitigant and the growth lever.

## CAPACITY MATH
Show the work. The binding bottleneck is **certified-tester hours**, not trucks or facility.

- Assume one productive certified tester works **[ASSUMED]** ~6 billable field hours/day × 5
  days = 30 field hours/week, ~46 working weeks/year ≈ **~1,380 field hours/year**.
- A routine backflow test, with drive time between clustered commercial/multi-family stops,
  runs **[ASSUMED 0.75–1.5 hr per assembly all-in]**; call it ~1.0 hr blended.
- That implies one tester can perform on the order of **~1,300–1,400 tests/year**.
- At a representative ~$75–$100 per-test fee [ASSUMED — verify vs local authority fee norms],
  testing-only revenue per tester is **~$100K–$140K/year**, plus the repair/replacement pull-
  through on failures (industry fail/rebuild rates [ASSUMED 5–15%] of devices, at materially
  higher ticket).
- Against **~$650K total revenue**, with the majority recurring testing, the math implies the
  current operation is running roughly **2 tester-equivalents** of capacity (the owner plus
  one), i.e., the business is **near its current certified-labor ceiling** — consistent with a
  one-or-two-tester shop. **Headroom therefore comes from (a) capture-rate gains on the existing
  list (software/automation, no new headcount) and (b) adding certified testers.** Trucks and
  test kits are cheap and not the bottleneck; the bottleneck is butts-in-certified-seats.

Verify all bracketed figures cheaply by pulling the seller's actual annual test count,
device-failure/repair rate, and per-test fee schedule from the trailing-12 invoices.

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- **Legally-mandated annual renewal demand** means the work recurs by statute, not by sales
  effort — the operation's *demand side* survives the owner's exit cleanly and is unusually
  predictable.
- **Cleanest possible modernization target on the ops side:** a paper renewal list converts
  directly into an automated recurring-revenue engine, lifting capture rate with no new headcount.
- **Asset-light, low-supply-chain, low-rework operation** — a couple of commodity trucks/kits,
  no single-source vendor exposure, standardized pass/fail delivery, and modest safety/insurance
  profile.

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- **Certified-tester capacity is the single point of failure** and is currently the departing
  owner — if it doesn't transfer, there is no business on the Monday after close.
- **Total owner-dependency of knowledge and scheduling** — the renewal list, filing calendar,
  and authority/PM relationships are tribal; lose them and revenue silently leaks.
- **Capacity ceiling is credentialed labor** — growth beyond capture-rate gains requires hiring
  or sponsoring scarce certified testers, which has real lead time.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The certified-tester key-person/transfer problem is the risk that can kill this deal outright.
The business legally cannot deliver its core service unless a properly **certified** tester is in
the field, and today that tester is the retiring owner who is ALSO the only person who knows the
route, the renewal calendar, and the water-authority filing process. This is a double single-
point-of-failure: the credential and the tribal knowledge are bundled in one departing human. If
the founders close without (a) a contractually committed transition long enough to train a
replacement and transfer the list, and (b) a specific, already-identified certified tester who
will be employed and retained — whether a current second tester confirmed to stay or a new
credentialed hire — then the route stalls, mandated tests get missed, the water-authority
referral goodwill erodes, and a $720K goodwill purchase loses the very goodwill it paid for.
Note this is a *bridgeable* credential (it can be hired in or earned in months, so it is NOT a
hard-constraint disqualifier per the objectives doc), which is exactly why the correct posture is
RE-TRADE on transition terms rather than NO-GO.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- **Certified-tester capacity:** Is there a second certified tester employed today, will they
  sign a stay agreement, and what is their certification status — OR, if the owner is the only
  tester, do we have an identified certified hire and a seller-transition long enough (6–12 mo)
  to onboard them before the seller leaves the wrench?
- **The renewal list:** Will the seller deliver a complete, exportable renewal database (every
  assembly: serial, location, owner/PM contact, water authority, test-due date) at close, and how
  does last year's *tested count* compare to last year's *due count* — i.e., what is the current
  capture-rate leakage we'd be modernizing into?
- **Water-authority filing access:** Do the portal logins, filing credentials, and incumbent-
  filer standing convey on change of control, or must each authority re-approve the new entity —
  and do any authorities require the *business* (not just the individual) to hold a registration?
- **Customer/PM concentration:** Do a handful of property managers control a large share of the
  assemblies, and will they stay through the ownership change?
- **Repair/replacement mix:** What share of revenue and SDE is the higher-margin repair/rebuild
  pull-through versus base testing, and does that work require the same certification or a
  separate plumbing license?

## RECOMMENDATION: RE-TRADE
The underlying operation is attractive — statutory recurring demand, asset-light, and a textbook
ops-modernization win — but it is not a clean GO as presented because the certified-tester
credential and the entire operating knowledge base both walk out the door with the seller. Make
this a GO by writing into the deal: (1) a **minimum 6-month (target 6–12 mo) contracted seller
transition** with tester ride-alongs and full renewal-list/filing-calendar handover; (2) a
**certified-tester contingency** — closing conditioned on either a confirmed retained second
tester on a stay agreement or an identified credentialed hire in place; (3) delivery of the
**complete exportable renewal database** at close as a condition; and (4) ideally a **seller note
on standby and/or a holdback/earnout** tied to renewal-book retention through the first annual
cycle, so the seller is paid out as the book proves it transfers. With those terms the
modernization thesis is real and the day-one capacity risk is neutralized; without them, the
buyer is paying $720K of goodwill for a route only the seller can drive.
