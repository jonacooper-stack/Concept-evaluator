# CMO REVIEW — Regional Secure Document-Destruction / Shredding Company

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is a genuinely durable, transferable book: hundreds of small B2B accounts on
recurring weekly/monthly/quarterly routes, no customer over ~5% of revenue, anchored to
**compliance demand** (HIPAA/FACTA Certificates of Destruction) that is non-discretionary
and renews on a calendar, not a whim. The goodwill is partly the owner's (he built it on
relationships and sells the key accounts himself), but the *structural* stickiness —
locked consoles physically on-site, route density, auto-renewing service agreements, and
switching friction — means most of the value lives in the operation, not the man, IF
contracts and the crew convey. The modernization upside is large and obvious: a business
running on word-of-mouth, a dated website, no SEO/paid search, no CRM, and paper
everything, in a category where small-account local demand is being actively *ignored* by
the national players. This is close to the archetype the thesis is hunting for.

## POSITIONING & MODERNIZATION DRAFT
Re-position from "the local shredding guy you heard about from your accountant" to **"the
compliance-grade destruction partner for clinics, law firms, and financial offices — NAID
AAA certified, instant digital Certificates of Destruction, online scheduling, locked
chain-of-custody you can audit."** The headline GTM shift: stop *waiting* for referrals
and start *capturing the searcher* — every office manager who Googles "shredding service
near me," "HIPAA document destruction [metro]," or "shred truck pickup" should hit a fast,
review-rich, bookable page that the national players (who only chase enterprise) don't
bother to defend locally. Lead with compliance and convenience, sell the certificate as the
product, and make the locked console on the customer's floor the visible everyday brand.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- **Shred-it (owned by Stericycle, which Waste Management acquired in late 2024)** — the 800-lb
  gorilla, but it chases **enterprise/national** accounts and is notorious for fuel/energy
  surcharges and rigid contracts that frustrate small offices. It is *not* fighting for the
  20-bin dental-and-law-firm local book; that neglect is the wedge.
- **Iron Mountain** — records management + destruction, again enterprise-tilted, premium-priced,
  bureaucratic for a 3-attorney firm. Leaves the small-account air wide open.
- **Regional/local independents and franchises** — **PROSHRED**, **Shred Nations** (a lead-broker
  network, not an operator), and assorted one-truck local shops. These are the real
  head-to-head competitors and most are *equally sleepy* — thin web presence, few reviews. This
  is exactly the fragmented field the thesis wants: whoever modernizes local marketing first wins
  the map pack.
- **Consolidation note / red flag check:** secure destruction has seen roll-up activity
  (Stericycle/Shred-it, Access Information Management buying records/destruction firms, and PE
  interest in route-based services generally). The national tier is consolidated, but the
  **sub-$3M local-route segment is still fragmented** and not, to my read, in an active bidding
  war at the small-operator level [ASSUMED — verify by pulling 3–5 BizBuySell shredding-route
  comps and recent broker-quoted multiples; if entry multiples are already 4.5–5x+ SDE, the
  consolidators are bidding and this softens].

## AVERAGE SCORE: 7.5 / 10

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- Durable, low-concentration, compliance-anchored recurring book (hundreds of accounts, none >~5%) — the rare combination of sticky AND fragmented.
- Textbook modernization greenfield: no SEO, no paid search, no reviews engine, no CRM, in a category the national players actively ignore at the small-account level.
- Untapped pricing on switching-cost-protected service ("not reviewed in years") — high-margin upside that drops nearly straight to SDE.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- The owner personally owns sales and key-account relationships; a chunk of goodwill may walk out the door if the handover is weak.
- Recycled-paper resale and the perception of "shredding" as a commodity invite price competition if positioning stays generic rather than compliance-led.
- If BizBuySell/broker comps show entry multiples already bid to 4.5–5x+, consolidators are awake in this segment and the fragmented-air thesis weakens.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The marketing/customer risk most likely to kill this is **owner-embedded goodwill on the key
accounts**. The business was "built entirely on his local relationships and word of mouth," and
he still personally does sales and key-account management after 25 years. The recurring,
console-on-the-floor, auto-renewing nature of the book is genuine structural protection for the
*long tail* of small accounts — those mostly won't churn over a name change. But the larger,
relationship-sold accounts (and any handshake pricing) are exactly where a quiet "the founder
retired, who are these new people" defection happens in year one, and that is also where revenue
concentration, however modest, sits. The mitigation is entirely executable — a 6–12 month seller
transition with warm introductions to every meaningful account, e-signed renewals before close,
retention of the route drivers (the customers' actual day-to-day face), and a fast review/brand
layer so the company *looks* like an institution rather than one retiring man — but it must be
priced into the deal and contractually committed. Without a named, time-bound, compensated
handover plan, I'd haircut year-one retention and re-trade the multiple.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What % of revenue is under a **written, auto-renewing contract** vs. at-will handshake, and
  what is the named, time-bound, compensated **seller transition/introduction plan** for the
  top 20–30 accounts?
- What are the **actual current GBP review counts and web/SEO footprints** for the target and its
  3–5 nearest local competitors (PROSHRED, local independents), and where do "shredding near me"
  and "HIPAA document destruction [metro]" rank today — to size the SEO/reviews wedge?
- Does the business hold (or can it quickly earn) **NAID AAA certification**, and what is the
  current **rate card vs. Shred-it/PROSHRED quoted rates** — to quantify the pricing reset?
- What do **3–5 BizBuySell / broker comps** for $1.5–3M shredding-route businesses show for entry
  multiples — i.e., are consolidators already bidding this segment up?
- What share of revenue is **commodity recycled-paper resale**, and how exposed is SDE to paper
  price swings (a margin, not a marketing, question, but it caps the growth story)?

## RECOMMENDATION: GO
This is a strong fit for the founders' edge: a durable, compliance-anchored, low-concentration
recurring book sitting on a near-total marketing greenfield, in a fragmented local segment the
national players ignore. The marketing upside (GBP/reviews, Local Service Ads, SEO, online
quoting, CRM-driven reactivation and reprice) is cheap, fast, and squarely in the founders'
wheelhouse, against $520K SDE. The one thing that turns this from GO to RE-TRADE is the
owner-goodwill/handover risk on the key accounts — make a named, contractually committed,
multi-month seller transition (plus driver retention and e-signed renewals) a condition of close,
and verify the comp multiples aren't already consolidator-inflated. Conditioned on those, GO.
