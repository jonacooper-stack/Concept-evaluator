CFO REVIEW — Regional Secure Document-Destruction (Shredding) Company

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This deal pencils, and it pencils comfortably — which is the first thing to be suspicious of. At ~$1.8M for ~$520K SDE (3.46x), with ~70–80% recurring route-based revenue and no customer over ~5%, the structure services SBA debt at a base DSCR around 1.7–1.8x even after a market owner salary, and clears the founder-income ladder in year one. The earnings quality is helped by genuine recurrence (locked consoles + scheduled routes are sticky), but two things gate my enthusiasm: (1) a meaningful slice of "SDE" is commodity recycled-paper resale that is price-variable and should NOT be capitalized at the same multiple as contracted service revenue, and (2) the seller personally holds the key-account relationships and sales function, so the durability is real but the transition risk to that recurring base is the number that actually decides this deal. At a defensible 3.0–3.5x on the service-only earnings, this is a GO; I want a quality-of-earnings that separates contracted service margin from commodity resale before I bless the full $520K.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.7 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Genuinely recurring, low-concentration revenue: ~70–80% scheduled route service across hundreds of accounts, no customer >~5% — the kind of cash flow a lender and a future buyer both like.
- Bankable, comfortable structure: 3.46x with ~$190K injection services SBA debt at ~1.52x base DSCR after a market salary, with room for a standby seller note to sweeten bankability.
- Untapped pricing power: pricing "not reviewed in years" on sticky, switching-cost-laden console service is direct margin the founders can capture with low execution risk.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Transition churn to the seller-held relationship/sales function: the recurring base partly rides on a retiring owner's word-of-mouth book; a −15% revenue slip pushes DSCR toward the 1.25x floor.
- Commodity-paper earnings embedded in SDE: a price-variable recycled-paper slice is being capitalized at a service multiple; if paper prices fall, both SDE and the implied value erode.
- Fleet CapEx / deferred maintenance: shred trucks and balers are expensive, wear-heavy assets with no disclosed age — a hidden replacement reserve that can quietly compress DSCR.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The number most likely to kill this deal is revenue retention through the ownership transition, because the recurring base — the entire reason this is worth 3.46x — was "built entirely on his local relationships and word of mouth," and the seller personally still runs sales and key accounts. If even 15% of the book walks or pauses in the first 12 months (a realistic boomer-handover outcome when the customer's relationship is with a person who just left), cash flow after a $100K salary falls from ~$420K toward ~$330K–$350K, and DSCR slides to roughly 1.19x–1.26x — at or below the SBA 1.25x floor — before any rate stress or fleet CapEx. That is the difference between a comfortable deal and a covenant problem. The mitigant is structural, not analytical: a meaningful standby seller note plus a paid 6–12 month transition/introduction period that contractually ties seller earn-out to retained recurring revenue, so the seller is financially motivated to hand the relationships over rather than just cash out.

QUESTIONS / DUE-DILIGENCE ITEMS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Quality-of-earnings: tie the ~$520K SDE to 3 years of tax returns and separate it into (a) contracted recurring service margin, (b) one-time purge revenue, and (c) recycled-paper resale — which add-backs survive, and what is the service-only SDE we should actually pay a multiple on?
- Revenue durability: what % of the recurring base is on written, auto-renewing contracts vs. handshake/word-of-mouth, what is the historical annual account churn, and what is the realistic retention curve if the owner exits in 6 months?
- Recycled-paper exposure: how many $ of revenue and SDE depend on baled-paper prices, and what does SDE look like restated at a low/trough paper price?
- Fleet/equipment schedule: age, hours/mileage, and condition of every shred truck and baler, plus a credible annual replacement reserve — is there deferred maintenance the seller skipped?
- NAID AAA certification & seller compensation: is the business NAID AAA certified (and transferable), and what exactly is the owner paid / what hours does he work, so the market-salary normalization is real, not assumed?

RECOMMENDATION: RE-TRADE
This is a fundamentally good, bankable, recurring-revenue business, but I will not bless $1.8M / 3.46x on un-verified SDE that bundles commodity-paper earnings and a seller-held sales function. Make it a GO at one of two structures: (a) price held at ~$1.8M ONLY if QoE confirms service-only SDE near $520K AND a $200K+ seller note goes on full standby with a retention-linked earn-out and a 6–12 month paid transition; or (b) re-trade to ~$1.5M–$1.6M (≈3.0–3.1x) if the recycled-paper slice is material or contracts are largely informal, which restores DSCR cushion to ~1.6–1.7x even under a −15% transition stress. As a single-founder platform with bolt-on optionality it clears the income ladder over time; as a two-founder full income solution day one, it is too small on its own.
