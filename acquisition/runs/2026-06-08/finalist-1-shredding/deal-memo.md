# DEAL MEMO — Regional Secure Document-Destruction (Shredding) Company

Date: 2026-06-08
Council reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO

## ONE-PARAGRAPH DEAL READ
This is a genuinely attractive, near-archetype sleepy target: ~70–80% recurring, compliance-anchored route revenue across hundreds of small B2B accounts with no customer over ~5%, sitting on a near-total marketing/ops/tech greenfield that is squarely in the founders' wheelhouse. It is bankable — base DSCR ~1.5x after a market salary at the asking 3.46x SDE — and operable by owner-operators with no credential trap. But it is NOT a PASS as priced and represented: the ~$520K SDE is unverified by tax returns, bundles a commodity recycled-paper slice that should not be capitalized at a service multiple, and the entire recurring base partly rides on a retiring owner who personally runs sales and key accounts. The two dimensions that decide this deal — earnings quality and retention through transition — are precisely the ones with the thinnest evidence today, and they cap the honest scores below the bar. This is a strong RE-TRADE candidate, not a clear GO.

## RED-TEAM (mandatory)
I take the highest sub-scores across the packet and argue each is one point too high, attacking earnings quality, DSCR, transferability, and retention hardest. The packet has only a handful of 8s and 9s, and almost all of them are modernization-upside scores (legitimately high — a paper-everything business genuinely IS a greenfield, and that evidence is in-hand, not a transition bet). The 9s on modernization survive. The challenges that LAND are on the durability/structure scores that are propped up by unverified numbers.

**Challenge 1 — CFO sub-6 "Revenue durability & concentration" = 8 → 7.**
The 8 rests on "~70–80% recurring" and "no customer >~5%," but BOTH figures are `[ASSUMED]` from the one-pager and unverified by tax returns or a contract review. CFO and CMO both flag that an unknown share of the "recurring" base is handshake/word-of-mouth rather than written auto-renewing contracts, and Legal flags that auto-renew agreements may carry anti-assignment clauses that don't survive an asset sale. "Recurring" that isn't contractually papered is weaker than an 8 implies. **Lower to 7.** CFO average recomputes: (6+6+8+7+7+8+7+5+6+7)/10 = **6.6** (was 6.7).

**Challenge 2 — CMO sub-1 "Customer base quality & concentration" = 8 → 7.**
Same root cause from the marketing frame: the concentration and recurrence numbers are unverified, and CMO's own Biggest Single Risk concedes the larger relationship-sold accounts — where the modest concentration actually sits — are exactly where year-one defection happens on a boomer handover. An 8 ("rare combination of sticky AND fragmented") overstates certainty before contracts are read. **Lower to 7.** CMO average recomputes: (7+5+6+9+8+8+7+9+7+8)/10 = **7.4** (was 7.5).

**Challenge 3 — CFO sub-4 "Debt-service coverage (DSCR)" = 7 → 6.**
The 7 is the base 1.52x case, but CFO's own stress test shows a −15% revenue shock (a realistic boomer-handover churn outcome given seller-held relationships) pushes DSCR to ~1.19–1.26x — AT or BELOW the 1.25x SBA floor — before any rate stress or fleet CapEx. A coverage that breaks the lender floor under the single most likely adverse scenario is "good but fragile," which is a 6, not a 7. **Lower to 6.** CFO average recomputes again: (6+6+8+6+7+8+7+5+6+7)/10 = **6.5**.

**Challenge 4 — CMO sub-8 "Lead-gen & channel modernization" = 9 — HOLD.**

The evidence is concrete and in-hand (no SEO, no paid search, no reviews engine, no CRM, paper certs), the levers are named (GBP/reviews, Local Service Ads, paid search, online quoting) with cited tools and costs against $520K SDE, and nationals demonstrably ignore the local small-account segment. ## WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE
- **The recurring, low-concentration route book is real and durable in shape** — every expert credits ~70–80% scheduled service, no customer >~5%, compliance-driven (non-discretionary) renewal, and physical switching friction from on-site locked consoles.
- **The modernization upside is large, cheap, and founder-executable** — unanimous high marks: marketing greenfield (CMO 9), ops/systems (COO 8), tech/AI (CTO 9), with named tools and costs trivial against $520K SDE. This is the clearest strength in the deal.
- **The single biggest live risk is the same for four of five experts: owner-embedded goodwill / retention through transition** — CFO, CMO, COO, and Legal independently land on the retiring owner's personal sales/key-account relationships as the thing that decides the deal.
- **No eligibility, licensing, or credential deal-killer** — Legal scores SBA eligibility 9 and license transferability 9; this is a clean owner-operator fit with no wrench-holding credential trap.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS DISAGREE
- **CFO (RE-TRADE) vs. CMO/CTO (GO) on whether to bless the asking price now.** Underlying assumption: CFO refuses to capitalize unverified SDE that bundles commodity paper and a seller-held sales function at 3.46x; CMO/CTO are scoring the *quality of the asset and the upside*, not the price. **My call: CFO's frame is load-bearing.** The synthesizer's job is price-and-structure, and you cannot pay 3.46x for an unverified, partly-commodity SDE — the CMO/CTO upside is real but is captured AFTER close, not paid for up front.
- **COO scores QC/operational-risk 4 (chain-of-custody catastrophe) while CMO treats compliance as a selling point (9 on positioning).** Same fact — the certificate is the product — read as tail-risk vs. wedge. **My call: both are right and they compound.** The compliance trail is simultaneously the strongest marketing differentiator AND the largest liability landmine; the resolution (digital chain-of-custody, COO item 2 / CTO item 3) serves both and must be installed in the first 30 days, not queued behind revenue features.
- **CTO/COO score inherited systems/process at 4 (a drag) while treating it as the upside.** The low base IS the modernization headroom, but it also means migration is manual transcription during the same window you're retaining drivers and customers. **My call: CTO's "capture the data pre-close while the owner is still here" is load-bearing** — it converts a 4 into the 9-upside only if sequenced correctly; mis-sequenced, it threatens the recurring base it's meant to grow.

## TOP 5 RISKS (RANKED)
1. **Earnings quality is unverified and partly commodity (CFO).** Ranks first because it underprices the entire deal: ~$520K SDE is `[ASSUMED]`, includes price-variable recycled-paper resale capitalized at a service multiple, and is stated after a seller doing real sales work. **Reduced by:** a QoE separating (a) contracted service margin, (b) one-time purge, (c) paper resale, tied to 3 yrs tax returns — re-price on service-only SDE.
2. **Retention through transition / owner-held relationships (CFO, CMO, COO, Legal).** A −15% boomer-handover churn drops DSCR to ~1.19–1.26x, at/below the SBA floor. **Reduced by:** a named, time-bound, compensated 6–12 month seller transition with warm intros to the top 20–30 accounts, e-signed renewals before close, a retention-linked seller earn-out, and a standby seller note.
3. **Driver/key-staff retention through change of control (COO).** In a 4–7 person shop, one or two departures strand ~25% of routes. **Reduced by:** stay agreements (~$5–15K/head `[ASSUMED]`) signed AT close for every driver and the dispatcher/office manager.
4. **Inherited data-security / chain-of-custody liability tail (Legal 4, COO 4, CTO 4).** A single pre-close HIPAA/FACTA breach, missing certificate, or lapsed cyber policy attaches to the going-concern goodwill even in an asset deal. **Reduced by:** reps on no-breach/no-inquiry/valid-certificates, ~10%/12–18mo escrow, in-force cyber + professional-liability insurance with pre-close tail, NAID AAA/process audit, and digital chain-of-custody installed in the first 30 days.
5. **Contract assignability / change-of-control leakage (Legal 5).** Auto-renew agreements + HIPAA BAAs may need hundreds of consents to assign in an asset deal; a master contract that voids on sale re-trades the deal. **Reduced by:** counsel reads the standard agreement + largest accounts' contracts + the plant lease, then picks asset vs. structured-equity on the consent burden.

## TOP 3 STRENGTHS
- **Durable, low-concentration, compliance-anchored recurring book** — hundreds of accounts, none >~5%, ~70–80% scheduled, renewing on a HIPAA/FACTA calendar, not a whim. The rare sticky-AND-fragmented shape the thesis hunts for.
- **Textbook modernization greenfield squarely in the founders' edge** — no SEO/paid/reviews/CRM, paper certs and invoicing, untouched pricing, in a category nationals ignore locally; cheap, fast, founder-executable lift on top of a cash-flowing operation (held at 9 through the red-team).
- **Bankable, credential-free owner-operator deal** — clean SBA 7(a) goodwill structure with trucks/bins as hard collateral, ~$190K injection, base DSCR ~1.5x after market salary, and no personal trade license required.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
What is the **service-only, contractually-papered SDE** — i.e., strip the recycled-paper commodity resale and the one-time purge lumpiness, isolate the margin that sits under *written, auto-renewing, assignable* agreements, tie it to three years of tax returns, and overlay a realistic retention curve if the owner exits in 6 months. Every other number in this deal hangs off that figure: the defensible multiple, the DSCR cushion, the personal-guarantee survivability, and whether ~$300K/founder is reachable. If the QoE shows service-only SDE near $520K AND a healthy majority of revenue is on assignable written contracts, this is a GO at ~3.0–3.5x with the right transition terms. If a material slice is commodity paper or handshake revenue, the price has to come down to ~3.0x ($1.5–1.6M) to restore cushion, and the deal becomes a single-founder platform play, not a two-founder income solution. Diligence answers this for the price of a QoE and a contract review.

## RECOMMENDATION: RE-TRADE

This is a fundamentally good, bankable, near-archetype business that I will not bless at $1.8M / 3.46x on un-verified SDE that bundles commodity-paper earnings and a seller-held sales function. It is a RE-TRADE, not a NO-GO — the cash flow, durability shape, and modernization fit are all real; the issue is price-for-certainty and transition structure, both fixable.

**What moves it to GO:**
- **Path A — hold ~$1.8M (3.46x)** ONLY if QoE confirms service-only SDE near $520K on assignable written contracts AND the structure adds: a **$200K+ seller note on full standby** with a **retention-linked earn-out**, a **named 6–12 month paid seller transition** with warm intros to the top 20–30 accounts, **e-signed renewals before close**, and **driver/office-manager stay agreements at close**. (Addresses CFO earnings/retention, CMO/COO transition, Legal assignment.)
- **Path B — re-trade to ~$1.5–1.6M (≈3.0–3.1x)** if the recycled-paper slice is material or contracts are largely informal — restores base DSCR cushion to ~1.6–1.7x even under a −15% transition stress. (Addresses CFO valuation/DSCR fragility.)
- Either path requires the **legal containment package**: asset structure (fallback structured-equity if consent burden is prohibitive), reps on data-security/compliance history, ~10%/12–18mo escrow, state tax-clearance certificates, in-force cyber + professional-liability insurance with pre-close tail, NAID AAA/process confirmation, Phase I if a plant site is owned, and an enforceable seller non-compete.

**Which expert concern each term verifies:** QoE → CFO earnings quality; seller note + earn-out + transition → CFO/CMO/COO retention; stay agreements → COO driver risk; escrow + insurance + reps → Legal data-tail; contract read → Legal assignment.
