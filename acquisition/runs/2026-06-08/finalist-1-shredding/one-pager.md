# Acquisition Target One-Pager

## The business
A regional **secure document-destruction and records-destruction company**. It provides
**scheduled, recurring shredding** to a base of small/mid B2B customers — medical and
dental practices, law firms, accounting/financial offices, insurance agencies, and local
government — plus periodic one-time "purge" jobs. Service is delivered on **routes**:
locked collection consoles/bins placed at customer sites, serviced on a recurring
schedule (weekly/monthly/quarterly) by a small fleet of trucks; material is shredded
(mobile shred truck and/or plant-based) and the baled paper is sold to recyclers as a
secondary revenue stream. Each destruction event generates a **Certificate of
Destruction** the customer retains for HIPAA/FACTA compliance.

## Why it's available
The founder-owner is **retiring after ~25 years** and has no successor. The business was
built entirely on his local relationships and word of mouth.

## Representative financials [ASSUMED — verify with QoE / tax returns / 3–5 comps]
- Revenue: **~$2.2M/yr**, of which ~70–80% is recurring scheduled service and the rest is
  purge jobs + recycled-paper resale.
- Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE): **~$520K/yr**, stable to slightly growing.
- Asking price: **~$1.8M (~3.5× SDE)** [ASSUMED].
- Indicative SBA 7(a) structure: ~10% equity injection, 10-yr term, ~10.5% variable rate,
  personal guarantee; trucks/bins are the hard-asset collateral.
- Customer concentration: no single customer >~5% of revenue; hundreds of small accounts.

## Revenue mechanism
Recurring scheduled-service contracts (often auto-renewing) billed monthly/per-service,
plus per-job purges, plus commodity resale of baled paper (price-variable).

## Operating shape
A small team: route drivers/shred-truck operators, a plant/baler operator if plant-based,
and office/dispatch staff. The owner currently does sales, key-account relationships, and
some dispatch. Compliance documentation (certificates, chain-of-custody) is central to the
value proposition. NAID AAA certification is common in the industry and may or may not be
held.

## Current go-to-market & systems (the "sleepy" picture)
Customers come almost entirely from **word of mouth and decades-old relationships**. The
business has a thin or dated website, **no real SEO or paid search**, no e-sign contracts,
no customer portal, and largely **paper-based** scheduling, certificates, and invoicing.
Pricing has not been reviewed in years. National competitors (e.g., Shred-it/Stericycle,
Iron Mountain) focus on large enterprise accounts and leave the small-account local market
fragmented and under-marketed.

## What a buyer would be taking on
Retaining the route drivers and the recurring customer base through the ownership change;
replacing the owner's sales/relationship role; modernizing marketing, contracts, billing,
and compliance documentation; and managing exposure to recycled-paper commodity prices and
data-security/chain-of-custody liability.
