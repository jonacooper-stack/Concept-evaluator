# DECISION MEMO — Ultimate Longevity Center (brand-new franchise startup)
Date: 2026-06-11
Council reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO
Framing: This is a GREENFIELD FRANCHISE STARTUP — buying the right to BUILD a new premium
longevity/wellness unit (recovery "Playground" + biomarker "Lab" + supplement "Apothecary"),
full-time owner-operator, ~$508K–$1.18M all-in. There is no existing cash flow, no operating
history, and no FDD Item 19 for this brand. The user asked two questions: (1) ECONOMIC
VIABILITY — does it service SBA debt AND pay the owner; (2) DEMAND — is the macro real AND
capturable by THIS concept.

---

## ONE-PARAGRAPH READ
The macro tailwind is real and large, the franchisor has a genuine scaling pedigree, and the
celebrity layer buys a real opening spike — and none of that fixes the unit. Five independent
reviewers, each adapting their rubric to a franchise startup, landed on PASS, and they did so
for structurally different reasons (thin unit margin, late-cycle me-too demand, a three-headed
clinical/hospitality hybrid, stacked medical/FTC/franchise regulation, and maximal PHI
liability with no franchisee tech edge). The decisive fact is supplied by the concept's own
best public comparable, Restore Hyper Wellness: even MATURE winning units in this exact
segment earn only ~$119K–$153K of owner earnings on ~$851K–$962K revenue, pay back in 8–10
years, and the category leader closed 8.6% of its units in a single year on over-saturation. A
brand-new unit must service a full-size SBA note from month one while ramping from zero into
those same thin numbers, with no Item 19 to underwrite against and soft, depreciating
collateral. A large growing market does not repair a 14–16% unit margin. This is a PASS on
economic viability, confirmed after a deliberate steelman of the bull case.

## RED-TEAM (mandatory — run in REVERSE: distrust the unanimous red light)

I first built the strongest honest bull case, then attacked the three lowest sub-scores to see
if any are a point too HARSH.

### The bull case, steelmanned (taken seriously, not strawmanned)
- **(a) The macro is genuinely real and large.** Global wellness economy ~$6.8T (2024) → ~$9.8T
  by 2029 (~7.6%/yr, Global Wellness Institute); narrower longevity market ~$31.6B (2026) →
  ~$46.9B by 2031 (~8.2%/yr, Mordor Intelligence). Three independent demand vectors converge —
  aging affluent Boomers, mainstreaming "biohacker" demand, and the GLP-1/metabolic-health
  cultural moment. This is not a tanning-salon fad; the CMO himself scored end-market category
  durability as "a legitimate 7-ish." The trend is not in dispute.
- **(b) The franchisor can actually scale.** Anthony Geisler / Sequel built and scaled Club
  Pilates, CycleBar, and Row House inside Xponential — real, demonstrated franchise-scaling
  machinery (site selection, buildout specs, vendor packages, a Mariana Tek-class membership/
  booking backbone). This is not a first-time franchisor with no apparatus.
- **(c) Celebrity reach drives real top-of-funnel.** Gary Brecka + Tony Robbins + Lifeforce can
  buy an opening-day awareness spike a no-name independent studio cannot. That is a genuine,
  if temporary, CAC advantage at launch.

### The three lowest sub-scores, challenged for being too HARSH

**Challenge 1 — Legal #2 (CPOM / clinical model = 2). Is a 2 too punitive?**
Bull argument: the compliance architecture is KNOWN and solvable — a competent franchisor with
Geisler's apparatus CAN supply a state-by-state friendly-PC/MSO template, and Legal itself
listed "the architecture is solvable in principle" as a strength. If the franchisor provides
it turnkey, the franchisee's burden drops materially, arguably a 4–5, not a 2.
Verdict: **Holds at 2 — does NOT rise.** The score is for the TYPE as specified, not for a
hypothetical franchisor who has already built and state-vetted the structure. The brand is
explicitly "very new" with few/no seasoned units; the existence of a vetted per-state
friendly-PC/MSO template is precisely the unverified make-or-break item Legal deferred to
diligence. Scoring the optimistic assumption would be exactly the gaming the discipline
forbids. A lay operator being asked to own the practice of medicine in CPOM states (CA/TX/NY/
NJ/CO) on an unproven template is correctly a 2 on current evidence. **Unchanged: 2.**

**Challenge 2 — CFO #4 (debt-service headroom / projected DSCR = 3). Too harsh on projections?**
Bull argument: the macro tailwind + celebrity launch spike could pull the ramp forward, beating
Restore's new-unit cohort; if Year-2 hits ~1.0x and mature lands above 1.15x, a 3 understates it.
Verdict: **Holds at 3 — does NOT rise.** The CFO's mature DSCR of ~1.15x is already GENEROUS: it
borrows Restore's mature 14–16% margin and assumes the new unit reaches it, yet still lands below
the 1.25x SBA comfort floor with a Year-1 at 0.38x and a downside that pushes mature coverage back
toward 0.9–1.0x. Raising this requires assuming a BETTER ramp than the only comparable delivers —
an unattributed convenient number. The celebrity spike is a launch-month effect against a multi-
year ramp and full-size debt from day one; it does not change the amortization. **Unchanged: 3.**

**Challenge 3 — CMO #5 (competitive structure / crowding = 3). Too harsh given the franchisor edge?**
Bull argument: Geisler's scaling machinery + the integrated three-zone bundle + celebrity reach
could let this concept out-compete the fragmented med-spa tail and carve open space; maybe a 4–5.
Verdict: **Holds at 3 — does NOT rise.** The evidence runs the other way: the direct comp closed
18 units (8.6%) in 2024 on acknowledged margin compression from over-saturation, and the affluent
"optimizer" customer is already courted by Restore, Next Health, Lifeforce, Equinox/Optimize, AND
a dense local med-spa tail. New units land INTO the compression, not ahead of it. A scaling
franchisor adds UNITS to a crowded segment faster — that is not the same as winning open air. The
crowding is a demonstrated fact, not a worry. **Unchanged: 3.**

## WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE
- **The macro trend is real and large — and irrelevant to the unit.** All five accept the GWI/
  Mordor numbers; all five separate "the trend is real" from "this unit captures it profitably,"
  and all five land on the second question failing.
- **Restore Hyper Wellness is the load-bearing reality check.** CFO, CMO, and COO all anchor to
  the same comp: ~$119K–$153K mature owner earnings on ~$851K–$962K AUV, 8–10 yr payback, 8.6%
  annual closures on over-saturation. Even winning units barely clear one owner's income.
- **The clinical "Lab" is the crux of the risk.** COO (medical-director dependency the owners
  can't perform), Legal (CPOM/scope/health-claims), and CTO (PHI/HIPAA/state-health-data) all
  independently flag the blood-draw/biomarker layer as the single most dangerous element.
- **The celebrity brand is a fragility, not a moat.** CMO (borrowed, contestable demand) and
  Legal (FTC substantiation + endorsement-guide enforcement amplifier) converge: Brecka's
  contested science raises enforcement odds and inverts overnight, and the franchisee can't fix it.
- **The strip-down sub-segment is the only survivable shape — and it's a different business.**
  COO, Legal, and CTO independently arrive at the same narrower path: drop the clinical "Lab,"
  run only the non-clinical recovery + retail spine. That removes the medical-director, CPOM, and
  PHI burdens — but it also removes the only differentiation, leaving a bare, me-too recovery studio.

There is no material expert DISAGREEMENT to adjudicate — the convergence is real, and the
red-team above confirms it is earned rather than reflexive.

## THE SINGLE STRONGEST BULL ARGUMENT — and why it does NOT change the call
The strongest bull argument is **(a): the macro tailwind is genuinely real, large, multi-vector,
and durable.** It is true, it is sourced, and it is seductive precisely because it is true. But it
is a TOP-LINE story attached to a 14–16% unit margin. A large, growing market increases the number
of customers in the category; it does not change what a single premium unit earns after rent, a
6–10-person payroll including licensed clinical labor, capital-equipment finance, ~9–10% royalty-
plus-marketing, and a full-size SBA note from month one. The concept's own comparable proves a
rising tide in this exact segment still produced 8.6% unit closures and 8–10 year paybacks. Demand
existing is necessary but not sufficient; the binding constraint is the unit's thin margin and its
multi-year cash-negative ramp against full debt service. The tailwind does not touch the
amortization schedule. It does NOT change the call.

## TOP 5 RISKS (RANKED)
1. **Thin unit economics → sub-1.25x DSCR + sub-ladder owner take-home.** Mature DSCR ~1.15x
   (below the SBA floor), Year-1 ~0.38x, mature owner earnings minus ~$117K debt service leaves
   roughly $18K of free cash — the unit pays a modest salary OR services debt, not both, and is
   nowhere near the $300K/founder target. This alone is decisive.
2. **Multi-year cash-negative ramp against full-size debt from day one, with no Item 19.** The
   owner funds the gap from the equity injection and reserves on an unproven brand with soft,
   depreciating collateral and likely a personal guarantee / home lien.
3. **CPOM + contested-science brand (the catastrophic-loss pattern).** A lay franchisee cannot
   lawfully own the clinical service in many states; if the franchisor hasn't built and vetted a
   per-state friendly-PC/MSO structure, the owner personally absorbs unauthorized-practice and
   medical-board exposure — amplified by FTC/FDA health-claims enforcement risk on Brecka's
   contested claims that the franchisee cannot control.
4. **Over-saturating, low-switching-cost, high-churn demand the franchisee can't defend.** A
   me-too bundle in a segment the leader is closing units in; demand flows to the lower-commitment
   med-spa or the owned-base incumbent (Next Health, Equinox/Optimize) with better real estate.
5. **Maximal PHI/data liability with zero franchisee tech control.** Biomarker/blood data under
   HIPAA + state health-privacy laws (WA MHMDA, CA CMIA, NV SB370) with private rights of action,
   on a franchisor-controlled stack the owner can't audit or replace — a single breach or
   "AI-protocol" claims action could be existential for a unit already carrying an 8–10 yr payback.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
Does an FDD Item 19 exist for Ultimate Longevity that shows materially BETTER unit margins and a
faster ramp than Restore Hyper Wellness — AND will an SBA lender actually finance the projection
at a low injection without a home lien? Everything in the base case is borrowed from the comp
because the brand has no track record of its own. If, and only if, a real Item 19 proved the
economics are structurally better than the comp (not merely projected to be), the central
financial objection would weaken. On current evidence — a "very new" brand with few/no seasoned
units — that proof does not exist, which is exactly why the verdict is a PASS rather than a MAYBE.

## RECOMMENDATION: **PASS**

This is a PASS on economic viability, and the unanimous red light survived a deliberate, full-
strength steelman of the bull case (real macro, real franchisor pedigree, real celebrity reach).
None of the three harshest sub-scores rose under challenge because each bull argument required
assuming an unverified favorable fact that the only available evidence contradicts. Honestly
scored, this is a 4.22 mean with a hard floor of regulatory 2s — a structurally thin, capital-
heavy, heavily-regulated unit chasing a real trend through the most fragile available vehicle.

### Explicit answers to the user's two questions

**(1) ECONOMIC VIABILITY — is it viable; does it service SBA debt AND pay the owner?**
No. On honest projections anchored to the only public comparable, the unit runs a multi-year
cash-negative ramp (Year-1 DSCR ~0.38x) into a mature DSCR of only ~1.15x — below the 1.25x SBA
comfort floor — with an 8–10 year payback and a mature owner take-home a fraction of the income
ladder. It can service the debt OR pay the owner a modest salary, not both comfortably, and only
on an optimistic, unproven ramp backed by a personal guarantee. The unit does not clear the bar.

**(2) DEMAND — is the macro real AND capturable by THIS concept?**
The macro is real, large, and durable (wellness $6.8T→$9.8T; longevity $31.6B→$46.9B) — but it is
NOT durably capturable by this specific concept. This is a late-cycle, me-too premium bundle in a
segment the direct comparable is actively closing units in (8.6% in 2024 on over-saturation),
differentiated only by a contested celebrity brand the franchisee cannot control, selling
discretionary, recession-sensitive, high-churn, low-switching-cost services. The trend creates
demand; this concept's claim on it is borrowed and contestable, and the demand flows to lower-
commitment med-spas and owned-base incumbents (Next Health, Equinox/Optimize).

---

## WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FLIP TO A MAYBE
All of the following would have to hold simultaneously — this is the bar, not a forecast:
1. **A real FDD Item 19 (or a seasoned-unit cohort) showing materially better economics than
   Restore** — meaningfully above 14–16% mature owner-earnings margin and a faster ramp to
   break-even DSCR — proving the unit is structurally better than the comp, not merely projected to be.
2. **A committed SBA lender financing the projection at a low (~10%) injection without a home lien**,
   confirming the brand is on the SBA Franchise Directory and that real underwriters will bank it.
3. **A turnkey, state-by-state-vetted friendly-PC/MSO clinical-compliance structure supplied and
   indemnified by the franchisor**, removing the CPOM/scope/health-claims exposure from the
   franchisee — OR the concept is restructured to the non-clinical recovery+retail "Playground"
   format that out-references the "Lab" entirely (which removes the clinical, PHI, and CPOM risk,
   but is a different, less-differentiated business with even thinner differentiation).

Absent all three, this remains a PASS. The single most important first verification is item (1):
whether the unit economics are demonstrably better than the comp — because if they are not, the
financial objection is fatal regardless of how the legal and demand questions resolve.
