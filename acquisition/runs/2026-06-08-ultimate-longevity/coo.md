COO REVIEW — Ultimate Longevity Center (brand-new longevity/wellness franchise startup)

> ADAPTATION NOTE: The Stage-1 industry-acquisition rubric assumes you are BUYING an
> existing operating business with crews, routes, and inherited cash flow. This is the
> opposite: a greenfield FRANCHISE STARTUP — you build the unit from zero, hire a fresh
> team, and ramp utilization on an unproven playbook. I have therefore re-pointed each of
> the ten dimensions to the questions that actually decide whether two owner-operators can
> LAUNCH and RUN this center: buildout/ramp risk, staffing a clinical+hospitality hybrid,
> utilization economics, and franchisor-support dependency. Where I rename a dimension I say
> so. "Roll-up/multi-site" and "density/route economics" do not map cleanly to a single
> destination site, so I re-cast them as "multi-unit/territory scalability" and "throughput/
> chair-utilization economics." I score harder than the comparable buy-side deal because a
> startup carries ramp risk and execution risk that an existing cash-flowing business does not.

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is operationally one of the HARDEST small-business models a first-time owner-operator
can pick: it fuses three businesses with different operating logics under one roof — an
experiential recovery spa (hospitality + equipment uptime), a phlebotomy/diagnostics clinic
(licensed clinical labor + MD/NP medical oversight + PHI handling), and a supplement retail
shop (inventory + compliant claims) — and asks two non-clinical generalists to run all three
at high utilization while leaning on a franchisor playbook that, for THIS brand, has almost no
seasoned units behind it. The recovery and retail halves are genuinely systematizable and the
founders can plausibly GM them; the clinical half (blood draws, lab ordering/interpretation,
medical-director relationship) is the operating crux they cannot personally perform and must
staff and supervise correctly from day one. The binding constraint is not building the box —
it is filling ~30–40 paid visits a day, every day, on a brand with no proven local demand and
a playbook that has not yet been pressure-tested across a network. The model is runnable, but
it is a high-complexity, high-fixed-cost launch where the franchisor's unproven support is
load-bearing.

THE LAUNCH & RUN WALKTHROUGH (realistic buildout + ramp)
Buildout: a 3,500–5,000 sq ft retail/medical-adjacent fit-out (plumbing for cold plunge/
hydrogen bath, electrical for hyperbaric + red-light, a compliant blood-draw/clinical room,
retail floor, member lounge). Expect 9–15 months from signing to open: site selection and
LOI (2–3 mo), lease negotiation, architect/permit/medical-room buildout (6–9 mo), equipment
procurement and install (hyperbaric chambers, plunge, sauna, red-light — long-lead capital
items), then staffing and pre-sale. The $508K–$1.18M range is wide precisely because the
medical room, equipment package, and market rents swing it; a realistic urban/affluent-
suburb build lands in the upper half ($800K–$1.2M), consistent with the Restore comp's
~$779K–$1.31M. [ASSUMED build timeline — verify against the FDD Item 11 and actual franchisee
open-dates; cheap check: ask the franchisor for the mean days-from-signing-to-open across
existing units.]

Staffing a high-touch center: a single unit needs, realistically, (a) a front desk / member
concierge (1.5–2 FTE across hours), (b) 2–4 cross-trained "wellness techs" to run modalities,
sanitize equipment between users, and turn rooms, (c) a phlebotomist or RN for blood draws,
(d) a contracted/part-time NP or MD medical director to own the standing orders, lab
requisition, and interpretation/telehealth sign-off — this is non-optional and is the
licensure backbone of "The Lab," (e) a membership/sales closer (often the owner). One owner
runs operations/staffing/equipment uptime; the other runs sales/membership/community. Neither
can personally draw blood or order labs, so the medical-director relationship is a vendor the
business depends on and must keep happy. Fully staffed, a unit is plausibly 6–10 FTE — a real
payroll to carry from day one against zero starting revenue.

Utilization economics & ramp: revenue is memberships ($150–$400/mo [ASSUMED, category norm —
verify]) + à-la-carte modality visits + diagnostic panels ($300–$1,000 each) + supplement
retail. The hard part is that ALL of it ramps from zero — month 1 opens near-empty and the
fixed nut (rent + 6–10 FTE + equipment finance + ~7–8% royalty + 2% marketing) runs whether
chairs are full or not. The Restore comp shows the MATURE outcome (~$851K–$962K AUV, ~14–16%
owner earnings, 8–10 yr payback) AND the failure rate (18 closures / 8.6% of units in 2024 on
over-saturation) — i.e., even at maturity this is a thin-margin grind, and a meaningful share
don't make it.

Franchisor-support dependency: a startup with no operating history leans almost entirely on
the franchisor's playbook for site selection, buildout specs, vendor packages, hiring/training
SOPs, the medical-oversight model, the member app, and the protocol library. Geisler's
Xponential pedigree means the franchise-SCALING machinery is real — but the longevity-center
PLAYBOOK itself is new and largely unproven, and Xponential's history includes documented
franchisee-profitability complaints and litigation. The founders would be early units
de-risking the franchisor's model on their own balance sheet.

CAPACITY / UTILIZATION MATH (show the work)
Target ≈ the comparable AUV of ~$850K/yr. Open ~360 days/yr ⇒ ~$2,360 of revenue PER DAY.
Blended ticket assumptions (mix of modality visits at ~$40–$80, panels at ~$300–$1,000,
retail, amortized membership): take a blended ~$70 per modality-visit-equivalent.
- $850,000 ÷ $70 ≈ 12,150 paid service-events/yr ≈ ~34 per operating day.
- A single modality station serving ~45-min sessions over a ~12-hr day = ~16 sessions/
  station/day. To clear ~34 events/day you need roughly 3–4 productive stations running near
  half-to-two-thirds utilization ALL day, plus diagnostics and retail layered on top.
- Memberships smooth this: e.g., 350 members × $250/mo ≈ $1.05M of recurring base alone IF
  you can recruit and RETAIN 350 paying members in a single trade area — that is the real
  operating bet. At the comp's $250/mo, you need ~280–350 active members just to anchor the
  AUV before à-la-carte and diagnostics.
The math is not arithmetically impossible — it is a DEMAND and RETENTION problem. The
bottleneck is not chairs (3–4 stations have headroom); it is putting ~34 paying butts/day in
them, day after day, on an unproven brand. Restore's closures say a large minority of operators
in this exact category cannot.

WHAT I AM DEFERRING TO DUE DILIGENCE
- FDD Item 11 (the actual scope of franchisor training/site/buildout support) and Item 19
  (whether ANY earnings claim exists yet) — the realism of the playbook is the whole game.
- The specific medical-oversight model the franchisor mandates (employed MD vs. telehealth
  network vs. franchisee-sourced medical director) and who carries the clinical liability.
- Real local buildout cost, permit timeline for a clinical room, and equipment lead times in
  the founders' actual market — these decide cash-burn-to-open.
- Actual member pricing, churn, and ramp curve from any existing unit (validation visits).

AVERAGE SCORE: 4.4 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS (of the type)
1. Throughput is not the bottleneck — 3–4 modality stations have ample daily session capacity,
   so a full center is operationally servable without a structural capacity wall.
2. The recovery + retail halves are genuinely standardizable, and the franchisor provides the
   app/protocol/vendor stack, lowering "invent it yourself" burden on those zones.
3. Franchisor has real franchise-SCALING machinery (Geisler/Xponential: Club Pilates, CycleBar)
   — the corporate apparatus for site selection, buildout, and multi-unit growth exists.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS (of the type)
1. Licensed-clinical dependency: the "Lab" requires phlebotomy + an NP/MD medical director the
   owners cannot replace personally — a permanent staffing/liability constraint and single
   point of failure.
2. Ramp-from-zero against an unproven playbook: full fixed cost (rent + 6–10 FTE + equipment
   finance + ~9–10% fees) runs from day one while demand builds slowly on a brand with no
   local track record.
3. Utilization/retention demand risk: hitting ~34 paid events/day and ~300 retained members in
   one trade area is hard; the closest comp closed 8.6% of units in 2024 on over-saturation.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The defining operating risk is launching a high-fixed-cost, three-headed hybrid (spa + clinic +
retail) on a franchisor PLAYBOOK that is itself unproven for this concept, with two operators
who cannot perform its licensed clinical core. Every dollar of rent, the 6–10-person payroll,
the capital-equipment finance, and the ~9–10% royalty-plus-marketing load begins burning the
day the doors open, while membership and visit revenue must be built from zero in a single
trade area — and the medical-oversight function (standing orders, lab requisition,
interpretation/telehealth sign-off) must be staffed and supervised correctly from day one or
the most differentiated revenue zone simply cannot legally operate. The closest public
comparable, Restore Hyper Wellness, shows the destination even when this is run WELL: ~$120–150K
owner earnings, 8–10-year payback, and 8.6% of units closing in a single year on margin
compression. So the founders are betting six-plus figures of equity and a full SBA note that
they can out-execute that comp on a NEWER, less-proven, more clinically-complex format —
without the operating history that would tell them the ramp curve in advance. That is execution
risk and ramp risk stacked on top of an already thin-margin model, and it is structural to the
TYPE, not fixable by good intentions.

QUESTIONS TO ANSWER WHILE SOURCING / VALIDATING
1. What EXACTLY does the franchisor's playbook mandate and provide for the medical-oversight
   model — employed MD, telehealth network, or franchisee-sourced director — and who holds the
   clinical liability and the lab-interpretation duty?
2. What is the real ramp curve and current membership count/churn/AUV of the few existing units,
   and what is the mean days-from-signing-to-open (FDD Item 11 + validation calls)?
3. What is the all-in monthly fixed nut at open (rent + fully-staffed payroll + equipment
   finance + royalty + marketing), and how many months of working capital beyond the $150K
   liquidity minimum are needed to reach breakeven utilization?
4. What is the equipment vendor package, lead times, warranty/repair model, and uptime SLA on
   the capital-intensive modalities (hyperbaric, plunge), and can it be serviced locally?

RECOMMENDATION: PASS
Operationally this is a high-complexity, high-fixed-cost, clinically-licensed hybrid that asks
two non-clinical first-time owner-operators to ramp an unproven brand's playbook from zero while
carrying a 6–10-person payroll and a permanent medical-oversight dependency. The throughput math
works on paper, but the binding constraints — licensed-clinical staffing/liability and
build-from-zero demand/retention against a comp that closes 8.6% of units annually at 8–10-year
payback — are structural to the type, not company-specific. If a version of this is pursued at
all, the only operationally defensible sub-segment is the LIGHTER, NON-CLINICAL recovery-and-
retail format (drop "The Lab" / blood draws entirely), which removes the medical-director
dependency, the PHI burden, and most of the safety/claims liability — but that is a different,
less-differentiated business. As specified, the operating model is too complex and too unproven
for these operators to launch with acceptable execution risk.
