# CFO REVIEW — Ultimate Longevity Center (brand-new franchise startup)

> FRAMING NOTE: This is a greenfield franchise unit, not an acquisition of existing cash flow.
> There is no operating history and no FDD Item 19 earnings track record for THIS brand. Every
> earnings figure below is a PROJECTION, sanity-checked against the closest public comparable
> (Restore Hyper Wellness). I have adapted the industry-acquisition CFO dimensions to a
> franchise-startup: where a dimension assumes existing SDE/multiple (e.g., "normal valuation
> multiples," "resale/multiple-arbitrage"), I re-cast it to the franchise economics it maps to

## ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This is a capital-heavy, premium-discretionary, multi-modality wellness build whose own best
public comparable — Restore Hyper Wellness — tells the unflattering truth: at a MATURE
~$851K–$962K AUV, owner earnings land at only ~$119K–$153K (14–16% of revenue) and payback runs
~8.1–10.1 years, with 18 units (8.6%) closing in 2024 on margin compression from
over-saturation. A startup unit will earn far LESS than that mature number for the first 12–24
months while it ramps, yet the SBA debt service is full-size from month one. The arithmetic
that follows shows the base case landing around break-even-to-thin DSCR and well short of a
$300K/founder take-home; the concept only "works" financially on an aggressive, unproven ramp.
The macro tailwind is real but is already priced into a crowded segment — a large growing market
does not rescue a unit whose own comp earns a 14–16% margin and closes 1-in-12 locations a year.

- **Year 1 base:** $45K ÷ $116.9K ≈ **0.38x** — does NOT cover debt; owner funds the shortfall from

**Bottom line of the math:** funded sensibly (15% down, 10.5%/10yr), the BASE case is a multi-year
cash-negative ramp into a **mature DSCR of only ~1.15x** and an owner take-home a fraction of the
income ladder. This clears SBA underwriting only on an optimistic projected ramp with a personal
guarantee, and has little downside cushion.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
1. Point-of-sale cash collection (memberships + retail) gives favorable working-capital timing vs. a long-AR services business.
2. Build cost ($509K–$1.18M) is in line with the public comp, so the unit is not being *overpriced* relative to the category — the problem is the category's economics, not a markup.
3. Real, large, growing macro market (wellness ~$6.8T→$9.8T; longevity niche ~$31.6B→$46.9B) — demand exists; this is a top-line tailwind, not a margin fix.

## TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
1. Mature owner-earnings margin is only ~14–16% (comp), producing a sub-1.25x projected DSCR and sub-ladder take-home.
2. Multi-year negative-to-thin ramp (Year-1 DSCR ~0.38x) against full-size debt service from month one — the owner funds the gap.
3. Documented over-saturation / margin compression and 8.6% annual unit closures in the exact comparable segment — a structural, not one-off, risk.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The defining financial problem is that the only available reality check — Restore Hyper Wellness, a
directly analogous cryo/recovery/wellness franchise with a public FDD — earns only ~$119K–$153K of
owner earnings on ~$851K–$962K of MATURE revenue, pays back in 8–10 years, and closed 8.6% of its
units in a single year on margin compression from over-saturation. A brand-new Ultimate Longevity
unit must service a full ~$117K/yr SBA payment from day one while ramping into (at best) those same
thin mature numbers, with NO Item 19 of its own to underwrite against and soft, depreciating
collateral behind the loan. The base case is therefore a multi-year cash-negative ramp into a
~1.15x mature DSCR and an owner take-home far below the $300K ladder — and that is the *good* outcome.
The downside, which the comp shows is common, is a unit that never reaches self-supporting DSCR and
becomes one of the closures. A large, growing macro market does not repair a 14–16% unit margin.
