# Acquisition Objectives & Industry-Screen Rubric

The source of truth for the **acquisition** council. This is the BUY-SIDE fork of the
build council; the build council in the repo root is separate and untouched.

## Two stages — read this first
We buy in two stages, and we score very differently in each:

- **STAGE 1 — INDUSTRY SCREEN (this council).** We decide **which TYPES of business
  (industries) are worth hunting in.** We score only what is true of the *kind* of
  business and knowable from the outside — without opening any one company's books or
  meeting its owner. The output is a short list of **~3 industries to go source deals in.**
- **STAGE 2 — TARGET DILIGENCE (`10-due-diligence.md`).** Once we have a **real company**
  for sale, we check the things you can only learn by looking at that specific business:
  earnings quality, owner-dependency, how the systems are set up, key-staff, customer
  concentration. The output is a buy / pass call on that one deal.

**Why split them?** Every sleepy, retiring-owner business has owner-dependency, un-verified
earnings, and old systems. Those are **universal** — they don't help you tell a good
*industry* from a bad one, they just drag every score down. So we do NOT score them in
Stage 1. They are deferred to Stage 2 due diligence, where they belong. Stage 1 asks a
cleaner question: **"Is this the kind of business worth looking for in the first place?"**

## The thesis — buy, don't build
Acquire a **profitable, durable, "sleepy" existing business** using an **SBA 7(a)
acquisition loan**, as a **faster path to founder free cash flow** than building from zero.
Retiring Baby Boomer owners are selling large numbers of cash-flowing small businesses,
often never modernized in marketing, operations, or technology. We buy proven cash flow at
a fair price, service the debt comfortably, and use the founders' edge — modern marketing,
modern operations, modern software/AI — to grow owner earnings.

## Founder role (decided): owner-operator, full-time
The founders step in **full-time as owner-operators / general managers**, leading sales,
marketing, strategy, finance, and modernization, and **managing an inherited workforce
(including blue-collar field crews).** They do **not** personally perform a licensed trade.
Inheriting and managing a field crew is fine and often the point; being the licensed
tradesperson is not.

## Deal size — let the council explore
Don't fix one size. Guardrails, not a number:
- **SBA 7(a) reality:** up to **$5M** loan; ~**10% equity injection** (part can be a
  standby seller note); **10-year** term (25 if real-estate-heavy); **personal guarantee**;
  variable rate ≈ **Prime + 2.75%** (≈ 10.25–10.5% in 2026 [ASSUMED — verify]).
- The typical deal in the industry should service debt at **DSCR ≥ 1.25x** (lender floor),
  ideally **≥ 1.5x**, after a market owner salary.

## Financial targets (a ladder, not a cliff)
- **By ~year 1–2: ~$300K+/founder/year** in combined owner earnings (salary + profit after
  debt service).
- **By ~year 3+: ~$500K+/founder and growing**, via modernization and/or bolt-on
  (tuck-in) acquisitions.
- **Multiple arbitrage is upside, not a requirement.**

## Competitive posture
Favor **fragmented industries full of sleepy local operators.** Avoid industries where
**well-funded PE roll-ups are actively buying** and have bid entry multiples up across the
board (e.g., much of residential HVAC, plumbing, dental, veterinary, auto repair, express
car wash). A bidding war is not our game.

## STAGE-1 HARD CONSTRAINTS (industry-level; any "no" = the industry is out)
These are properties of the TYPE of business, not of a specific company.
1. **The industry isn't SBA-eligible** (passive/real-estate-holding, lending/investment,
   speculative, MLM, federally-illegal like cannabis, gambling-dominant).
2. **The model can't service SBA debt** at the industry's typical multiples and margins
   (e.g., margins structurally too thin, or multiples bid up so high no SBA structure works).
3. **The model has no inherent recurring/repeat/essential revenue** (pure one-off project
   work, fads, or structurally declining demand).
4. **The model structurally requires the owner to personally hold a non-bridgeable
   credential** (the business legally cannot run unless an owner personally holds an active
   license that can't be transferred, hired in via a qualifying employee, or earned in
   reasonable time).
5. **The industry is in an active PE bidding war** that has pushed entry multiples beyond
   sane, SBA-financeable levels across the board.
6. **The model typically needs capital beyond** an SBA loan + a reasonable equity injection.

(Company-specific knock-outs — owner-is-the-business, fake earnings, dead systems, one
giant customer — are NOT screened here. They are Stage-2 due-diligence items in
`10-due-diligence.md`.)

## STAGE-1 RUBRIC — 1–10 each, weighted to 100 (industry-level only)
Score what is true of the TYPE of business and knowable from the outside.

| # | Dimension | Weight | A 10 | A 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Revenue durability of the model** | 20 | The TYPE inherently has recurring/contracted/route/mandated or essential-repeat revenue (e.g., annual mandated inspections, service contracts, consumable reorders) | The TYPE is one-off project work, fads, or discretionary with no repeat |
| 2 | **Deal economics & financeability** | 15 | Typical deals price at sane multiples (~2.5–3.5x SDE) with margins that service SBA debt at DSCR ≥1.5x | Typical deals are thin-margin or bid-up so DSCR doesn't clear 1.25x |
| 3 | **Deal supply (pipeline depth)** | 15 | Thousands of these are owned by retiring owners, fragmented, and actively listed/sourceable | Rare, closely held, almost never for sale |
| 4 | **Modernization upside of the type** | 15 | The industry is broadly sleepy/un-modernized (no digital marketing, manual ops, old tech) so the founders' edge applies across most operators | Already a modern, professionalized, well-marketed industry |
| 5 | **Income-ladder headroom** | 10 | The typical or scalable business in this space reaches ~$300K→$500K+/founder (alone or via bolt-ons) | The model caps owner earnings well below the ladder |
| 6 | **Competitive structure** | 10 | Fragmented, sleepy local field; essential/defensible local moats; no consolidator war | Active PE roll-up bidding multiples up and competing margins down |
| 7 | **Operator fit (the TYPE)** | 5 | Founders can run this type as managers; field work done by a managed crew; no non-bridgeable personal credential | Model structurally needs the owner to personally do a licensed trade or sustained manual labor |
| 8 | **Industry durability & tailwinds** | 5 | Essential, non-discretionary, stable/growing, recession-resilient end-market | Structurally declining or faddish demand |
| 9 | **Growth & exit optionality of the type** | 5 | Room to grow organically + a bolt-on/roll-up path + resale at a higher multiple (multiple arbitrage) | Capped, no roll-up logic, no step-up buyer |

**Scoring discipline:** 5 = honestly mediocre, 7 = good, 9+ = rare and earned. Don't
cluster in 6–8.

## STAGE-1 scorecard template
```
INDUSTRY / BUSINESS TYPE: <name>
ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION: <what kind of business, who it serves>

HARD CONSTRAINT CHECK (industry-level)
- SBA-eligible industry? [Y/N + note]
- Model can service SBA debt at typical multiples/margins? [Y/N + note]
- Inherent recurring/repeat/essential revenue? [Y/N + note]
- Runnable without the owner personally holding a non-bridgeable credential? [Y/N + note]
- Not in an active PE bidding war? [Y/N + note]
- Capital needs fit SBA + reasonable injection? [Y/N + note]
> Any N: STOP. Industry is out.

DIMENSION SCORES (1–10)
1. Revenue durability of the model:     [score] — [reason]
2. Deal economics & financeability:     [score] — [reason]
3. Deal supply (pipeline depth):        [score] — [reason]
4. Modernization upside of the type:    [score] — [reason]
5. Income-ladder headroom:              [score] — [reason]
6. Competitive structure:               [score] — [reason]
7. Operator fit (the type):             [score] — [reason]
8. Industry durability & tailwinds:     [score] — [reason]
9. Growth & exit optionality:           [score] — [reason]

WEIGHTED TOTAL: [sum(score×weight)/10] / 100

TOP 3 STRENGTHS / TOP 3 RISKS / RECOMMENDATION (PURSUE / MAYBE / PASS)
```

## Thresholds
- Weighted total >= 70 AND no dimension below 5 -> PURSUE-grade; advance to full council.
- 55–69 -> MAYBE; tighten the definition (a sub-segment) and rescore.
- < 55 OR any dimension at 3 or below -> PASS (drop the industry).

## Plain-language verdicts (no jargon)
- **Stage 1 (this council):** an industry is rated **PURSUE** (worth hunting in), **MAYBE**
  (worth hunting in a narrower sub-segment), or **PASS** (don't bother).
- **Stage 2 (due diligence on one company):** a specific deal is **GO**, **PASS**, or
  **OFFER A LOWER PRICE**. ("Offer a lower price" is what M&A pros call a "re-trade" — going
  back to the seller after you've agreed a price and asking them to accept less because
  diligence changed what the business is worth. We use the plain phrase.)

## What this doc is NOT
Not a business plan, not a deal model, and not a substitute for real due diligence and
real advisors (CPA / SBA lender / M&A attorney). It is the Stage-1 filter for choosing
industries. Company-specific scoring lives in `10-due-diligence.md`.
