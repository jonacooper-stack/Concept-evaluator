# Acquisition Objectives & Evaluation Rubric

The source of truth for the **acquisition** council. Every expert agent (CFO, CMO,
COO, Legal, CTO) and the PM/Deal synthesizer reads from THIS doc when evaluating a
business to **buy**, not a business to build. The build council's `01-objectives.md`
in the repo root is a separate, untouched system; do not mix them.

## The thesis — buy, don't build

### Primary objective
Acquire a **profitable, durable, "sleepy" existing business** using an **SBA 7(a)
acquisition loan**, as a **faster path to founder free cash flow** than building from
zero. The "silver tsunami" of retiring Baby Boomer owners is putting a large, steady
supply of cash-flowing small businesses on the market, often owned by operators who
never modernized their marketing, operations, or technology. We buy day-one cash
flow at a fair multiple, service the debt comfortably, and use the founders' edge —
modern marketing, modern operations, modern software/AI — to grow owner earnings.

### Why this is attractive for THESE founders
The founders are versatile, marketing-strong, software-capable operators. The classic
seller is a 60–70-year-old owner with a great book of business, loyal customers, a
working crew, and **no website worth the name, no CRM, paper invoices, no paid
acquisition, and prices they haven't raised in a decade.** That gap between a sound
business and an un-modernized one is exactly the value the founders create. We are not
inventing demand; we are inheriting proven demand and running the same business better.

### What "good" looks like (the archetype)
A boring, essential, recurring-revenue local/regional business with:
- **5+ years of stable or growing cash flow** (Seller's Discretionary Earnings / SDE
  or EBITDA that holds up under scrutiny and clean add-backs);
- **recurring or highly repeat revenue** (service contracts, maintenance plans, route
  density, a sticky B2B customer base) — not one-time project lumpiness;
- **low customer concentration** (no single customer is a large share of revenue);
- a **transferable** operation — it runs on systems and a crew/GM, not solely on the
  departing owner's personal relationships;
- a **sleepy seller** who left obvious modernization money on the table (marketing,
  pricing, systems) that the founders can capture;
- a **fragmented competitive landscape** of other sleepy local operators — NOT a
  market being rolled up and bid to high multiples by well-funded PE consolidators;
- **SBA-eligible**, at a price where the deal math services debt at a comfortable DSCR
  and still pays the founders a real income.

### Founder role (decided): owner-operator, full-time
The founders step in **full-time as owner-operators / general managers** — leading
sales, marketing, strategy, finance, and the modernization program, and **managing an
inherited workforce (including blue-collar field crews).** They do **not** personally
perform a licensed trade. Inheriting and managing a field crew is fine and often the
point; being the licensed tradesperson who must hold the wrench and the credential is
not. (This is the key difference from the build council, which avoids field ops
entirely. Here we will happily *own* a field-service business — we just won't *be* the
field crew.)

### Deal size — let the council explore
Do NOT fix a single target size. The idea-generator proposes representative size
ranges per business type; the council scores the trade-offs. Guardrails rather than a
fixed number:
- **SBA 7(a) reality:** up to **$5M** loan; minimum **~10% equity injection** (a portion
  can be a seller note on full standby); **10-year** term for a goodwill/business
  acquisition (25 years if real estate dominates); **personal guarantee required** from
  any 20%+ owner; **variable rate** roughly **Prime + 2.75%** (with Prime ≈ 7.5% in
  2026, ≈ **10.25–10.5%** all-in) [ASSUMED — verify current Prime and lender spread].
- **The deal must service debt at DSCR ≥ 1.25x** (lender floor) and we want **≥ 1.5x**
  cushion, **after** a market-rate owner salary, and still clear the founder-income
  ladder below.

### Financial targets (a ladder, not a single cliff)
- **By ~year 1–2: ~$300K+/founder/year** in combined owner earnings (owner salary +
  distributable profit after debt service). Two founders may run one larger business
  together, or this can be read as combined owner earnings the partnership splits.
- **By ~year 3+: ~$500K+/founder and growing**, via organic modernization-driven growth
  and/or bolt-on (tuck-in) acquisitions.
- **Multiple arbitrage is the upside, not the requirement:** buy at a small-business
  multiple, professionalize, and the business is worth a higher multiple to the next
  buyer (PE platform, strategic, or SBA-financed searcher). Do not kill an otherwise
  strong deal for lacking a home-run exit.

### Capital posture
Levered, not bootstrapped: an **SBA loan + a fundable equity injection** (founders'
cash and/or a standby seller note), with enough **working-capital cushion** for the
transition. No outside equity raise assumed. The founders can fund the ~10% injection
and reasonable closing/working-capital needs [FILL IN: confirm actual cash available
for injection + reserves].

### Competitive posture
Favor **fragmented markets full of sleepy local operators.** Beware industries where
**well-funded PE roll-ups / consolidators are actively buying** (they bid entry
multiples up and compete down margins — e.g., parts of HVAC, plumbing, dental,
veterinary, auto repair). A moat is fine; a bidding war is not.

### Probability of success
Favor **proven, in-place cash flow** over turnaround bets. We are buying a working
business and improving it — not rescuing a failing one. Distressed turnarounds are a
different (higher-risk) game we are not playing.

## Hard constraints (any "no" = automatic disqualification)
1. **Not SBA-eligible.** Must be a for-profit, US-based operating business that meets
   SBA size standards and is not on the ineligible list (no passive/real-estate-holding
   companies, lending/investment businesses, speculative ventures, multi-level
   marketing, businesses illegal under federal law such as cannabis, gambling-dominant,
   etc.).
2. **Cash flow cannot carry the deal.** If realistic SDE/EBITDA cannot cover debt
   service at **DSCR ≥ 1.25x** AND a market-rate owner salary AND a path to the income
   ladder, it is dead.
3. **Non-transferable.** The business collapses on the owner's exit — revenue is the
   seller's personal relationships/reputation/credential and does not survive handover,
   with no GM, contracts, or systems to carry it.
4. **No durable/repeat revenue.** Pure one-time project pipeline with no backlog,
   a fad/declining category, or **single-customer concentration > ~35%** with no path
   to diversify.
5. **Founder must personally hold a non-bridgeable credential** to operate (e.g., the
   business legally cannot run unless an owner personally holds an active professional
   license that cannot be transferred, hired in, or earned in reasonable time). NOTE:
   a licensed-trade business where a **qualifying licensed employee** holds the license
   is acceptable — flag, don't disqualify.
6. **Capital beyond reach.** Requires an equity injection / working-capital cushion the
   founders cannot fund, or a price so far above lendable value that no SBA structure
   works.

(Note: "modernization upside" is NOT a hard constraint — it is a heavily-scored
dimension below. A clean but already-modern business isn't disqualified, just scored
lower on the founders' edge.)

## Evaluation rubric — 1–10 each, weighted to 100

| # | Dimension | Weight | A 10 | A 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cash-flow quality & durability** | 20 | 5+ yrs stable/growing SDE with clean add-backs; recurring/contracted revenue; no customer >10% | Lumpy one-time project revenue, declining, undocumented add-backs, one customer = 40% |
| 2 | **Deal math / debt-service coverage** | 15 | DSCR ≥ 2.0x at a market multiple (~2.5–3.5x SDE) after market owner salary; injection easily fundable | DSCR < 1.25x at any defensible price; deal only works if you overpay |
| 3 | **Transferability & owner-independence** | 15 | Runs on a GM/crew + documented systems + company-owned customer relationships; licenses transfer/hireable | Owner IS the business; everything is in their head and their personal relationships |
| 4 | **Modernization upside (founders' edge)** | 15 | Sleepy seller: weak/no web presence, no CRM, no paid acquisition, under-priced; obvious modern-marketing/ops/tech gains the founders can execute | Already professionally run and modern; little for these founders to add |
| 5 | **Path to founder income (post-debt ladder)** | 10 | After debt service + reinvestment, conservative math clears ~$300K/founder yr1–2 and ~$500K+ yr3 | No credible path to ~$300K/founder even after modernization |
| 6 | **Deal availability & close probability** | 5 | Thousands of similar businesses listed at sane multiples; deep, fragmented supply; financeable | Rare, auction-priced, strategic/PE buyers bidding it up; hard to finance |
| 7 | **Operational tractability for owner-operators** | 5 | Founders can run/oversee it without a credential they lack; field WORK is done by an inherited managed crew, not by the founders personally | Founders must personally perform a licensed trade or sustained manual labor they can't bridge |
| 8 | **Competitive landscape** | 5 | Fragmented, sleepy local/regional competitors; no consolidator bidding war | Market actively rolled up by well-funded PE platforms compressing multiples and margins |
| 9 | **Scalability / growth & exit optionality** | 10 | Organic modernization growth + bolt-on/tuck-in roll-up path + resale at higher multiple (multiple arbitrage) | Capped, declining industry, no growth lever, no step-up buyer |

**Scoring discipline:** 5 = honestly mediocre, 7 = good, 9+ = rare and earned.
Don't cluster in 6–8.

## How to score a TARGET PROFILE vs a real listing
At the **discovery** stage we score **target profiles** — an industry × size ×
situation archetype (e.g., "boomer-owned commercial HVAC service company, ~$2M
revenue, ~$550K SDE, Sunbelt metro, contract-heavy, no digital marketing"). Use
**representative financials**, clearly labeled `[ASSUMED]` and given as ranges, and
treat the cheap verification as **"pull 3–5 comparable listings (BizBuySell, broker
CIMs, industry multiples)."** When a **real listing** is in hand, the SAME council
re-runs on its **actual** financials (P&L, tax returns, CIM, customer list, equipment
schedule) — and the evidence bar becomes the real numbers, not assumptions.

## Scorecard template
```
TARGET PROFILE: <name / archetype>
ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION: <what kind of business, size, situation>

HARD CONSTRAINT CHECK
- SBA-eligible (for-profit, US, operating, not on ineligible list)? [Y/N + note]
- Cash flow covers debt service ≥1.25x DSCR + market owner salary? [Y/N + note]
- Durable/repeat revenue, no fatal customer concentration? [Y/N + note]
- Transferable (survives owner exit; licenses transfer/hireable)? [Y/N + note]
- Runnable by owner-operators WITHOUT a non-bridgeable personal credential? [Y/N + note]
- Equity injection + working capital fundable by founders? [Y/N + note]
> Any N: STOP. Disqualified.

DIMENSION SCORES (1–10)
1. Cash-flow quality & durability:       [score] — [reason]
2. Deal math / debt-service coverage:    [score] — [reason]
3. Transferability & owner-independence: [score] — [reason]
4. Modernization upside (founders' edge): [score] — [reason]
5. Path to founder income (post-debt):   [score] — [reason]
6. Deal availability & close probability: [score] — [reason]
7. Operational tractability:             [score] — [reason]
8. Competitive landscape:                [score] — [reason]
9. Scalability / growth & exit:          [score] — [reason]

WEIGHTED TOTAL: [sum(score×weight)/10] / 100

TOP 3 STRENGTHS / TOP 3 RISKS / RECOMMENDATION
```

## Thresholds
- Weighted total >= 70 AND no dimension below 5 -> advance to full acquisition council.
- 55–69 -> refine the profile (tighten the target) and rescore.
- < 55 OR any dimension at 3 or below -> drop.

## What this doc is NOT
Not a business plan, a deal model, or a substitute for real due diligence and real
advisors (CPA, SBA lender, M&A attorney). It is the filter and yardstick the
acquisition council scores against.
