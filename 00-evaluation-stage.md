# 00 — Evaluation Stage, Evidence Standard & Calibration

Read this BEFORE 01-objectives.md and before scoring anything. Every reviewer
(CFO, CMO, Competitive Analyst, COO, Legal, CTO) and the PM synthesizer applies
this doc. Where any older doc contradicts this one on stage, evidence, or
calibration, THIS doc wins.

## What stage we are at — CONCEPT stage

We are evaluating **concepts**, not operating businesses and not post-validation
plans. At this stage the following are **true and expected**, and must NEVER be
treated as a deduction:

- No primary customer interviews have been run yet.
- No product is built; no pilot, no live traction, no revenue data exists.
- Numbers are estimates, comparables, and bottoms-up models — not booked actuals.

**The rule:** Do not deduct points for the absence of primary research, a built
product, or live traction. Those are the *next steps*, not flaws in the concept.
Every review ends by listing the cheapest experiments that would validate the
thesis — as **recommendations**, never as score penalties. Judge the *quality and
strength of the thesis on the best evidence available now*.

What "best evidence available now" means at concept stage:
- Named comparable companies / competitors and their public data (pricing,
  funding, headcount, reviews, filings).
- Observable, existing spend and budget lines ("buyers in this segment already
  pay $X/yr for Y").
- Analogous markets where the same pattern already worked.
- Public filings, analyst data, regulator publications, trade-association data.
- Sound bottoms-up arithmetic with explicit, stated assumptions.

## Evidence standard for scoring (stage-aware)

A high score must be *earned with reasoning or evidence* — but at concept stage,
**a named comparable, a credible bottoms-up calculation, or a documented market
signal is fully sufficient to support an 8 or 9.** You do NOT need internal
actuals you couldn't possibly have yet.

- A score of **8+** must rest on at least one of: (a) a **named** competitor /
  comparable / source with real data, (b) a **shown** bottoms-up calculation with
  its assumptions stated, or (c) a **documented** market signal (an existing
  budget line, a mandate, a cited analog).
- Every material number is either **attributed to a named source** OR labeled
  **[ASSUMED]**, given as a **range**, and paired with a **cheap experiment** that
  would verify it. A labeled, ranged, testable assumption is legitimate evidence
  at this stage — it does not invalidate a score.
- Unsupported adjectives ("strong", "robust", "huge", "proven") do not *raise* a
  score on their own — back the claim with a reason or comparable. But a
  well-reasoned estimate tied to a named analog is NOT a "generic adjective"; it
  is exactly the evidence this stage runs on.

> If you have web access (the CMO and the Competitive Analyst do), you are
> **required to actually look things up** — name real competitors, find real
> pricing, size the market from real sources — rather than guess or hedge. Do not
> return "unknown" for something a 10-minute search would answer.

## Calibration — honest in BOTH directions

Calibrate like a seasoned operator/investor who has seen hundreds of these and is
neither a cheerleader nor a cynic. **Do not inflate, and do not deflate.**
Suppressing a genuinely strong concept to "play it safe" is exactly as dishonest
as inflating a weak one — and it is the failure mode this framework is correcting.

Anchor every sub-score to what the band actually means:

- **9–10 — Exceptional / rare.** A real, evidenced standout on this axis; you would
  personally want in. Earn it with hard evidence, but when it's there, **give it**.
- **7–8 — Good to strong.** A genuine, evidenced strength. Most dimensions of a
  genuinely good concept should land here. This is not a ceiling to fear.
- **5–6 — Average / mediocre.** Works but unremarkable, or the evidence is thin.
- **3–4 — Weak.** A real problem on this axis.
- **1–2 — Fatal or near-fatal** on this axis.

A genuinely excellent concept **should** produce a field of 8s and 9s with
evidence behind them. That is a correct result, not a red flag. Do not manufacture
a low score for "balance," and do not manufacture a high one to clear a bar. There
is no bar disclosed to you; score on absolute merit and let the number land.

## What this fixes (context, not instructions)

Earlier versions asked reviewers to cite named competitors and hard numbers while
giving them no way to research, then capped any unsupported claim at 6 and feared
high scores as "gaming." The mechanical result was an honest field stuck at
~6.6–7.2 that could never clear an 8.0 bar — the system looked rigorous but was
quietly rigged to fail. The fixes: (1) give the market-facing reviewers real
research tools, (2) make the evidence standard match the concept stage, and
(3) make calibration symmetric. Honesty means the number is *right*, not that it
is *low*.
