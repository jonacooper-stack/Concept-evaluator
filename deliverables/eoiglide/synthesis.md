## ONE-PARAGRAPH PM READ
This concept has a genuinely strong half and a genuinely weak half, and they belong to different framings of the same business. The strong half is demand: an evidenced 8 that all three demand-touching reviewers reached independently — a multi-billion-dollar voluntary life+disability premium pool (LIMRA ~$4.5B workplace life 2024), documented EOI leakage, and four named DOL settlements (Prudential, Unum, Lincoln) that turn "collect premium only after EOI approval, and prove it" into a real compliance forcing function. The weak half is the competitive position, and it is the worst of any recent concept: the two web-enabled reviewers independently destroyed the one-pager's load-bearing premise that "the chase-to-completion workflow is owned by no single party." It is owned — by carriers shipping ~85% instant-decision digital EOI (Unum, The Hartford), by ben-admin platforms shipping it natively (PlanSource Boost, Selerix, bswift), by Employee Navigator ($1.5–2.0B, owns the broker), and by benefits-data APIs (Ideon $44.7M, Noyo $57M) that own the cross-carrier rails — with HIGH encroachment risk. The honest call: the strong, narrow wedge the analysts surfaced (a contingency-priced premium-recapture service for the stalled-case long tail plus a DOL-audit-grade cross-carrier disclosure ledger) is real but materially smaller than the headline, and it sits one bundled product-release away from absorption by funded platforms. Competition, weighted at its full 18, drags this out of contention as written. This is a **REFINE → Tier C (top of the band, borderline B)**, not a GO.

# PLAIN-ENGLISH DELIVERABLES (for Mike)

## CONCEPT DOSSIER — EOIGlide
**Tier: C — Pass (borderline B; refine before any build) | Objectives Score: 65/100 | 2026-06-23**

### 1. IN ONE PARAGRAPH
When an employee signs up at work for extra life or disability insurance above a certain "no-questions-asked" amount, the insurer makes them fill out a health form first — "evidence of insurability," or EOI. A lot of people never finish it. So the employee thinks they bought coverage, the company thinks it sold coverage, but the extra coverage never turns on — and the premium that should have been collected just leaks away. EOIGlide is software that would chase every unfinished health form to a yes/no decision, across all of an employer's carriers at once, and keep an audit-proof record showing exactly when each person was approved (which matters because the U.S. Department of Labor has started fining insurers for collecting premium before approval). We'd sell to brokers and carriers — a monthly per-employee fee plus a cut of the premium we help them actually capture.

### 2. WHO PAYS — AND WHY THEY CAN'T SAY NO
The buyers are **benefits brokers** (who lose commission when coverage doesn't turn on) and **carriers / their group divisions** (who lose the premium). The forcing function has two faces, unequal in strength: **Face 1, "stop the leak"** is real money but weaker than it sounds because the big players already chase these forms themselves; **Face 2, compliance**, is the stronger, closer-to-true must-have — a regulatory/risk-mitigation forcing function created by four recent government settlements (Prudential, Unum, Lincoln) making it legally dangerous to collect premium before approval and to *prove* it. Honest catch: a couple of competitors already advertise "avoid these compliance breaches" too.

### 3. THE MONEY (PLAIN TERMS)
A **subscription** of ~$0.50–$1.50 per employee per month (normal for this market) — one 2,000-person employer at $1/month ≈ $24,000/year. Plus a **success fee**: a slice (10–20%) of premium we help capture. To get each of us to ~**$300K/year by month 12** we'd need ~**20–35 active employer groups**, reachable through a **handful of broker/carrier relationships**; doubling that gets toward **$500K each by month 24**. The math works *on the subscription alone* — **but** the success fee is shaky (carriers may refuse to share premium; a regulator might call a premium-based fee unlicensed insurance selling), so treat it as a bonus, not the plan. (Estimates to verify with a few calls.)

### 4. WHY WE WIN
Our edge is real but narrow: **one genuinely empty spot** — an *independent* service working **across all of an employer's carriers at once** (no carrier builds that for rivals), paid **only when it recovers money** (big platforms prefer flat fees and dislike this), keeping the **compliance audit trail** regulators now demand. A real-world parallel (Certificial, in a different insurance niche) pushed completion from ~60–70% to ~90% as a neutral "someone finally owns the chase" layer. Our founder edge (marketing, positioning, software, founder-led selling) fits the broker channel; the DOL settlements make the timing good.

### 5. THE COMPETITION (FROM THE ANALYST — the hard truth)
The worst competition picture of any concept we've looked at. "Nobody owns this chase" is mostly **not true** on the modern path: **carriers** (Unum, Hartford, Pacific Life, Mutual of Omaha) run digital forms with ~**85% instant decisions** for their own policies; **ben-admin platforms** (PlanSource Boost, Selerix, bswift) ship this feature built-in; **Employee Navigator** ($1.5–2.0B, ~5,000 brokers) can bolt it on and hand it to thousands for ~free; **Ideon and Noyo** ($44.7M, $57M) already own the cross-carrier pipes. The scariest item in the review: any of them could **bundle this within 2–4 quarters at near-zero price**. The only durable corner is the **stalled-case long tail** the big platforms ignore, plus the compliance audit record — real, but smaller than the headline and in someone else's shadow.

### 6. THE BIGGEST RISKS (TOP 3, PLAIN)
1. **A big platform copies it cheaply** — once we prove the money's there, Employee Navigator or PlanSource ships the same thing to their installed base at ~$0 and squeezes us into the leftovers.
2. **The exciting "success fee" might not be collectible — or might be illegal as designed** — fix by leaning on the plain subscription and re-shaping the fee with a lawyer first.
3. **We can't get the data we need from the two ends we don't control** — if the enrollment file doesn't flag who skipped, and carriers won't share decisions, the product shrinks to "a nicer form plus a person typing in statuses."

### 7. WHAT WE'D TEST NEXT (cheap, ~2–4 weeks each, before building anything)
1. One friendly broker hands over a real (de-identified) enrollment file + plan rules — can we even tell who needs a form from the data we can get?
2. One mid-size carrier ops person, 30 minutes: will you let a third party chase your forms and tell us the decisions — and would you pay a cut?
3. Email Employee Navigator/Ease and PlanSource partner programs: what data access can a small vendor get, in weeks or quarters?
4. Pull completion stats from 5–10 *non-integrated* carriers/TPAs + ~10 broker books to size the leftover market, and get one broker/carrier to verbally commit to a success fee. **The make-or-break test.**

### 8. THE SCORECARD (plain)
- **Must-have (7/10):** real and partly compliance-driven, but the "leak" half is undercut and even the compliance half is partly pre-empted.
- **Competition (4.4/10):** the weak axis — funded modern players already ship the core on both sides; high copy/bundle risk within a few quarters.
- **Path to income (7/10):** reaches ~$300K→$500K each on subscription alone, but the wedge is smaller than the headline and the upside fee is shaky.
- **Recurring revenue (7/10):** real monthly base; the success fee is per-cycle and contestable.
- **Demand evidence (8/10):** the clear strength — big premium pool, four named DOL settlements, existing budget line.
- **Capital efficiency (8.5/10):** pure software, ~3–4 months for one developer, low five figures.
- **Operations (7/10):** clean remote shape, but a Q4 rush and a support load that grows with headcount could drag margins.
- **Founder fit (8/10):** in our wheelhouse; HIPAA/insurance background makes the scary compliance part home turf.
- **Scalability (7/10):** can scale or sell, but encroachment caps the ceiling.
- **Legal risk gate: SERIOUS BUT MANAGEABLE** — regulated but navigable; the one landmine is making the premium-based fee look like unlicensed insurance selling. Does NOT lower the tier.
- **Honest tier: C (Pass), right on the B border. What moves it up:** stop pitching "the platform nobody built," shrink to the independent cross-carrier compliance-and-recovery service for the leftovers, prove one broker/mid-tier carrier will pay a success fee, and confirm that leftover market is big enough for our income ladder.
