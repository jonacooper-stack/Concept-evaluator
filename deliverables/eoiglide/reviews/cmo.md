# CMO REVIEW — Evidence-of-Insurability (EOI) Orchestration for Voluntary Benefits

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
There is a real, recurring, money-on-the-table problem here, and after four DOL settlements it has grown a genuine compliance forcing function — so this is not a vitamin. But the central positioning claim, "the chase-to-completion workflow that no single party owns," is the part the market has already contradicted: Employee Navigator (65,000+ employers, 2,500+ brokers), PlanSource, Selerix, Unum, bswift, and the carriers themselves (Guardian, Principal, The Hartford, Pacific Life, Mutual of Omaha, plus Alight/New York Life) already ship digital EOI with real-time decision-return APIs, status dashboards, automated multi-touch reminders, and self-reported completion rates of 85-95% on the integrated path. So the honest read is: the pain is real and the budget exists, but a standalone vendor is trying to wedge into the one seam — the carrier-funded integration between ben-admin and underwriting — that the two best-resourced parties in the chain are actively closing for free. This is sellable, but only with a sharply narrower wedge than "we own the orchestration" — most likely the cross-carrier compliance/reconciliation layer for the long tail that the modern integrated path leaves behind.

## POSITIONING DRAFT
For a benefits broker or a carrier's group division, as a customer would hear it: *"Every above-guaranteed-issue election you don't get approved is premium you already sold and then lost — and since the DOL settlements, collecting that premium before EOI is approved is now a liability. We're the independent layer that chases every pending EOI to a decision across all your carriers and gives you one auditable record that proves approval before premium is collected."* That is a clear sentence, and the second half (compliance + cross-carrier audit trail) is the part that has edges. The first half (we chase EOI to completion) is NOT differentiated — it is verbatim what PlanSource, Selerix, and Unum already market. If the founders lead with "we close the EOI gap," they sound like every ben-admin platform's existing feature page. If they lead with "independent, cross-carrier, audit-grade proof-of-approval that survives a DOL look," they have a sentence the incumbents cannot say, because each carrier integration is single-carrier and each ben-admin EOI dashboard stops at the carriers it has APIs with.

## THE WEDGE
The first 100 customers do NOT come from selling carriers (12-18 month enterprise cycles, and the carrier is the party whose own portal you're implicitly indicting). They come from mid-market benefits brokers and the regional enrollment firms — the parties who feel the leaked-commission pain personally and carry the new fiduciary anxiety for their employer clients. Concrete entry vector: the DOL-settlement compliance hook as the door-opener, not the premium-capture pitch. Tactic and cost: (1) Targeted outbound + content to benefits brokers via the trade channel — NAHU/NABIP chapters, BenefitsPRO and the enrollment-firm conference circuit, and LinkedIn outbound to benefits-agency principals; a focused founder-led outbound + webinar motion realistically runs an [ASSUMED] blended CAC of $1,500-$4,000 per broker logo (cheap to verify: run one $300 LinkedIn campaign + a "Are you DOL-EOI exposed?" checklist landing page and measure booked demos). (2) Land through a single sleepy carrier or a TPA that lacks modern EOI APIs and white-label the reconciliation layer for them — one such partner can deliver dozens of broker/group relationships at once. The wedge that actually exists is narrow but real: brokers writing voluntary on carriers that are NOT on the Employee Navigator/PlanSource/Selerix integrated rails, plus any group whose stack spans 2+ carriers where no single dashboard reconciles all pending EOI. The wedge that does NOT exist is "sell employers a better EOI nudge" — the employer already gets that bundled in their ben-admin platform.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- **Employee Navigator** — the broker-distributed ben-admin gorilla (65,000+ employers, 2,500+ brokers). Best at: being the system brokers already log into daily, with EOI APIs to Guardian, Principal, and The Hartford that return underwriting decisions instantly. The incumbent that owns your buyer's attention; if EOI orchestration becomes a visible revenue/compliance line, this is who adds it as a checkbox.
- **PlanSource** — best at: a polished, fully-integrated electronic-EOI experience (submission API, real-time Decision Notification API, admin audit checklists/dashboards). Already markets nearly the exact words in this one-pager.
- **Selerix (BenSelect)** — best at: carrier-integration depth for core AND voluntary (e.g., the Pacific Life digital-EOI integration, "95%+ near-real-time decisions, decisions auto-transmitted, no manual broker updates"). Priced ~$3-5 PEPM. Directly occupies the "orchestration" claim.
- **The carriers' own digital EOI** — Unum ("85% get a decision instantly," admin dashboard of pending/outstanding), Guardian (automated decision return into Employee Navigator), Mutual of Omaha, Pacific Life, Alight/New York Life. Best at: owning the underwriting decision itself and funding the integration. Simultaneously your would-be customer AND the party whose single-carrier portal you're routing around.
- **Enrollment / EOI services firms** — EOI Service Company (founded 1979, acquired by CENTRO Benefits Research Oct 2024), bswift (already "tracks EOI approvals and coverage changes" as a service), and broker-side enrollment firms. Best at: human benefit counselors who chase completion at enrollment via call center and on-site — the manual version of what this software automates.

Where is the air? In three places, all narrower than the one-pager assumes: (1) Cross-carrier consolidation — every named tool is single-carrier or single-platform; nobody gives a broker ONE reconciled view of pending EOI across all their carriers. (2) Compliance/audit-grade proof-of-approval — the DOL settlements (Prudential, Unum, Lincoln; flagged by EPIC, Stinson, Groom, Kutak Rock) created a brand-new "did you confirm approval before collecting premium, and can you prove it" workflow the legacy EOI dashboards were not designed around. (3) The long tail of non-integrated carriers/TPAs that never built the modern API. Where the concept is NOT different: the core "guided digital questionnaire + reminders + status visibility" is table stakes shipped by four named incumbents today.

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- A real, recurring, already-budgeted pain with a documented dollar pool: ~$12B/yr in new workplace life+disability+supplemental premium (LIMRA), and the EOI gap leaks elected premium every cycle — demand is contracted-for, not invented.
- A genuine, named compliance forcing function: four DOL settlements (Prudential, Unum, Lincoln) created employer liability for collecting premium before confirming EOI approval and an implied weekly-reconciliation workflow — this upgrades the pitch from "capture upside" to "manage a liability you can't ignore."
- A reachable, specific buyer the founders can market to directly: mid-market benefits brokers and enrollment firms via well-defined trade channels (NABIP/NAHU, BenefitsPRO, conferences, LinkedIn), fitting a marketing-led + founder-close motion.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- The core differentiation is already shipped by named, well-marketed incumbents (Unum, PlanSource, Selerix, Employee Navigator) with 85-95% completion on the integrated path — "we chase EOI to completion" is a feature page, not a company.
- Channel conflict / disintermediation: the highest-value buyer (carriers) funds the very ben-admin integrations that make this redundant, and a horizontal platform like Employee Navigator or PlanSource can add cross-carrier reconciliation as a checkbox within ~2 quarters once it sees revenue.
- The success-fee revenue model is exposed to attribution disputes and post-DOL timing rules (carriers may only request EOI within the first year; can't deny after 90 days of premium) — proving "we captured this premium that would otherwise have leaked" is contestable, which weakens the most attractive part of the pricing.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely thing to kill this on market grounds is that the wedge sits inside the strike zone of the parties best positioned to close it for free. The one-pager's foundational claim — that the chase-to-completion workflow is "owned by no single party" — is not what the market shows. The two best-resourced links in the chain have already built it: ben-admin platforms (PlanSource's real-time Decision Notification API, Selerix's auto-transmitted decisions, Employee Navigator's Guardian/Principal/Hartford decision-return integrations) and the carriers themselves (Unum's "85% instant decision" dashboard). They build it because they capture the value directly — the carrier keeps the premium, the ben-admin keeps the broker on its platform — and they fund the integration, so a paid third party inserting a toll between them is structurally disadvantaged. A standalone EOI orchestrator therefore wins only in the residual seams: cross-carrier consolidation for multi-carrier groups, the long tail of carriers/TPAs without modern APIs, and the new DOL-driven audit/reconciliation layer. Those seams are real, but they are smaller than the headline TAM, and the moment this vendor proves the seam is worth money, Employee Navigator or PlanSource can extend their existing EOI module to cover it within roughly two quarters and bundle it at no incremental price. The concept survives only if the founders explicitly retreat from "we own the orchestration" to "we are the independent, cross-carrier, DOL-audit-grade layer the single-carrier portals structurally cannot be" — and even then they must win distribution faster than the incumbents notice.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What, precisely, can you do that PlanSource, Selerix, Unum, and Employee Navigator's already-shipping EOI modules cannot — and is it durable, or a checkbox they add in two quarters? (If the honest answer is "we consolidate across carriers and produce DOL-audit-grade proof-of-approval," say that is the product and drop the generic "chase-to-completion" framing entirely.)
- Who actually pays, and does the success fee survive contact with reality? Carriers fund the existing integrations and have little incentive to pay a toll to a router; brokers feel the commission leak but are price-sensitive and already pay $3-6 PEPM for ben-admin — can you prove a broker will pay a per-approved-application fee on top, and attribute "premium we captured" cleanly enough to bill it post-DOL-timing-rules?
- How large is the genuinely addressable wedge — the volume of above-GI voluntary elections sitting on carriers/groups NOT already on a modern integrated EOI path — versus the much smaller real-world abandonment on the 85-95%-completion integrated rails? (Cheap test: pull EOI completion stats from 5-10 non-integrated carriers/TPAs and from 10 mid-market broker books.)
- What is your beachhead distribution partner? Is there a specific sleepy carrier or TPA without modern EOI APIs that would white-label your reconciliation layer and hand you a book of broker/group relationships?

## RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The bones of a need-to-have are here — a real, recurring, already-elected premium pool plus named DOL/ERISA compliance teeth — and the demand evidence genuinely earns its 8. But as written, the concept's headline positioning ("the orchestration layer no one owns") is contradicted by named, well-marketed incumbents who ship that exact feature and fund the integrations that make a paid middle layer redundant, which is why differentiation (4) and competitive air (4) drag the average to mediocre. To make this fundable, refine on three specific axes: (1) Positioning — abandon "we close the EOI gap" and lead with the one claim the single-carrier portals structurally cannot make: "independent, cross-carrier, DOL-audit-grade proof-of-approval." (2) Customer/wedge — target brokers and the long-tail/non-integrated carriers and TPAs (not employers, who get this bundled, and not modern carriers, who built it), entering on the compliance-liability hook, not the premium-capture upside. (3) Revenue — stress-test whether anyone will pay a third-party toll on top of $3-6 PEPM ben-admin spend, and de-risk the success fee against attribution and post-DOL timing rules; if the success fee won't hold, the compliance-reconciliation subscription has to carry the model on its own. If the founders can credibly answer the four questions above — especially "what can't the incumbents trivially copy, and who hands us distribution" — this could move from mediocre toward promising; without that retreat to the narrow, defensible compliance-and-cross-carrier wedge, it is a feature competing against four companies' product pages.
