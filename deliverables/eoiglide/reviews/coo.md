# COO REVIEW — Evidence-of-Insurability (EOI) Orchestration for Voluntary Benefits

## ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is, at its operational core, a software workflow-orchestration and message-automation business — exactly the lean, remote-first, capital-light shape two software-capable founders can run, with the heavy lifting (election ingestion, questionnaire routing, reminders, status tracking, audit trail) done by code rather than humans. The repeated unit of work is *moving a pending EOI case to resolution*, and the entire thesis depends on automating that chase so it is NOT a human chasing employees one-by-one. There is real operational friction — carrier/ben-admin integrations, the manual-services tail of denials and exceptions, and a brutally seasonal Q4 spike — but none of it is field ops, none of it requires a crew, and the unit economics live or die on how much of the chase is genuinely software-driven versus a call-center quietly hiding in the model. If the founders keep the operating model "software orchestrates, a tiny ops bench handles exceptions," two people plus 2–4 hires can plausibly run this; if it degrades into "we staff humans to phone employees about their life-insurance forms," the model breaks and this becomes a BPO with a bad margin.

## THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Picture month 12. The platform is live with ~6–10 paying carrier/broker accounts covering ~40–80 employer groups and an EOI population of maybe 25,000–60,000 employees with *pending* above-GI cases at any time [ASSUMED]. March is NOT peak — peak is Q4 open enrollment (Sept–Dec) plus the January effective-date crunch. By March the operation is in "long-tail chase and resolution" mode: running down EOIs elected in the fall but still unresolved, handling new-hire/life-event EOIs that trickle in year-round, and onboarding the brokers/carriers that signed in Q1 to be ready for next Q4.

Founder A ("ops/integrations") starts in the admin console. Overnight, the platform pulled election files/API feeds from connected ben-admin systems (a mix of Employee Navigator, bswift, Workday, and carrier feeds), identified new elections that breach GI, and auto-created EOI cases. Most flowed cleanly. But there's an exception queue: 40 cases where the file format changed, a field was null, or an employee couldn't be matched to a carrier policy. Founder A (or an ops analyst) spends 60–90 minutes clearing it — re-mapping a field, pinging a broker for a corrected census, flagging two cases to the carrier. This is the recurring integration-hygiene tax, the single most underestimated line of work in this business.

Meanwhile the automated engine has been doing the actual product all night: sent reminder #2 to 1,800 employees with open EOI, reminder #3 (with a "your coverage is NOT yet in force" warning) to 600 past the 14-day mark, and an "approved — here's your effective date" confirmation to 220. No human touched any of those. The dashboard shows broker/employer/carrier-facing status: completion rate trending, dollars of in-force premium secured this week, stalled-case count. A broker logs in self-serve to see why 120 cases are stuck.

Now the human tail. Founder B (or a benefits-ops specialist) works escalation/support — where the manual time goes: ~25 inbound employee questions ("I got an email saying I'm not covered — what do I do?", "I can't finish the health questions"), ~10 broker/carrier emails about stuck or denied cases needing a clean audit record, and one carrier asking for a quarterly report. None hard, but human minutes, and they cluster around the warning emails the system sends. Plus the denied-case workflow: when a carrier denies/modifies, someone must ensure the disclosure trail is clean and the employee/broker correctly notified — ~15 cases today.

Where it breaks on a bad Tuesday: (1) a ben-admin partner silently changes their export schema and 1,200 cases fail to ingest overnight — a fire until the mapping is fixed, because every day is premium leaking and an SLA carriers care about. (2) A carrier's underwriting intake is slow, so "handed-off cleanly" cases stall on the *carrier's* side, the dashboard shows red, and the broker blames the platform. (3) The support queue spikes because a blunt reminder email scared a workforce and HR is fielding angry calls. On a normal March Tuesday, two founders + one or two hires clear all of it by 5pm. On a Q4 Tuesday, that volume is 4–6x.

## CAPACITY MATH
The right way to size this is "how many *human-touch events* per week, and how many hours." The automated layer scales with compute, not headcount. The constraint is the exception/integration queue + support/escalation queue + denied-case handling + new-account onboarding.

Revenue frame: ~$300K/founder by mo12, ~$500K+/founder by mo24 — ~$600K–$1M combined run-rate at mo12 building toward ~$1M–$2M+ by mo24. Subscription assume **$0.50–$1.50 PEPM** or per-EOI ~**$8–$20 per pending application** [ASSUMED; verify with 6 calls]. Success fee a slice of recovered annual premium: above-GI voluntary life premium recovered per approved case ~**$150–$600/yr**; at 10–25% of first-year that's **$20–$120 per approved case** [ASSUMED; the single most important number to validate].

To reach ~$1M combined ARR at an effective **$12 blended revenue per pending EOI application per cycle**, you need ~**83,000 pending EOI applications/year**. Sanity-check: if a 2,000-employee group generates a few hundred above-GI EOI cases per enrollment, you need ~**150–400 employer groups** served through **8–20 broker/carrier accounts** [ASSUMED]. Very reachable account count for a founder-led, channel-leveraged motion.

Human work, steady-state (a March week):
- **Integration-exception hygiene:** 2–4% of ingested cases throw an exception. In a normal March week ~150–300 exceptions at ~3–5 min = **8–25 hours/week** → ~**0.25–0.6 FTE** off-season.
- **Support/escalation:** ~0.5–1.5% of the active pending population contacts support per week. With 20,000–40,000 actively pending in March, that's **100–600 contacts/week** at ~6–10 min = **15–80 hours/week** → ~**0.5–2 FTE** off-season — and this scales with population, not revenue logos.
- **Denied/modified-case integrity:** 10–20% of resolved cases need a human to finalize the record → **100–300 cases/week** at ~4–8 min = **10–40 hours/week** → ~**0.3–1 FTE**.
- **Account onboarding:** each new connection is a real engineering+config project — bursty, ~**0.5–1 FTE** of founder/eng time continuously in year one.

Steady-state off-season total: ≈ **1.5–4.5 FTE of human work** — two founders + **2–4 hires** can cover. **The peak (Q4) is the real test:** multiply exception/support lines ~4–6x for Sept–Jan; off-season 0.5–2 support FTE becomes **3–10 FTE-equivalent** at peak. The sane answer is **seasonal/contract support + help-desk tooling + aggressive automation of the top 10 question types**, NOT permanent peak headcount. The *median* day is two-founders-plus-a-few; the model is operable, but the Q4 surge + population-scaling support line are where staffing discipline determines whether margins are software-grade or BPO-grade.

## SCORES (1–10)
1. Delivery clarity & repeatability: **8** — The unit of work (drive one pending EOI to resolution) is standardized and largely software-executed: ingest → route → nudge → track → confirm → log; same across carriers; only per-carrier intake quirks are bespoke.
2. Supply chain resilience: **6** — "Supply chain" is data feeds and carrier intake; inputs come from ben-admin systems and carriers via files/APIs (multiple, somewhat substitutable), but each connection is its own build and schema changes break ingestion; carrier underwriting uptime is a real recurring fragility.
3. Logistics tractability: **10** — Pure software/remote; zero physical goods, on-site work, routing, or travel; every delivery event is an email/API call/dashboard render — the cleanest possible logistics profile and a direct fit for "NOT field ops."
4. Customer-ops scalability: **6** — Buyer-facing side (carriers/brokers) is low-touch/self-serve via dashboards; but the *end-user* (employee) support tail scales with the pending population, not paying accounts, and warning emails manufacture inbound. Manageable with strong deflection; without it, drifts high-touch.
5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: **5** — Sits in the seam between ben-admin systems and carriers; *both* could own this themselves or change terms. Cross-carrier independence is the defensible angle, but the platform rides multiple third-party integrations and feeds it doesn't control — real, ongoing dependency.
6. Hiring feasibility: **8** — Needed hires are remote benefits-ops specialists, support/ops analysts, and integration engineers — deep national remote pools at reasonable cost, with seasonal/contract flex for Q4.
7. Throughput capacity at target: **8** — Shown math: steady-state human work ~1.5–4.5 FTE for ~$1M ARR, within two founders + 2–4 hires; the automated layer carries the volume; binding constraint is the Q4 surge, solvable with seasonal staff + deflection.
8. Quality control simplicity: **7** — Most QC is software-deterministic (routing, status, effective dates, audit trail); variance risk is in two human-sensitive spots: the *content/tone* of employee comms (a blunt "you're not covered" email can trigger HR backlash) and the *integrity of the denied-case disclosure record* (a liability artifact). Controllable with templates/checklists.
9. Geographic / seasonality risk: **5** — Geography a non-issue (national, remote, weather-neutral), but **seasonality is a genuine structural drag**: workload concentrates hard in Q4 + the January crunch; year-round new-hire/life-event EOIs soften but don't remove it.
10. Tooling maturity available: **7** — Much of the stack is buyable (help desk, messaging, workflow engine, dashboards, e-sign, BI), but the *core orchestration + multi-carrier/multi-ben-admin integration layer + auditable disclosure ledger* must be built — that's the actual product and IP.

**AVERAGE SCORE: 7.0 / 10**

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- **The repeated unit of work is software-executed, not human-executed.** Reminders, status, confirmations, and the disclosure trail are code; humans only touch the 2–4% exception tail and denied-case integrity — a lean, remote, NOT-field-ops profile.
- **Throughput scales with compute, not headcount.** ~$1M ARR within reach of two founders + 2–4 hires; you serve hundreds of employer groups through ~8–20 accounts.
- **Clean, trainable delivery spine.** The chase-to-completion workflow is identical across carriers, making the offering productizable and easy to staff against from deep, affordable, remote labor pools with seasonal flex.

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- **The employee-support tail scales with the pending population, not paying logos** — and the platform's own warning emails manufacture inbound; weak self-serve deflection drifts toward a call-center cost structure that craters the margin.
- **Q4 seasonality concentrates 4–6x the workload into one quarter**, forcing either painful permanent over-staffing or a disciplined seasonal-contractor + automation strategy that must be built before the first big open-enrollment season.
- **Integration fragility and carrier-uptime dependence** — schema changes and carrier-side slowness break ingestion or stall cases, and the broker blames the platform for failures it partly can't control; integration hygiene is a permanent recurring tax.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to quietly kill this is **the employee-support and chase load degrading from "software nudges" into "humans calling people about their life-insurance forms."** The entire economic premise is that orchestration software — not labor — drives EOI to completion; that is what separates this from the existing failure mode (no one chases) and from a low-margin BPO (humans chase). But the support load is structurally coupled to the *pending population*, which grows with every group you add, and the product's own "you are not yet covered" warnings deliberately provoke confused, anxious employees to reach out — so the more effective the nudge, the more inbound it can generate. If completion only meaningfully improves when a human personally walks employees through the medical questionnaire (because the digital form is intimidating, the health questions are sensitive, or older/less-digital workforces stall), then the founders are forced to staff humans per-case, the FTE count climbs with population rather than revenue, the Q4 surge becomes an annual staffing crisis, and the success-fee economics invert. The whole thesis hinges on one empirically testable question: **what share of the completion lift is achievable by automation alone versus human-assisted intake?** If it's mostly automation, this is a beautiful lean software business; if it's mostly humans, two people cannot do it at scale and the margin is BPO-grade.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What fraction of EOI completion lift comes from pure automation (reminders, plain-language digital intake) versus human-assisted hand-holding? Cheap test: one broker, one group, A/B automated-only nudges vs. nudges-plus-human-follow-up, measure completion-rate delta and minutes-of-human-time per completed case.
- What is the real per-group above-GI EOI volume, and how peaked is it across the calendar? Get one carrier's/enrollment firm's actual book: pending EOIs per 1,000 covered lives, month-by-month.
- For the integration layer, how many distinct ben-admin/carrier connections must exist before the platform is sellable, and how brittle are those feeds? Which 3–5 ben-admin platforms and carrier intake methods cover 80% of target groups, what's the build time per connection, and how often do schemas change?
- Who actually owns the disclosure-integrity workflow on a denied/modified case, and what's the liability posture if the record is wrong?
- Can the Q4 surge be met with seasonal/contract support + automated deflection rather than permanent headcount — and is that labor reliably available and trainable on a 6–8 week ramp?

## RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally this is a strong, founder-fit shape — remote-first, software-leveraged, no field ops, with a clean repeatable delivery spine and capacity math that genuinely fits two founders plus a handful of remote, seasonally-flexed hires. I am not asking for a different business; I am asking for two specific operational simplifications proven *before* committing. First, **prove the automation-vs-human ratio** (the biggest-risk question) and design the product so the human touch is the exception, not the path — pre-build self-serve broker/employer dashboards and templated employee FAQ/chat deflection so the support load is decoupled from population growth and the Q4 surge is absorbed by software plus seasonal contractors, never by permanent peak staffing. Second, **scope the integration layer deliberately** — pick the 3–5 ben-admin platforms and carrier intake methods that cover the bulk of target groups, treat schema-change hygiene as a permanent budgeted task with monitoring/alerts, and don't sell ahead of the connections you can keep healthy. Nail those two and the steady-state operation is clearly two-founders-plus-a-lean-team runnable; leave them unresolved and the employee-support tail plus Q4 seasonality can quietly convert a software business into a staffing-heavy one. Refine on operating model, then go.
