# Clean-Room Concept One-Pager
**Internal label:** Evidence-of-Insurability (EOI) Orchestration for Voluntary Benefits

> Neutral, standalone one-pager handed to each council reviewer. No scores, no lineage, no tier bar, no target.

## 1) CUSTOMER
Three linked parties in the voluntary/supplemental group benefits chain, with two economic buyers. The economic buyers are (a) the group/voluntary divisions of life and disability insurance carriers, and (b) the benefits brokers/agencies and enrollment firms that run employer group enrollments. The end users are the employees who elect coverage and the employer's HR/benefits team. Primary focus: voluntary and supplemental term life and disability insurance offered through employers, where coverage is elected at open enrollment. Target employers are mid-market and up (a few hundred to several thousand employees) where above-guaranteed-issue elections are common.

## 2) THE MUST-HAVE / FORCING FUNCTION
When an employee elects voluntary life or disability coverage above the plan's "guaranteed issue" (GI) limit — the amount a carrier will issue without medical questions — the excess coverage does not take effect until the employee completes Evidence of Insurability (EOI): a medical-history attestation the carrier underwrites and approves. In practice a large share of EOI applications are never completed or approved: the employee elects the coverage at enrollment, the EOI form sits in a separate, clunky, per-carrier portal, no one chases it to completion, and the above-GI coverage is never put in force. Two concrete consequences follow, every enrollment cycle: (1) the carrier AND the broker permanently lose the recurring in-force premium they would have earned on the approved excess coverage — premium that was already elected and is simply abandoned in the workflow gap; and (2) the employee believes they are covered for an amount they are not, creating a coverage-gap and liability exposure that surfaces at claim time. The demand is contracted-for and already elected; it leaks because the hand-off between the enrollment system and the carrier's underwriting is broken and owned by no single party. This is a critical input to a revenue process (the premium only materializes if EOI completes) and a risk/liability-integrity issue (the coverage record must be accurate).

## 3) PRODUCT
A software platform that orchestrates the EOI lifecycle end-to-end and across carriers. At the point of enrollment it ingests the elections that trigger EOI (from the enrollment/ben-admin system or a file feed), routes each affected employee into a guided, plain-language digital EOI questionnaire (or hands off cleanly into the carrier's underwriting intake), then tracks every pending EOI to resolution: automated reminders and nudges to employees, status visibility for the broker/employer/carrier, escalation of stalled cases, and confirmation when coverage is approved, denied, or modified. Throughout, it maintains a complete disclosure and attestation trail — what was elected, what is pending, effective dates, what the employee was told about when coverage begins — so the record is auditable. It is built to sit between many carriers and many enrollment systems, owning the "chase-to-completion" workflow that neither side currently owns.

## 4) REVENUE MODEL
Hybrid. A recurring subscription — priced per-employee-per-month (PEPM) for covered groups, or per-EOI-application — for the orchestration platform, paid by the carrier group division and/or the broker/enrollment firm. Plus a per-approved-application success fee tied to the in-force premium that is secured (and would otherwise have leaked). The success fee aligns the vendor's pay with the premium it helps capture; the subscription is the durable recurring layer. Pricing scales with the volume of above-GI elections an enrollment generates.

## 5) POSITIONING
The independent, cross-carrier layer that turns elected-but-unapproved coverage into approved, in-force coverage — closing the gap between enrollment and underwriting that no single carrier portal or enrollment system owns. It captures voluntary-benefit premium that otherwise leaks every cycle, gets employees the coverage they actually elected, and leaves a clean, auditable disclosure record.
