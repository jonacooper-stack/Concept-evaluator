COO REVIEW — Defense-Sub Security-Questionnaire Answer Engine (self-serve SaaS for small CUI subcontractors)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is operationally one of the cleanest shapes the founders can pick: a self-serve, credit-card SaaS where the unit of work delivered to each customer is software running, not a human delivering a service. There is no truck, no install, no on-site visit, no per-customer human onboarding required by design — the customer seeds their own knowledge base, drops in their own forms, and the engine does the auto-fill. Two founders plus a tiny team can absolutely run this at hundreds-to-thousands of customers IF the support model stays self-serve. The entire operational risk does not live in delivery or logistics — it lives in three specific seams: (a) the "interview-to-fill-the-gaps" step at a customer that has no CTO, which is the place a self-serve product silently becomes a high-touch compliance-advisory desk; (b) keeping the CMMC/NIST 800-171 content current when the founders are not credentialed and don't plan to hire experts; and (c) rebuilding an engine they've built before but no longer have the code for. Delivery is a 9; the seams are where the real number lives.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday, March of year two, ~month-15. [ASSUMED] ~450 paying subscribers at ~$350/mo (verify; plausible band 300–600). Two founders in two cities; nobody drives anywhere. Overnight the product worked autonomously: 40 free gap-analyses ran for new visitors who uploaded an SSP and got an estimated SPRS score; 22 inbound prime questionnaires were dropped in by existing customers and auto-filled to ~80%; the learning loop re-ingested 18 completed forms. None touched a founder. The founders' Tuesday is two jobs: (1) marketing/content/channel (their edge), and (2) the support queue, which decides whether this is lean. Today ~25–40 tickets [ASSUMED]. ~15 are pure SaaS (upload failed, PDF parsed wrong, add a user, billing) — fast, scriptable. ~10 are the dangerous kind: "The auto-fill answered control 3.13.11 'encryption of CUI at rest' but I don't know if our shop encrypts the laptops — what do I put?" The product flags "route to your IT provider (MSP)," but the owner doesn't understand it and replies "Can you just tell me the right answer?" THIS is the seam: if the founders answer, support time jumps 4 min → 25+ min AND they cross from "software vendor" toward "we advised on a federal attestation"; if they don't, the customer is stuck (their MSP may not know either) → churn/bad-review risk. ~3–5 are content-currency tickets (a new prime format mapped half to "unknown"; DoD updated guidance and a template is stale) — these land on a founder, not a junior rep, because they require judgment about the correct current answer. Realistic Tuesday: software does the high-volume work invisibly; one founder spends ~2–4 hrs triaging support, of which the painful 30–40% is gap-answer and content-currency tickets that can't be deflected; the other runs marketing. Where it breaks is not capacity to serve 450 customers — it's the slow capture of founder time into being the customers' outsourced compliance brain, plus the standing content-currency obligation.

CAPACITY MATH (show the work)
The binding constraint is SUPPORT MINUTES per customer per month, specifically the irreducible human-judgment fraction — not delivery labor (auto-fill scales with infra).
Revenue/ladder: $300K/founder mo12 = $600K ≈ $50K MRR ÷ $350 ≈ ~143 customers; $500K/founder mo24 = $1.0M ≈ ~240 customers; upside well beyond. Gross margin [ASSUMED] 80–90% (a 110-control auto-fill is plausibly cents-to-low-dollars of inference). The income ladder clears at modest customer counts — a genuine strength.
Support-minutes model: each customer generates [ASSUMED] ~1.5 touches/month (band 0.5–3; spikes around renewals and the Nov-2026 phase-in). Split ~60% easy @ 4 min; ~40% hard @ 20 min (the 20 min is the load-bearing assumption — the cost of NO credentialed person and the no-CTO customer).
- 240 customers: easy 216×4=864 min; hard 144×20=2,880 min → ~3,744 min/mo ≈ ~14–15 hrs/week. One founder part-time holds this. PASSES.
- 450 customers: easy 405×4=1,620; hard 270×20=5,400 → ~7,020 min/mo ≈ ~27 hrs/week. Edge of one person; trigger for the first hire.
First hire (named/costed): a Customer Success / Support Specialist with light security literacy running a documented playbook, deflecting 80% to KB and ESCALATING (not answering) compliance-judgment tickets. [ASSUMED] $55–80K/yr fully loaded, remote, abundant labor pool (support + runbook, not a scarce credentialed role). At ~$1M ARR that's ~5–8% of revenue — trivially affordable. Founders + one CS hire comfortably serve into the many-hundreds.
What breaks the math: the 20-min hard-ticket assumption and its share. If, because customers have no CTO, a much larger fraction (say 60%) become 30-min "just answer it for me" exchanges the founders feel obligated to handle, load roughly doubles AND converts from "support" into "unlicensed compliance advice at volume." That is the operational + legal seam, not a throughput cliff. Cheap verify: instrument the gap-interview in beta; measure % the owner answers alone, % the MSP resolves, % that bounce back to support. That single metric decides whether this is an 8-tier or 6-tier ops business.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.6 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- True software delivery with no per-customer human work: the core unit is the engine running (auto-fill 80%, learn, re-feed). No trucks/installs/onboarding labor — logistics a 10, delivery a 9. The lean, remote, capital-light shape the founders want.
- The income ladder clears at low customer counts: ~143 at $350/mo hits $300K/founder (mo12), ~240 hits $500K/founder (mo24). High SaaS margin means founder income is gated by acquisition (their edge) and support discipline, not delivery labor.
- Cheap, abundant first hire: a remote CS/support specialist on a runbook (~$55–80K), not a scarce credentialed role, not needed until the mid-hundreds. Staffing is a non-problem in range.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- The gap-interview seam at a no-CTO customer turns support into unlicensed compliance advice: when the owner can't answer and the MSP doesn't resolve, the ticket bounces back as "just tell me." Inflates support cost (4→20–30 min) AND drags founders across a service/advice line. The single biggest determinant of 8 vs. 6.
- Content-currency obligation the founders can't fully own: CMMC guidance, NIST 800-171 revisions, and primes' changing formats require ongoing expert judgment; the founders aren't credentialed and don't plan to hire. A DoD/DIB advisor "informing" content is thinner than a standing maintenance function; stale content silently degrades auto-fill accuracy.
- Rebuild execution risk on the differentiated core: proven by the prior build, but the prior code is unavailable, so the learning-loop/auto-fill/parsing core is from-scratch and must handle messy real-world prime forms well enough to hit the promised ~80%.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The gap-interview-to-support conversion at a customer with no in-house technical staff. The whole self-serve, two-founder, lean-ops thesis rests on the engine auto-filling ~80% and the remaining ~20% being answerable by the owner or cleanly routed to the MSP. But the target customer is, by description, a cost-sensitive, non-technical owner with no CTO and only an outside MSP who may or may not know a specific NIST 800-171 control answer. When that owner hits a gap question they can't answer and their MSP can't either, the natural move is to email support and ask the vendor to just tell them what to put. If the founders answer, support time per hard ticket triples and — more dangerous — they slide from "we provide a drafting tool, you decide and attest" into effectively advising small businesses on a federal attestation at volume, which is both a liability posture and an unscalable workload no $55–80K support hire can absorb. If they refuse and hold the line, a slice of customers are stranded on the exact 20% the product couldn't do for them → churn and "it didn't actually answer my forms" reviews, undercutting the wedge. The business doesn't break from too many customers; it breaks slowly as founder time gets captured into being the customers' outsourced compliance department. The deciding metric — cheaply measurable in beta — is the real distribution of gap tickets: % owner resolves alone, % MSP resolves, % that bounce back demanding a substantive answer.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- In beta, what is the measured distribution of gap-interview questions: % answered by the owner unaided, % resolved by routing to the MSP, % that bounce back to support asking you to supply the substantive answer? What's your hard rule and product mechanism for NOT answering compliance-judgment questions while keeping the customer unstuck (curated MSP-partner network, in-product sourced-rationale answer library, a pre-written "ask your MSP" template)?
- Who owns content currency as a standing function, at what cost and cadence? When DoD updates guidance or a prime ships a new format, what's the process and SLA to update the answer library and parsing maps — advisor (contracted hours? retained? goodwill?), founder, or future hire — and how do you detect/roll back a content error silently auto-filling wrong answers?
- What is the demonstrated auto-fill accuracy on real, heterogeneous prime questionnaires (not your own seeded forms), and how long is the rebuild to reach it? How many distinct formats must the parser handle, what % auto-fill and answer-accuracy on a held-out set, and the QA plan for the long tail of weird PDF/Excel/portal formats?

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with one operational guardrail to design in before scale)
Operationally this is the lean, remote-first, capital-light, non-field-ops shape the founders should want — delivery is software, logistics nil, the income ladder clears at low customer counts, and the only in-range hire is a cheap, abundant support role. On absolute ops merit it earns 7.6. The reason it isn't 8.5 is not throughput or logistics (9s and 10s) — it's the two judgment-dependent seams (the no-CTO gap-answer step and content currency) that a self-serve SaaS can't fully script away and the founders have chosen to staff thinly. I'm not asking them to abandon self-serve; I'm asking them to engineer the boundary so the product, not the founders, absorbs the gap questions: a hard "we draft, you decide, your MSP resolves" support policy backed by an in-product sourced-answer library and a curated MSP-referral channel, plus a named, costed, recurring content-maintenance function (even fractional). Do that and the gap-interview seam stops being a slow leak of founder time and the ops score is genuinely 8+. Prove the beta gap-ticket distribution and the auto-fill accuracy on real prime forms, and this is one of the most operationally tractable concepts on the board.
