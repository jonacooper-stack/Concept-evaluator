CFO REVIEW — Defense Sub Security-Questionnaire Answer Engine (Self-Serve SaaS)

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The economics here are unusually clean for a bootstrap: a published $200–600/mo subscription against near-zero marginal cost software, sold self-serve so there is no sales-team drag on the P&L. The model's entire viability rests on one number — how many small subs you can convert with PLG alone — and the honest answer is that the path to ~$300K/founder by mo12 is reachable but tight, while ~$500K+/founder by mo24 is very plausible IF retention holds and a referral channel (APEX/MEP) does real work. The "answers your forms forever" framing is genuinely a stronger LTV and lower-churn story than a one-time score/document tool, and that is the single most important financial fact in the concept's favor — recurring, embedded, data-accreting subscriptions are exactly the asset class that finances and sells well later.

THE MATH
Target ladder (per 01-objectives): ~$300K/founder mo12 → ~$600K combined ARR run-rate; ~$500K+/founder mo24 → ~$1.0M+ combined ARR run-rate.
ARPU. Tiered $200–600/mo. A 35-person machine shop sits mid-range. Blended ARPU [ASSUMED] $300–400/mo = $3,600–$4,800/yr. Model the mid-point at $350/mo = $4,200/yr ARR per customer. (Cheap test: publish three tiers and watch the actual tier mix on the first 50 signups.)
Customers needed.
- Mo12 ($600K ARR ÷ $4,200) = ~143 paying customers.
- Mo24 ($1.0M ARR ÷ $4,200) = ~238 paying customers.
At conservative $300/mo ($3,600/yr): mo12 ~167; mo24 ~278. At optimistic $450/mo ($5,400/yr): mo12 ~111; mo24 ~185.
So the real target is roughly 110–170 paying logos by mo12 and 185–280 by mo24, net of churn.
Gross margin. SaaS holding documents/answers, not CUI. Hosting + LLM inference + storage per customer [ASSUMED] $15–40/mo against $350 ARPU. Add payment processing ~3%. Gross margin ~88–94%. (Comparable: Vanta, Drata, Loopio/Conveyor run high-80s%+ software margins; LLM inference is a few dollars per questionnaire.)
CAC and channel feasibility (the load-bearing question). No sales team. Acquisition = content + free gap-analysis hook + APEX/MEP referrals. Self-serve niche B2B converts a free tool at [ASSUMED] 3–8% to paid. To net ~150 paying logos by mo12 you need ~1,900–5,000 gap analyses run in year one (~160–420/month). Achievable for two strong marketers with a free hook + warm institutional referrers, but NOT trivial — the make-or-break variable. (Cheap test: stand up the free gap-analysis + SPRS-estimate landing page now, pre-product, and measure run-rate + email capture before building the answer engine.)
CAC math. Spend [ASSUMED] $4K–10K/mo on content/SEO/paid year one, net ~12 paying logos/month at steady state → blended CAC ≈ $330–830. Against $4,200 annual ARR at ~90% margin, LTV at [ASSUMED] 3-year life = ~$11,300 gross profit. LTV/CAC ≈ 14–34x on paid alone. Even halving life and tripling CAC, LTV/CAC stays > 5. Payback at CAC ~$500 / (~$315 monthly gross profit) ≈ 1.6 months. Strong unit economics IF funnel volume materializes.
Cash flow. Credit-card monthly billing. Monthly (not annual-upfront) means growth is not pre-funded by the customer — but with <2-month CAC payback and ~90% margin, the cash drag is small and self-funding arrives fast. Annual prepay (offer 2 months free) would flip this cash-positive-on-acquisition; worth doing.
Verdict on the math: it pencils. The binding constraint is not margin, capital, or ARPU — it is whether PLG + referral can produce ~110–170 retained logos in 12 months without a salesperson. Plausible but unproven, and where I'd put every validation dollar.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Best-in-class margin and cash dynamics for a bootstrap: ~90% gross margin, ~1.6-month CAC payback, no inventory/AR/deposits, launchable in the low four/five figures — growth self-funds within a quarter.
- The recurring/embedded structure is materially better than the one-time tool it replaces: "answers your forms forever" + an accreting per-customer answer database creates real switching cost, supports higher willingness-to-pay, and depresses churn — the LTV story that makes this financeable and acquirable.
- Diversified revenue across hundreds of small logos: no customer-concentration risk, and a low price point ($200–600/mo) that is an easy credit-card "yes" against consultant retainers at multiples of this.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Funnel velocity without a sales team: the whole ladder depends on PLG + referral producing ~110–170 retained logos by mo12. If free→paid is 2% instead of 5%, mo12 slips 6–12 months.
- Churn / engagement risk in a cost-sensitive, non-technical segment: an owner who runs two questionnaires a year may cancel between forms ("I'll resubscribe when the next one comes"). Episodic need + monthly billing is a churn hazard that compresses LTV.
- Regulatory-timing and ARPU-ceiling risk: demand intensity is partly tied to the CMMC Nov-2026 phase-in; the deliberately low ARPU caps revenue-per-logo, so the model needs volume it cannot buy with a sales motion the founders won't staff.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The model is financially sound on every per-unit axis — margin, payback, capital, concentration — so the one thing that can kill it is the denominator: customer acquisition volume at this price without a sales team. At $350 ARPU you need ~110–170 retained logos for ~$300K/founder by mo12, all arriving through content, a free gap-analysis hook, and institutional referrals. PLG works beautifully when a free tool's "aha" is instant; the danger is a leaky middle — a non-technical owner runs the free SPRS estimate, sees a scary number, then stalls because answering a prime's questionnaire still feels like work he'd rather hand to his MSP. If free→paid lands at 2–3% instead of 5–8%, the unit economics stay gorgeous but the business grows too slowly to clear the mo12 rung, and the founders face the decision they tried to avoid: hire a salesperson (breaking the lean thesis) or accept a longer ramp — compounded by between-forms churn. The good news: this is the cheapest possible risk to test pre-build — a landing page with the free gap analysis measures both top-of-funnel volume and willingness-to-pay before a line of the answer engine is written.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the real free-gap-analysis → paid conversion rate, and at what monthly top-of-funnel volume? Stand up the free SPRS-estimate landing page now and measure run-rate + email capture pre-product.
- What does the APEX/MEP referral channel actually deliver in signups per month, and is it contractible/repeatable or a one-time goodwill burst?
- What is expected monthly logo churn, and does "continuously ready" genuinely convert an episodic need into a continuous subscription — will an owner keep paying between questionnaires?
- What is the true tier mix and blended ARPU? Publish $200/$400/$600 and watch where the first 50 signups land — the customer-count math swings ~50% across the ARPU range.
- Will the absence of an in-house CTO at the customer (only an MSP) raise the gap-fill burden enough to hurt activation or drive support cost — and does that change price or margin?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
The financial structure is genuinely strong: ~90% gross margin, sub-2-month CAC payback, near-zero startup and working capital, diversified recurring revenue, improving scale economics, and a clean acquirable asset — with the meaningful upgrade over a one-time tool being real switching cost from an accreting per-customer answer database. The mo24 income rung (~$1.0M combined ARR) is very plausible and the mo12 rung is reachable. The one honest caveat is that the entire thesis is gated on PLG funnel velocity at a deliberately low price with no sales team, and on retention in a possibly-episodic segment — so this is a GO explicitly conditional on the cheapest experiment in the deck: run the free gap-analysis landing page and prove top-of-funnel volume, free→paid conversion, and willingness-to-pay BEFORE building the answer engine. If that test returns 5%+ conversion at a few hundred analyses/month, this is an A-tier financial profile; if 2% at low volume, the fix is a channel/pricing rethink (annual prepay, a higher "done-with-you" tier, a contracted referral motion), not abandonment.
