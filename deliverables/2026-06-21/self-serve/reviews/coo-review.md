COO REVIEW — CMMC/NIST 800-171 Self-Serve Compliance SaaS for Small Defense Subcontractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is, on paper, one of the cleaner operational profiles a two-founder bootstrapped team could ask for: a true self-serve SaaS with no per-customer human delivery, no field work, no consultant sign-off gate, and a deliberate design choice to keep CUI and operational security data OUT of the system — which strips away the single heaviest ops burden (FedRAMP-grade infrastructure, audit logging of customer secrets, breach liability over classified-adjacent data). The unit of work that scales is software plus content, not labor, so throughput is not gated by headcount the way a managed service would be. Where this concept actually breaks operationally is NOT delivery or capacity — it breaks at two specific seams: (1) the customer-support load from a non-technical, anxious, compliance-obligated buyer who is self-attesting to the federal government and will treat the tool as a lifeline, and (2) content-currency upkeep — keeping the 110-control mapping, SSP/POA&M templates, and SPRS scoring logic correct as DoD/CMMC guidance shifts, when neither founder is CMMC-credentialed and they don't plan to hire experts. Those two seams are real and must be engineered around, but neither is a field-ops problem and neither is unbridgeable; this is a manageable-but-not-trivial ops profile, not a hand-wave.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday in March, ~12-month mark. Assume ~250 paying subscribers at a blended ~$350/mo (verify; range 150–400). No installs, no site visits, no scheduled calls — the product transacts itself. Overnight: 8–15 free gap analyses ran (someone uploaded their IT policies at 11pm because a prime emailed a flow-down questionnaire), 1–2 converted to paid on a credit card with nobody talking to them. The first real work is the support queue. A non-technical owner emails: "The tool says my SPRS score is 27 but my MSP told me I'm at 88 — which is right and what do I tell my prime?" That is not a bug ticket; it's a 20–40 minute, judgment-heavy, anxiety-laden compliance-interpretation question, and the customer is about to post a number to a federal system based on the answer. [ASSUMED] 8–20 such tickets/day; a meaningful fraction are "help me understand what the regulation means for ME," not "how do I click." One founder works the queue; the dangerous tickets are where the customer conflates "estimated score the tool generated" with "official assessment," and the answer must be worded so the company never appears to be acting as an accredited assessor. Meanwhile the other founder is on content: DoD published a clarification on an 800-171 objective, and the control-mapping text, remediation guidance, and possibly the deterministic SPRS weight need review across every customer's auto-generated SSP/POA&M — and since the founders aren't credentialed, they route the ambiguous interpretation to the DoD/DIB advisor and wait. By afternoon: marketing/SEO (the growth engine), a few churn saves (a customer who posted their score and wonders why they still pay), and Stripe billing runs itself. Nothing physically breaks. The pressure points are: support volume per anxious non-technical user, liability-careful phrasing, and the latency/quality of content updates dependent on an advisor the founders don't employ.

CAPACITY MATH
No delivery event per customer — software delivers itself; size by support-hours and content-hours.
Scale to the ladder (blended ~$350/mo = ~$4,200/yr): ~$300K/founder mo12 = ~$600K ≈ $50K MRR ÷ $350 ≈ ~143 accounts; ~$500K/founder mo24 = ~$1.0M ≈ ~238 accounts; $2M-upside = ~950 accounts (fewer at higher ASP). So the whole ladder is reachable at 150–250 self-serve accounts — a small absolute count, operationally favorable.
Support load (the real bottleneck): [ASSUMED] 0.4–0.8 tickets/account/month (higher than a dev-tool SaaS — non-technical, federal stakes); take 0.6. At 250 accounts × 0.6 = ~150 tickets/mo ≈ ~7–8/business day. [ASSUMED] ~20 min/ticket blended → ~50 hrs/mo ≈ ~12 hrs/week — comfortably inside one founder part-time. Scale to 950 accounts → ~190 hrs/mo ≈ ~44 hrs/week = a full-time support person; the FIRST hire (a compliance-aware Tier-1 rep, ~$55–75K loaded) lands at ~500–700 accounts, AFTER the income ladder is hit.
Content currency: built once, maintained on a regulatory cadence. [ASSUMED] ~4–10 material guidance events/yr × ~4–20 hrs each ≈ ~80 hrs/yr ≈ ~1.5 hrs/week steady-state, spiking around Nov 10, 2026. The constraint isn't hours — it's that the founders aren't credentialed to make the interpretive call, so the critical-path dependency is the DoD/DIB advisor's availability/SLA. If the advisor is informal/uncompensated, content currency is a single point of failure.
Net: delivery throughput is effectively infinite (software), the ladder is reachable at a low two-founder-supportable count, and staffing is only forced (a) for support beyond ~500–700 accounts and (b) a formalized expert-content relationship — both AFTER the ladder is hit.

AVERAGE SCORE: 8.0 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- No delivery labor and no field ops — throughput is software-bound; the entire income ladder is reachable at only ~150–250 self-serve accounts with ~7–14 hrs/week combined load on two founders. The lean, remote-first, non-field-ops profile the founders require.
- The data-minimization design removes the heaviest ops/security burden: holding only compliance documentation (no CUI/configs/logs/scans) sidesteps FedRAMP/IL-level hosting, custody of classified-adjacent secrets, and the catastrophic-breach tail. That single decision makes a two-founder, no-credentialed-staff operation realistic.
- Self-serve acquisition + commoditized, swappable vendor stack; growth isn't gated by founder selling time; no bet-the-business platform dependency.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Support load is interpretive, not mechanical, and brushes against giving compliance advice: a non-technical owner self-attesting to the government asks "what do I post?" — 20–40 min, liability-careful answers. Volume is tractable; the judgment-and-phrasing burden per ticket is the hidden cost.
- Content currency depends on expertise the founders don't hold and don't plan to hire: keeping the 110-control mapping, scoring, and templates correct as guidance shifts routes the interpretive call to a single advisor with no formalized obligation. Stale/wrong content silently degrades every customer's output.
- Correctness-under-regulation QC hazard with federal-attestation consequences: output is posted to a government system; a subtle error risks a customer mis-attesting — a far worse failure mode the non-credentialed founders aren't positioned to catch unaided.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single operational issue most likely to hurt this is the content-currency dependency on uncredentialed founders plus one informal advisor, colliding with a moving regulatory target. Not a "can two people do the work" problem — the hours are small (~1.5/week steady-state). It is a "can two non-credentialed people reliably and correctly keep a deterministic SPRS engine and SSP/POA&M generator in sync with shifting DoD guidance, when the cost of being wrong is a customer posting a bad number to a federal system and self-attesting to it" problem. The founders have genuine compliance-operator instincts (SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR) which makes framework-handling learnable, but NIST 800-171/CMMC interpretation has real edges where practitioners disagree, and the model leans the entire interpretive backbone on a single advisor who, as described, "informs the methodology" rather than being contractually on the hook for currency. If that advisor goes quiet or the founders ship a content update faster than the advisor can vet it, the product can drift out of correctness invisibly — every account keeps generating subtly-wrong documents, blast radius the entire customer base at once. Mitigations are cheap (a paid retainer/SLA with the advisor or a named CCP/RP on contract for content sign-off only; a versioned, regression-tested content pipeline; conspicuous estimate/not-an-accredited-assessment labeling; a documented change-log), but they MUST be built in — this is the one seam where "self-serve software" stops protecting them and the lack of an in-house credential becomes load-bearing.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the actual support-contact rate and ticket mix for this buyer? Instrument the free gap-analysis hook: of the first 200 free + 50 paid users, what fraction email a question, what % are "how do I click" vs "interpret the regulation for me," and what's real average handle time?
- Is the DoD/DIB advisor contractually committed to content currency with a defined SLA, and what's the backup if they're unavailable? "An advisor informs the methodology" is not an operational answer — convert the currency dependency from a favor into a process (paid retainer, named CCP/RP sign-off, turnaround commitment).
- What is the exact liability/QC firewall on support answers and generated output — scripted boundaries/disclaimers so support never crosses into accredited-assessor territory, and regression-testing of the deterministic scorer against reference cases on every content change?
- What is the content-update runbook around Nov 10, 2026 and each rule revision — who watches the Federal Register/DoD/Cyber AB feeds, who makes the interpretive call, who regression-tests, how fast does a corrected template propagate, and what's the support-surge plan?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Operationally this is among the most tractable shapes the founders could pick: no field ops, no per-customer delivery labor, self-serve acquisition, a commoditized/swappable vendor stack, and a data-minimization design that removes the FedRAMP-grade hosting and breach-liability burden that would otherwise sink a two-person DIB tool. Capacity math shows the entire founder-income ladder is reachable at ~150–250 self-serve accounts with combined support+content load comfortably inside two founders' part-time capacity, and the first forced hire (a compliance-aware support rep, not a scarce credentialed expert) doesn't arrive until well past the income targets. GO, not REFINE, because the two genuine seams — interpretive support load and content currency under shifting regulation by non-credentialed founders — are real but bridgeable with cheap, known mechanisms. Don't launch without: (1) convert the advisor from informal input into a paid, SLA-bound content-currency relationship (or contract a named CCP/RP for sign-off); (2) build a versioned, regression-tested content pipeline + guidance-change runbook before Nov 10, 2026; (3) instrument the free gap-analysis hook to measure real support-contact rate and ticket mix. Do those, and two people can absolutely run this.
