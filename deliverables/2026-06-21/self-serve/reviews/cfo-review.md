CFO REVIEW — CMMC/NIST 800-171 Self-Serve Compliance SaaS for Small Defense Subcontractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This is one of the cleanest financial shapes I see at concept stage: pure software, ~$200–600/mo recurring, no per-customer human delivery, no credentialed payroll, credit-card self-serve, against a federal forcing function with a real deadline (CMMC Level 2 phasing into new CUI contracts Nov 10, 2026). The gross margin is genuinely SaaS-grade (85–90%+) and the customer is forced to buy by their primes, which is the rare combination of high margin AND non-discretionary demand. The math pencils — but the entire question rides on ONE number: can two founders with no sales team acquire ~700–1,400 paying small machine shops purely via product-led/marketing-led growth, in a buyer segment (non-technical, low-digital manufacturers) that is notoriously hard to reach online and that has a strong cultural pull toward "just hire a consultant to do it for me." The income ladder is reachable on paper; whether self-serve PLG clears the volume is the live risk, and it is a marketing/distribution risk, not a unit-economics risk.

THE MATH
Target per the ladder: ~$300K/founder by mo12 → ~$600K total business income mo12; ~$500K+/founder by mo24 → ~$1.0M+ total mo24. Treat "founder income" as roughly the cash the two-person business throws off (revenue minus lean opex of a no-headcount software business).
ARPU [ASSUMED]: blended ~$350/mo = $4,200/yr/customer (range $2,400–$7,200/yr; midpoint conservative because the smallest 15–35-person shops dominate and skew to the $200–300 tiers). Verify with a pricing-page A/B test and 20 buyer calls.
Opex of a two-founder, no-headcount SaaS [ASSUMED]: $120K–$220K/yr all-in (hosting, gap-analysis/doc-gen engine, content/SEO, paid acquisition, tooling, contractor design, DoD/DIB advisor stipend, legal/insurance). ~$170K/yr midpoint. At 85–90% gross margin, COGS is small; the real "cost" is acquisition + content.

MONTH 12 — need ~$600K founder take: Revenue ≈ $600K + ~$170K opex ≈ ~$770K ARR. At $4,200 ARPU → ~183 paying customers. Range: low ARPU $2,400 → ~320; high $7,200 → ~107. So mo12 needs ~110–320 paying subs, midpoint ~185.
MONTH 24 — need ~$1.05M founder take: lean opex grows to ~$300K. Revenue ≈ $1.35M ARR. At $4,200 → ~320 customers. Range ~190–560, midpoint ~320.
Obtainable market: DoD CMMC rulemaking scoped 100K+ entities across levels; the small-sub CUI slice is in the tens of thousands. Conservatively 40,000 small CUI subs → ~320 customers at mo24 is ~0.8% penetration. Constraint is CHANNEL, not market size.
Unit economics [ASSUMED]: CAC via PLG/content/SEO + some paid + channel (APEX/MEP) blended $400–$1,200; the free gap analysis is a strong high-intent hook pulling CAC low. ARPU $4,200, ~88% margin → gross profit ~$3,700/yr. Payback ~2.6–3.9 months. LTV at 12–20% churn, life ~5–8 yrs → ~$18K–$30K → LTV/CAC ~10–37x. Excellent IF CAC and churn hold.
Cash flow: credit-card monthly billing → cash at point of sale, near-zero COGS, growth self-funds except marketing spend. Annual-prepay would make it strongly self-funding.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Rare margin + forced-demand combination: 85–90%+ SaaS margin (no human delivery/credentialed payroll) on a non-discretionary, federally-forced, already-budgeted recurring buy.
- Pristine cash mechanics and capital efficiency: credit-card-at-signup, ~zero per-customer COGS, no inventory/AR/deposits, low five-figure launch; growth self-funds, the only cash sink (marketing) is throttleable.
- Excellent unit economics on a low penetration requirement: payback ~2.6–3.9 months, LTV/CAC plausibly 10–37x, needing only ~0.8% of a conservative 40,000-firm pool by mo24.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Acquisition velocity to a hard-to-reach buyer: pure PLG/self-serve to non-technical, low-digital small manufacturers, no sales team, may not generate ~185 paying subs in 12 months; if CAC runs 2–3x assumed or free-to-paid conversion is low, the mo12 rung slips.
- "Buy vs. self-serve" cultural drag: the segment's instinct is to hire a consultant; a self-serve tool asks the non-technical owner to do the work, suppressing conversion and lifting post-score churn ("we passed — cancel"), compressing LTV.
- Single-framework, single-timeline dependency: revenue entirely tied to CMMC/SPRS; a DoD delay or softened enforcement alters urgency/budget timing, and the product (by design CMMC-only) has no second leg.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single financial issue most likely to kill this is acquisition economics for an intrinsically hard-to-reach, self-serve-resistant buyer — whether two founders without a sales team can convert non-technical machine-shop owners at the assumed ~$800 CAC and a high enough free-gap-analysis-to-paid rate to reach ~185 paying subs by mo12. Everything downstream (margin, payback, LTV/CAC) is multiplied by a conversion-and-volume number that is currently [ASSUMED]. Small defense manufacturers search the least, distrust "another subscription," and reflexively reach for "just pay someone to handle it." If the real motion requires founder-led or partner-led human selling, then either the model needs a sales/onboarding layer (adding cost, breaking clean PLG economics) or growth stalls at a few hundred customers and the mo24 rung becomes heroic. This is a distribution risk, not an economics risk — and the first, cheapest thing to test.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the realistic free-gap-analysis → paid conversion rate and blended CAC for THIS buyer? (Stand up the free gap analysis as a landing page, drive 1,000 visits via SEO + targeted paid + one APEX/MEP partner email, measure cost-per-qualified-lead and lead-to-paid intent before building the full product.)
- What blended ARPU does the tiering actually produce once you see real scope mix (% in $200–300 vs $400–600)? The mo12 count swings from ~110 to ~320 on this.
- What is the true retention curve after a customer reaches their SPRS score and later passes CMMC L2 — do they stay for monitoring/quarterly re-scoring/annual affirmation, or churn at "mission accomplished"?
- Can channel partners (APEX, MEP, primes' supplier-development) be turned into a repeatable low-CAC referral pipe, and on what terms? Get one signed pilot before committing to no-sales-team.
- What happens to demand/budget if the Nov 10, 2026 phase-in slips — is there a present-tense SPRS/flow-down revenue stream that stands on its own?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
The financial structure is genuinely strong and rare: SaaS-grade margin (85–90%+) on a federally-forced, recurring, budgeted buy, with pristine cash mechanics, near-zero marginal cost, low five-figure launch, and an income ladder requiring only ~0.8% penetration of a conservative 40,000-firm pool by mo24. Unit economics (payback ~3 months, LTV/CAC 10x+) clear the bar with room. GO rather than REFINE because the model itself is sound; the one thing that can break it is distribution — whether pure self-serve PLG can move a hard-to-reach, consultant-preferring buyer at the assumed CAC and conversion. De-risk it cheaply and fast: build the free gap-analysis hook + a real pricing page, spend a few thousand on qualified traffic, sign one channel partner, and measure cost-per-lead, free-to-paid conversion, blended ARPU, and early retention BEFORE building the full engine. If those come in near assumptions, this is an A-grade financial profile; if conversion or CAC is materially worse, the fix is a partner-led or light founder-assisted close (with a modest onboarding fee) rather than abandoning the concept.
