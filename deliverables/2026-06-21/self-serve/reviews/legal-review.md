LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Self-Serve CMMC / NIST 800-171 / SPRS Readiness Software for Small Defense Subcontractors

I am not a licensed attorney and this is not formal legal advice. I am an experienced operator with regulatory pattern-recognition flagging issues the founders must investigate with real counsel before launch.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
This is a self-serve GRC/compliance-documentation SaaS in a heavily regulated demand environment (DFARS/CMMC), and that regulatory backdrop is a demand asset, not a legal liability — there is no license required to sell software into it. The genuinely meaningful exposure does not come from "being in defense" or "needing a CMMC license" — it comes from a narrower place: the tool auto-generates the SSP, POA&M, and an estimated SPRS score that the customer copies into a federal system and personally attests to under DFARS 252.204-7019/7020 (and ultimately under the civil False Claims Act), while the founders hold no CMMC credential and use no credentialed human review. The good news, and why this is navigable not fatal: the self-serve, customer-does-the-work, customer-self-attests architecture structurally pushes this toward the same liability posture as Vanta/Drata/Totem (a tool the customer relies on, not a preparer who signs for them); the design choice to hold only compliance documentation and not CUI dramatically shrinks the data-security and ESP/CUI-scope problem; and the controlling risks (negligent-tool/product-liability framing, FCA "caused-to-submit" theory, UPL-type noise, contractual limitation-of-liability) are all manageable with disclaimers + a hard liability cap + tech-E&O insurance + clean self-attestation framing.

RISK-GATE RATING: SERIOUS BUT MANAGEABLE
A real due-diligence list — FCA exposure from auto-generating an estimated SPRS score the customer attests to, the negligent-tool/disclaimer posture from non-credentialed founders with no human review, and an honest UPL/CUI-scope check — but every one has a named, bridgeable path (clean self-attestation framing, contractual liability cap, tech-E&O insurance, a documented "estimate/draft only, customer is the assessor" posture). Nothing here is FATAL: no license is required to publish compliance software, the founders' SOC 2/HIPAA/GDPR background makes the data-handling regime bridgeable, and the architecture (customer self-attests; tool holds no CUI) is specifically the design that keeps this out of the worst exposure. Per doc-01, SERIOUS BUT MANAGEABLE does not lower the tier.

REGULATORY MAP
- DFARS 252.204-7012 (Safeguarding CUI / NIST 800-171 / 72-hr breach reporting) — Regulator: DoD/DCMA; DoD CIO owns SPRS methodology. Applies to the contractor, NOT to a software vendor that holds no CUI. Burden on founders near-zero as a regulated party because the tool holds no CUI. The customer's obligation; the product helps document it.
- DFARS 252.204-7019 & -7020 (NIST 800-171 DoD Assessment / SPRS posting & flow-down) — DoD/DCMA. Requires the contractor to have a current self-assessment score in SPRS. The forcing function and the most legally sensitive surface: the tool computes an estimated score the customer posts/attests. Zero burden as a filer; high care obligation in how the estimate is labeled/bounded.
- Civil False Claims Act, 31 U.S.C. §§3729–3733 (and DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative, Oct 2021) — DOJ; enforced increasingly against contractors for false/overstated cyber attestations. The contractor "makes" the claim; a vendor could theoretically be reached only under a "caused to be submitted"/reckless-disregard theory. Biggest single risk; mitigated, not eliminated, by self-attestation framing.
- CMMC Program Rule — 32 CFR Part 170 (final, effective Dec 16, 2024) and DFARS 252.204-7021 (phasing into solicitations from Nov 10, 2026) — DoD / Cyber AB for the C3PAO ecosystem. Defines who may perform certified assessments (C3PAOs) and the credential ladder (RP/RPO, CCP, CCA). The product does NOT perform certified assessments and provides no certification — so it is not in the regulated assessor lane and needs no C3PAO accreditation. The credential question is reputational/marketing, not a licensing bar.
- FTC Act §5 / UDAP — FTC + 50 state AGs. A "free automated gap analysis" and claims like "get CMMC-ready" or "accurate SPRS score" must be substantiated and not imply certification, a guaranteed pass, or accredited status. Low burden if disciplined.
- FTC ROSCA / state auto-renewal laws (CA ARL; Click-to-Cancel landscape) — recurring credit-card subscription needs clear renewal disclosure, affirmative consent, easy cancellation. Bread-and-butter SaaS compliance the founders have done.
- Data privacy (CCPA/CPRA; GDPR only if EU data) — limited: B2B, compliance documentation about a company, not consumer PII/biometric/health/children's data. Squarely bridgeable and low-stakes.
- ITAR / EAR — a landmine in defense generally, but designed out here because the product holds no technical data/CUI/drawings/operational security data — only the contractor's own policy/compliance text. Worth a one-time counsel confirmation that no customer-uploaded artifact drags export-controlled technical data into the platform.
- Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) / "practice of a licensed profession" — state bars + the LegalZoom/DoNotPay analog line. Generating SSP/POA&M via software is well-trodden (document-automation tools survived UPL challenges by being self-serve tools the customer drives, with disclaimers); CMMC is not a licensed legal profession, so classic UPL is weak. Realistic risk is a softer "holding yourself out as a CMMC professional/assessor" deception claim — handled by disclaimers and honest positioning.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No license is required to build, publish, or sell this software. There is no federal/state license to operate a GRC/compliance-documentation SaaS, and the product is not entering the one regulated lane that requires accreditation — certified CMMC assessment, reserved for C3PAOs via the Cyber AB. The founders do not perform certified assessments, issue certifications, or sign attestations, so they need no C3PAO authorization and no individual CMMC credential to sell the tool. The absence of a CMMC credential is a marketing/trust gap and a standard-of-care consideration, NOT a licensing barrier — they are legally free to ship. Standard business formation, clickwrap terms, and ordinary state business registration are the only "licensing" items. Optional later "done-for-you" services would not require a license either, but layering hands-on advisory shifts the liability posture toward "preparer/consultant," and that is when retaining/partnering with credentialed staff (CCP/CCA) becomes worth doing — a "before-scale," not "before-launch," item.

LIABILITY POSTURE
The core exposure is negligent-tool / product-liability-flavored: a customer relies on the auto-generated SSP/POA&M and the deterministic estimated SPRS score, posts it to SPRS, attests, later fails a real C3PAO assessment or suffers a breach, and claims the software's output was wrong and they relied on it. The self-serve architecture is the founders' best friend: because the customer does the work, controls the inputs, decides what to post, and personally self-attests, the legal frame is "the customer is the assessor of record; the software is a drafting and estimation aid" — the same posture Vanta/Drata/Totem/FutureFeed occupy. That materially LOWERS the FCA "preparer" exposure relative to a done-for-you preparer who produces and effectively signs the package. The remaining exposure is handled with three standard instruments: (1) contract — a clickwrap MSA with "estimates and drafts only; not a certified assessment; no guarantee of passing; customer is solely responsible for what it posts and attests; outputs are not legal advice," a strong limitation-of-liability cap (fees paid / 12 months), an 'as-is'/no-warranty-of-result clause, and an indemnity for customer misuse; (2) insurance — technology E&O / professional liability + cyber, readily available and modestly priced for a B2B SaaS holding no CUI; (3) product design — labeling every score an estimate with its basis shown, every document a draft for customer review, and an explicit "you are responsible for verifying and attesting" gate. Personal founder exposure collapses to ordinary levels once a proper entity is formed before any customer touches the product. The data-breach tail is deliberately minimized: the system holds only compliance documentation, not CUI/configs/logs/scans, so the platform is most likely NOT an ESP and is not storing/processing/transmitting CUI or (arguably) Security Protection Data — the design decision that keeps this out of FedRAMP-Moderate-equivalent / DFARS-7012 flow-down territory. This ESP/CUI/SPD scope question is the one item counsel should pin down in writing.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most consequential issue is the False Claims Act / DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative exposure created by auto-generating an estimated SPRS score (and the SSP/POA&M behind it) that a non-credentialed, no-human-review tool hands to a customer who then posts it to SPRS and attests under DFARS 252.204-7019/7020. Mechanism: SPRS scores are self-assessment scores the contractor attests to; DOJ has been settling FCA cases against contractors for overstating cyber posture; the customer is the one "making" that attestation (the founders' protection), but the FCA also reaches anyone who "knowingly causes" a false claim, and a tool that systematically produces inflated scores could, in a bad-facts case, be argued to have caused-to-be-submitted false claims with reckless disregard. The realistic worst case at concept stage is less "the vendor is named as an FCA defendant" (a stretch given the self-serve, customer-attests posture) and more "a marquee customer gets hit or fails a C3PAO assessment, blames the tool's score, and the resulting lawsuit + reputational damage in a small DIB community is existential for an early bootstrapped brand." Mitigations (and why this is MANAGEABLE): label every score a conservative estimate with its basis shown (never round up); present every document as a draft for review/editing; route the customer through an explicit attestation-responsibility gate; cap liability to fees; carry tech-E&O; and consider an optional credentialed (CCP/CCA) human-review tier so the fully-automated, no-review path is a deliberate, disclosed product choice. None requires a credential the founders cannot scaffold; it requires disciplined product design, disciplined marketing, and a tight contract.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
- FCA causation: given a self-serve tool whose estimated SPRS score and drafted SSP/POA&M the customer independently adopts, edits, posts, and self-attests, what is our realistic exposure under the FCA "knowingly causes to be presented" theory and the Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative — and what disclaimer language, methodology-conservatism, and attestation-gating most strongly establish the customer as the maker of the claim?
- Standard of care / negligent tool: with non-credentialed founders and no credentialed human review, how do we draft warranty disclaimers, an "estimate/draft-only" and "no-guarantee-of-passing" posture, an as-is clause, and a liability cap (fees/12 months) that hold up — and does adding an optional CCP/CCA review tier strengthen or weaken the fully-automated path?
- ESP / CUI / Security Protection Data scope: can counsel (with a DIB-savvy advisor) confirm in writing that a platform holding only compliance documentation — no CUI/configs/logs/scans — is NOT an ESP and does NOT store/process/transmit CUI or SPD under 32 CFR 170 / DFARS 7012, and what input-handling controls keep a customer from inadvertently uploading CUI/export-controlled technical data?
- Marketing substantiation / "holding out": where is the FTC §5 / UDAP line on "get CMMC-ready," "accurate SPRS score," and "free gap analysis," and how do we use the marks CMMC / NIST 800-171 / reference the Cyber AB without implying certification, accredited status, endorsement, or a guaranteed pass?
- Subscription compliance: confirm ROSCA + state auto-renewal disclosure, affirmative-consent, and easy-cancel for a self-serve recurring subscription, satisfied from day one.
- Entity & contract stack: entity choice/timing (before first customer), clickwrap enforceability, IP-assignment for contractor-built code, and indemnity running from the customer for misuse of outputs.
- Optional later "done-for-you" pivot: at what point does layering hands-on advisory convert us from "tool" to "preparer/consultant," materially raising FCA and professional-liability exposure — and what credential (CCP/CCA) or partnership do we need before offering it?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
This is a GO from the legal/regulatory seat. The regulatory environment is a demand asset — a forced, recurring, federally-uniform, budgeted buy with a deadline-driven tailwind — and the company sells software into that environment without needing any license, any CMMC credential, or any place in the accredited-assessor lane. The two design decisions at the heart of the concept (customer does the work and self-attests; the tool holds only compliance documentation and no CUI) are precisely the choices that keep the worst exposures off the table: they push the FCA frame toward "tool the customer relied on," keep the platform out of ESP/CUI/SPD scope, and keep the data-breach tail small and insurable. The residual risk is real and named — FCA "caused-to-submit" on the auto-generated estimated score, a negligent-tool standard-of-care gap, and marketing-claims/UPL "holding out" — but every piece is a due-diligence item, not a wall, handled with disciplined product design (conservative, clearly-labeled estimates; draft documents; an explicit attestation gate), a tight clickwrap MSA (estimate/draft-only, no-guarantee, hard cap, customer indemnity), tech-E&O + cyber insurance, and clean self-attestation positioning. I would not REFINE the core structure — the self-serve, no-CUI, customer-attests model is already the de-risked version of this business; a done-for-you preparer model would be materially riskier. The one structural enhancement worth weighing (not requiring) is an optional credentialed (CCP/CCA) human-review tier so the fully-automated path is a disclosed customer choice. Brief counsel on the questions above before the first paying customer, build the disclaimers and attestation gate in from day one, and proceed.
