COO REVIEW — Managed NIST 800-171 / SPRS Readiness for Small Defense Subcontractors ("Artifact-Only" Managed Compliance)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
Yes — two founders plus a fractional credentialed practitioner and, eventually, one or two delivery analysts can absolutely operate this. This is remote knowledge work with a fixed-scope deliverable (an SSP/POA&M/SPRS package, refreshed quarterly), not trucks or installs, so it lands squarely in the founders' tractable zone. The real operational question is not "can they do the work" but "how much of each customer is repeatable software output versus how much is irreducible credentialed-human review and back-and-forth chasing evidence out of a 35-person machine shop that has no security staff" — and the honest answer is that the practitioner sign-off on the official score, plus the human chasing of remediation evidence, is the bottleneck that caps how many customers one delivery person carries. The "artifact-only" design (no CUI custody, no environment hosting) is the single smartest operational decision in the concept: it strips out the heaviest, most variance-prone, most liability-laden work and keeps the unit of work to documents-and-evidence, which is what makes a two-founder lean team feasible at all.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Picture month 12. The business has roughly 40 active subscription customers across the three tiers, plus 3–4 in active onboarding. It is a Tuesday in March 2027 — four months into CMMC Level 2 phasing into new CUI contracts, so inbound pain is high.

Founder A (compliance operator, owns delivery) starts at the quarterly-cadence board. Of the 40 active accounts, ~13 fall in this quarter's review window (the book is staggered so the load is level, not a cliff at quarter-end). The software has already fired evidence-refresh reminders 30 and 14 days out; the customer's named point person has uploaded (or failed to upload) updated artifacts — access-control list, latest vulnerability-scan summary, training-completion records, incident-response tabletop notes. This Tuesday, Founder A works three of the 13: opens each artifact record, sees what came in vs. what is stale, and where evidence is missing sends a templated-but-specific chase. Two are clean and move to practitioner review; one is painful — a CNC-controller firmware change touched a CUI-handling workstation, rippling into three control objectives — so Founder A drafts the updated SSP section and a POA&M line and flags it for the practitioner.

The fractional Registered Practitioner / CCP (retained ~10–20 hrs/week) logs in mid-morning and does what only they can: reviews every deliverable that will move an official SPRS score before it is finalized — say 4 packages this Tuesday. They are not generating documents (software did that); they check control-objective judgment calls, confirm the SSP language defends the claimed score, and sign off internally. Crucially, the customer — not the provider — posts the score to SPRS and signs the government attestation, so the practitioner's job is to make the prep defensible, not to attest.

Founder B (GTM closer) is not in delivery today. Two free-assessment leads came in overnight off an SEO page targeting "blank SPRS score lost purchase order"; the guided assessment already produced each prospect's estimated score and remediation report, so Founder B's calls are warm and consultative. One prospect signs; Founder B kicks off onboarding via Founder A's intake checklist.

Where it breaks on a bad Tuesday: (1) the missing-evidence chase — an owner on the shop floor won't return the access-review template and the quarter's clock is ticking; (2) onboarding concurrency — if Founder B closes well, four onboardings (the 4–8 week labor-heavy baseline build) can stack on Founder A at once and starve quarterly maintenance; (3) practitioner availability — if the one fractional practitioner is on vacation or oversold, the sign-off queue backs up and nothing can be finalized. None are truck-rolls; all are schedulable, remote, software-assistable — which is exactly why this is operable.

CAPACITY MATH
Two streams: one-time onboarding and recurring quarterly maintenance.

Maintenance (recurring spine) — human hours per customer per quarter:
- Founder/analyst review + evidence chase + SSP/POA&M edits: [ASSUMED] 4–8 hrs/customer/quarter (≈6 midpoint).
- Credentialed practitioner sign-off: [ASSUMED] 1–2 hrs/customer/quarter (≈1.5).
- Total ≈ 7.5 hrs/customer/quarter ≈ 2.5 hrs/customer/month.
At 40 active customers: 40 × 7.5 = 300 human hrs/quarter = ~100 hrs/month ≈ ~23 hrs/week delivery labor. Practitioner-only slice = 40 × 1.5 = 60 hrs/quarter = ~20 hrs/month ≈ ~5 hrs/week — comfortably inside one fractional practitioner. The remaining ~18 hrs/week is carry-able by Founder A alone or plus a part-time analyst. So 40 maintenance customers do NOT require a second full delivery hire.

Onboarding (lumpy, labor-heavy) — human hours per onboarding:
- Build baseline SSP/POA&M/SPRS, intake, map 110 objectives, draft remediation: [ASSUMED] 30–50 hrs over 4–8 weeks (≈40).
- Practitioner sign-off on initial package: [ASSUMED] 3–5 hrs (≈4).
To grow ~40 net new over the year (≈3–4 onboardings/month): 3.5 × 40 = 140 onboarding hrs/month ≈ ~32 hrs/week. THIS is the larger, lumpier load. Combined with ~23 hrs/week maintenance, total delivery labor at month 12 ≈ ~55 hrs/week — beyond one founder. It means: Founder A leads methodology and hardest cases, ONE delivery analyst absorbs bulk onboarding + routine chase, fractional practitioner gates the score. A 3-person delivery org carrying ~40 active + ~40/yr new — feasible.

Revenue sanity check: 40 active at blended ~$30K/yr = ~$1.2M ARR + ~40 onboardings × ~$12K = ~$480K one-time = ~$1.68M revenue. With a lean delivery org (one analyst ~$90–120K loaded; one fractional practitioner ~$80–140K part-time) plus software/SEO, two founders clearing ~$300K each by month 12 is reachable but requires hitting ~40 customers — a sales-pace question, not an ops-capacity question. Per-customer human hours fall as software and the questionnaire library mature.

The bottleneck, stated plainly: credentialed-practitioner review hours on score-affecting deliverables (a hard gate that cannot be software-replaced), and second, onboarding throughput during a hot sales quarter. Neither is field ops; both are schedulable knowledge-work constraints relievable by adding fractional practitioner hours and one analyst.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.6 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- The "artifact-only / no CUI custody" architecture is an operational masterstroke: it removes the heaviest, highest-variance, highest-liability work (enclaves, email security, hosting the environment) and reduces the unit of work to documents-and-evidence — which lets two founders plus a thin expert layer scale this.
- Per-customer human hours fall as the software and reusable library mature, so margins improve with scale; the recurring maintenance spine (~7.5 human hrs/customer/quarter, only ~1.5 credentialed) is genuinely lean and level-loadable across a staggered book.
- Pure remote knowledge work with national reach and no logistics, inventory, field crews, weather, or routing — the cleanest possible fit for founders who are explicitly not a field-service crew.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Credentialed-practitioner sign-off is a hard, non-software-replaceable gate and, at small scale, a key-person dependency: one oversold/unavailable fractional RP/CCP backs up the entire finalization queue, and the founders do not yet hold the credential themselves.
- Onboarding is the lumpy, labor-heavy stream (~40 human hours over 4–8 weeks each); a hot sales quarter can stack four concurrent onboardings and starve the recurring maintenance book, causing quality slips at the worst moment.
- Evidence-chasing from customers with zero in-house security staff is irreducibly high-touch and partly outside the provider's control — if owners won't return artifacts, quarterly cycles slip and the provider eats the schedule risk even though the customer is responsible.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to bite is the collision of onboarding lumpiness with the practitioner-review gate during a deadline-driven demand surge. The whole concept is timed to the Nov 10, 2026 CMMC phase-in, which is a feature for demand but a hazard for operations: the same forcing function that fills the pipeline will cluster onboardings and quarterly deadlines into the same windows. Onboarding is ~40 human hours of judgment-heavy work each, cannot be fully parallelized by one founder, and every score-affecting deliverable — initial baseline and each quarter's refresh — must pass through a single fractional credentialed practitioner who is the one resource the founders can least easily multiply on short notice (RP/CCP holders are in tight supply precisely because the same regulation created the demand). If Founder B closes a strong quarter, you get four onboardings, thirteen quarterly refreshes, and a queue of score-affecting packages all converging on one practitioner and one delivery founder in the same three weeks. The failure isn't a truck that doesn't show up; it's a backed-up sign-off queue that delays a customer's SPRS posting past a prime's flow-down deadline — the exact pain the customer hired you to prevent. It is manageable (stagger the book, contract a second fractional practitioner before you need them, cap concurrent onboardings, get at least one founder credentialed so the gate isn't single-threaded), but they need the analyst hire and a second practitioner lined up before the Nov 2026 wave, not after.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the measured human-hours-per-customer-per-quarter for maintenance, and per onboarding, once the software exists — split into non-credentialed vs credentialed review? Time the first 10 real onboardings and 10 quarterly cycles; the staffing model and margin hinge on these numbers.
- How is the credentialed-practitioner gate de-risked from a single point of failure — two fractional RP/CCP from day one, at least one founder pursuing the credential, and a documented backup during a deadline crunch?
- What is the concrete onboarding-concurrency cap and the staggering policy that keeps a hot sales quarter from starving the recurring maintenance book?
- When a customer with no security staff stalls on returning evidence and a prime deadline approaches, where do the schedule risk and liability sit contractually, and what is the escalation path?
- How mature must the reusable prime-questionnaire auto-answer library be before it actually removes human hours, and what's the build sequence so labor-saving tooling exists before the customer count that requires it?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Operationally this is a strong concept and a clean founder fit — remote knowledge work with a fixed-scope, software-leveraged, recurring deliverable, national reach, no logistics/inventory/field crews. The shown capacity math demonstrates two founders + one delivery analyst + a fractional credentialed practitioner can carry the ~40 active + ~40/yr new customer count implied by the income ladder, with the hard-gated practitioner work landing at just ~5 hrs/week at that scale. The "artifact-only, no CUI custody" design deliberately removes the heaviest, most liability-laden operational work. The open risks — practitioner key-person dependency, onboarding lumpiness colliding with the Nov 2026 surge, customer evidence-chasing — are all schedulable, staffable knowledge-work risks with obvious mitigations, not structural defects. The build of the control-mapping/SSP-generation/questionnaire-library tooling is the real near-term operational dependency and should be sequenced so labor-saving software exists before the volume that needs it.
