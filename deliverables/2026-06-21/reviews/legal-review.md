LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Managed NIST 800-171 / SPRS Readiness for Small Defense Subcontractors ("Artifact-Only" Productized Compliance Service)

Note: I am not a licensed attorney and this is not formal legal advice. This is an experienced operator's regulatory pattern-recognition, written to brief the founders on what a real lawyer should investigate.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
This is a regulated-adjacent service business, not a regulated entity — the founders are selling preparation and program operation, not certification, custody of CUI, or a government attestation, and that single design choice ("we prepare, the customer attests; we never touch CUI") is what keeps the risk profile navigable rather than fatal. The genuine exposure clusters in three places: (1) the company prepares the SPRS score estimate and SSP/POA&M artifacts that the customer then attests to the U.S. government, which creates an indirect False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§3729-3733) shadow — if a customer materially over-states its score on the strength of the provider's work, the provider can be pulled into the dispute as the preparer; (2) marketing-claims discipline around "audit-ready" and any implied guarantee of passing a CMMC assessment, which is both an FTC §5 deceptive-claims surface and the single biggest E&O-insurability lever; and (3) ITAR/EAR export-control questions that are largely designed out by the artifact-only/never-touch-CUI architecture but must be confirmed, not assumed. None of these is a licensing wall and none is an unbridgeable credential — the founders' SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR operator background plus a fractional Registered Practitioner/CMMC professional and a DoD/DIB advisor put a named, well-trodden compliance path squarely within reach. This is a clean SERIOUS-BUT-MANAGEABLE: a due-diligence checklist and a disciplined contract, not a tier penalty.

RISK-GATE RATING: SERIOUS BUT MANAGEABLE
Real liability-allocation and claims-discipline work exists (FCA preparer shadow, no-guarantee discipline, E&O scoping, an export-control confirmation), but every piece has a named, navigable, insurable path — there is no license the founders cannot scaffold, no uninsurable tail, and no legal prohibition. Per doc-01 and doc-05 this rating is a due-diligence list, NOT a tier reduction; the heavy regulation here is the demand engine (credited on the must-have dimension elsewhere), and the company is deliberately positioned on the low-liability side of every line that matters (no CUI custody, no attestation, no certification guarantee, not a C3PAO).

REGULATORY MAP
- DFARS 252.204-7012 / 7019 / 7020 (DoD acquisition regulation; regulator = DoD/DCMA, enforced through the prime's contract flow-down). This is the customer's obligation, not the provider's — the sub must maintain the SPRS score, SSP, and POA&M. The provider's burden is to know these cold and produce conforming artifacts; it carries no direct regulatory duty under 7012 because it neither holds the contract nor stores CUI. Burden on provider: knowledge/methodology, not registration. This is the forcing function, not a provider compliance cost.
- CMMC (32 CFR Part 170, program rule effective Dec 16, 2024; 48 CFR/DFARS 252.204-7021 contract rule phasing into new CUI awards from ~Nov 10, 2026; regulator = DoD via the Cyber AB / C3PAO accreditation ecosystem). Critical line: the provider is explicitly NOT a C3PAO and offers no certification — so it needs no CMMC accreditation and avoids the "you cannot both consult and assess the same client" independence prohibition. Burden: use accredited/registered individuals (RP/RPO/CCP) correctly and do not misrepresent its role. Low direct burden, high knowledge requirement.
- Cyber AB — RP / RPO / CCP credentialing and the CMMC Code of Professional Conduct. To use the "Registered Practitioner"/"Registered Provider Organization" marks the company (or its retained practitioner) must register and adhere to the CoPC. Burden: a registration and code adherence, not an exam wall for the entity; the individual credential is bridgeable via the fractional hire.
- False Claims Act, 31 U.S.C. §§3729-3733 (DOJ; the live enforcement engine here, including the DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative launched Oct 2021, which has produced settlements against contractors for misrepresented cybersecurity posture, e.g., Aerojet Rocketdyne 2022). The customer attests; the provider prepares the estimate and artifacts that underlie the attestation. Direct FCA liability sits with the attesting contractor, but a preparer can face exposure as a cause of a false claim or via qui-tam discovery if it knowingly inflated a score. Burden on provider: a conservative, documented scoring methodology, an explicit "estimate, customer verifies and attests" handoff, and contractual allocation of attestation responsibility to the customer.
- FTC Act §5 / deceptive-advertising (FTC; plus state UDAP). "Keeps your SPRS score correct," "audit-ready," and any phrasing implying a guaranteed pass are claim surfaces. Burden: claims-substantiation discipline and a written no-guarantee disclaimer. Low cost, high importance.
- ITAR (22 CFR 120-130; DDTC/State) and EAR (15 CFR; BIS/Commerce). Defense subs frequently handle ITAR-controlled technical data inside their CUI. The artifact-only design means the provider's system of record holds compliance proof and explicitly NOT the customer's technical data — precisely how you avoid being an "exporter" or needing DDTC registration. Burden: a hard contractual and technical rule that no ITAR/EAR-controlled technical data enters the provider's environment, plus a foreign-person-access screen on the provider's own team (deemed-export risk). Confirm, don't assume.
- Data privacy (CCPA/CPRA, etc.). Low: B2B compliance metadata and business contacts, not consumer PII/special categories. The founders already run GDPR/CCPA. Minimal burden.
- Standard commercial regimes — entity formation, B2B contract law, IP assignment from contractors/the fractional practitioner, and (minor) auto-renewal/ROSCA disclosure on the recurring subscription. Routine.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No occupational or government license is required to operate this business. The company is deliberately NOT a C3PAO (the only accredited role) and does not seek to be — so the heaviest credential in the space is sidestepped by design. The one credential that matters operationally is the RP/RPO designation and a CCP on staff to review every score-affecting deliverable; the founders do not yet hold this but plan to retain it fractionally — a textbook bridgeable expertise barrier under doc-01 hard-constraint #6, consistent with the founders' compliance-operator profile. The entity itself needs only ordinary business registration. To use the RPO marks in marketing, register with the Cyber AB and follow mark-usage and CoPC rules. Net: a rare regulated-adjacent business with effectively no entity-licensing wall.

LIABILITY POSTURE
The dominant liability is professional liability / errors & omissions: the company is paid to keep a government-facing score and package correct, so a wrong score, a missed control, or a defective SSP/POA&M that contributes to a lost contract, a failed C3PAO assessment, or an FCA matter is the realistic claim. This is the standard E&O fact pattern for compliance consultancies and IS insurable — Tech E&O / Professional Liability with a cyber endorsement is a known product; premium will reflect the defense-sector clientele, but the never-touch-CUI / no-guarantee design materially improves insurability by removing the two worst tails (a breach of customer CUI in custody, and a guaranteed-outcome promise). The provider should NOT take custody of CUI (it doesn't — the single best risk decision), should carry cyber liability for its own artifact-only system anyway, and should form the entity before first revenue. Contractually, the allocation must be explicit and load-bearing: the customer is sole attesting party; the provider delivers an estimated score and prepared artifacts the customer reviews/verifies before posting/attesting; the engagement disclaims any guarantee of certification or award; and the agreement includes a limitation-of-liability cap (commonly fees-paid or a multiple), mutual indemnities, and a clause that the customer is responsible for the accuracy of inputs it supplies. Residual after insurance + contract + entity: reputational/qui-tam discovery risk (you may be subpoenaed as preparer even if not liable), and the residual that no cap fully survives a fraud allegation — both managed by documented conservative methodology and the practitioner-review gate.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most consequential issue is the False Claims Act exposure created by the "we prepare / you attest" liability hand-off around the SPRS score and the SSP/POA&M. The government treats the SPRS self-assessment score and the §7012 representations as material to contract eligibility and payment, and DOJ's Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative has explicitly made misrepresented cybersecurity posture a qui-tam and FCA target — with treble damages, per-claim penalties, and a whistleblower bounty that incentivizes disgruntled employees or competitors to file. The customer is the attesting party and bears primary liability, but the provider produced the number and the evidence narrative, which is exactly the role a relator's counsel will name and subpoena, and in a worst case argue the provider "caused" a false claim if it knowingly or recklessly inflated a score. The mitigation is entirely within the founders' control and is the make-or-break of the operating model: (1) a documented, conservative, defensible scoring methodology with the practitioner-review gate on every score-affecting deliverable; (2) delivering the score as an estimate the customer must independently verify before posting/attesting, papered explicitly; (3) contractual allocation making the customer the sole attesting party responsible for accuracy of its own inputs, plus indemnity and a cap; and (4) never marketing or pricing in a way that rewards a higher score. Get the methodology, the handoff, and the contract right and this is a managed E&O risk insurers will write; get sloppy on any of the three — especially under commercial pressure to tell a client what they want to hear — and it becomes the issue that ends the company and personally entangles the founders in federal litigation.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
- FCA preparer exposure: To what extent can a non-attesting preparer of SPRS scores and SSP/POA&M be reached under 31 U.S.C. §3729 as a "cause" of a false claim, and what methodology documentation, customer-verification handoff, and contractual language best insulate the provider given Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative precedent?
- Liability allocation and limitation: Draft and pressure-test an MSA that names the customer as sole attesting party, characterizes the output as an estimate requiring verification, caps liability with indemnity, and survives the argument that a fees-based cap shouldn't apply to fraud/gross negligence.
- No-guarantee claims discipline: Review all funnel/marketing assets against FTC §5 and state UDAP, and define the exact disclaimer language and substantiation file needed.
- ITAR/EAR scoping: Confirm the artifact-only system of record keeps the provider outside "exporter" status and DDTC registration, and define the contractual + technical controls that keep controlled technical data out of scope, plus any deemed-export screen for foreign-person access.
- Cyber AB / CMMC role compliance: Validate RP/RPO/CCP registration, mark-usage, and CoPC requirements, and confirm a prep/consulting provider that is NOT a C3PAO faces no independence/conflict prohibition.
- E&O / cyber insurance scoping: Identify carriers writing Tech E&O + cyber for defense-sector compliance preparers, the underwriting questions, expected premium/retention, and confirm the never-touch-CUI / no-guarantee posture is reflected in the policy.
