CMO REVIEW — Managed CMMC/DFARS Compliance for Small Defense Subcontractors ("Artifact-Only Operated Program")

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is one of the cleanest forced-demand B2B markets I have looked at in a long time: 118,000+ DoD-estimated companies need CMMC Level 2, the vast majority are small machine shops and suppliers, the buy is contractually mandatory and recurring, and the consequence of inaction (a blank/stale SPRS score and loss of purchase-order eligibility) is fast and concrete. The honest news for a CMO is that the demand thesis is excellent but the field is already crowded and noisy — Summit 7, CyberSheath, PreVeil, Cuick Trac, Totem, Ardalyst, and a swarm of regional RPOs are all here, and several already run the exact "free SPRS calculator" lead magnet this plan leans on (Greypike, Petronella, Peerless). The wedge — "operated for you, artifact-only, fixed published price, you keep your data and environment" — is genuinely sharp and addresses a real seam, but it is a positioning wedge, not a structural moat, and the whole game will be won on message discipline, channel access, and trust signaling against incumbents who got there first.

POSITIONING DRAFT
"We keep your SPRS score correct and your CMMC evidence audit-ready every quarter for one fixed monthly price — without ever touching your CUI or taking over your systems." That is a real, ownable sentence — it has edges, it names the enemy (custody-taking enclave bundlers and shelf-ware GRC tools), and a 35-person machine shop owner panicking about a prime's flow-down questionnaire would understand it instantly. The risk is not that the sentence is mush (it isn't); it's that "artifact-only / we don't host your CUI" is a nuance the buyer may not grasp as a benefit until educated — many owners assume they want someone to take the whole problem (and the data) away, which is exactly what enclave players sell. So the positioning is sharp but requires one extra beat of buyer education ("you keep control AND you stay eligible"), a real conversion step, not a freebie.

THE WEDGE
The first 100 customers come from the free guided SPRS self-assessment as the intake funnel, fed by three converging channels: (1) Present-tense pain SEO/SEM on terms like "blank SPRS score," "lost DoD purchase order CMMC," "prime sent me a security questionnaire" — lower-volume, higher-intent long-tail terms (good, because the head terms are saturated by Summit 7, Kiteworks, Vanta, and a dozen RPOs). Expect cybersecurity/compliance CPLs of ~$300–$500 and cost-per-SQL toward $3,500 on paid Google (2026 B2B SaaS benchmarks; cybersecurity is the most expensive segment) — so paid is a supporting channel, not the engine. (2) The real engine should be channel partnerships with APEX Accelerators (former PTACs, federally funded, ~95 centers nationally whose literal job is helping small firms win DoD work) and the MEP National Network (51 Manufacturing Extension Partnership centers, one per state, NIST-funded, already running CMMC awareness programming) — warm, trusted, near-zero-CAC referral sources pointed at exactly the 15–200-employee machine-shop archetype, and the incumbents have largely under-mined them. [ASSUMED: APEX/MEP will refer to a non-assessor managed-prep vendor — verify by booking 5 intro calls with state MEP/APEX directors; cheap and fast.] (3) Prime-driven pull: when Lockheed/RTX flow CMMC requirements down, subs scramble — a content/PR play targeting that exact moment compounds. The wedge is real and founder-fit, but NOT uncontested: the free-SPRS-tool lead magnet is already operated by Greypike ("SPRS score in 3 minutes"), Petronella (AI SPRS calculator), and Peerless. So the funnel mechanic is table stakes; the differentiation has to live in the offer (operated, artifact-only, fixed price) and the close, not in owning the lead magnet.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- Summit 7 — the category gorilla for DoD CUI; best at the all-in / managed GCC High enclave (self-models ~$265K all-in for a 25-employee Level 2 project, ~$504K at 250). They take over the environment. Air: they are heavy, expensive, custody-taking — you are light, fixed-price, data-sovereign.
- PreVeil — strong encrypted email/file enclave for CUI, popular because it shrinks scope; best at "cheap compliant enclave." A tool/environment, not an operated program — your air: "we run the program around whatever enclave you choose."
- CyberSheath — large managed-services/vCISO DIB compliance shop; best at full managed security + compliance for mid-size DIB. Heavier/more enterprise; your air is the under-50-employee shop they don't economically serve well at a published price.
- Cuick Trac / Ardalyst / Totem — small-contractor specialists; Cuick Trac and Ardalyst sell scoped enclaves/managed environments, Totem sells affordable DIY-leaning software + templates (and runs a free SPRS tool). Closest competitors; Totem is the low-end price anchor, Cuick Trac the enclave anchor. Your air is the explicit middle: "more than a tool, lighter than an enclave, one published price."
- Vanta / Drata — horizontal GRC automation; Vanta NIST 800-171 starts ~$12K/yr and now markets CMMC content. Best at automated evidence collection across frameworks. Your air (and literal pitch) is they hand the owner a tool he cannot operate — you operate it. Watch them: a horizontal platform adding a "CMMC done-for-you" partner tier is a plausible 2–4 quarter encroachment.
Where the air actually is: the published-price, operated-but-artifact-only middle for the <50-to-200-employee sub who can't run Vanta himself, doesn't want to hand custody and pay enclave prices, and is sick of consultants who leave. That seam is real, also narrow, and the differentiation is a positioning/operations difference, not a technology moat — a determined RPO could rebrand toward it in 90 days.

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- Forced, recurring, already-budgeted demand at scale with a deadline: 118,000+ DoD-estimated Level 2 companies, a hard Nov 10, 2026 milestone, and a fast concrete consequence (blank/stale SPRS = lost PO eligibility) — a painkiller, not a vitamin, no market creation needed.
- A genuinely edged, ownable positioning sentence and a near-perfect motion-to-founder fit: "operated, artifact-only, fixed published price, you keep your data" cleanly differentiates from the two loud incumbent shapes, and the marketing-led + founder-close motion is what a compliance-operator and a regulated-enterprise closer are built to run.
- Low-CAC trusted channels incumbents under-exploit: APEX Accelerators (~95 federally funded centers) and the MEP National Network (51 NIST-funded centers) are warm, credible referral pipes aimed at the archetype, plus high ACV ($18K–$42K/yr) that easily tolerates a real consultative sale.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- The market is crowded and partly well-run, so "air" is a narrow seam: Summit 7, CyberSheath, PreVeil, Cuick Trac, Totem, Ardalyst are all here, and the free-SPRS lead magnet is already operated by Greypike, Petronella, Peerless — win on message and trust, not on owning an empty category.

- Trust/credibility gap at point of sale: the buyer is betting his prime relationships; the founders do not yet personally hold the RP/CCP credential and are not a C3PAO — against incumbents who wear those badges, the brand must manufacture trust fast, or paid CAC (SQL ~$3,500) becomes the only growth lever and unit economics tighten.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely thing to kill this from a marketing standpoint is not demand and not the wedge — it is differentiation durability inside a market that has already woken up. The forcing function is so obvious that everyone already sees it: Summit 7 built an authority brand, PreVeil and Cuick Trac own the scope-shrinking enclave story, Vanta and Drata are bolting CMMC onto a horizontal platform, and the exact free-SPRS-score funnel this plan uses as intake is already live at Greypike, Petronella, and Peerless. The "operated, artifact-only, fixed price" position is genuinely good, but it is a service-design and messaging difference, not a technological or network moat — so the founders' real defensibility is being first to plant a flag on that seam, market it relentlessly, and convert APEX/MEP into a structural referral lock before a well-resourced RPO or a horizontal GRC vendor copies the language. If they win the positioning land-grab and the channel relationships in the next 12–18 months (into the Nov 2026 tailwind), the accumulated switching costs make retention sticky and the business compounds. If they are slow, generic, or forced to buy growth on $300–$500 CPL paid search against incumbents with bigger budgets and the badges, they become one more undifferentiated CMMC vendor in a noisy auction — and the CAC math gets ugly.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Channel proof: Will APEX Accelerators and MEP centers actually refer subs to a non-assessor, for-profit "managed prep" vendor, and on what terms? Book 5–8 calls with state MEP/APEX directors in the next 30 days — if even 2 will co-market, the wedge's primary engine is real; if none, the CAC story collapses back onto expensive paid search.
- The "artifact-only" benefit comprehension: Does the target owner hear "we don't take custody of your CUI" as a benefit (control, lower cost, no lock-in) or as "wait, so I still have to deal with my data myself"? Test two landing-page variants (custody-light vs. done-for-you) and measure completion and call-book rates.

Run 10 founder-led discovery calls and track how often "are you certified/an assessor?" stalls the close.
