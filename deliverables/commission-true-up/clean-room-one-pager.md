# Clean-Room Concept One-Pager
**Internal label:** Carrier Commission Reconciliation & Recovery Service

> Neutral, standalone one-pager handed to each council reviewer. No scores, no lineage, no tier bar, no target.

## 1) CUSTOMER
U.S. insurance distribution intermediaries whose own revenue *is* commission income: brokerage general agencies (BGAs) and independent/field marketing organizations (IMOs/FMOs) in life and annuity distribution, and general agencies in employee/voluntary benefits. These firms receive first-year commissions, renewal/trail commissions, production overrides, and bonuses from many insurance carriers, and in turn pay downline agents/producers. Primary focus on small-to-midsize agencies (roughly $1M–$50M of annual commission revenue) that place business across many carriers but lack a dedicated revenue-assurance function. The economic buyer is the agency principal/owner or CFO/controller.

## 2) THE MUST-HAVE / FORCING FUNCTION
The agency's entire revenue is the commission/override/bonus/renewal compensation paid by carriers — and that compensation is chronically mispaid. Carrier commission statements arrive monthly in many incompatible formats, computed by legacy carrier systems against contract terms the agency cannot easily see or verify. In practice, carriers underpay or omit renewal/trail commissions, miscalculate overrides and bonus tiers, fail to pay on some policies, and issue wrongful or duplicate chargebacks (clawbacks on business that did not actually lapse or that was mis-attributed). Because no one re-derives what the contract entitled the agency to and compares it line-by-line to what was actually paid, the shortfall is invisible and recurs every payment cycle. Industry-experienced operators estimate the silent leakage at roughly 2%–8% of commission revenue per cycle [ESTIMATE — to be validated]. For a commission-revenue business this is a direct, continuous hit to the firm's own earned income and cash flow — a financial-continuity and revenue-integrity forcing function: the money is already owed, already contracted, and simply not being collected.

## 3) PRODUCT
A software-plus-service platform that: (a) ingests carrier commission statements across carriers and formats (PDF, spreadsheet, EDI/flat-file, carrier-portal downloads) and normalizes them to a common ledger; (b) encodes the agency's contractual compensation terms — the comp grid: first-year and renewal schedules, override and bonus tiers, by product and carrier; (c) re-computes the expected compensation for each policy/transaction and reconciles it against what the carrier actually paid; (d) flags underpayments, missing or stopped renewals/trails, mis-tiered overrides/bonuses, and wrongful/duplicate chargebacks; (e) assembles a documented recovery package the agency submits to each carrier to claim the shortfall; and (f) runs continuously each cycle as an ongoing reconciliation and monitoring layer, so new discrepancies are caught as statements arrive. Delivered as a portal for the agency's principal/finance team, with onboarding support to load historical statements and build the comp grid.

## 4) REVENUE MODEL
A hybrid model. Entry is a contingency/success fee — a percentage of the dollars actually recovered from carriers on the initial historical audit — so the agency pays nothing out of pocket to start and the vendor is paid only from found money. Once the historical recovery is complete and the data/integration are in place, the relationship converts to a recurring subscription (monthly or annual) for ongoing cycle-by-cycle reconciliation and monitoring, priced by agency size / statement volume. The recurring layer is the durable revenue; the contingency wedge is the low-friction way in.

## 5) POSITIONING
An independent commission revenue-assurance layer for insurance agencies: it verifies what carriers actually paid against what the agency's contracts owe, recovers the silent shortfall, and then prevents it every cycle — turning the agency's most important and least-audited revenue stream into one that is checked the way any other major receivable would be.
