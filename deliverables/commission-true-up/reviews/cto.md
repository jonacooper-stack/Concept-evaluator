# CTO REVIEW — Carrier Commission Reconciliation & Recovery Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH TECHNICAL READ
This is a financially deterministic problem wrapped in a genuinely hard data-ingestion problem. The reconciliation math (re-derive expected comp from a grid, diff it against what was paid, line by line) is boring, provable, off-the-shelf arithmetic — that part is not where the risk lives. The risk lives almost entirely in the front door: ingesting and normalizing hundreds of incompatible carrier commission statements (PDFs, spreadsheets, EDI/flat files, portal scrapes) into one clean ledger, and in entity-resolving each paid line to the agency's book of policies so "expected vs. paid" is comparable at all. This is buildable by a two-founder team with one strong developer — but it is a per-carrier extraction grind that is service-heavy at the start, not a clean SaaS you flip on; the honest plan is to ship a narrow, semi-automated, human-in-the-loop MVP against a handful of carriers and a few design-partner agencies, earn contingency revenue immediately, and let coverage compound. The biggest non-obvious bomb is data acquisition: the agency's *contractual* comp terms (the grid) and a clean policy book are the two inputs the whole engine depends on, and both are messy, partly tribal knowledge, and not sitting in a queryable system.

## ARCHITECTURE SKETCH
- **Frontend:** a thin web portal for the agency principal/finance team — upload statements, view a normalized ledger, review flagged discrepancies, approve/edit, download recovery packages. Next.js + React on Vercel, or Retool/Refine for the internal-ops console early. Tables, review queues, PDF/Excel exports — no exotic UI.
- **Backend:** a standard Python service (FastAPI) — Python because the document-extraction and data-wrangling ecosystem (pandas, openpyxl, pdfplumber/camelot, regex/template parsers) is richest there. Postgres as the system of record (a normalized commission ledger, a policy book, a comp-grid store, a discrepancy/case table). Object storage (S3) for raw statements and generated recovery packages. A job queue (Celery/RQ or managed equivalent) for async ingestion and recompute runs each cycle.
- **The ingestion layer is the real system, and it is a per-source adapter farm, not one parser:** (a) spreadsheets/CSV/EDI flat files — deterministic, column-mapped parsers, high reliability; (b) PDF statements — table extraction (pdfplumber/Camelot/AWS Textract or Azure Document Intelligence) plus per-carrier templates, with an LLM as a *fallback structuring/field-mapping assistant* over already-extracted text, never as the source of truth for dollar amounts; (c) carrier-portal downloads — ideally human-pulled files dropped into the portal at the start, NOT brittle Playwright scrapers behind MFA logins (scraping carrier portals is a ToS and reliability minefield and should be explicitly out of scope for the MVP).
- **Comp-grid engine:** a data-modeled rules store (first-year %, renewal/trail schedule by policy year, override tiers, bonus tiers, keyed by carrier × product × contract). Configuration data plus a deterministic evaluator, not ML. Versioned and effective-dated, because comp schedules change and you must recompute against the terms in force at the transaction date.
- **Reconciliation/matching engine:** deterministic. The subtle part is entity resolution — joining each carrier-paid line to the right policy and the right expected-comp rule when carriers use inconsistent policy numbers, producer IDs, product names, and dates. Fuzzy-match + rules + a human review queue for low-confidence joins; not a research problem, but where the engineering hours actually go.
- **ML/AI:** deliberately on the periphery — LLM/OCR assists *extraction and field mapping over text*; all money math is deterministic and reproducible. The architecture must be auditable: every flagged dollar traces to "this paid line, this expected rule, this delta," because the output is a dispute you hand a carrier.
- **Auth/hosting:** managed auth (Auth0/Clerk/Cognito) with SSO-readiness, single managed cloud (AWS or GCP), encryption in transit and at rest from day one. Where the real complexity lives: ingestion coverage + matching accuracy + acquiring the comp grid. Everything downstream is conventional.

## BUILD PLAN TO REVENUE-EARNING MVP
Estimate: roughly **10–16 weeks of one strong developer's time** to a *contingency-revenue-earning* MVP against a narrow carrier set and 1–3 design-partner agencies, with the second founder running onboarding/comp-grid capture and recovery submission by hand. This MVP *earns* (you can run a real historical audit and submit real recoveries) — not a full self-serve SaaS, which is quarters away.
1. **Ledger + comp-grid data model and the deterministic reconciliation/recompute engine (~2–3 wks).** The spine and the easy, provable part — build it first so the math is trustworthy before you fight the documents.
2. **Spreadsheet/CSV/EDI ingestion for the first handful of carriers your design partners actually use, plus the policy-book import (~2–3 wks).** Start with structured formats; they cover real dollars immediately and de-risk the thesis fastest.
3. **PDF table extraction + per-carrier templates for the top 3–6 statement formats your design partners actually receive (~3–4 wks, and this never fully "ends").** Human-in-the-loop review queue so a low-confidence extraction is corrected, not silently wrong.
4. **Discrepancy detection + recovery-package generator + the review/approval UI (~2–3 wks).** This converts found money into a claim and therefore into your contingency fee.
5. **Recurring-monitoring loop, multi-tenant hardening, audit logging, onboarding tooling (~2–3 wks)** — partly punt-able to v2; the contingency historical audit can be delivered semi-manually before the recurring layer is polished.
**Punt to v2:** portal scraping/automation, broad carrier coverage, self-serve onboarding, downline/producer-payment features, SOC2 formalization. Reality check for two non-engineer-heavy founders: one capable dev can build this spine; the *coverage* and *comp-grid capture* are ongoing labor the non-dev founder absorbs as a service motion early — the right bootstrap shape, but "automation %" is low at launch and improves per carrier over time.

## CRITICAL ASSUMPTIONS
1. **The comp grid is obtainable and machineable.** The entire engine assumes the agency's contractual comp terms can be captured into structured rules. In practice these live in carrier contracts, addenda, and the principal's head, and can be ambiguous. If grids can't be reliably encoded, "expected" is undefined. Cheap test: take 3 real agencies, attempt to fully encode the comp grid for their top 5 carriers in a spreadsheet, time it / log ambiguities — before writing any engine code.
2. **A clean, matchable policy book exists.** Reconciliation needs the agency's in-force/sold policies to join paid lines against. If the book lives only in carrier portals or a thin AMS with bad keys, matching quality collapses. Cheap test: pull one agency's policy export and one month of statements and hand-measure what % of paid lines auto-join.
3. **Structured-format coverage carries enough dollars early.** The bet is that spreadsheet/EDI/DTCC-style feeds plus a few PDF templates cover enough commission volume to find recoverable money in phase 1. Cheap test: for design partners, tally structured vs. PDF-only format share.
4. **Recoverable money actually exists at the claimed rate.** The 2%–8% figure is unvalidated; the contingency model dies if real leakage is <0.5% or dominated by items carriers won't honor. Cheap test: a manual spreadsheet-only audit of one agency's last 12 months against 3–5 carriers — the single highest-value spike, needs no product.
5. **Carriers will pay on a documented dispute.** Technically you can flag a shortfall; commercially the carrier must accept the package and remit. If carriers stonewall or the dispute process is adversarial, found money isn't collectible. Cheap test: interview 2–3 principals on their actual dispute experience and hit rate.

## INFORMATION SECURITY POSTURE
Data is sensitive but bounded: carrier commission statements, the agency's policy book, comp-grid/contract terms, recovery correspondence. Financial and commercial data, very likely containing PII (insured names, producer SSNs/tax IDs on some statements, policy numbers) — treat as PII/financial-data from day one. Single managed cloud: Postgres for structured data, S3 for raw statements/packages, encrypted at rest (KMS) and in transit (TLS). Threat model: (a) multi-tenant data leakage — one agency seeing another's commissions is catastrophic to trust; enforce strict tenant isolation (RLS / tenant-scoped queries) and test it explicitly; (b) credential/secrets exposure — secrets manager, never embed carrier-portal creds, prefer human-pulled files over storing carrier-portal logins at all (storing carrier MFA credentials would be a meaningful liability to avoid in the MVP); (c) raw-statement store as crown jewels — least-privilege, no public buckets, signed URLs with expiry. Launch controls: managed auth with MFA, RBAC, encryption everywhere, audit logging of who-viewed/exported-what (table stakes for financial-dispute data), automated encrypted backups, lightweight written incident-response plan. At scale / first enterprise-ish ask: SOC 2 Type II is attainable but a deliberate 6–12 month program; design now (logging, least privilege, change control) to avoid retrofit. Not HIPAA, but producer SSNs mean minimize/mask/avoid ingesting fields you don't need.

## TOP 3 TECHNICAL STRENGTHS
- The core economic computation is deterministic, auditable, and provable — re-deriving expected comp from a versioned grid and diffing against paid lines is plain arithmetic, so "is this number right?" risk is low and the output is defensible to a carrier.
- The proprietary data assets compound: a growing library of per-carrier statement templates, encoded comp grids, and normalized historical ledgers is a real, owned, portable moat that gets cheaper-per-carrier and harder to replicate over time.
- Revenue can start before the full product exists — the contingency historical audit is deliverable semi-manually on structured formats in the first ~4–6 dev-weeks, so the founders are not staring down a 12-month pre-revenue build.

## TOP 3 TECHNICAL RISKS
- Ingestion/matching long tail: hundreds of carriers × evolving PDF layouts × inconsistent policy/producer keys means extraction and entity resolution are a perpetual grind, and accuracy directly determines whether flagged discrepancies are real or noise.
- Input-data availability: the comp grid and a clean, matchable policy book are the two required inputs, and both are messy, partly tribal, and not sitting in queryable systems — if they can't be captured reliably, "expected" is undefined.
- Carrier-portal dependency and credential handling: if the model drifts toward scraping portals behind MFA, you inherit ToS exposure, brittle automation, and a serious security liability (storing carrier logins) — must be deliberately bounded to human-pulled files in the MVP.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The biggest single risk is not the code — it's the data acquisition the code depends on, specifically the comp grid combined with a matchable policy book. The entire value proposition is "we re-compute what you were *owed* and compare it to what you were *paid*." That requires, per agency, a faithful machine-readable encoding of every relevant compensation rule (first-year, renewal/trail by policy year, override tiers, bonus thresholds, by carrier and product, effective-dated as schedules change) and a clean policy book whose keys actually join to the carrier's paid lines. In reality, comp terms are scattered across carrier contracts and addenda and live partly in the principal's memory, with genuine ambiguity about edge cases; and many small agencies' books are fragmented across carrier portals and a weak AMS with inconsistent identifiers. If encoding a single agency's grid takes days of expert interpretation and the policy book auto-joins at, say, 60%, the system either produces low-confidence noise (false "underpayments" that embarrass the agency with its carriers) or requires so much human reconciliation per client that the unit economics collapse into a labor-bound consultancy rather than a software-leveraged product. This is testable for near-zero cost before a line of engine code — encode 3 real agencies' top-5-carrier grids by hand and measure the auto-join rate on one month of statements — and that single spike should gate whether this is a software business or a services business.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- For a representative design-partner agency, how long does it take to fully encode their comp grid for their top 5 carriers, where does ambiguity arise, and how often do comp schedules change in ways that force re-encoding and historical recompute?
- When you take one agency's policy-book export and one cycle of real statements, what percentage of paid lines auto-join to the right policy + expected rule on available keys, and what does the residual require to resolve?
- What share of a target agency's commission dollars arrives in structured/EDI/DTCC-style formats vs. PDF-only vs. portal-only, and is structured-format coverage alone enough to surface collectible recoveries in phase 1?
- For the contingency wedge, when a real shortfall is documented and packaged, will carriers actually accept and remit — what is the realistic dispute hit rate and time-to-collection?
- Are you committing to keep carrier-portal credential storage and scraping out of scope (human-pulled files only) for the MVP, given the ToS and security liability — and if not, who owns that risk?

## RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The technology is feasible and well-shaped for a two-founder bootstrap, and the revenue-before-full-product structure is genuinely attractive — but two cheap, decisive spikes must run before committing engineering: (1) hand-encode 3 real agencies' top-5-carrier comp grids and measure effort + ambiguity, and (2) measure the policy-book-to-statement auto-join rate on real data, ideally alongside a manual spreadsheet-only historical audit to confirm collectible leakage actually exists at a meaningful rate. Scope-wise: build the deterministic ledger + comp-grid engine first (the provable spine), start ingestion with structured/EDI formats and the top 3–6 PDF templates the design partners actually receive (not broad coverage), keep portal scraping and carrier-credential storage out of the MVP, and run the contingency audit human-in-the-loop so a low-confidence extraction is corrected rather than shipped as a false dispute. If the two data spikes come back favorable (grids encodable in hours not days, auto-join in the 80%+ range, real collectible dollars), this converts to a confident GO as a software-leveraged product; if they come back poorly, it's a labor-bound services business wearing a SaaS costume, and the model needs rethinking before code is written.
