# COO REVIEW — Carrier Commission Reconciliation & Recovery Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is a remote-first, software-plus-light-services operation with no field component whatsoever — no trucks, no installs, no on-site visits — which puts it squarely inside what two versatile, software-capable founders can actually run. The repeated unit of work is data: ingest carrier commission statements, encode each agency's comp grid once, re-compute expected pay, reconcile against actual, and assemble a dispute package. The genuine operational hazard is not delivery scale — it's the *onboarding cliff*: building a reliable parser/normalizer across dozens of incompatible carrier statement formats and accurately encoding each agency's contractual comp terms is real, judgment-heavy human labor that does not self-serve at the start, and it is front-loaded onto exactly the two people who also have to sell and build. The business is operable by two founders plus 2–3 hires, but only if the founders ruthlessly narrow the initial carrier set, treat the comp-grid build as a structured intake (not bespoke consulting), and let recurring-cycle reconciliation — which IS highly automatable once the data pipeline exists — become the scalable spine.

## THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
It is a Tuesday in March, ~12-month mark. The business has ~18 agencies live on recurring reconciliation [ASSUMED] plus 2 historical-audit onboardings in flight. Founder A (operations/product) starts with the cycle queue: 11 of the 18 agencies receive carrier statements monthly, and a batch of February-cycle statements landed over the weekend — Lincoln Financial, Nationwide, a couple of aggregator feeds, two BGA carrier-portal CSV exports, and three PDFs from smaller annuity carriers. The ingestion pipeline auto-parsed ~80% of the lines cleanly. The other 20% kicked out to an exceptions queue: a carrier silently changed its PDF column layout in February, a renewal block came through with policy numbers that don't match the agency's book-of-record IDs, and one override statement is missing the bonus-tier breakout. Founder A spends ~90 minutes triaging exceptions, mapping the changed PDF template (a one-time fix that auto-applies going forward), and flagging the ID-mismatch policies for the agency to confirm.

By mid-morning, the reconciliation engine produced this cycle's variance reports for the 11 agencies. Most are clean. But three are interesting: one shows ~$14K of renewal commissions that stopped paying on a block of policies still in-force (a classic carrier "trail just quietly fell off the statement"), another shows a wrongful chargeback of ~$6K on a policy the agency can prove didn't lapse, and a third shows an override mis-tiered one band low because the carrier didn't credit a production threshold the agency crossed. The Recovery Specialist (first hire, ~$60–80K, started month 7) assembles the documented recovery packages — the contractual comp-grid entitlement, policy-level evidence, the specific statement lines — into the format each carrier's commission-accounting team will accept, and submits through the carrier's broker-comp channel. She also chases two recovery claims from last cycle; carrier commission departments are slow, so follow-through cadence is the actual work — persistence, not genius.

Meanwhile Founder B (GTM/build) is half on a new-agency onboarding and half on sales. The onboarding agency placed business across 22 carriers; the team prioritized the top 9 carriers (~85% of the agency's commission dollars) and is encoding those comp grids first — reading the actual carrier-agency agreements, override schedules, bonus addenda, and entering terms. This is the labor-intensive step: ~15–25 hours of structured intake per agency [ASSUMED], part judgment and part data entry that a trained Onboarding Analyst (second hire, ~$50–65K) handles under review. Founder B fields two support pings, runs one sales demo with a $12M-commission BGA, and reviews the analyst's comp-grid entries before they go live (a wrong comp grid produces wrong variances and erodes trust fast).

Where it breaks on a bad Tuesday: a carrier reformats its statement and the parser silently mis-maps a column, so a batch of "underpayments" are actually parsing artifacts — if that reaches an agency as a recovery claim, the agency submits garbage to its carrier and the vendor's credibility takes the hit. That is why the exceptions queue and a human QC gate on first-of-cycle outputs per carrier are non-negotiable. The second break point is recovery-claim limbo: a carrier slow-walks a claim, the contingency fee doesn't convert, and the founders eat the onboarding labor with no payday on that line. Neither is fatal, but both are why this is "software-plus-service," not pure SaaS, in year one.

## CAPACITY MATH
**Target.** mo24 binding test: ~$1.0–1.2M of founder income, which on a lean services-light SaaS at ~45–55% owner margin implies roughly **$2.2–2.6M annual revenue** by mo24. Two streams: (A) one-time contingency fees on historical audits, (B) recurring subscription.

**Unit inputs [ASSUMED — testable, ranged]:** target agency ~$6M commission revenue; conservative 2–4% of one year's commissions recovered = $120K–$240K found, say ~$150K recovered; contingency 25–30% = ~$40–45K one-time per agency; recurring ~$1,500–$4,000/mo, model ~$2,500/mo = $30K/yr.

**How many agencies to hit ~$2.4M by mo24:** ~50–70 recurring agencies = $1.5–2.1M recurring ARR, plus $1.5–2.5M of historical-audit contingency across the ramp. Blended, **~55–65 live agencies** clears ~$2.2–2.6M. Even halving the recurring price, ~90–110 agencies gets there — still a *small-account-count* business. Two-founders-plus-a-handful tractable.

**Recurring-cycle delivery load (scalable spine):** 60 live agencies, ~70% monthly → ~52 cycle-events/month ≈ 12/week. Per event, automation does the bulk; human exception-handling + variance QC ~1.5–3.0 hrs/event [ASSUMED] once parsers mature → ~27 hrs/week ≈ **0.7 FTE**. Recovery-claim assembly + chase: ~4–5 actions/week × ~1.5 hrs ≈ **0.3 FTE**. **Steady-state delivery ≈ ~1.0–1.3 FTE for 60 agencies.** The recurring spine does NOT scale linearly with headcount.

**Onboarding load (the bottleneck):** ~20–35 hours/agency [ASSUMED — biggest uncertainty], front-loaded. ~3–4 onboardings/month → ~98 hrs/month ≈ **0.6 FTE** plus founder QC. This is where the people go in year one — hires #1–#2 are Onboarding/Recovery Analysts, not engineers.

**Staffing conclusion:** by mo24, **2 founders + Recovery Specialist (mo7) + Onboarding Analyst (mo10–12) + a second Analyst/QC (mo18–20)** — a **~5-person team** runs ~60 agencies generating ~$2.4M. The math closes. The carrier-format long tail is the variable that can inflate onboarding hours; mitigate by constraining the initial carrier universe to the ~15–20 carriers that dominate small-BGA/IMO volume.

## SCORES (1–10)
1. Delivery clarity & repeatability: **8** — Once the comp grid is encoded, each cycle is a standardized ingest→re-compute→reconcile→flag→package pipeline that repeats identically; the variance is in statement formats, not delivery logic, and format handling is a bounded accumulating asset.
2. Supply chain resilience: **7** — "Supply chain" is inbound carrier statements (the agency already receives these) plus commodity cloud/OCR infra; the one real dependency is carrier statement *format stability*, which carriers change unilaterally — a recurring maintenance tax, not a clean software supply chain.
3. Logistics tractability: **10** — Zero physical logistics: no routing, trucks, install windows, regional density, or travel; statements arrive digitally, recovery packages submitted digitally — a fully remote operation servable from anywhere.
4. Customer-ops scalability: **7** — Steady-state is low-touch (~1.0–1.3 FTE serves 60 accounts), but *onboarding* is genuinely high-touch (~20–35 hrs/agency) — scalable on the recurring layer, labor-heavy on the front end; a fundable shape but not self-serve in year one.
5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: **7** — Cloud/OCR/LLM are replaceable commodities; no marketplace or channel chokepoint; the soft dependency is the absence of carrier *cooperation* (carriers are the counterparty being audited), which affects recovery speed but cannot shut the product off.
6. Hiring feasibility: **8** — Needed roles are detail-oriented, insurance-comp-literate finance/ops analysts (not scarce specialists), available remotely (and offshore) at ~$50–80K [ASSUMED]; founders' insurance/finance background covers the judgment layer.
7. Throughput capacity at target: **8** — ~$2.2–2.6M at mo24 needs only ~55–65 live agencies served by a ~5-person team, with the recurring spine NOT scaling linearly with headcount — small-account-count, high-revenue-per-account.
8. Quality control simplicity: **6** — The softest axis: a wrong comp-grid entry or mis-mapped column produces *wrong variances*, and a bad recovery claim to a carrier damages credibility directly — QC is mandatory (human review on first-of-cycle outputs per carrier, comp-grid sign-off); tractable but real recurring labor and where a sloppy operator gets burned.
9. Geographic / seasonality risk: **9** — National from day one, no regional concentration, no weather exposure; commission cycles run monthly year-round (mild year-boundary bonus-tier true-up uptick is a *feature* — more to find).
10. Tooling maturity available: **6** — The reconciliation/ledger/portal layer is buildable with standard stacks, but the core differentiator — robust ingestion/normalization across dozens of non-standard carrier formats — is NOT off-the-shelf; document-AI/OCR + LLM gets most of the way, yet the parser/exception library must be built and continually maintained (it becomes the moat over time).

**AVERAGE SCORE: 7.6 / 10**

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- **No field operations, ever** — fully remote, digital-in/digital-out; logistics is a non-issue (10) and the "not a field crew" founder constraint is satisfied cleanly.
- **High revenue per account → tiny account count** — ~55–65 agencies clears the mo24 ladder, so the business serves *dozens*, not thousands, and the recurring delivery spine runs on ~1.0–1.3 FTE.
- **Land-and-expand wedge that pays for its own onboarding** — the contingency historical audit (~$40K one-time/agency) funds the labor-heavy onboarding before the recurring subscription begins.

## TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- **Carrier-format long tail / parser maintenance tax** — every new carrier (and every silent reformat) is incremental parser + exceptions work that can inflate onboarding hours and create wrong-variance QC events.
- **Onboarding is the hidden field-ops-equivalent** — the ~20–35 hr/agency comp-grid build is the labor that scales with growth; underestimate it and the founders' time is consumed by intake instead of sales/build.
- **Recovery-claim conversion risk** — carriers are slow, self-interested counterparties; if claims stall or get disputed, the contingency revenue (and the "we got you money" proof point) lags the work performed.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to hurt this is the **carrier-statement ingestion-and-encoding burden colliding with the QC requirement** — normalizing dozens of incompatible carrier formats and accurately encoding each agency's comp grid, while guaranteeing the outputs are clean enough to send to a carrier. This is the one part of the model that does *not* automate cleanly at the start: a midsize agency spans 20+ carriers, each with its own statement layout and contractual override/bonus schedule, and a single wrong grid entry or mis-mapped PDF column converts the product from "trusted revenue-assurance layer" into "the vendor that sent us a bogus claim our carrier rejected." If the founders treat onboarding as bespoke consulting, per-agency hours balloon, the human QC gate becomes a permanent founder bottleneck, and growth throttles to whatever the two of them can personally review — the trap that turns a clean SaaS thesis into a services treadmill. Manageable, not fatal: bounded by aggressively narrowing the initial carrier universe to the ~15–20 highest-volume carriers, productizing the comp-grid build as a structured intake with templates and analyst-plus-review, and accumulating the parser library as a reusable asset. But it must be designed for deliberately on day one.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What is the real per-agency onboarding labor, in hours, for a representative 15–20-carrier agency, split into (a) statement loading, (b) comp-grid encoding from actual contracts, (c) net-new parser coverage? (Cheap test: hand-onboard ONE friendly agency end-to-end and stopwatch every step.)
- How concentrated is small-BGA/IMO commission volume across carriers — do the top ~15–20 carriers cover ~80–90% of dollars, so a bounded parser library serves most of the market?
- What is the actual recovery-claim cycle time and win rate per carrier — time from submitted package to paid, and what fraction of flagged underpayments carriers actually pay vs. dispute?
- How fragile are the carrier parsers to format changes — how often do top carriers reformat, and what is the remap effort?
- What is the QC failure mode and gate — what human review stands between an automated variance and a recovery claim leaving the building, and how many minutes per cycle per carrier?

## RECOMMENDATION: GO
Operationally this is a strong fit and a genuinely tractable two-founder business: fully remote, zero field/logistics burden, small account count at high revenue per account (~55–65 agencies clears the mo24 ladder), and a recurring spine that scales on roughly one FTE per several dozen accounts rather than linearly with headcount. The one discipline that must be locked in before scaling — and the reason this is GO rather than an unqualified rave — is the **front-end ingestion/comp-grid build and its QC gate**: productize it as a bounded, templated, narrow-carrier intake with analyst-plus-review (not open-ended consulting), and treat the parser library as a reusable accumulating asset. Validate per-agency onboarding hours, carrier-volume concentration, and recovery-claim time-to-pay/win-rate with one or two hand-run pilots before hiring ahead of revenue, and the operating plan closes cleanly inside a ~5-person, two-founder-led team.
