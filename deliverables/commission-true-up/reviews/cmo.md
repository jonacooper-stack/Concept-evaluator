# CMO REVIEW — Carrier Commission Reconciliation & Recovery Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This can be sold, positioned, and distributed — and the demand thesis is unusually well-evidenced for concept stage. The pain is real, recurring, dollar-denominated, and already being publicly quantified by the incumbents themselves: AgencyBloc cites a single agency uncovering $40,000 in missing compensation from two carriers and another recovering $69,000 in 18 months; unLocked CRM claims $10,000–$16,000/year recovered for agents with 300+ policies; BrokerageAudit (a P&C-leaning competitor) markets ~$18,500 recovered per $1M GWP in the first retrospective audit cycle. The category is real but the air is in the angle: nearly every named player sells software-first, DIY-detection ("run the Commission Not Received report yourself"), while the founder's wedge — a done-for-you, success-fee historical recovery that converts to a subscription — is a sharper, lower-friction GTM than anything I can find packaged for the life/annuity BGA-IMO channel specifically. The risk is not "is there demand" (there clearly is); it's that this is a known, named software category, so the differentiation has to live in the service-led recovery motion and the depth of life/annuity comp-grid modeling, not in "we reconcile statements," which a dozen tools already claim.

## POSITIONING DRAFT
For the agency principal: *"We find the renewal, override and bonus money your carriers quietly owe you and never paid — recover it on a success fee, then make sure it never goes uncollected again."* That sentence has edges because it leads with found money at zero upfront cost (the contingency wedge) and names the specific leakage types (renewals/trails, overrides, bonus tiers, wrongful chargebacks) rather than generic "commission management." It is NOT mush, and it is differentiable from the incumbent positioning, which is almost uniformly "commission processing/tracking software" (AgencyBloc, Core Commissions, ComTrack) or "carrier-side compensation" (Vertafore Sircon). The one caution: "reconciliation software" as a phrase IS crowded, so the brand must lead with recovery and outcome ("we get the money back"), not with the tooling, or it disappears into the AgencyBloc/Core/ComTrack pack.

## THE WEDGE
The first 100 customers come from a service-led, contingency-fee historical audit sold through the life/annuity intermediary channel — and that channel is unusually concentrated and reachable. NAILBA (now under Finseca) gathers 800+ independent life brokerage agency principals and senior management at its annual meeting; LIMRA-NAILBA's "Inside the Intermediary" study series is the named research spine of this exact buyer segment. Specific tactics: (1) Sponsor/attend NAILBA and regional IMO/FMO events and offer a free "leakage assessment" on one month of carrier statements — "send us last month's statements from your three biggest carriers and we'll show you what you're owed" — which converts because the prospect risks nothing and the output is a dollar figure [ASSUMED close rate 10–25% on a free-audit-to-paid-recovery funnel; cheap test: run 20 free assessments and measure conversion]. (2) Outbound to agency principals/controllers on LinkedIn and via the NAILBA member directory with a single-number hook ("agencies your size are recovering $15K–$70K per carrier — you pay only from what we find"). (3) Content/SEO on the long-tail the incumbents under-serve: "carrier renewal commission underpayment," "annuity trail commission not paid," "wrongful chargeback dispute" — BrokerageAudit is already ranking on the P&C version, which proves search demand exists and life/annuity terms are comparatively open. Estimated blended CAC for a service-led close: [ASSUMED $1,500–$5,000 fully loaded including event + founder sales time per closed agency; cheap test: track cost-per-closed-deal across the first 15 logos]. The contingency model makes the wedge nearly frictionless because the buyer's first dollar of risk is zero — the single best feature of this GTM.

## COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- **AgencyBloc (Commissions+)** — best at: the integrated life/health agency AMS + commissions module; explicitly markets "identify missed commissions" with real recovered-dollar case studies ($40K, $69K). Pricing ~$59–$109/user/mo plus a Commissions+ add-on. The most direct positioning threat because it already owns "find missed commissions" for life/health agencies — but it sells software you operate, not done-for-you recovery.
- **Core Commissions** — best at: a deep rules/splits engine for complex hierarchies; statement consolidation. Pricing $69/mo (Core) / $149/mo (Pro). Strong on calculation, light on the recovery service and historical-audit wedge.
- **ComTrack** — best at: life & health-specific tracking with AI detection of missing renewal commissions and "clients who fell off the books" month-by-month. Closest in feature intent to the renewal/trail leakage thesis; still a self-serve tool, not a recovery service.
- **unLocked CRM** — best at: marketing the underpayment-detection narrative loudly (332 carrier feeds, "$10K–$16K recovered/year"); competing for the same search and message.
- **Vertafore Sircon Compensation** — best at: carrier-SIDE compensation processing (serves carriers, not agencies) — a substitute on the supply side, not a direct competitor for the agency buyer.
- **eNoah iSolutions** — best at: on/offshore BPO commission processing and reconciliation specifically for FMO/IMO/BGA — the closest analog to a SERVICE model and the most underrated competitor; if they package contingency recovery, they have the labor and the channel.
- **BrokerageAudit** — best at: the recovery/audit narrative, but P&C/GWP-framed; proves recovery-as-positioning works and the SEO demand is real, while leaving the life/annuity comp-grid complexity comparatively open.

Where the air is: nobody I can find packages a done-for-you, success-fee historical recovery → subscription motion aimed squarely at the life/annuity BGA-IMO comp-grid. The adjacent proof that this MODEL works is in healthcare (PMMC, MD Clarity, R1 RCM run contingency-only underpayment recovery for hospitals; the Medicare/Medicaid RAC program is a contingency-fee recovery regime by statute). The concept is essentially porting a proven recovery-service model into a fragmented, sleepily-tooled insurance niche. Where it is genuinely different: service-led (not software-handed-over), outcome-priced entry, and life/annuity comp-grid depth versus the P&C-or-generic incumbents.

## TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- The pain is pre-quantified by the competitors and trade research themselves (AgencyBloc $40K/$69K; unLocked $10–16K/yr; BrokerageAudit ~$18.5K per $1M GWP), so the founders never have to convince a principal the problem exists — only that they recover more of it.
- A near-frictionless, zero-upfront-risk wedge: the contingency "we get paid only from what we find" entry is a proven recovery model in healthcare being ported into a sleepily-tooled insurance niche, and it converts to durable subscription once the data/grid are loaded.
- A concentrated, reachable, founder-fit channel (NAILBA/Finseca 800+ principals, LIMRA-NAILBA universe, LinkedIn principal outbound) where word-of-mouth on recovered dollars compounds — fitting the founders' marketing/positioning + software strengths with no field ops.

## TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- This is a NAMED, populated software category — AgencyBloc Commissions+, Core Commissions, ComTrack, unLocked CRM, Vertafore Sircon, eNoah — so "we reconcile carrier statements" is undifferentiated; the entire edge rests on the recovery-service motion and life/annuity grid depth, which must be defended hard in messaging.
- The most natural competitive response is a fast follow: AgencyBloc already markets "identify missed commissions," and eNoah already runs FMO/IMO commission BPO at scale — either could package a contingency recovery offer, and they have the channel and labor.
- The contingency wedge can train the market to view the recurring subscription as optional ("you already found the money, why pay monthly?"); if the ongoing-monitoring value isn't proven cycle-by-cycle, the durable revenue erodes after the one-time recovery sugar high.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely marketing/competitive killer is differentiation collapse — being filed by the buyer as "another commission reconciliation tool" alongside AgencyBloc Commissions+, Core Commissions, ComTrack and unLocked CRM, all of which already advertise "find missed commissions" with their own recovered-dollar case studies. The capability the concept describes (ingest statements across formats, encode the comp grid, re-derive expected pay, flag deltas) is exactly what these named tools claim, at $59–$149/mo, often bundled into an AMS the agency already owns. If the founders lead with the software ("a reconciliation platform"), they enter a crowded category against an incumbent (AgencyBloc) that owns the life/health agency relationship and can cross-sell the module for the price of a checkbox. The concept only escapes this if it is sold as a recovery OUTCOME on a success fee — a fundamentally different purchase (a service that hands you a check, not a tool you operate) — and if the life/annuity comp-grid depth (renewals, trails, multi-tier overrides and bonuses, wrongful chargebacks) is visibly deeper than the P&C-or-generic competitors. The whole thesis hinges on that positioning discipline; lose it, and the wedge's frictionlessness is wasted because the prospect mentally substitutes a $79/mo module.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What is the real, validated leakage rate and recovery yield in life/annuity specifically (not the P&C/GWP ~$18.5K figure)? Run 15–25 free historical assessments on real BGA/IMO statements and produce a hard distribution of "dollars found per $1M of commission revenue" and "share of found dollars actually recoverable from the carrier" — this number is the entire pitch and is currently [ESTIMATE 2–8%].
- Will carriers actually pay the recovery claims, and how contentious is the dispute process? The money being "contractually owed" does not mean it's collected without friction; the founders need carrier turnaround, denial rate, and whether pursuing it strains the agency's carrier appointments (a principal will not jeopardize appointments for a $20K claim) — talk to 5–10 principals and 2–3 ex-carrier comp-ops people.
- What stops AgencyBloc or eNoah from launching the same contingency-recovery offer within two quarters, and what is the defensible compounding asset (proprietary multi-carrier grid/exception library, integration depth, recovered-case brand in the NAILBA channel)? Name the specific moat beyond "we got here first."
- Is the recurring subscription genuinely sticky after the one-time recovery, or does value evaporate once the back-money is collected? Define and price the ongoing per-cycle value (caught underpayments per month, prevented chargebacks) and test whether principals keep paying after the historical windfall — otherwise this is a one-time audit business wearing a SaaS costume.

## RECOMMENDATION: GO
The demand is documented in dollars by the competitors themselves, the buyer is specific and concentrated (NAILBA/Finseca 800+ principals; LIMRA-NAILBA universe), the wedge is a proven contingency-recovery model ported from healthcare into a sleepily-tooled niche, and the motion (positioning-led + founder close, no field ops) fits the founders precisely — this is a genuinely sellable, distributable concept, not a vitamin. It is a GO rather than a no-go because the forcing function is the agency's own revenue and the entry is zero-risk to the buyer. The one non-negotiable condition is positioning discipline: sell the recovery OUTCOME on a success fee and the life/annuity comp-grid depth, NOT "reconciliation software," or it dissolves into the AgencyBloc/Core/ComTrack pack at $79/mo. Lead with found money, prove the life/annuity yield with 15–25 free assessments, nail down whether carriers pay claims cleanly, and name the compounding moat before a funded incumbent copies the offer.
