# CTO REVIEW — Welfare-Plan Compliance Documents & Testing Service

## ONE-PARAGRAPH TECHNICAL READ
This is a fundamentally tractable build for a two-founder bootstrap with one developer: at its core it is a guided-intake form that drives document assembly (templated wrap SPD and §125 POP), a deterministic rules engine for nondiscrimination testing (these are arithmetic ratio tests, not ML), a structured-data preparer for the welfare Form 5500, and a multi-tenant broker/TPA portal with a document vault and a deadline calendar. None of the hard parts are research problems — the genuinely difficult work is regulatory correctness of the templates and test logic (a legal/SME problem, not an engineering one) and the one real integration with the DOL's EFAST2/IFILE system for Form 5500 submission. The architecture is boring CRUD-plus-rules-plus-PDF, which is exactly what you want for a bootstrap; the chief risks are the accuracy/liability of the generated artifacts, the EFAST2 filing mechanics, and the fact that this handles employee compensation/HCE data, which makes information security a day-one obligation rather than a later concern.

## ARCHITECTURE SKETCH
- **Frontend:** a single multi-tenant web app — a thin React/Next.js (or Remix) SPA with two persona surfaces: a broker/TPA console that lists many employer clients, and a lighter employer-facing surface for intake, e-signature, and document distribution. Off-the-shelf component library; no exotic UI.
- **Backend:** a conventional application server (Node/TypeScript, Python/Django, or Rails — pick the founder's strongest). Core services: (1) guided-intake/questionnaire engine, (2) document-generation service, (3) nondiscrimination test engine, (4) 5500 preparation/validation service, (5) calendar/reminder scheduler.
- **Data store:** a single relational database (Postgres) with row-level tenant isolation. Schema is naturally relational — agencies, employers, plans, plan-years, participants/census rows, documents, filings, test runs, audit events. Plus object storage (S3 or equivalent) for generated/executed PDFs and uploaded census files.
- **Document generation:** templated assembly. The wrap SPD and §125 POP are well-structured documents with conditional clauses driven by intake answers. Use a templating approach (DOCX templates via docxtemplater, or HTML-to-PDF via a headless renderer / DocRaptor) plus a clause library. The largest custom-content effort, but it is content authoring, not algorithmic difficulty.
- **Nondiscrimination testing:** a deterministic rules engine. §125 eligibility/contributions/benefits tests, §105(h) eligibility and benefits tests, §129 55%/concentration/eligibility tests, §79 — all defined ratios and counts over a census with HCE/key-employee classification. Spreadsheet math expressed in code; the difficulty is encoding the rules correctly and keeping them current.
- **Form 5500:** structured-data capture mapping to the official schedules (5500 + Schedule A + Schedule C), generation of the machine-readable filing payload, validation against published EFAST2 edit checks, and submission via EFAST2/IFILE. The one external system on the critical path. [ASSUMED — verify by reading the DOL EFAST2 Web Services / IFILE developer documentation, ~1–2 days] direct programmatic submission is supported but is the gated piece; a v1 fallback is to generate a fully-validated filing package the broker uploads through IFILE manually.
- **Async/background:** a job runner (SQS/BullMQ, or cron + worker) for the deadline calendar, reminders, email/notification dispatch, batch test runs, and PDF rendering. Low volume, low complexity.
- **E-signature:** integrate a vendor (Dropbox Sign/DocuSign/SignWell) rather than build; capture proof-of-distribution as audit records.
- **ML/AI:** not required for the core. Optional contained uses (LLM to make intake plain-language or draft correction-guidance prose), but the legally-load-bearing outputs must come from deterministic templates and rules, never a generative model.
- **Auth/hosting:** managed auth (Auth0/Clerk/Cognito) with org-scoped roles (agency admin, agency user, employer user); hosted on a mainstream PaaS/cloud. Nothing demands bespoke infrastructure.

## BUILD PLAN TO REVENUE-EARNING MVP
Total estimate: roughly **16–24 weeks of one developer's effort** to a version a broker would pay for, with the regulatory content authoring running in parallel and being the true critical path (it needs an ERISA SME). The smallest revenue-earning slice is documents + testing + calendar, sold through one or two friendly brokers; full automated 5500 e-filing can follow.
1. **Multi-tenant foundation + intake engine (~4–5 weeks).** Auth, agency/employer/plan data model with strict tenant isolation, the guided questionnaire, census upload and parsing. Do it cleanly because retrofitting tenant isolation later is painful.
2. **Document generation: wrap SPD + §125 POP (~4–6 weeks dev, plus parallel SME-authored content).** Template engine, clause library wired to intake answers, PDF output, e-signature integration, distribution tracking, audit-ready vault. Content portion is the gating dependency.
3. **Nondiscrimination testing engine (~3–5 weeks).** HCE/key-employee determination, the §125/§105(h)/§129/§79 tests as deterministic functions, dated/audit-ready output, plain-language correction guidance. Heavily test-covered with SME-validated worked examples.
4. **Compliance calendar + reminders (~2 weeks).** Per-plan-year deadline derivation, scheduler, dispatch, renewal/refresh prompts. Low risk, high perceived value to brokers.
5. **Form 5500 preparation + filing (~3–6 weeks; PUNT automated submission to v2).** Structured capture, schedule mapping, EFAST2 edit-check validation, filing-package generation. v1 ships "validated package + assisted manual IFILE upload"; v2 adds direct EFAST2 web-services submission. De-risks the one external integration without blocking revenue.
**Punt to v2:** direct EFAST2 programmatic submission; §79 and §129 if early customers are §125/§105(h)-driven; deep analytics; employer self-serve onboarding (start white-glove).

## CRITICAL ASSUMPTIONS
1. The legally-load-bearing artifacts can be produced deterministically from templates and rules, with no generative-model reliance. Verify: have the ERISA SME hand-author outputs for 8–10 representatively varied real plans and confirm they decompose into a finite, parameterized clause set — ~1 week SME time.
2. Direct or assisted Form 5500 filing through EFAST2/IFILE is achievable by a third-party filer at the data level. Verify: read the DOL EFAST2 developer/web-services docs and schemas, confirm credential/registration requirements; ~1–2 days. Manual-upload fallback still ships a paying product.
3. Brokers/TPAs will distribute and white-label. Technically means the multi-tenant, agency-administers-many-clients model is the right shape — which it is — but raises the bar on tenant isolation, white-label theming, and per-agency branding from day one. Verify: 5–8 channel conversations.
4. The nondiscrimination test rules are stable enough to encode and maintain without a full-time compliance engineer. Verify: SME worked numeric examples per test + a change-log of how often thresholds move year to year; ~3–4 days. These tests are decades-old and slow-moving.
5. Census/HCE data can be ingested from the messy spreadsheets brokers actually have. Verify: collect 5–10 real (anonymized) census files and confirm a guided column-mapping importer handles them; ~3 days.

## INFORMATION SECURITY POSTURE
Data collected: employer/plan metadata, executed legal documents, Form 5500 filing data, and — most sensitively — employee census data including names, compensation, HCE/key-employee status, and dependent-care/benefit elections. This is PII plus compensation data; not regulated PHI in the clinical sense for most functions, but sensitive personnel and pay data that an enterprise buyer (and a broker reselling to one) will scrutinize, so "security later" is not acceptable. **Threat model:** multi-tenant with brokers administering many employers' workforce data, so the dominant risk is a tenant-isolation failure (one agency/employer seeing another's census/compensation), then credential compromise of a broker account that fans out across an entire book, then exfiltration of stored compensation data. **Controls needed at launch:** enforced TLS; encryption at rest for DB and vault (managed KMS); secrets in a managed store; strict row-level/tenant-scoped authorization in the data layer, not just UI; RBAC (agency admin/user, employer user) with least privilege; mandatory MFA at least for broker/agency accounts; comprehensive immutable audit logging (also a product feature — "audit-ready" — so it pays double); encrypted, tested backups; a written incident-response plan with breach-notification awareness. **SOC 2 implications:** brokers reselling into mid-market employers will ask for SOC 2 Type II in the first serious enterprise/large-broker conversations — highly attainable if controls are designed in from day one (use Vanta/Drata); budget 6–12 months of operating evidence for Type II. **At scale:** per-tenant segmentation hardening, key rotation, anomaly detection on cross-tenant access, data-retention/purge controls, vendor-security review of e-sign and filing integrations. None of this is exotic, but all of it is non-negotiable given compensation/PII at the center.

## TOP 3 TECHNICAL STRENGTHS
- The entire core is deterministic and proven: templated document assembly and ratio-based tests are arithmetic and conditionals, not ML or unsolved research — a small team can build and fully test it for correctness.
- Excellent build-vs-buy leverage: auth, e-signature, PDF rendering, email, and hosting are bought off the shelf; the custom surface is a thin, well-bounded wedge (clause library + test engine + 5500 mapping).
- Benign scaling and dependency profile: low-QPS, batch-oriented workload on a single Postgres + worker tier with thousands-of-plans headroom, and the one non-replaceable dependency (DOL EFAST2) is a free, stable government system.

## TOP 3 TECHNICAL RISKS
- Regulatory correctness of the generated artifacts: a wrong clause in a wrap SPD/POP or a miscomputed §105(h)/§125 test isn't a bug, it's potential client liability — the load-bearing content must be SME-authored and exhaustively validated, and that content is the true critical path, not the code.
- Form 5500 / EFAST2 filing mechanics: the one external system on the path; if programmatic submission is gated for third-party filers, automated e-filing slips to a manual-upload workaround.
- Multi-tenant security of compensation/HCE PII: a tenant-isolation failure or compromised broker account exposes employees' pay data across an entire book — must be designed in from day one and will be SOC-2-gated by serious buyers.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single biggest risk is not engineering effort but **regulatory correctness liability in the load-bearing outputs**. The product's entire value proposition is that the wrap SPD, the §125 POP, the test results, and the Form 5500 are correct and audit-ready; a templating bug, an out-of-date clause, or a misencoded test threshold doesn't merely degrade UX, it can produce a defective legal document or a wrong tax determination that creates real, quantifiable harm for the employer (taxable benefits to HCEs, payroll-tax corrections, adverse audit findings, DOL penalties) and flows straight back to the founders as professional-liability exposure — amplified by the channel model, where a single bad template propagates across a broker's entire book at once. This is squarely a technical-process risk because the only defenses are engineering disciplines applied to compliance content: a versioned clause library and rules engine with an authoritative SME sign-off gate before any rule/template goes live, golden-file regression tests pinned to SME-validated worked examples, dated/immutable output records so any artifact can be reproduced exactly as generated, and a clear "we prepare, a qualified party reviews/signs" liability boundary baked into the workflow. If the founders treat the document and test logic as ordinary application code that "looks right," this becomes the thing that breaks the business after launch; if they treat it as regulated content with a sign-off pipeline and regression coverage, it is entirely manageable.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- Form 5500 / EFAST2: have you confirmed, from the DOL's own EFAST2/IFILE developer documentation, whether a third-party software filer can submit programmatically (and under what registration/credentialing), or are you committing to a validated-package + manual-upload model for v1?
- Regulatory content ownership: who is the ERISA SME that authors and signs off on the wrap SPD/POP clause library and the test logic, and what is the explicit gate and cadence that keeps templates and thresholds current each plan year — a named advisor, a hire, or a content partner?
- Liability boundary: where does the platform's responsibility end and a qualified human's begin — generate-and-they-sign, or do you assert correctness yourself — and is that boundary enforced in the workflow and backed by professional/tech E&O insurance?
- Tenant isolation and access model: how is per-employer data isolation enforced at the data layer, how do you prevent a compromised broker account from fanning out across its client book, and is MFA mandatory for agency users from launch?
- Census ingestion reality: have you collected real, messy, anonymized census files from prospective broker partners to confirm a column-mapping importer handles them?

## RECOMMENDATION: GO
Technically this is a well-scoped, bootstrap-appropriate build with no research-grade unknowns: boring relational CRUD plus a deterministic rules/templating core plus one stable government integration, it scales easily for the workload, and the build-vs-buy posture is sound. The score is held off the top band by three honest, addressable facts — a ~16–24 dev-week build, a permanent (if modest) regulatory-content maintenance obligation, and an information-security bar that must be met on day one. I would proceed, with these treated as scope, not afterthoughts: (1) line up the ERISA SME and content sign-off pipeline before writing template/test code; (2) verify the EFAST2 third-party-filing path in the first two days and design Form 5500 with the manual-upload fallback; (3) build multi-tenant isolation, MFA, encryption-at-rest, and audit logging in from the first commit and instrument toward SOC 2; and (4) ship the documents + testing + calendar slice to one or two friendly brokers first, punting automated 5500 submission to v2. Done this way, a single developer plus a part-time SME can reach a revenue-earning MVP in roughly one to two quarters.
