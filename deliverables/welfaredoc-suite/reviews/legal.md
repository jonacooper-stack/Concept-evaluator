# LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Welfare-Plan Compliance Documents & Testing Service

*Note: I am not a licensed attorney and this is not formal legal advice. I am an experienced operator with strong regulatory pattern-recognition, raising the issues the founders must investigate with retained counsel — not resolving them.*

## ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
This concept sits squarely inside a well-mapped, decades-stable federal regime (ERISA Title I and Internal Revenue Code §§ 79, 105(h), 125, 129), administered by two known agencies (DOL/EBSA and the IRS) whose rules, forms, deadlines, and penalties are public, codified, and largely unchanging. The legal exposure here is almost entirely **professional/E&O liability for getting the document, the filing, or the test wrong** — not licensing, not jurisdictional patchwork, not industry entry barriers. There is no federal or state license required to prepare ERISA wrap documents, file a Form 5500, or run nondiscrimination tests; an entire incumbent industry (TASC, Newfront/wrap vendors, third-party administrators, payroll companies) already does exactly this without special credentials. The principal real risks are (1) the line between permissible document-preparation/recordkeeping and the **unauthorized practice of law (UPL)** if marketing strays into "legal advice," and (2) the **professional-liability tail** when a filing or test the platform produced is wrong and the client eats a penalty or a disqualified tax benefit — both of which are known, named, insurable, and contractually allocable.

## RISK-GATE RATING: **MINOR** (bordering on SERIOUS-BUT-MANAGEABLE on the professional-liability axis only)
This is a standard-business-law concept with no special licensing regime gating entry, a stable federal rulebook, and an incumbent field that already operates this exact model without credentials — so the gate is **MINOR**. The single axis that pulls toward "serious but manageable" is professional/E&O liability for erroneous filings and tests, which is real but is precisely the kind of exposure that named E&O carriers underwrite for benefits-compliance and TPA firms every day, and that contracts can further bound. Nothing here is FATAL: there is no unbridgeable credential, no uninsurable tail, and no legal prohibition. The forced, recurring demand that the underlying federal mandates create is a demand asset and is credited elsewhere, not penalized here.

## REGULATORY MAP
- **ERISA Title I — DOL/EBSA.** Requires sponsors of group health & welfare plans to maintain a written plan document and furnish an SPD to participants, and to file Form 5500 where the threshold is met. Vendor burden: LOW-to-MODERATE — obligations, forms, and instructions are public and codified (29 U.S.C. §§ 1021–1024; 29 C.F.R. § 2520). Penalties attach to the *employer/plan sponsor* as fiduciary, not to the document preparer.
- **IRC §§ 79, 105(h), 125, 129 — IRS / Treasury.** Nondiscrimination testing and the written §125 cafeteria/POP requirement that conditions favorable tax treatment. Vendor burden: MODERATE — the tests are deterministic, rules-based calculations well-suited to software; the risk is computational correctness and applying the right test to the right plan design, not regulatory ambiguity.
- **DFVCP (Delinquent Filer Voluntary Compliance Program).** The correction pathway when a 5500 was missed; relevant because "correction guidance on failure" steps near regulated remediation — route DFVCP filings as a documented process, not bespoke legal counsel.
- **EFAST2 (DOL electronic filing).** Form 5500 must be filed electronically; the constraint is *who signs* (the plan administrator/sponsor signs; the vendor prepares/transmits where authorized). Must be architected so the sponsor remains the signer/filer of record.
- **Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) — state bar regulators (all 50 states, variable).** Preparing legal documents and giving "compliance guidance" can be characterized as practicing law if the company holds itself out as advising on legal rights or tailors documents through individualized legal judgment. The genuinely cross-jurisdictional issue — MODERATE and **mitigable by design** (attorney-reviewed templates, self-help tools the customer drives, disclaimers, keeping individualized legal judgment with the customer/retained attorney).
- **Data privacy — CCPA/CPRA + growing state acts + ERISA/HIPAA boundary.** The platform stores employer/plan data and limited employee identifiers (compensation, ownership status, DOB for §79). Mostly employment/benefits administrative data, not PHI in the HIPAA-covered-entity sense for the *testing* function — but the boundary must be drawn carefully. Burden: LOW-to-MODERATE, within the founders' stated HIPAA/HITECH and GDPR/CCPA operating experience.
- **Channel/white-label law — FTC + standard commercial law.** Reselling through brokers/TPAs raises responsibility-allocation and advertising-substantiation questions; handled through channel agreements and clear allocation of "preparer of record." Burden: LOW.

## LICENSING SUMMARY
**No occupational or industry license is required to operate this business, and that should be stated confidently.** Preparing ERISA wrap/SPD and §125 POP documents, preparing and transmitting Form 5500 filings, and running §125/§105(h)/§129/§79 nondiscrimination tests are **not** licensed activities. There is no federal "benefits compliance preparer" license, no state license to prepare a Form 5500 (it is not a tax return requiring a PTIN/enrolled-agent status — Form 5500 is an information return, and TPAs/payroll firms/brokers prepare them routinely without enrolled-agent or attorney credentials), and no state license to generate a plan document via software. The existing market demonstrates the model operates license-free at scale. **[ASSUMED — HIGH confidence, ~95%]** that no special license is required; cheap test: a 2–3 hour memo from ERISA counsel confirming "document preparation, 5500 transmission, and NDT are non-licensed activities, and here is the UPL line." The only credential-adjacent consideration is not a license but a competence/sign-off posture: having a named ERISA attorney and/or credentialed benefits professional (e.g., CEBS / ERISA counsel) review the templates and testing logic, both for correctness and to anchor the UPL and E&O defense.

## LIABILITY POSTURE
The entity's exposure is **professional liability / errors-and-omissions**: if the wrap omits a required provision, the 5500 is filed late or wrong, or a nondiscrimination test produces a false "pass," the employer may incur DOL/IRS penalties, lose a tax benefit, or face fiduciary exposure — and may then look to the vendor. This is a known, named, insurable exposure: **E&O / professional-liability insurance** is routinely written for benefits-compliance firms and TPAs; **[ASSUMED]** annual premium ~**$3K–$25K** early-stage depending on revenue and limits — cheap test: two broker quotes (a tech-E&O carrier and a benefits/TPA-specialty carrier). Cyber/data-breach coverage layers on top. Contractually, exposure is bounded by a well-drafted MSA/EULA: **limitation of liability capped at fees paid, disclaimer of legal advice, an explicit "customer/their counsel is responsible for legal determinations and for signing/filing," accuracy-of-customer-inputs warranties (garbage-in/garbage-out), and indemnification flowing from customer data errors.** In the white-label/broker channel, the channel agreement must allocate who is the "preparer of record," who makes compliance representations to the employer, and back-to-back indemnities. **Personal founder exposure** is the ordinary pre-entity gap, closed the normal way (form the entity before any customer contract or filing; never let a founder personally sign as plan administrator or "preparer of record"). There is **no catastrophic, uninsurable liability tail** here (no bodily injury, no consumer-safety, no custody of large client funds).

## TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY STRENGTHS
- **Federal uniformity with named, stable regulators.** One rulebook (ERISA Title I + IRC §§ 79/105(h)/125/129 under DOL/EBSA and the IRS), no 50-state licensing patchwork, public forms and deadlines, decades of stability.
- **No license to enter.** Preparing wrap/SPD/POP documents, transmitting Form 5500, and running NDT are non-licensed activities an entire incumbent industry already performs credential-free.
- **Founder-fit on the compliance and channel posture.** The risks that exist (data handling, professional-grade process discipline, regulated-channel contracting) map directly onto the founders' SOC 2/HIPAA-HITECH/GDPR-CCPA and regulated-enterprise sales background.

## TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY RISKS
- **Professional/E&O liability for a wrong filing or test.** A late/erroneous 5500, a defective wrap, or a false-"pass" test can cost the employer real penalties or a disqualified tax benefit, and the vendor is the natural target — insurable and contract-bounded, but the live risk axis.
- **Unauthorized practice of law (UPL) characterization.** Marketing "compliance guidance" and generating "plan documents" can be cast as practicing law in some states; requires an attorney-reviewed-template + self-help-tool + disclaimer architecture from day one.
- **White-label channel liability allocation.** When a broker resells/embeds and makes compliance representations to the employer, an unclear "preparer of record" and weak channel indemnities can route a partner's error back to the vendor.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The most likely thing to seriously slow (not kill) this business is **the professional-liability/E&O tail colliding with the unauthorized-practice-of-law line** — and they are linked. The product's most defensible features (generating a "plan document," giving "correction guidance on failure," telling an employer they "passed" or "failed" a test) are exactly the activities a state bar could characterize as practicing law, *and* the activities most likely to produce an E&O claim if the output is wrong. The failure mode is concrete: a 200-employee self-insured employer relies on the platform's §105(h) test that erroneously reports a "pass," the IRS later finds discrimination on audit, the HCEs' benefits become taxable with payroll-tax corrections and interest, and the employer (or the broker who white-labeled it) sues — simultaneously, opposing counsel argues the company was practicing law without a license and so its limitation-of-liability and disclaimers are unenforceable. The mitigation is well-trodden and must be designed in before launch: position and build as **software/self-help with attorney-reviewed templates** where the customer (or their attorney) makes the legal determination and signs/files as the party of record; carry **E&O and cyber insurance** sized to the book; and paper every deal with a **limitation of liability capped at fees, an explicit legal-advice disclaimer, customer-input warranties, and (in the channel) back-to-back indemnities and a clear preparer-of-record definition**. Done this way, the exposure is ordinary professional-services risk that incumbents already carry.

## QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
- **UPL line:** Across the states we'll sell into, where does "generating plan documents and providing compliance/correction guidance via software" cross from permissible self-help/document-automation into UPL, and what product, disclaimer, and attorney-review architecture keeps us safe?
- **Preparer/filer of record & e-filing authority:** For EFAST2 transmission, who must legally sign and be the filer of record (plan administrator/sponsor vs. our platform), what authorization/credentialing is required to transmit on a client's behalf, and how do we structure "where authorized" e-filing so the sponsor retains signer responsibility?
- **E&O coverage and contractual liability cap:** What E&O policy form and limits actually cover erroneous wrap documents, 5500 filings, and NDT for a SaaS-plus-service ERISA vendor, and will a fees-capped limitation plus advice-disclaimer hold up given the regulated subject matter — including in the white-label channel?
- **HIPAA/privacy boundary:** Does storing the testing/document data (compensation, ownership status, DOB, any health-plan information) make us (or our brokers) a HIPAA business associate for any module, triggering BAAs and the Security Rule, or does it stay within ordinary employment/benefits-administration data under CCPA/CPRA?
- **Channel/white-label allocation:** How do we define "preparer of record," allocate responsibility for compliance representations to the employer, and structure back-to-back indemnities so a partner's error or overstatement does not flow to us?

## RECOMMENDATION: GO
This is a structurally low-legal-risk concept built on top of a high-value, federally-forced, recurring demand — close to the ideal posture this thesis wants: the regulation drives the must-have buy, while the *legal risk to the vendor* is ordinary professional-services risk with no license to enter, no jurisdictional patchwork (the mandate is federal), a stable-to-expanding regulatory trajectory, and no uninsurable or catastrophic tail. The only items that warrant genuine pre-launch diligence — the UPL line, the E&O/contractual-liability posture, the EFAST2 filer-of-record mechanics, and the channel liability allocation — are named, well-precedented, and squarely within the founders' wheelhouse, forming a due-diligence checklist rather than a barrier. I would proceed, retaining ERISA counsel early to (1) write the UPL/disclaimer-and-template architecture, (2) bless the testing logic and document templates for correctness, and (3) lock the MSA/channel agreements with capped liability and back-to-back indemnities before the first dollar of revenue.
