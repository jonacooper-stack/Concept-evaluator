# CFO REVIEW — Self-Serve CMMC / NIST 800-171 Compliance Software for Small Defense Subcontractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil unusually well for a bootstrap: this is a pure-software, freemium SaaS with near-zero COGS, no inventory, no field ops, credit-card billing that collects before costs are incurred, and a forced, recurring, already-budgeted buy (DFARS/SPRS today, CMMC L2 phasing in Nov 10, 2026). At $200–$600/mo against consultants and managed services that run $20K–$100K+/year, the value gap is enormous and pricing is defensible. The two real financial fragilities are (1) the freemium model's notoriously low free-to-paid conversion combined with a self-serve-only motion (no founder close) creating CAC/LTV uncertainty, and (2) a "self-attest, software does the work" thesis selling into a non-technical, high-anxiety, audit-fearing buyer who may demand human reassurance — which, if true, forces a services layer that compresses margin and breaks the headcount-light promise. Neither is fatal; both are testable cheaply before scale.

THE MATH
I'll reverse-engineer the founder-income ladder ($300K/founder ≈ $600K combined by mo12; $500K+/founder ≈ $1.0M+ combined by mo24), then sanity-check against the addressable market.

Pricing and ARPU. Published range $200–$600/mo. I'll model a blended ARPU. Most of this segment is the ~35-person shop (the stated typical customer), which skews toward the low-to-mid tier. Blended ARPU [ASSUMED] $300–$400/mo = $3,600–$4,800/yr. I'll use **$350/mo = $4,200 ARR/customer** as the working figure. Cheap test: post the three tiers on a landing page behind the free assessment and measure which tier real signups select.

Gross margin. Pure SaaS. COGS = cloud hosting + LLM inference (the AI guidance layer) + payment processing (Stripe ~2.9% + $0.30). LLM inference is the only non-trivial variable cost; at $200–$600/mo price points with bounded document/questionnaire volume per customer, inference is [ASSUMED] $5–$25/customer/mo. Net gross margin [ASSUMED] **85–92%**. I'll use **88%**. This is consistent with public vertical-SaaS comparables — Vanta and Drata operate freemium/self-serve GRC at SaaS gross margins north of 80%. Cheap test: instrument token usage per customer in beta and divide by price.

To $600K combined ARR (mo12 ladder rung):
$600,000 ÷ $4,200 ARR = **~143 paying customers.** At 88% margin that's ~$528K gross profit; against a two-founder, no-new-headcount cost base (their own comp + tools + hosting + marketing spend, call it [ASSUMED] $150K–$250K all-in), the residual to founders plausibly clears the ~$600K *income* rung only if the cost base stays lean. Stated more carefully: 143 customers generates ~$528K gross profit; the ladder's "$300K/founder income" is reachable at mo12 only if combined operating costs (incl. paid acquisition) stay roughly ≤$0 net after that — i.e., the income rung at mo12 is **tight**, gated by how much paid marketing is needed. More realistic: ~143 customers is a mo12 *revenue* milestone; the $300K/founder *income* rung lands closer to mo15–18 once acquisition spend normalizes. I score the ladder on plausibility, not on a guarantee of exact-month timing.

To $1.0M+ combined ARR (mo24 rung):
$1,000,000 ÷ $4,200 = **~238 paying customers.** At 88% margin = ~$880K gross profit. With a still-lean cost base, $500K/founder by mo24 is plausible. To exceed it (the "growing" requirement), the levers are obvious: ARPU lift via the questionnaire-auto-fill and re-assessment tiers, plus the Nov 2026 CMMC phase-in pulling the whole segment into forced buying right inside the mo12–24 window. That timing tailwind is real and material.

Customer-acquisition feasibility — does 143→238 fit the funnel? Freemium math:
- Free-to-paid conversion for self-serve freemium [ASSUMED] **2–5%** (typical published PLG benchmark; Vanta/Drata-style compliance freemium may run higher because the free assessment surfaces an acute, scary gap and the paid product is the remedy — call the optimistic case 5–8%, but I'll model conservatively at 3%).
- To net 143 payers at 3% conversion you need ~4,800 free assessments completed in year one (~400/mo); at 5% you need ~2,860 (~240/mo). Both are achievable via SEO/content + APEX Accelerator / MEP channel partners against a segment of [ASSUMED] 60,000–220,000 small DIB subs touching CUI (DoD has cited a CMMC-affected base on the order of ~80K Level 2 entities; the small-sub layer is a large share of that). Cheap test: stand up the free assessment now, drive a small paid + content cohort, measure assessment-completion and free→paid conversion before committing to scale.

Market sanity. Even at a conservative 80,000 affected entities and capturing **0.3%** (238 customers) by mo24, the founders barely scratch the market — exactly the "large enough that two founders can't exhaust it" condition. SOM is not the constraint; conversion and trust are.

Bottom line on the math: revenue model is clean and high-margin; the ladder is reachable with low-hundreds of customers; the binding variables are free→paid conversion and whether self-serve alone converts a non-technical buyer.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Structurally negative working capital + ~88% gross margin + zero capital intensity: customers pay in advance by credit card, COGS is thin software/inference, and there is nothing to inventory, install, or finance — about as cash-efficient a bootstrap shape as exists.
- Enormous, defensible value-to-price gap into a forced buy: $2,400–$7,200/yr priced against $20K–$100K+ consultants/managed services for an obligation the customer must satisfy to keep purchase orders — pricing power is real and the segment already has the budget line.
- Demand tailwind with a date on it: the Nov 10, 2026 CMMC Level 2 phase-in pulls the entire small-DIB segment into mandatory, recurring buying squarely within the mo12–24 income window, and the recurring re-assessment/upkeep design makes the revenue sticky after the initial gap-close.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Free-to-paid conversion is the whole P&L and is unproven: freemium typically converts 2–5%; the free assessment is a great magnet, but if the "doing" tier under-converts (owners take the free score and hire a person anyway, or stall), the customer-count math stretches and the ladder slips.
- The self-serve-only, no-credential thesis may collide with buyer psychology: a non-technical owner personally liable for compliance, facing a real assessment, may refuse to "self-attest" off software alone and demand human review — forcing a services/assisted tier that adds headcount and compresses the 88% margin the whole model depends on.
- Single-regime top-line concentration: 100% of revenue depends on DFARS/CMMC enforcement and timing; a phase-in delay, a softening of SPRS-score eligibility consequences, or a competitor bundling CMMC free into an existing platform would hit revenue directly.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely financial killer is a conversion-and-trust failure at the free→paid boundary that quietly breaks the unit economics. The model's entire attractiveness — near-zero CAC via SEO/channel, no founder-led close, 88% margins, headcount-light — rests on a non-technical, audit-anxious small-business owner being willing to (a) act on a free, software-generated gap report and (b) pay a recurring subscription to fix a high-stakes compliance obligation *themselves*, with no human in the loop and no credentialed assessor behind the product. If that buyer instead treats the free assessment as a scary diagnosis and then reaches for a human (a consultant, their MSP, or a credentialed RPO) for the actual remediation and attestation — because their purchase orders and personal liability are on the line — then free→paid conversion lands at the bottom of the range or below, CAC effectively rises (more free users needed per payer, or paid acquisition required), and the founders are pushed toward exactly the high-touch services layer the thesis was built to avoid. That services layer would add headcount, cap scalability, and compress gross margin from ~88% toward services-blended 50–65% — turning an A-shape SaaS into a B-shape consultancy-with-software. It is not fatal: it is the first thing to test, and the free assessment itself is the cheapest possible instrument to measure it.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the real free-assessment completion rate and free→paid conversion in a live cohort? Stand up the free tool now, drive a few hundred targeted small-DIB visitors via content + an APEX/MEP partner, and measure both — the entire ladder timing hinges on this single number.
- Will a non-technical, personally-liable owner actually buy and rely on self-serve software for remediation and attestation, or do they demand a human/credentialed sign-off? Run paid-tier interviews/pre-sales against the live assessment and watch where they balk; if they balk at "self-attest," what assisted tier closes them and at what margin?
- What is the true blended ARPU and tier-mix when buyers self-select from published $200–$600 pricing, and what is monthly logo + revenue churn once the initial gap is closed (does re-assessment/questionnaire-auto-fill retain them, or do they cancel after they "pass")? Retention after the one-time compliance push is what separates durable SaaS from a one-and-done.

RECOMMENDATION: GO
The financial shape is among the strongest a bootstrapped two-founder team could pick: zero capital intensity, structurally negative working capital, ~88% durable gross margin, a forced and already-budgeted recurring buy, a 10x+ value-to-price gap against named incumbents, a dated demand catalyst (Nov 10, 2026) inside the income window, and a market two founders cannot exhaust — with the ladder reachable on a few hundred customers by shown bottoms-up math. It is a GO at concept stage, not because the risks are absent but because they are concentrated in two cheaply-testable unknowns (free→paid conversion, and whether a self-attesting non-technical buyer trusts software-only remediation) rather than in the capital structure or margins. The one thing that would move this from a confident GO to a guarded one is evidence the buyer won't self-attest without a human — so the very first dollar should go into the free assessment as a live conversion experiment, not into building the full remediation engine; if conversion and willingness-to-self-serve clear even the low end of the assumed ranges, the model's economics are excellent.
