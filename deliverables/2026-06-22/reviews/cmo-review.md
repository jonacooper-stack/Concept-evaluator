# CMO REVIEW — Self-Serve CMMC / NIST 800-171 Compliance Software for Small Defense Subcontractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ

This is a real, forced, recurring, already-budgeted buy with a hard deadline (CMMC Level 2 phases into new CUI contracts November 10, 2026) and a large, reachable, fragmented customer base — roughly 80,000–118,000 firms need Level 2, and 73% of the DIB is small business ([CMMC.com](https://www.cmmc.com/newsroom/how-does-cmmc-impact-small-businesses), [CyberSheath](https://cybersheath.com/resources/blog/why-small-defense-contractors-cant-afford-to-ignore-cmmc/)). The pain is genuine and the spend is observable: small businesses average ~$138K all-in for Level 2 and consultants run $250–$400/hr ([PreVeil](https://www.preveil.com/blog/cmmc-certification-costs/), [Paramify](https://www.paramify.com/blog/cmmc-cost)). The free-assessment top-of-funnel is a smart, proven magnet, and "fraction of a consultant" pricing at $200–$600/mo is the right shape. BUT the central positioning claim — "become CMMC compliant yourself without a consultant" — runs directly into the hardest fact in this market: DoD's own framing is that ~95% of CUI contractors will need a C3PAO third-party assessment, and only ~2% can self-attest at Level 2 ([Summit 7](https://www.summit7.us/blog/cmmc-l2-self-assessments), [Fortreum](https://fortreum.com/cmmc-self-assessment-vs-third-party-assessment-which-path-applies-to-you/)). So the product can credibly sell "get ready for and pass your assessment, cheaply, yourself," but the literal "DIY compliance, no consultant" promise is in tension with a market where an outside assessor is mandatory for nearly everyone. This is sellable — the wedge is real and the air exists in the under-$600/mo self-serve tier — but the positioning needs a precision edit, and the differentiation is thinner than the one-pager implies because StrikeGraph, Vanta, FutureFeed, and Totem already offer free CMMC self-assessments and AI-assisted remediation.

POSITIONING DRAFT

As the owner of a 35-person machine shop would hear it: *"See your CMMC score for free in an afternoon, then let software walk you through fixing every gap and filling out your prime's security forms — for the price of a phone line, not a consultant."* That is a clean, ownable sentence and it has edges. The problem is the implied promise underneath it ("you won't need anyone else"), which is partly false for ~95% of these buyers who will still have to pay a C3PAO to certify. The honest, still-sharp version: *"The fastest, cheapest way for a small shop to get assessment-ready and stay compliant — do 90% of the work yourself in software, and walk into your C3PAO assessment already passing."* That preserves the wedge (cost, simplicity, self-serve) without making a claim a knowledgeable prime's quality manager could puncture in one question. I can write a positioning here that doesn't sound like every other startup — the "free score, then self-serve fix, priced 10–30x below a consultant" framing is genuinely differentiated on *price point and motion* even though it is not differentiated on *features*.

THE WEDGE

The wedge is identified, specific, low-cost, and squarely founder-fit — this is the strongest part of the GTM. First 100 customers come from three stacked motions: (1) **The free assessment as the magnet.** A no-signup tool that returns an estimated SPRS score and a plain-English gap list is the right top-of-funnel; it is exactly the move StrikeGraph ([strikegraph.com/self-assessments](https://www.strikegraph.com/self-assessments)) and Totem ([totem.tech/free-tools](https://www.totem.tech/free-tools/)) already use, which both validates the tactic and warns that the front door is not unique. (2) **SEO/content on present-tense pain** — "what is my SPRS score," "DFARS 7012 flow-down questionnaire," "CMMC for small machine shops," "POA&M template." This is a keyword set with clear commercial intent and incumbents (CyberSheath, Summit 7, Secureframe, PreVeil) already invest heavily in it, so it is winnable but contested; expect to spend 6–12 months and real content effort to rank. [ASSUMED] organic blended CAC in the $150–$600 range once content compounds — verify by standing up 15–20 cornerstone pages and measuring assisted conversions over a quarter. (3) **Channel partners — APEX Accelerators and MEP centers** — which counsel exactly these firms on CMMC and "can facilitate direct referrals" ([apex.tennessee.edu](https://apex.tennessee.edu/our-services), [marylandapex.org](https://www.marylandapex.org/resources/cmmc-resources)). This is a high-trust, low-cost channel and a genuine asset. The caveat that must be stated: MEP centers frequently provide *subsidized or grant-funded* CMMC readiness help themselves ([NR Labs](https://www.nrlabs.com/blog-posts/how-the-dod-is-helping-small-dib-companies-afford-cmmc), [Cyber Grants Alliance](https://www.cybergrantsalliance.org/cmmc-gap-assessment-grant)), so they are simultaneously a referral partner *and* a free substitute — the partner pitch has to be "we're the tool your clients use to do the work," not "we replace what you do." No founder-led sales close is required to transact at $200–$600/mo on a credit card, which fits the founders perfectly.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE

The named players you must out-position, and what each is best at:

- **FutureFeed** — the closest direct competitor and the one to beat on price-for-purpose. CMMC/NIST/DFARS-native, flat-rate (not per-seat): Innovator (<25 FTEs) $99/mo, Standard $399/mo, plus a Level 2 add-on at $168/mo and dynamic SPRS scoring ([futurefeed.co/pricing](https://futurefeed.co/pricing/)). It also runs a "Powered-by-FutureFeed" partner program at $9,995/yr for MSPs/RPOs. This is your most dangerous lookalike: small-biz-priced, CMMC-specialized, channel-savvy. Your differentiation against them is consumer-grade simplicity + AI guidance for a non-technical owner; theirs is incumbency and an RPO channel.
- **Totem Technologies** — CMMC/NIST-native GRC tool plus free self-assessment tools, with productized services (Gap Assessment $21,200; Readiness Review $9,200) ([totem.tech](https://www.totem.tech/cybersecurity-compliance-pricing/)). Best at: trusted, contractor-built tooling with optional expert help. They already own the "free self-assessment → paid tool" funnel you're proposing.
- **StrikeGraph** — AI-native, multi-framework (25+ incl. CMMC/800-171), offers a free guided CMMC self-assessment and AI SSP generation; Scale plan with full 800-171 + "Verify AI" carries an ~$18K/yr list value ([strikegraph.com/nist-800-171](https://www.strikegraph.com/nist-800-171)). Best at: AI-assisted, polished UX across many frameworks. This is the most direct threat to your "AI replaces the consultant" claim — they already market exactly that, just priced higher and aimed broader.
- **Vanta / Drata** — the GRC giants. Vanta lists ~$7.5K–$50K/yr; Drata ~$2.5K–$90K/yr; Drata has raised $328M ([Vanta](https://www.vanta.com/collection/cmmc/cmmc-certification-cost), [Capchase](https://www.capchase.com/compliance-solutions-comparisons/drata-vs-vanta)). Best at: SOC 2/ISO automation for funded SaaS. They are well-funded, strong-tech players — but they are *aimed up-market and horizontally*, and they are complex and expensive for a non-technical 35-person shop. That mismatch is precisely your air.
- **PreVeil** — CMMC enclave + compliance at $450/mo, 85+ customers at perfect 110 scores ([preveil.com](https://www.preveil.com/cmmc-compliance/)). Best at: the encrypted CUI-handling enclave (a different, adjacent need you deliberately don't touch).

Where is the air? It is real but narrow: **a non-technical, owner-operated small shop wants the simplest possible self-serve path at the lowest published price, and no incumbent is both consumer-simple AND CMMC-native AND <$600/mo with AI hand-holding.** FutureFeed is cheap but tool-ish; StrikeGraph is AI-slick but broad and pricier; Vanta/Drata are over-built and over-priced for this buyer. The wedge is the *intersection* of simplicity + CMMC-specialization + price + AI guidance. The danger is that none of those four attributes is individually defensible, and the field is consolidating (predicted 20–35% vendor reduction; active MSP/MSSP M&A) ([StrikeGraph](https://www.strikegraph.com/blog/five-2026-predictions-on-cmmc)) — so the question is whether you can plant the "TurboTax-for-CMMC" brand before a funded incumbent simply ships a $299/mo small-shop tier.

7. **Brand / story compounding: 8** — "TurboTax for CMMC" is a story that compounds: a free score creates word-of-mouth inside tight prime-supplier networks, and the private knowledge base that improves with every answer is a genuine compounding-value narrative; the deadline (Nov 10, 2026) gives the story present-tense urgency ([CMMC.com](https://www.cmmc.com/newsroom/how-does-cmmc-impact-small-businesses)).

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.0 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS

- **A genuinely forced, deadline-driven, already-budgeted buy with a reachable, fragmented audience.** ~80,000–118,000 firms, 73% small business, a Nov 10, 2026 trigger, and a concrete "lose your prime's POs" consequence — this is real market pull, not invented demand ([CMMC.com](https://www.cmmc.com/newsroom/how-does-cmmc-impact-small-businesses)).
- **A GTM motion and price point that fit the founders like a glove.** Free-assessment PLG + SEO + APEX/MEP partners + $200–$600/mo credit-card self-serve needs no sales team and leverages exactly the marketing/software skills on hand; the free-tool funnel is a proven tactic in this exact category ([Totem](https://www.totem.tech/free-tools/), [StrikeGraph](https://www.strikegraph.com/self-assessments)).
- **A compounding brand story with a clear, ownable frame.** "TurboTax for CMMC — free score, fix it yourself, fraction of a consultant" is memorable, the knowledge base gives a real compounding-value narrative, and the price gap vs. $138K consultant-led programs is a story that sells itself ([PreVeil](https://www.preveil.com/blog/cmmc-certification-costs/)).

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS

- **The core positioning claim is partly false for ~95% of the market.** Most Level 2 CUI contractors must still pay a C3PAO to certify; "become compliant yourself without a consultant" overpromises and a knowledgeable prime QM or RPO will say so, undercutting trust at the moment of conversion ([Summit 7](https://www.summit7.us/blog/cmmc-l2-self-assessments), [Fortreum](https://fortreum.com/cmmc-self-assessment-vs-third-party-assessment-which-path-applies-to-you/)).
- **Zero feature differentiation and a copyable wedge.** Free assessment, AI guidance, SSP/POA&M generation, and questionnaire help already ship at StrikeGraph, FutureFeed, and Totem; the only edge is packaging/price/simplicity, and a funded incumbent can match the price tier in a quarter ([strikegraph.com](https://www.strikegraph.com/nist-800-171), [futurefeed.co](https://futurefeed.co/pricing/)).
- **A thin moat against both encroachment and substitution.** No network effect; switching cost is light; the Exostar CCRA reciprocal questionnaire could commoditize auto-fill; and MEP centers offer subsidized/free CMMC help that competes with a paid subscription for the most cost-sensitive buyers ([Exostar](https://www.exostar.com/press-releases/exostar-enhances-the-exostar-platform-onboarding-module-to-include-defense-industrial-bases-cybersecurity-compliance-and-risk-assessment/), [NR Labs](https://www.nrlabs.com/blog-posts/how-the-dod-is-helping-small-dib-companies-afford-cmmc)).

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The single most likely thing to kill this from a marketing standpoint is **trust collapse at the conversion point caused by an over-claimed "DIY, no consultant" promise in a market where an outside assessor is mandatory for nearly everyone.** The product's entire funnel hinges on a non-technical owner believing the software can get them across the line themselves. But DoD's own posture is that self-assessment is the exception (~2%) and a C3PAO third-party assessment is the rule (~95%) for CUI at Level 2 ([Summit 7](https://www.summit7.us/blog/cmmc-l2-self-assessments)). The first time an owner shows a software-generated "you're compliant" estimate to their prime's security contact or an RPO and is told "that's not a real assessment — you still need a C3PAO," the brand becomes "the cheap tool that gave me false confidence," which is fatal in tight, gossipy prime-supplier networks where reputation travels fast. This is not a fatal *product* flaw — the software genuinely does the readiness/remediation work, which is valuable — it is a *messaging* flaw. The fix is a disciplined repositioning from "become compliant yourself" to "get assessment-ready and pass — do the work yourself, walk in already scoring 110," paired with a clear, honest in-product explanation of when a C3PAO is required and partnerships with C3PAOs/RPOs so the product *hands off* to certification rather than pretending to replace it. Get the message right and the wedge holds; leave it as written and the funnel leaks trust at exactly the point where money changes hands.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE

- **How do you reposition around the C3PAO reality without losing the "DIY, cheap, fast" wedge?** Will you reframe to "assessment-ready / pass-your-C3PAO" and build explicit C3PAO/RPO handoff partnerships — and does that change the willingness-to-pay vs. a buyer who thought they were buying "done"?
- **What is the real free→paid conversion and blended CAC?** Stand up the free-assessment tool plus 15–20 SEO cornerstone pages for 90 days and measure: traffic, free-assessment completions, free→paid rate, and assisted-conversion CAC. Is the $2,400–$7,200 ACV supporting a CAC under ~$600 with a conversion rate you can actually hit (test the [ASSUMED] 2–5%)?
- **How do you out-position FutureFeed specifically at $99–$399/mo?** They are CMMC-native, small-biz-priced, and channel-savvy; what makes a 35-person machine shop choose you over the incumbent already sitting in the RPO channel — and is "simplicity + AI guidance" enough, or do you need a wedge feature (e.g., the auto-fill of the Exostar CCRA / prime questionnaires) that they don't have?
- **Can the APEX/MEP channel be a referral partner rather than a free substitute?** MEP centers offer subsidized CMMC help; will they refer paid software, and what is the partner pitch ("the tool your clients use") that makes you complementary rather than competitive?
- **What is the retention/defensibility plan once a customer hits 110 and passes?** Is recurring re-assessment + questionnaire auto-fill + upkeep enough to hold a price-sensitive owner annually, or does churn spike post-certification — and does the Exostar CCRA standard erode the auto-fill stickiness you're counting on?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE

This is a strong concept with a real forcing function, an excellent GTM-founder fit, and a well-evidenced market — but it needs one specific, non-cosmetic change to be defensible: **reposition from "become CMMC compliant yourself, no consultant" to "the cheapest, fastest way to get assessment-ready and pass your C3PAO — do the work yourself in software,"** and back it with explicit C3PAO/RPO handoff partnerships so the message survives contact with an informed buyer. The differentiation problem is the second thing to fix: since every feature already exists at StrikeGraph, FutureFeed, and Totem, the founders must win on a sharp, ownable brand ("TurboTax for CMMC"), ruthless simplicity for the non-technical owner, the lowest credible published price in the segment, and ideally one wedge feature competitors lack — the prime/Exostar-CCRA questionnaire auto-fill is the most promising candidate because it touches a present-tense pain primes are actively enforcing. Validate the free→paid conversion and blended CAC with a 90-day landing-page-plus-content test before committing real money. Do those things and the wedge is genuinely investable; ship the positioning as written and it leaks trust at the point of sale.
