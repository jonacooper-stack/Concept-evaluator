# CMMC Market Map — Where the Money and the Defensible Position Actually Are
**Date: 2026-06-22  |  Plain-English strategy memo, built on current (2026) market data**

## The one-line answer
The CMMC market is large and forced — but the money and the defensibility are **not** in the
cheap "tell me my score and write my policies" software layer we've been scoring. They're in
the layers above it: getting the work actually done, holding the protected data, and passing
the mandated assessment. The cheap layer is crowded and racing toward free; the valuable
layers are under-served.

## Follow the dollars — the CMMC value chain
| Layer | What the shop actually buys | What it costs | Who owns it now | How contested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Readiness & docs** (the entry sliver) | Gap analysis, SSP/POA&M, SPRS estimate | $0–$600/mo | FutureFeed ($99), Vanta/Drata, **free** DoD Project Spectrum, Greypike, SMPL-C | **Very crowded; racing to free** ← *what we scored (B/73)* |
| **2. Managed services / MSP** | Someone does the actual IT fixes and runs compliance | $2.5K–$50K/mo; **$100K–$250K first year** | Summit7, CyberSheath, hundreds of regional MSPs | A **~$2B and growing** market; fragmented |
| **3. Secure enclave** | An environment that actually holds the protected data (GCC High) | part of a **~$265K** all-in L2 program (25-person shop) | Summit7 (Microsoft reseller), PreVeil | Specialized, sticky, heavy |
| **4. The assessment (C3PAO)** | The mandated third-party certification | **$35K–$80K** every 3 years | **Only ~80–100 authorized C3PAOs for ~80,000 companies** | Severe **supply bottleneck** |
| **5. Ongoing upkeep** | Re-assessment, questionnaire answers, staying current | bundled | everyone | A retention feature, not a market |

Two numbers reframe the whole thing: the CMMC **consulting/managed market is ~$2 billion and growing**, and a 25-person shop's all-in Level 2 bill is **~$265,000** — of which the assessment is ~25% and the documentation software is a rounding error. **We were selling the rounding error.**

## Three facts that change the strategy
1. **Most small shops are Level 1, not Level 2 — and Level 1 is honestly self-serve.** Level 1 (handles only "FCI," 17 basic controls) is *always* self-attested, every year, no assessor. "Do it yourself, no consultant" is **100% true for Level 1** — the exact claim that's false at Level 2 (where ~35% of all defense contractors land and most of those need a C3PAO). The big players ignore Level 1 because it's cheap. That's an honest, thin-competition self-serve segment hiding in plain sight.
2. **The assessment is the real bottleneck.** ~80–100 assessors for ~80,000 Level 2 companies, and only ~1% feel ready. The scarce thing isn't policy-writing software — it's *assessment capacity* and *getting shops genuinely ready to pass*. Whoever relieves that bottleneck (arms the assessors/RPOs, or guarantees a shop walks in passing) owns something valuable and defensible.
3. **The MSP is the trusted path — and "powered-by" turns the competitor into a channel.** Small shops ask their IT provider, not a website. MSPs are growing cybersecurity revenue ~71% YoY, and FutureFeed already arms ~300 of them. Selling *through* MSPs sidesteps both the "tiny shop won't self-serve" problem and the direct-to-owner customer-acquisition cost problem.

## Where an A-tier version could live (structurally-different candidates)
These aren't tweaks to the B-tier concept — each changes the customer, the layer, or the revenue mechanism (which is what the discovery loop calls for after a concept caps at B).

**Option 1 — Arm the channel (sell to MSPs & RPOs, not to owners).** A white-label / "powered-by" platform that lets the MSPs and Registered Providers already serving these shops deliver readiness + assessment-prep faster and to more clients. *Customer = the delivery channel (hundreds of MSPs/RPOs), not the 35-person shop.* Defensible because you become their daily workflow; rides the ~$2B managed market; pure software; strong founder fit (sell to businesses, no field ops). The fight is with FutureFeed's partner program — smaller and more winnable than the direct-to-owner free-for-all.

**Option 2 — Own the certification cycle (readiness → partner C3PAO → re-cert).** Be the software that carries a shop from readiness to *passing* with a **partner** C3PAO, then keeps them through the mandated 3-year re-certification. You don't become the assessor (you can't — it needs accreditation); you own the customer relationship around the scarce, recurring, mandated event, with referral economics flowing both ways. Form-fill becomes the ongoing glue — your instinct, now attached to a real moat. *This is your "specialist platform" vision with the missing defensibility bolted on.*

**Option 3 — Level 1 self-serve at scale (the honest DIY play).** Dead-simple self-serve for the large FCI-only / Level 1 population where self-attestation is real and the giants don't bother. Lower price, much bigger volume, a truthful "no consultant" message, thin competition — with an upgrade path into Level 2 readiness as shops grow into CUI. The cleanest fit for "self-serve software, no humans," aimed at the one segment where that's actually true.

## My recommendation
Council **Option 2 next** — it keeps the specialist-platform vision you keep coming back to, but fixes the exact thing that capped the last three runs: it attaches the product to the **mandated, recurring, scarce** certification event instead of the commoditized doc layer, which is where durable lock-in and pricing power live. **Option 1 is the strongest pure-defensibility play** (and the most founder-fit GTM-wise) if you're willing to sell to MSPs rather than owners — I'd council it second. **Option 3** is the cleanest honest-self-serve, but its low price-per-customer makes the founder-income ladder harder, so it's the weakest of the three despite being the "truest" to the original self-serve idea.

The current concept stays a legitimate **B** worth cheap validation. But if the goal is an **A**, the move is to climb out of the cheap layer — toward the cert cycle (Option 2) or the channel (Option 1).
