COO REVIEW — CMMC Level 2 Certification Lifecycle Platform ("certified, and still certified")

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ

This is a software-leveraged, recurring SaaS business whose core unit of work is data and content, not trucks or installs — which is exactly the operational shape two software-building, compliance-experienced founders should be able to run lean. The genuine operational question is not field logistics; it is whether the two founders can stand up and maintain two non-trivial assets — (a) a control-by-control content/evidence library that tracks a young, still-evolving CMMC rule set across 110 controls, and (b) a partner network of accredited assessors — without that work quietly metastasizing into a services team they explicitly don't want. The customer-facing delivery is self-serve-leaning but realistically low-to-medium-touch on the first certification (a high-anxiety, high-stakes event for the buyer), and the operational risk concentrates in content maintenance and partner-relationship management rather than per-customer throughput. My read: operable by two founders plus a small contracted bench, provided they ruthlessly resist becoming the assessor, the remediation implementer, or the help desk for every panicked owner two weeks before an audit.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH

It's Tuesday, March 2027 (~mo 4–8 of live operation, picking a concrete 12-month-ish steady state). The platform has roughly 120 paying contractors on subscription. The rule has been phasing into contracts since November 2026, so demand is present-tense and urgent — owners are calling because a prime put a CMMC requirement on a flow-down with a date attached.

Founder A (product/content) starts the morning in the content backlog. The DoD/CIO and the Cyber AB have pushed a clarification on how one of the 110 controls (say, a specific aspect of multi-factor authentication scoping for CUI assets) should be evidenced. Founder A's job is to translate that into (1) updated plain-English guidance for the non-technical owner, (2) an updated evidence checklist of exactly what artifact the assessor will want, and (3) a flag to every existing customer whose stored evidence for that control may now be stale. This is the load-bearing operational task of the whole business and it recurs whenever guidance shifts. Today it takes ~3 hours including QA with the DoD/DIB advisor over email/Slack.

By mid-morning, support tickets. Of 120 customers, [ASSUMED] ~10–15% have an open ticket in any given week (12–18 tickets), and on a Tuesday in March maybe 5–8 land today. The bulk are "the platform says I need an asset inventory — what counts as a CUI asset?" and "my IT provider says we already do this, do I still need to document it?" These are answerable from the content library and a help-doc, ~15–25 min each — call it 2 hours. One ticket is the dangerous kind: a customer is six weeks from a scheduled assessment, just realized they can't actually meet a control (their email is on a consumer Google Workspace tier that won't support the required CUI handling), and is panicking. That is NOT a software problem — it is a remediation/procurement problem the platform can guide but cannot do for them. Founder A points them to the remediation roadmap, the relevant managed-service or GCC High migration partner, and resists the gravity to "just hop on a call and fix it." That gravity is the whole ballgame for staying lean.

Founder B (GTM/partners) spends the morning on the partner side. There are [ASSUMED] ~6–10 partner C3PAOs in the network. Two things happen on a Tuesday in March: (1) a customer just hit "audit-ready" and needs to be matched and handed off — Founder B confirms which partner assessor has capacity in the customer's timeframe, packages the evidence bundle, and makes the warm intro; net ~30–45 min per handoff, and today there are 2; (2) relationship maintenance — a quarterly check-in with the two highest-volume partners on lead quality, the referral-fee reconciliation, and whether the evidence packages are actually saving them assessment hours (the entire partner value prop). Afternoon is marketing: a new SEO piece on "what a CMMC Level 2 assessment actually checks," a co-marketing webinar being planned with an APEX Accelerator (procurement assistance center), and outbound to two MEP centers.

Where it breaks on this Tuesday: it does NOT break on per-customer delivery throughput — 120 customers generating ~15 tickets and ~2 handoffs a week is comfortably inside two people plus tooling. It breaks, if it breaks, on (a) the content-maintenance load if CMMC guidance churns faster than one founder can keep 110 controls current and re-flag the installed base, and (b) the temptation to absorb the "I can't pass and my audit is in six weeks" customer's remediation as a paid service to save the logo — which is precisely how a software company turns into a 12-person consultancy.

Per-customer DELIVERY capacity (the COO question — can two people serve the installed base?). Treat the customer events as: onboarding, ongoing support, the audit handoff, and the annual/recurring affirmation.
- Onboarding: software-guided self-assessment across 110 controls. [ASSUMED] ~2–4 hours of human assist per new customer concentrated in the first month (kickoff + a few scoping questions), front-loaded, not recurring.
- Ongoing support: [ASSUMED] ~0.3–0.5 human-hours per customer per month once onboarded (1–2 tickets/customer/quarter at ~20 min).
- Audit handoff: ~0.5–0.75 hr per customer, once per ~3-year cycle (plus the panic-ticket tail).
- Recurring affirmation/monitoring: largely automated; ~0.25–0.5 hr human-touch per customer per year.

Steady-state at 160 customers (mo-24 level):
- Support: 160 × ~0.4 hr/mo ≈ 64 hrs/mo ≈ ~16 hrs/week.
- New onboardings at ~13/mo × ~3 hrs ≈ 39 hrs/mo ≈ ~10 hrs/week.
- Handoffs: ~160/36 months ≈ ~4.4 handoffs/mo × ~0.6 hr ≈ ~3 hrs/mo (trivial weekly).
- Affirmations: ~160 × 0.4 hr/yr ≈ 64 hrs/yr ≈ ~1.3 hrs/week.
Total customer-facing delivery ≈ ~30 hours/week at the mo-24 scale. That is roughly 0.75 of one full-time person — i.e., one founder part-time, or a single ~$45K–$60K/yr contracted CMMC-literate support/onboarding specialist, covers ALL per-customer delivery for the entire book. This is the strong part: delivery throughput is not the bottleneck.

The REAL capacity constraint — content maintenance (show the work). The 110 controls each need: plain-English guidance, an evidence checklist mapped to what an assessor checks, and document/SSP/POA&M generation logic. Initial build [ASSUMED] ~110 controls × ~3–5 hrs each to author + advisor-QA ≈ ~330–550 hours one-time (≈ 2–3.5 months of one founder, front-loaded, partially pre-launch). Ongoing maintenance is the recurring load and the thing that decides whether two people can hold this: [ASSUMED] ~10–20 hrs/week of one founder's time in the first 18 months while the rule set is young and guidance is actively churning (assessment process memos, ED/CIO clarifications, prime questionnaire variants), tapering to ~3–6 hrs/week as it stabilizes. Verify cheaply by logging the actual frequency and size of CMMC guidance changes over a 60–90 day window before committing (the change cadence is public via DoD/Cyber AB channels). Net: one founder is effectively the "Chief Content Officer" for the first ~18 months; the other runs GTM + partners. That is a viable two-founder allocation — but it is NOT slack-free, and a sharp uptick in rule churn is the scenario that forces the first hire (a contracted CMMC content analyst / RP-credentialed writer, ~$70K–$110K/yr).

Partner-network capacity. With ~6–12 partner C3PAOs, partner management is [ASSUMED] ~3–6 hrs/week of one founder (handoffs + relationship + referral reconciliation) — light, and it is leverage, not drag: each partner absorbs many customers' actual assessments, which is the whole reason the founders don't need an assessment team. The scarcity of assessors (~100 for tens of thousands of firms) cuts BOTH ways operationally: it makes the matchmaking valuable, but it also means partner capacity — not the platform — can become the customer's bottleneck, and the founders must manage a waitlist they don't fully control.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.5 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS

- Delivery throughput is decisively NOT the constraint: ~30 customer-facing delivery hours/week serve a ~160-customer (mo-24) book — under one FTE — with no trucks, installs, or site visits anywhere in the unit of work. This is a clean software-ops shape that fits two founders plus a single contractor.
- The product is structurally standardized and self-checking: every customer runs the same 110 controls and the same SSP/POA&M/evidence artifacts, and an external assessor provides an objective, built-in pass/fail QC signal that most SaaS businesses never get.
- The recurring engine (annual affirmation + drift monitoring + auto-answered flow-down questionnaires + 3-year re-cert) is largely automatable, turning a one-time compliance event into a low-touch recurring subscription — exactly the operational profile that compounds rather than re-selling from zero each cycle.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS

- Content-maintenance treadmill: keeping 110 controls' guidance + evidence checklists current against a young, still-churning CMMC rule set AND re-flagging the installed base is ~10–20 hrs/week of one founder early — the single largest recurring operational load and the trigger for the first hire if churn accelerates.
- Dependence on a partner pool the founders don't control: only ~100 accredited C3PAOs nationally, an industry independence rule that prevents internalizing assessment, and partner capacity/terms/disintermediation all sit outside the founders' direct control.
- Scope-creep gravity into services: panicked, six-weeks-from-audit, can't-pass customers will pull hard for the founders to "just do the remediation," which is how a lean SaaS quietly becomes the 12-person consultancy the founders explicitly don't want to build.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The operational issue most likely to break the lean two-founder model is the content-maintenance treadmill colliding with services-scope gravity. The product's entire value rests on the control-by-control content/evidence library being accurate and current — and CMMC in 2026–2027 is a young, actively-clarified regime, so guidance will shift, assessment interpretations will evolve, and prime flow-down questionnaires will mutate. That means one founder is effectively a full-time "Chief Content Officer" for the first ~18 months (estimated ~10–20 hrs/week, tapering), which is survivable but slack-free. The danger is what happens on a bad Tuesday: a meaningful share of first-time customers will discover late that they cannot actually meet a control (their IT posture is genuinely deficient — wrong email tier, no GCC High, no MFA), they have a hard contract deadline, and they will beg the founders to fix it for them. Each "yes" feels rational to save a logo and protect the pass rate the business's reputation depends on — and each "yes" adds bespoke, labor-heavy, un-productized work. Do that twenty times and the founders have accidentally built the hourly-consultant business they were positioning against, with a services headcount they didn't want and margins that don't look like SaaS. The thing that makes me say "I'm not sure two people can do this" is not the customer count — that math is comfortable — it's the combination of an authoring load that never fully stops and a customer base whose worst-off members will, predictably and sympathetically, pull the founders across the productization line one rescue at a time. Surviving requires a hard, pre-committed operating discipline: software guides remediation, named partners (managed-service / GCC High migrators) DO remediation, and the founders never pick up the wrench.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE

- Content cadence: Over a logged 60–90 day window, how often and how materially does official CMMC guidance / assessment interpretation actually change, and how many human-hours/week does it take to keep all 110 controls + the installed base current? (This single number decides whether one founder can hold content or whether a content-analyst hire is required in month 1.)
- The hard line on remediation: When a customer is six weeks from assessment and genuinely cannot pass, what EXACTLY does the platform do — and what does it refuse to do? Is there a named, contracted bench of remediation/MSP/GCC-High partners ready to take that handoff, and have those partners agreed to it?
- Partner-network reality: Can you secure written LOIs/agreements from enough accredited C3PAOs (target ~6–12) that (a) will accept your matched, evidence-organized referrals, (b) have actual capacity in customers' deadline windows, and (c) won't build competing in-house prep — and what are the agreed referral economics and lead-quality expectations?
- Onboarding touch, measured: In a small pilot (even 5–10 design-partner shops), what is the actual human-assist time per customer for the first-certification journey — is it the ~2–4 hrs assumed, or does the average non-technical owner need materially more hand-holding (which would change the support-hire timing and the per-customer margin)?
- Pass-rate accountability: How is the platform positioned/contracted so that a customer who FAILS an assessment after following it doesn't become a reputation-killing liability in a small DIB community — what is the make-good, the disclaimer posture, and the early-warning mechanism that flags "this firm is not actually ready" before they pay for an audit?

RECOMMENDATION: GO

On purely operational grounds this is a strong fit for two software-building, compliance-experienced founders who refuse to run field ops: the delivery shape is remote, standardized, and software-leveraged; the per-customer throughput math is comfortable (~30 delivery hrs/week at the mo-24 book — under one FTE); the recurring engine is automatable; and there are no trucks, installs, or site visits anywhere in the unit of work. It clears the COO bar that kills field-service models outright. The two operational caveats that keep this a GO-with-discipline rather than an unqualified GO are both manageable but must be designed in from day one, not discovered later: (1) one founder must own the content/evidence library as a near-full-time role for the first ~18 months and the business must be ready to convert that into a contracted content-analyst hire the moment rule-churn outruns one person (verify the churn rate cheaply before committing); and (2) the founders must hold an iron line against absorbing customer remediation — software guides, named partners implement, founders never become the consultant — or the lean model erodes one sympathetic rescue at a time. Build the partner bench (assessors AND remediation/MSP partners) with written agreements before scaling acquisition, instrument onboarding touch on a small design-partner cohort, and put an explicit "not-ready" early-warning gate in front of the assessment handoff to protect the pass rate the whole reputation rides on. Do those, and two people can operate this.
