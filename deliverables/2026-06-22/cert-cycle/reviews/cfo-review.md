CFO REVIEW — CMMC Level 2 Certification-Lifecycle Platform for Small Defense Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil unusually well for a bootstrapped software business: this is annual-billed recurring SaaS attached to a hard regulatory deadline, sold into a segment that is already spending tens of thousands of dollars per certification cycle, with an 80%+ software gross margin and a near-zero working-capital cycle (annual prepay funds growth). The forcing function is concrete and recurring (assessment every 3 years + annual affirmation), and the platform deliberately avoids the two things that would wreck the economics — it does not become a C3PAO (no accreditation drag, no independence conflict) and it does not store CUI (lower liability/infosec cost). The central financial fragility is not whether one customer pays, but whether a self-serve / lightly-assisted software product can actually get a non-technical 40-person machine shop *through a 110-control audit* without quietly becoming a high-touch services business — because if it does, the margin, the CAC payback, and the founder-income math all degrade toward a consultancy, not a SaaS.

THE MATH

**Pricing (stated, labeled [ASSUMED] in the one-pager):** $400–$1,200/mo, i.e. ~$5,000–$15,000/yr. I'll model a blended ARPU of **$9,000/yr** [ASSUMED midpoint; verify with 10 pricing calls to shops mid-CMMC-prep — willingness-to-pay is anchorable because the traditional path is observable]. This is credible because the *displacement* number is real: consultant-led readiness + a C3PAO assessment for a small CUI shop is widely cited at **~$20K–$50K+ per cycle** all-in, recurring every 3 years, plus internal labor — so $5K–$15K/yr ($15K–$45K per 3-yr cycle) is genuinely positioned as a fraction of the alternative, not a price the market has to be taught.

**Gross margin:** Pure-software COGS (hosting, support tooling, content updates, minimal customer success) on a compliance SaaS runs **80–90%** [comparable: Vanta, Drata, Secureframe — GRC-automation SaaS operate at ~75–85% gross margin per standard SaaS benchmarks for this category]. I'll use **82%** to be conservative, accounting for more hand-holding than a pure GRC tool because the buyer is non-technical. Contribution per customer ≈ $9,000 × 0.82 = **$7,380/yr**.

**Path to the founder-income ladder.** Treat "founder income" as owner cash, i.e. roughly gross-profit pool minus the modest non-founder opex of a two-person software company (a contractor or two, tooling, marketing). Two founders, target ~$300K each by mo12, ~$500K+ each by mo24.

- *Rung 1 — ~$600K combined owner cash by ~mo12.* With an 82% gross margin and a lean ~$200K/yr non-founder opex base [ASSUMED: 1–2 contractors + tools + ad spend; verify against actual hiring plan], the founders need roughly **$600K + $200K = $800K ARR**. At $9K ARPU that is **~89 customers**. Build to ~90 paying customers in 12 months ≈ **7–8 net adds/month** — a real but ordinary bootstrapped-SaaS ramp for a product with a deadline-driven, channel-fed pipeline.
- *Rung 2 — ~$1.0M+ combined owner cash by ~mo24.* At a slightly higher opex base (~$350K, a few more contractors / more marketing), founders need ~**$1.35M+ ARR ≈ ~150 customers**. Going from ~90 to ~150 customers in the second year is **~5 net adds/month**, *decelerating* in pace even as revenue grows — i.e., the ladder does not require accelerating heroics. The referral-fee stream from the assessor network is upside on top of this and I've intentionally excluded it from the base case.

**Sanity vs. market size (bottoms-up SOM).** The DIB has on the order of **~80,000 firms** the DoD has cited as touching CUI / needing CMMC L2 [DoD CMMC rule estimates; verify against the latest DoD/Federal Register figures — the order of magnitude, tens of thousands, is well documented]. Even taking a conservative serviceable slice — say **20,000** small CUI shops that fit the 15–100-employee, no-internal-security profile and need third-party certification — capturing just **150 customers is 0.75% of that slice.** Two founders are not remotely supply-constrained by market size; 150 logos is a rounding error against the addressable base, and the Nov 2026 phase-in creates a multi-year wave of first-time buyers.

**Working capital / cash flow.** Annual prepay (or even monthly at $400–$1,200) against ~$0 inventory and no deposits-out means the customer funds the business *before* meaningful cost is incurred. CAC is the only real cash-out. **CAC [ASSUMED] $1,500–$4,000** for a partner-/content-led motion (no enterprise field sales, no paid-auction dependency); use **$3,000**. Payback = $3,000 / ($7,380/yr contribution) ≈ **0.4 years (~5 months)** — under the 6-month bar. **LTV/CAC:** at $7,380/yr contribution, a defensible ~4–5 year life (the obligation is permanent for as long as the firm holds CUI contracts; churn is mostly "lost the contract" or "got acquired," not "didn't want it"), gross LTV ≈ $7,380 × 4.5 ≈ **$33,200**, so LTV/CAC ≈ **~11x** even before applying any discount, comfortably above the 5x "10" threshold. Apply a haircut for early churn/discounting and it's still well north of 5x.

**Where the math breaks:** if the product cannot actually carry a non-technical owner to a *passing* audit and the founders are pulled into per-customer remediation labor, then (a) gross margin collapses from ~82% toward a 40–55% services margin, (b) CAC payback stretches because each sale carries delivery hours, and (c) customer count per founder gets capacity-capped — converting a SaaS into a boutique consultancy and breaking Rung 2.

- **Pricing/ARPU is unvalidated and potentially squeezed by free/cheap substitutes** — $400–$1,200/mo is [ASSUMED]; low-cost documentation tools and broad GRC platforms anchor price downward, and if blended ARPU lands nearer $5K than $9K, the customer count to hit the ladder roughly doubles (~180 for Rung 1).

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely financial killer is **the silent conversion of a software margin into a consultancy margin.** The entire investment thesis rests on the platform being able to walk a non-technical owner — someone running a machine shop, not a security program — through closing gaps on 110 controls and assembling an evidence package good enough to *pass* an independent assessor on the first attempt. If the product is genuinely self-serve, the unit economics above hold and this is an 8.4-grade business. But CMMC L2 is not SOC 2; failed first assessments are common and expensive, and the buyer here has explicitly no in-house security competence. The commercial gravity, when a customer is staring at a contract-threatening deadline and is stuck on a control, is for the founders to "just get on a call" — and one platform-led services creep at a time, the company drifts from 82% software margin toward a 40–55% services margin, the ~5-month payback stretches because every sale now carries delivery hours, and customers-per-founder hits a capacity ceiling that breaks Rung 2 of the income ladder (~$500K/founder). The financial difference between this concept clearing 8.4 and landing at ~6 is almost entirely *delivery model discipline*: does the software actually do the work, or does it become lead-gen for two founders' billable hours? Everything downstream — margin, payback, scalability, exit multiple — is a function of that one answer.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- **What is the realistic blended ARPU and how price-sensitive is this buyer?** Run 10–15 pricing conversations with shops currently mid-prep (and ask what they actually paid consultants); does $400–$1,200/mo hold, or does the floor get dragged toward $300–$500/mo by cheap documentation tools? (Cheap experiment: a pricing-page smoke test + 10 discovery calls via an MEP/APEX center.)
- **Can the product carry a non-technical owner to a *passing* assessment without founder labor — and what is the true onboarding cost per customer?** Instrument an early cohort: how many support hours, how many "get on a call" escalations per customer in the first 90 days, and what is the first-attempt pass rate? (Cheap experiment: concierge-MVP 5 shops by hand, measure the hours, then ask which steps software can truly absorb.)
- **What does the assessor-partner economics actually look like, and is the referral fee real?** Will C3PAOs pay a meaningful qualified-handoff fee (or rev-share), and how many partners are needed for capacity given ~100 accredited assessors nationwide? (Cheap experiment: sign 2–3 partner LOIs with a stated fee before building the matching feature.)
- **What is the demand and revenue impact if the Nov-2026 phase-in slips again?** Model the cash runway and ramp under a 6–12-month regulatory delay — does the partner/content motion still produce paying customers, or does the pipeline stall until contracts actually require certification?
