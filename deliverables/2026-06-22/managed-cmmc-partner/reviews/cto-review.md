CTO REVIEW — Managed CMMC Level 2 Readiness & Continuity for Small Defense Contractors (AI-Leveraged "Done-With-You")

ONE-PARAGRAPH TECHNICAL READ

This is, at its technical core, a compliance-workflow and document-generation platform wrapped around a human-led service — and that is a fundamentally buildable shape for two software-capable founders with real SOC 2/HIPAA/GDPR operator experience. The genuinely hard technical bet is exactly one component: high-accuracy CUI discovery across heterogeneous, messy data (CAD, scanned PDFs, images, email, file shares), which is a real classification/extraction problem and is NOT a "we'll just use AI" hand-wave. The other four components (AI-drafted SSP/POA&M/policies, remediation orchestration, evidence/assessment management, continuity monitoring) are LLM-assisted document and workflow tooling that is well within reach, provided the founders treat the AI as a drafting copilot with a human-in-the-loop reviewer rather than an autonomous author. The single most important technical-and-trust decision is the security posture of a company that, by design, stores the map of where a defense contractor's CUI lives — that metadata is itself sensitive, and the architecture must be built to a near-CUI standard (FedRAMP-Moderate-equivalent enclave, FIPS-validated crypto) from day one, not retrofitted.

ARCHITECTURE SKETCH

- Frontend: a standard multi-tenant web app — Next.js/React on the client, a customer portal showing readiness status, the 110-control checklist (NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 / the CMMC L2 assessment objectives), evidence repository, POA&M tracker, and questionnaire auto-answer flow. Boring, off-the-shelf, the right call. [ASSUMED stack; verify by building the control-tracker UI as a 1-week spike.]
- Backend / app tier: a conventional API service (Node/TypeScript or Python/FastAPI) with Postgres as the system of record for control state, evidence pointers, policy versions, client environment facts, and audit trails. Background/async workers (a queue — SQS/Celery/Temporal) for scans, document generation, drift checks, and the annual-affirmation cadence. This is the productizable spine and it is genuinely simple.
- The CUI discovery engine (the hard part, the real complexity): connectors that read M365/Google Workspace, SharePoint/OneDrive, on-prem/NAS file shares, and email, then a pipeline that handles file-type-specific extraction — OCR for scanned PDFs/images (Textract/Azure Document Intelligence/Tesseract), text extraction for Office/PDF, and the genuinely awkward case of CAD/drawing files (SolidWorks, AutoCAD, STEP) where the "CUI" signal is often in title blocks, export-control markings (ITAR/EAR/distribution statements), filenames, or embedded metadata rather than free text. On top of extraction sits a classifier: pattern/keyword/regex for known markings (CUI banners, distribution statements, contract/CAGE numbers) plus an LLM or trained classifier for ambiguous content, emitting location-metadata + a confidence score, NOT a copy of the file. This component carries essentially all of the technical risk.
- AI document generation layer: an LLM (Claude/GPT-class via API) behind a structured prompt+RAG pipeline that takes the captured environment facts (enclave type, identity provider, endpoint stack, data flows, responsibility matrix) and emits a tailored SSP, POA&M, and policy set mapped control-by-control to the 110 objectives. Critical design point: this must be templated + retrieval-grounded + human-reviewed, not free-form generation. The "fact base" per client is the real asset; the LLM is a renderer.
- Remediation orchestration layer: mostly an orchestration/runbook and ticketing engine, NOT a build-your-own-MDM. Realistically this is (a) configuration playbooks and checklists, (b) thin integrations/IaC to configure identity (Entra ID/Okta MFA, conditional access), logging (SIEM/log forwarding), and endpoint (the chosen EDR), and (c) hand-offs to implementation partners and the GCC High / enclave vendor. The smart architecture buys the enclave (Microsoft GCC High, PreVeil, or a managed CMMC enclave provider) and orchestrates around it rather than building infrastructure.
- Integrations / third-party surface: Microsoft Graph (M365/GCC High), Google Workspace, identity providers, an EDR vendor, a SIEM/log store, OCR/document-AI APIs, an LLM API, the C3PAO/assessor referral relationship, and a billing system (Stripe). Several of these sit on the critical path.
- Auth / hosting / tenancy: SSO + enforced MFA for the portal, strict per-tenant data isolation, and — this is the load-bearing decision — hosting that meets at least NIST 800-171 / FedRAMP-Moderate-equivalent controls because the platform stores compliance evidence and CUI location-metadata for DoD supply-chain firms. Practically: a US-only, access-controlled enclave (GCC High or AWS GovCloud) for the sensitive store, even though raw CUI is deliberately never ingested.

Where the complexity hides: 90% of the engineering risk lives in the CUI discovery engine (heterogeneous extraction + accurate classification at low false-negative rates) and in the security/compliance posture of the platform itself. The rest is competent, well-understood SaaS.

BUILD PLAN TO REVENUE-EARNING MVP

The crucial insight for time-to-revenue: this is a SERVICE first. The founders can sell and deliver the first "get-certified" engagements with a thin internal toolset and heavy human labor while the automation matures. Revenue does not wait on the full platform. I estimate roughly 14–22 dev-weeks of one developer's time to a revenue-earning MVP, sequenced so cash starts on the early end.
1. Knowledge spine + control tracker + evidence repo (3–4 dev-weeks). Encode the 110 NIST 800-171 objectives, build the Postgres data model, the client portal, the control-state tracker, and the evidence-upload/versioning store. This alone makes engagements deliverable. Off-the-shelf, low risk.
2. AI document-generation pipeline for SSP/POA&M/policies (3–5 dev-weeks). Structured environment-intake form -> RAG over a curated control/template library -> LLM drafting -> mandatory human-review workflow with versioning and diffing. This is the highest-leverage automation and is buildable now; the work is in the templates and guardrails, not the model.
3. CUI discovery v1 — narrow (4–6 dev-weeks). Start with the highest-value, lowest-difficulty surface: M365/SharePoint/OneDrive + email via Microsoft Graph, marking/keyword/regex detection for explicit CUI/distribution markings, plus OCR for scanned PDFs/images via a managed API. Emit location-metadata + confidence. Deliberately PUNT CAD/drawing parsing and file-share crawling to v2 — handle CAD via guided manual interview at first. This staging is what keeps the timeline honest.
4. Remediation orchestration v1 (2–4 dev-weeks). Runbook/playbook engine + ticketing + partner hand-off workflow; thin MFA/logging/endpoint config checklists rather than deep automated config. Buy the enclave; orchestrate it.
5. Continuity/monitoring + annual affirmation + questionnaire auto-answer (2–3 dev-weeks, largely v2). Scheduled re-scan, drift flags, affirmation cadence, and answering flow-down questionnaires from stored evidence. Can launch in a manual/checklist form and automate after the first cohort.

Punt-to-v2 list: CAD/STEP parsing, full on-prem file-share crawling, automated drift remediation, the questionnaire auto-answer engine, and any GovCloud/GCC-High self-service tenant provisioning. None of these block the first paid engagement.

CRITICAL ASSUMPTIONS

- Assumption: automated CUI discovery can hit acceptably low FALSE-NEGATIVE rates across messy data. This is the bet-the-product assumption. A missed CUI location understates scope and can blow an assessment — the cost of a false negative is asymmetric and severe. Cheap verification: a 2-week bench test running OCR+marking-detection over a sample corpus of real (or realistically synthesized) defense-shop documents including scanned drawings and marked PDFs; measure recall on known-marked files. If recall on explicit markings isn't strongly high, the "automated discovery as the wedge" claim weakens and the model leans more human. [ASSUMED recall on explicitly-marked docs is high (80–95%); CAD/unmarked content materially lower — verify with the bench test.]
- Assumption: an LLM can reliably draft an SSP/POA&M/policy set that a C3PAO will accept after human review. Plausible and well-supported by current LLM document-generation capability IF grounded in templates + retrieval and human-reviewed. The risk is hallucinated control claims (writing that a control is met when it isn't), which is a compliance-fraud-shaped failure. Cheap verification: generate one full SSP for a synthetic environment and have the senior DoD/DIB advisor or a friendly C3PAO red-line it; count substantive errors per control.
- Assumption: GCC High / government-cloud enclave and the assessor network are available as PARTNER capabilities, not things the founders must build or accredit. Strongly true — Microsoft GCC High, PreVeil, and multiple managed-enclave providers exist, and C3PAOs are a defined, listable population. Verify by signing two enclave-partner and two C3PAO referral conversations before launch (a business call, not engineering).
- Assumption: customer environments are mostly cloud (M365-centric) rather than sprawling on-prem. If most target shops are heavily on-prem with NAS/file-server CUI and legacy CAD vaults, discovery is meaningfully harder and connector work multiplies. Cheap verification: ask the first 10 prospects "where do your drawings and contract docs actually live?" — a discovery interview, not a build.
- Assumption: storing CUI location-metadata and evidence does NOT itself constitute taking custody of CUI in a way that triggers the contractor's own flow-down obligations onto the provider. This is as much a legal/contractual question as technical, but it shapes the entire infra-cost model. Verify with counsel + advisor before architecting the data store; design as if it does (treat the metadata store as in-scope).

THE FOUNDER-FIT NOTE (technical): The pairing is good for this build. SOC 2 Type II / HIPAA / GDPR operator experience maps directly onto control-framework thinking, evidence discipline, and policy authorship — the founders already know what an auditor wants to see, which is exactly the judgment the AI layer must be supervised against. They can build the SaaS spine and the document pipeline themselves. The honest gaps are (a) deep CUI/CAD-extraction ML, which I'd partner or buy rather than build, and (b) CMMC-specific assessment nuance, which the RPO registration + credentialed delivery lead + DoD/DIB advisor scaffolds appropriately. This is a bridgeable-moat profile, not an unbridgeable one.

INFORMATION SECURITY POSTURE

Data collected: per-client compliance documentation (SSP, POA&M, policies), evidence-of-control (screenshots, configs, attestations, logs), a structured fact base about the client's environment (identity provider, endpoint stack, data flows, network/enclave design), and — the sensitive crown jewel — CUI LOCATION-METADATA: a map of where a defense contractor's controlled information lives. The founders are right to deliberately NOT take custody of raw CUI; that is the single best architectural decision in the concept and it dramatically shrinks the blast radius. But it does not make the platform low-sensitivity: an SSP plus a CUI-location map plus evidence-of-control for a DoD supply-chain firm is a near-ideal reconnaissance package for a nation-state targeting the defense industrial base. The threat model must assume sophisticated, well-resourced adversaries (APTs), not opportunistic criminals.

Threat model & where it lives: the store must be US-persons, US-soil, access-controlled, and built to NIST 800-171 / FedRAMP-Moderate-equivalent controls. Practically that means hosting the sensitive tenant data in GCC High or AWS GovCloud, FIPS 140-2/3-validated encryption at rest and in transit, hardware-backed key management (KMS/HSM) with rotation, strict per-tenant isolation, enforced phishing-resistant MFA for all staff and clients, least-privilege RBAC, and comprehensive immutable audit logging (who viewed which client's CUI map, when). Secrets in a managed vault, never in code. The LLM pipeline introduces a specific risk: client environment facts must NOT be sent to a model endpoint that trains on inputs or sits outside the compliance boundary — use a zero-retention, in-boundary model offering (e.g., an enterprise API with no-training guarantees, or an in-GovCloud model) and document the data flow. OCR/document-AI services touch potentially-sensitive file content during scanning even if only metadata is persisted — that transit path is itself in-scope and must run in-boundary.

Controls at launch vs. at scale: AT LAUNCH (non-negotiable, because the first customer's data is the most sensitive data the company will ever hold relative to its maturity) — encryption everywhere with validated crypto, enforced MFA, RBAC, audit logging, per-tenant isolation, a written incident-response plan with DFARS 252.204-7012 72-hour-style breach reporting discipline baked in, vendor due diligence on every subprocessor, and US-only data residency. AT SCALE — formal continuous monitoring, a third-party penetration test, and a clear path to the provider's OWN compliance posture: customers (and primes) will reasonably ask whether the compliance vendor is itself compliant, so the company should target SOC 2 Type II early and plan toward its own NIST 800-171 self-assessment / CMMC posture as a sales and trust asset. Incident readiness is not optional: a breach of this dataset is existential — it would simultaneously be a customer catastrophe, a reportable event up the DoD chain, and a brand-ending story for a security company. The founders' existing SOC 2 Type II experience is a genuine asset here; they already know how to stand up and operate an evidence-backed control program.

- Founder fit is real and specific: hands-on SOC 2 Type II / HIPAA / GDPR operating experience is exactly the judgment needed to supervise an AI document-generation pipeline and to stand up the company's own control program, making this a bridgeable rather than unbridgeable technical moat.

TOP 3 TECHNICAL RISKS
- High-accuracy CUI discovery across CAD/scanned/heterogeneous data is the one hard component, and false negatives are asymmetrically costly (a missed CUI location can blow a real assessment) — the marketing wedge ("automated discovery generic tools miss") writes a check the engineering must cash.
- LLM-generated SSP/POA&M/policies can hallucinate a control as "met" when it is not — an authoritative-sounding but wrong compliance document is a fraud-shaped, liability-bearing failure unless every output passes a disciplined human-in-the-loop review.
- The platform stores a nation-state-grade reconnaissance dataset (CUI location maps + evidence for DoD-supply-chain firms); the threat model is APT, not script kiddies, and the company's own security maturity (pen tests, continuous monitoring, its own 800-171/SOC 2 posture) must be built to a higher bar than a typical seed-stage SaaS.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The most likely thing to break the business after launch is a credibility-and-liability failure stemming from over-trusting the AI layer — specifically, the discovery engine missing a pocket of CUI (an unmarked drawing, a scanned contract in an email attachment, a CAD vault on an old file server) or the generated SSP asserting a control is implemented when it is not, such that a client the company certified as "ready" fails a C3PAO assessment or, worse, suffers an incident and a DoD investigation reveals an inaccurate SSP the provider authored. In a market where the entire product promise is "you will pass, and you will stay compliant," a single public failure of that promise is disproportionately damaging — and the failure mode is technical (recall/accuracy) but the consequence is existential (reputation, referral network, and potential professional liability). The mitigation is architectural and procedural, not magic: scope discovery conservatively and surface confidence/uncertainty rather than over-claiming completeness, keep a mandatory human (the credentialed delivery lead) accountable for every SSP and scoping decision, carry appropriate professional-liability/E&O insurance, and never let the AI be the system of record for a compliance claim — it drafts, a qualified human signs.

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with one named de-risking spike before heavy build)

Technically this is a sound, bootstrap-appropriate concept: the bulk of it is competent SaaS plus LLM-assisted document generation that two software-capable, compliance-experienced founders can build and ship, it earns revenue as a service well before the platform is complete, and the security architecture starts from a genuinely smart decision (no raw CUI custody). It is a GO rather than a slam-dunk because two technical risks are real and asymmetric — CUI-discovery accuracy on messy/CAD data, and AI-document reliability in a context where a wrong answer is a liability event. Before committing serious engineering, run the cheap de-risking experiments named above: (1) a 2-week CUI-discovery bench test (OCR + marking detection over a realistic document/drawing corpus, measure recall) to decide build-vs-partner and to calibrate what completeness you can promise; and (2) generate one full SSP for a synthetic environment and have the DoD/DIB advisor or a friendly C3PAO red-line it. If discovery recall on marked documents is strong and the generated SSP survives expert review with few substantive errors, this is a confidently buildable business; if discovery recall is poor, lean the model toward human-led discovery + AI documentation (still viable) and stop marketing automated discovery as the headline wedge. Either way, architect the sensitive store to a near-CUI standard (GovCloud/GCC High, FIPS crypto, in-boundary LLM/OCR, immutable audit logging, enforced MFA) from line one — for this dataset, "fix security later" is "fix it after the breach that ends the company."
