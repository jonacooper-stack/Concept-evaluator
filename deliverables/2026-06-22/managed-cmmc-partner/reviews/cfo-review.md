CFO REVIEW — Managed CMMC Level 2 Readiness & Continuity for Small Defense Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers here are unusually attractive for a bootstrapped two-founder venture: a federally-mandated, deadline-driven buy with a hard date (Nov 10, 2026), a high-ticket upfront engagement ($15K–$40K [ASSUMED]) plus a genuine recurring retainer ($1.5K–$5K/mo [ASSUMED]), into a buyer who is forced to transact and has no internal alternative. The central financial question is not demand — demand is structurally forced — it is the clients-per-FTE ratio under a "done-with-you" human-delivery model: if AI tooling lets one credentialed success manager carry 15–25 active continuity clients and shepherd a reasonable pipeline of certifications, blended gross margin lands in the 55–70% range and the founder-income ladder is reachable inside 24 months; if the human work resists automation and the ratio is 5–8 clients/FTE, this degrades toward a 35–45%-margin boutique consultancy that caps out. The model self-funds beautifully (cash collected before or alongside cost), startup capital is trivial, and the recurring layer is a real asset — the whole investment thesis rides on proving the automation leverage and the renewal rate, both of which are cheap to test.

I'll work this bottoms-up, state every assumption, and show where it breaks.

(A) Per-customer revenue (annual contract value).
- Year-1 customer = upfront engagement + partial-year retainer. Engagement midpoint ≈ $27.5K (range $15K–$40K [ASSUMED]). Retainer midpoint ≈ $3,250/mo → ~$39K/yr [ASSUMED], say ~$20K captured in the partial first year.
- Year-1 ACV per new customer ≈ $45K–$50K (engagement + ~6 mo retainer).
- Steady-state recurring ACV per retained customer ≈ $18K–$60K, midpoint ~$36K–$39K.
- Sanity check against named comparables: traditional CMMC L2 readiness consulting commonly runs $35K–$150K+ for a small business (multiple RPO/C3PAO-adjacent firms publicly quote $40K–$90K for a ~40-seat shop; secure-enclave plays like PreVeil, Summit 7, and Kiteworks price the tooling/enclave alone at ~$15K–$50K+/yr before labor). An engagement priced at $15K–$40K is therefore a credible "fraction of incumbent" wedge, not a lowball that destroys margin — there is real pricing headroom. The retainer at $18K–$60K/yr is consistent with what managed-compliance and vCISO retainers fetch ($2K–$8K/mo is common in SOC 2 / managed-compliance land per Vanta/Drata-ecosystem service partners).

(B) Gross margin under a human-delivery model — the load-bearing calculation.
This is where the whole concept is won or lost, so I'll model the FTE economics explicitly.

A loaded CMMC-credentialed delivery lead / success manager costs ~$120K–$180K fully loaded [ASSUMED] (a CMMC CCP/CCA-credentialed practitioner is scarce; mid-$100s is realistic). Call it $150K loaded.

- Bear case — low AI leverage (5–8 clients/FTE): One FTE carries ~6 continuity clients + ~6–8 new certifications/yr. Revenue/FTE ≈ 6 × $36K (recurring) + 7 × $27.5K (engagement) ≈ $216K + $192K ≈ $408K. Delivery labor + partner pass-through (assessor handoff, enclave/implementation partners take a cut) ≈ $150K labor + ~25% of revenue in partner/COGS ≈ $150K + $100K ≈ $250K. Gross margin ≈ 38–45%. This is a consultancy, not software economics.
- Base case — moderate AI leverage (12–18 clients/FTE): One FTE carries ~15 continuity clients + ~10–12 new certs/yr. Revenue/FTE ≈ 15 × $36K + 11 × $27.5K ≈ $540K + $302K ≈ $842K. COGS ≈ $150K labor + ~20% partner/tooling pass-through ≈ $150K + $168K ≈ $318K. Gross margin ≈ 60–63%.
- Bull case — high AI leverage (20–25 clients/FTE): Revenue/FTE ≈ $1.0M–$1.2M, COGS ≈ $150K + ~18% ≈ $340K–$360K. Gross margin ≈ 68–72%.

The honest read: this lands at 55–65% blended gross margin in the realistic-to-good range — materially below an 80%+ pure-SaaS business because a human owns the outcome, but materially above a pure labor consultancy because AI absorbs the SSP/POA&M/policy drafting and gap analysis that historically was the billable bulk. The CUI-discovery and document-generation steps are exactly the high-volume/low-judgment work software is good at; the remediation orchestration and assessor-prep are where the human time concentrates and where the ratio is most at risk.

(C) CAC and payback.
GTM is partner-led + content/SEO, NOT paid-auction or founder-enterprise-close. Assume blended CAC of $3K–$8K per closed customer [ASSUMED] (content + co-marketing with assessors/MEP/APEX centers + partner referral fees of perhaps 10–15% of first engagement). Against a Year-1 ACV of ~$45K–$50K at ~55–65% margin, first-year gross profit per customer ≈ $25K–$32K. CAC payback ≈ well under 3 months on the engagement fee alone; the recurring retainer is then ~$36K/yr of high-margin annuity on top. LTV/CAC: at a 3-year cert cycle with strong renewal logic (the affirmation and re-cert are themselves forced), 3-yr gross-profit LTV ≈ engagement margin (~$16K) + 3 × retainer margin (~3 × $22K ≈ $66K) ≈ ~$80K+; LTV/CAC ≈ 10–25×. Even halving every favorable assumption, this clears 5×. This is a genuinely strong unit-economics profile if retention holds.

(D) Working capital / cash-flow shape.
Excellent. Engagement fees are typically billed with a deposit upfront and milestones — cash arrives before or during the cost of delivery, not after. Retainers bill monthly or annually in advance. There is no inventory, no equipment, no AR float of consequence, no deposits-out beyond modest partner pass-through (which can be structured back-to-back). This is a negative-working-capital-leaning, self-funding model — growth is paid for by customer cash, the gold standard for a bootstrap.

(E) Path to the founder-income ladder (~$300K/founder mo12, ~$500K+ mo24).
Founder income = gross profit − non-delivery opex (founders' own time is the GTM/product engine; delivery FTEs are COGS). Target ~$600K combined founder income by mo12, ~$1M+ by mo24.
- Mo-12 target (~$300K/founder = ~$600K combined): Need ~$600K of gross profit above delivery cost and lean overhead. At 55–65% margin, that implies ~$1.1M–$1.4M of revenue net of a small overhead. Translating to logos: ~25–35 first-year certifications at ~$45K ACV ≈ $1.1M–$1.6M revenue. Plausible but not trivial in 12 months — it requires landing ~2–3 new shops/month from a standing start, which the forced Nov-2026 deadline and partner channel make realistic but not guaranteed. Call this the stretch end of mo-12.
- Mo-24 target (~$500K/founder = ~$1M+ combined): By mo24 the recurring base compounds. If ~50–70 cumulative clients are on retainer (~$36K each ≈ $1.8M–$2.5M recurring) plus a fresh cohort of engagements (~$1M+), revenue ≈ $3M–$3.5M at ~60% margin ≈ ~$1.8M–$2.1M gross profit, less ~$400K–$600K overhead → ~$1.2M–$1.5M combined founder income. This comfortably clears the mo-24 rung if the ratio and renewal hold. The math reaches the ladder; it does not require $2M/founder and does not depend on it.

Bottoms-up: the DIB has on the order of ~80,000 firms in the CMMC ecosystem; DoD's own CMMC rulemaking estimated ~80,000 organizations require Level 2 certification in the rollout. TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Clients-per-FTE / margin risk (the whole thesis). If AI leverage underdelivers and one FTE carries only 5–8 clients, blended margin collapses toward 35–45% and this becomes a capacity-capped boutique consultancy whose growth is rate-limited by scarce credentialed hires — not the software-leveraged 60%+ business the pitch promises.
- Regulatory-timeline / policy concentration. All demand rides on one federal program's enforcement cadence. A rollout delay, a softening of the third-party-assessment requirement, a shift back toward self-attestation, or slow contracting-officer enforcement would flatten the demand curve the whole model assumes — a single point of macro failure.
- Credentialed-talent cost and scarcity as a scaling tax. The delivery lead and success managers must be CMMC-credentialed (CCP/CCA) — a thin, in-demand labor pool. Wage inflation or hiring lag directly compresses margin and caps the certification throughput that drives the mo-12 rung; the founders cannot personally deliver the audit-prep at volume.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single financial issue most likely to break this is the clients-per-FTE ratio under the human-delivery model — the gap between "AI-leveraged managed service" (the pitch) and "boutique compliance consultancy with a chatbot" (the failure mode). Every attractive number above — the 60%+ gross margin, the founder-income ladder by mo24, the scaling curve, the financeable multiple — depends on AI genuinely absorbing the analytical and documentation labor (CUI discovery, gap analysis, SSP/POA&M, policy drafting) so that a single credentialed success manager can carry and keep compliant 15–25 clients rather than 5–8. The hardest part of CMMC is not the paperwork the AI can draft; it is the remediation orchestration (standing up a compliant enclave, MFA, logging/monitoring, incident response across heterogeneous small-shop environments) and the assessor-prep accountability — judgment-heavy, environment-specific, human work that resists automation and is exactly where billable hours historically pile up. If that work doesn't compress, throughput is gated by scarce, expensive credentialed labor, margin falls to consultancy levels (35–45%), the mo-12 income rung becomes unreachable without over-hiring (which burns the self-funding advantage), and the "fraction of a traditional consultant's price" wedge becomes a margin trap rather than a moat. The good news for a CFO: this is the cheapest possible thing to test — deliver 3–5 real certifications end-to-end, instrument the hours by task, and measure how many the tooling actually removes. The entire investment case can be de-risked on a handful of paid pilots before any meaningful capital is committed.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the measured clients-per-FTE ratio after AI? Run 3–5 full paid engagements, time-track every task, and report: (a) hours the AI tooling actually eliminates vs. expert hours that remain, and (b) the resulting number of active continuity clients one credentialed FTE can carry while also closing new certs. This single number determines whether margin is 45% or 65%.
- What is the real renewal/affirmation attach and retention rate? What fraction of certification clients convert to the recurring retainer, and what is the expected annual churn given the buy is itself mandated? Cheap test: pre-sell the retainer as a bundled term to the first cohort and measure attach at the point of sale, not after.
- What is the true delivery COGS stack per engagement? Specifically the partner pass-throughs — government-cloud enclave (e.g., GCC High / Microsoft 365 GCC, PreVeil-class enclaves), CUI-discovery tooling licenses, implementation-partner labor, and the assessor handoff. Are these marked up, passed through at cost, or netted as referral income? This sets the COGS line the 55–65% margin depends on.
- How concentrated and durable is the partner-led channel, and what does it cost? What referral economics do assessors/MEP/APEX centers/MSPs expect (flat fee vs. % of engagement), and how many independent channel sources exist so the pipeline isn't hostage to one partner relationship?
- What is the contingency if the Nov-2026 enforcement timeline slips or softens? How quickly can the same delivery spine and CUI-discovery entry point be repointed to adjacent forced-compliance demand (NIST 800-171 self-assessment prep, FedRAMP-adjacent, ITAR/EAR data handling, CMMC L1) to diversify off single-program risk?
