COO REVIEW — Cosmetics Regulatory Compliance Program (MoCRA)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
Yes — this is operationally one of the cleaner shapes a two-founder bootstrapped business can take. The unit of work is a regulatory filing and a recurring records-keeping cadence, both software-submittable and remote: no trucks, no installs, no on-site visits, no inventory, no physical supply chain. The genuine operational risk is concentrated in two places — (1) the safety-substantiation dossier review and adverse-event triage are judgment work that does not fully productize and sits on the founders plus one named regulatory-affairs advisor, and (2) onboarding (the bulk initial product/ingredient listing load) is a real labor spike per new customer. Neither is field ops; both are throughput-and-judgment bottlenecks managed with templates, a reviewer hire, and tiered SLAs rather than headcount that scales linearly with revenue. The default failure mode is a missed 15-business-day adverse-event report or a botched safety substantiation — a quality/liability event, not a logistics one.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Month 12, ~60 paying brands [ASSUMED 50–70]. A Tuesday, no FDA deadline cluster.
- 8:30am — Founder A opens the compliance dashboard; overnight job pulled the queue: 3 brands with biennial facility-registration renewals due in 90 days, 2 brands with stale product listings (formula/SKU change), 1 adverse-event report in the intake inbox. The dashboard is the operational spine.
- 8:45am — The adverse event is priority (clock-driven). Platform timestamps intake, starts the 15-business-day countdown. Founder A / advisor judges: serious adverse event (report) or routine dissatisfaction (log + retain six years, no filing)? Highest-stakes 20 minutes. Today: scalp irritation, not serious — logged, no FDA filing.
- 9:15am — 3 renewals: facility data already on file, platform re-validates, submits to FDA's electronic system, ~10 min each.
- 9:45am — 2 stale listings: 4 new SKUs flow through the guided listing template; a reformulated serum (new preservative) flagged for the safety-review track (advisor on Thursday).
- 10:30am — One onboarding in progress (new $1M+ skincare DTC, 40 SKUs, one contract manufacturer). The labor-heavy lifecycle event. Founder B collects SKU/ingredient breakdowns, facility details, existing safety data; bulk-loads listings (40-SKU ≈ 4–8 hrs over the window). Advisor reviews dossiers.
- Afternoon — Long tail: 2 support emails (KB-deflected), an automated renewal-reminder batch, an hour improving the fragrance safety-dossier template (leverage work — reduces per-customer judgment load).
- Whole day operable by two founders, nothing physical/same-day. Day-blowers: a serious adverse event with ambiguous severity, or a renewal-deadline cluster.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Zero field ops, zero physical supply chain, national-remote from day one — cleanest possible operational shape (10 logistics, 8s supply/geography).
- Capacity math closes comfortably: ~1,350 delivery hours at 90 brands inside ~3,600 founder-hours; ~1,515 hours at 155 brands with one associate + retained advisor.
- The bottleneck is benign and concentrated: only hard-to-scale work is safety-dossier/adverse-event judgment, behind a guided-template layer, escalating to ONE advisor, later offloaded to ONE trainable associate.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- The 15-business-day serious-adverse-event reporting obligation is clock-driven, judgment-laden, liability-bearing — highest-consequence failure mode.
- Onboarding is a real labor spike (~9 hrs/brand, ~810 front-loaded hours to 90 brands); the safety-substantiation dossier within it is the least productizable step.
- Key-person dependency on the named regulatory-affairs advisor — the judgment/credibility layer leans on one relationship.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The issue most likely to hurt this isn't volume (capacity math says two people can do it) — it's whether two generalist founders plus one fractional advisor can carry the judgment-and-liability tail at quality every time across a growing book. The business sells "we will keep your FDA posture correct," and the two places that posture is made or broken — a serious-adverse-event severity determination inside a hard 15-business-day clock, and a safety-substantiation dossier that survives FDA scrutiny — resist productization and carry the worst consequences when wrong. A blown listing is embarrassing; a missed serious-AE report or indefensible safety dossier is a regulatory/reputational event for the customer who hired you to prevent it. Mitigation is structural and known (guided templates, a documented severity-decision tree, a mandatory four-eyes/advisor-escalation rule on every serious AE and novel-ingredient dossier, E&O insurance, clear scope-of-service language), and must be designed in from customer one because the failure mode is liability, not a refund. Keeps the concept a strong GO while flagging QC design + the advisor relationship as the two things that must be right.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Adverse-event judgment: who makes the "is this serious" call, on what documented decision tree, with what four-eyes/escalation rule and SLA guaranteeing the 15-business-day clock is never missed across 150+ brands? Show the workflow.
- Safety substantiation: how much of a defensible dossier can templates generate self-serve vs. requiring the advisor — true expert-hours-per-brand at onboarding, and does it fall as templates mature? (Time the first 5 onboardings.)
- The advisor: is there a named, contracted RA advisor with cosmetics/FDA depth, at what fractional cost and response SLA, and what's the plan (second advisor, SOPs, associate cross-training) so the business isn't one relationship away from losing its judgment layer?
- FDA system dependency + liability: what does FDA's interface allow programmatic vs. manual filing of (so "platform submits" is real, not 150 hand-keyed filings), and what E&O coverage + scope-of-service terms cap exposure when a customer's compliance fails?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Operationally a strong GO: a forced, recurring, remote, software-leveraged, capital-light service with no trucks/inventory/install crews and national reach from a laptop. Capacity math closes with margin at mo12 and needs only one associate + retained advisor to reach mo24 — not a model that quietly needs 30 people. Two real caveats are design problems to solve before customer one: (1) build the adverse-event and safety-substantiation work as a documented, four-eyes, advisor-escalated workflow with E&O coverage; (2) lock a named, contracted RA advisor and a plan to de-risk that single relationship. Solve those two and two founders can run this.
