ONE-PARAGRAPH PM READ
This is the field's strongest competitive position attached to a genuine continuity forcing function — and it lands a hair below Certified-Payroll only because its forcing function (a permit could lapse and close a location) is a notch softer than a payment that is withheld every single week. Five of six experts returned GO; the CMO and the Competitive Analyst returned REFINE, and they converge on the same precise truth: the operations-permit managed-filing layer is genuinely fragmented and sleepy (Harbor Compliance is "communication drops off after signup," the vertical trackers only remind), the wedge is the done-for-you filing service (not the commodity tracker software), and the one real threat is Avalara (Vista-owned) reaching down within ~2–4 quarters. The CFO is the most bullish in the entire run (8.2) — high ARPU ($3K–$8K/mo per 40-site operator) means only ~30 logos clear ~$300K/founder and ~60–80 clear ~$500K. Honest landing: 75.5/100, Tier B (top of band), REFINE-to-GO on a vertical narrowing — pick ONE vertical (fitness, childcare, car-wash, or convenience/liquor over the already-contested restaurant space), lead with the managed filing service, and run an explicit niche-dominance-or-acquisition endgame so the Avalara reach-down becomes an exit, not a death.

TOP 5 RISKS: 1) Avalara (Vista) reach-down within ~2–4 quarters — it already has the platform, managed-services motion, per-location pricing, and content pointed at restaurant permits. 2) The software half is a 90-day commodity (ExpiryEdge ships it; differentiation must rest entirely on the managed service). 3) Pricing-positioning collapse — the per-location managed price ($3K–$8K/mo) must be sold as an outcome ("you never touch a portal") vs a $39–$50/mo self-serve tracker. 4) Managed-filing labor + government-issuance latency — the "never go dark" promise against a long tail of mail/in-person/appointment jurisdictions and a gap the company is accountable for but doesn't control (COO QC 5). 5) Onboarding data-build cost overrun (inventorying 600–1,500 permits) eroding the onboarding fee.
TOP 3 STRENGTHS: best-in-field competitive position (a genuinely fragmented, sleepy operations-permit managed layer with mis-aimed giants); the strongest income math in the run (high ARPU → only ~30 logos for rung one, per CFO 8.2); and the cleanest bootstrap shape (negative working capital, ~8–12 dev-week MVP, fully remote, per-location land-and-expand, financeable/acquirable).
