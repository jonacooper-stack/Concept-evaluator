LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

Disclaimer, stated once: I am not a licensed attorney and this is not formal legal advice. I am an experienced operator with strong regulatory pattern-recognition, flagging issues the founders should investigate with retained counsel before launch.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
This is a near-ideal regulatory posture for these founders: a B2B SaaS-plus-thin-service that sits adjacent to a heavily regulated activity (Davis-Bacon / state prevailing-wage certified payroll) without the founders themselves becoming the regulated party. The contractor remains the employer, sets no wages the government hasn't already determined, and — critically — the contractor's own authorized officer signs the Statement of Compliance under penalty of perjury, so the founders are a recordkeeping/filing tool, not the perjuring/certifying party. The regulation here is overwhelmingly a demand asset (it forces the weekly buy) rather than a risk the founders must personally absorb; the residual legal work is real but routine — a professional-services agreement with a tightly drafted disclaimer-of-certification clause, E&O/tech-E&O insurance, data-security hygiene around payroll PII, and disciplined accuracy in the wage-determination logic. The one genuine exposure to manage is professional-liability/negligence if the software systematically miscalculates a wage determination or fringe credit and a contractor relies on it into a withholding, back-wage, or False Claims Act problem — a known, insurable, contractually-limitable risk, not an existential one.

RISK-GATE RATING: SERIOUS BUT MANAGEABLE (toward the lower/MINOR end)
The activity the customer performs is heavily regulated, so not a bare MINOR. But the founders' own exposure is modest and well-trodden: a software-and-filing vendor, not the employer, not the wage-setter, not the certifying officer. No occupational license, no multi-state credential to scaffold; liability insurable and contractually limitable. SERIOUS-BUT-MANAGEABLE is a due-diligence checklist, NOT a tier penalty — and this sits at the gentle end. Nothing FATAL.

REGULATORY MAP
- DOL Wage and Hour Division; Davis-Bacon Act (40 U.S.C. 3141 et seq.) + ~60 Related Acts. Mandates prevailing wage + fringes by classification and weekly certified payroll on WH-347 with a signed Statement of Compliance (29 C.F.R. Parts 1, 3, 5). Burden on the contractor; the product renders the form correctly + preserves the audit trail. The 2023 DOL Davis-Bacon final rule (eff. Oct 2023) updated determination methodology — a content-maintenance obligation.
- Copeland "Anti-Kickback" Act (18 U.S.C. 874; 40 U.S.C. 3145; 29 C.F.R. Part 3) — underlies the weekly statement + deduction rules. Implementation spec, not a license.
- State prevailing-wage ("Little Davis-Bacon") regimes — ~26+ states, each with its own determinations/forms/portals: CA DIR (Labor Code 1720 et seq.; eCPR; DAS/CMU registration), NY (Labor Law Art. 8/9, NYSDOL), WA (L&I Intent/Affidavit, RCW 39.12); also NJ, IL, MA, OH, PA, MN, CT. Variance-as-engineering-scope, not licensing exposure.
- False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. 3729) + state analogs — the serious downstream penalty for the contractor if false certified payrolls are submitted. Founders not the certifier; exposure only derivative and only if knowingly complicit/grossly negligent — controlled by certification-stays-with-officer + services agreement. The customer's pain (good for demand) and the contour of the founders' worst-case negligence theory.
- Data privacy / safeguarding payroll PII (names, addresses, SSN/last-four, wages, hours). Triggers all-50-state breach-notification + reasonable-safeguards (NY SHIELD, MA 201 CMR 17.00, CCPA/CPRA with HR/B2B nuance). No HIPAA, no GLBA. Standard SaaS infosec, within founders' SOC 2 / GDPR/CCPA experience. WH-347 needs only SSN last-four — minimizes the footprint.
- FTC/state auto-renewal (ROSCA; CA ARL; click-to-cancel) — applies to SaaS billing; low burden B2B with clear disclosure + easy cancel.
- UPL / advice line. The thin managed layer must be a bright line: the product applies published government determinations to the contractor's data; it does not render legal opinions or represent contractors before WHD/DIR. Positioned as software + clerical/administrative preparation (payroll service bureau / tax-prep analog) it is not UPL. Counsel sign-off on "done right"/"handled" language so it reads as execution accuracy, not a legal guarantee.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No occupational or industry license is required for the founders to operate. Not contractors (no contractor's license), not a PEO/employer of record (they don't pay workers or remit workers' taxes — they prepare/file from contractor-supplied data), not law/accounting practitioners. Ordinary LLC/C-corp + local business license. One item to confirm (not a blocker): some state e-filing portals require the submitting party to be a registered contractor or authorized agent (e.g., CA eCPR filed under the contractor's DIR registration). Clean design: the contractor grants the platform delegated/agent access to file under the contractor's own credentials/registration — exactly how third-party payroll/tax filers operate. An access/authorization configuration, not a license the founders hold. If a state barred third-party filing agents, fallback is "generate-the-exact-file, contractor one-clicks-submit." No credential gap beyond the already-named prevailing-wage specialist.

LIABILITY POSTURE
Center of gravity is professional liability / negligence (E&O), not regulatory enforcement against the founders. Bad day: the software applies the wrong determination, mis-maps a classification, or miscalculates a fringe credit; the contractor relies; the agency withholds payment, assesses back wages, or it feeds an FCA theory. Because the contractor's officer signs the Statement of Compliance under penalty of perjury, certification liability + FCA/perjury exposure stay with the contractor by design — the single most important liability-allocation feature, never to be blurred by marketing. Residual founder exposure is a contract/negligence claim ("your tool got it wrong and it cost me"). It is: Insurable at modest premium via Technology E&O / Professional Liability + general liability + cyber for payroll PII; Contractually limitable via a SaaS + professional-services agreement (cap at fees paid; disclaimer of warranties beyond accurate processing of data-as-supplied; "customer responsible for input-data accuracy and the certification it signs; vendor does not provide legal advice or guarantee outcomes"; indemnity for customer-data errors); Personal exposure closed by forming the entity, contracting through it, no personal guarantees. Nuance: a "thin managed service" that does the work invites a higher reliance/duty-of-care than pure self-serve — manageable (how every payroll service bureau operates), but the reason Tech-E&O + a tight scope clause are must-do-before-launch.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single risk most likely to seriously slow (not kill) this is professional-liability exposure from a systematic, embedded error in the wage-determination/fringe-credit logic that contractors rely on at scale. Certified payroll is a domain where being precisely correct is the whole value proposition: "done right, so you get paid," and the determinations are intricate (correct classification per locality, split-classification, fringe-credit math, apprentice ratios, the 2023 rule changes, ~27 jurisdictions). A wrong rule shipped across the base harms many contractors simultaneously — withheld payments, back-wage assessments, worst case an FCA theory — with the founders named in the resulting negligence/breach claims and reputational fallout. Manageable rather than fatal because of the concept's architecture: the certifying officer signs (perjury/FCA primary liability is the contractor's), and the residual vendor exposure is insurable via Tech-E&O + cyber and cappable via limitation-of-liability + scope-of-services that ties the vendor's duty to accurate processing of data-as-supplied while disclaiming legal advice and outcome guarantees. The mandate is operational + contractual discipline — robust QA on the determination engine, a clear input-accuracy-is-the-customer's-responsibility allocation, the insurance stack bound before the first paying customer, and marketing reviewed so "done right" reads as execution accuracy, not a legal warranty.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
1. Filing-agent authorization per state (CA DIR eCPR, NY NYSDOL, WA L&I): is a third party permitted to submit as the contractor's authorized agent under the contractor's registration, and what authorization docs are required? Identify any state prohibiting third-party submission → default to "generate-file, contractor-submits" there.
2. Scope-of-services + limitation-of-liability drafting: cap at fees paid; allocate input-data accuracy + the signed certification to the contractor; disclaim legal advice + outcome guarantees; indemnify for customer-supplied-data errors — confirm enforceable against business customers in the governing-law state.
3. UPL/advice line on the managed layer: confirm the workflow is administrative/clerical preparation (payroll-service-bureau/tax-prep analog), not UPL or a heightened "compliance advisor" duty; adjust marketing copy.
4. Secondary/derivative FCA + aiding-and-abetting exposure: what conduct (knowledge, recklessness, deliberate ignorance) could pull a preparer into FCA, and what design/contract/audit-trail safeguards keep the vendor on the clerical-preparer side?
5. Data-security + breach posture for payroll PII: applicable reasonable-safeguards (NY SHIELD, MA 201 CMR 17.00, CCPA/CPRA scope), whether SSN-last-four-only suffices, breach-notification obligations, DPA terms — align cyber coverage.
6. Insurance stack adequacy: confirm Tech-E&O/Professional Liability + GL + cyber respond to (a) a systematic calculation-error claim and (b) a payroll-PII breach, at what premium — bind before the first paying customer.
7. Entity formation + personal-exposure closure: form the entity, contract exclusively through it, no personal guarantees of customer compliance obligations.

The genuine residual risks (E&O if the engine is systematically wrong; multi-state variance; the advice/UPL line) are all named, navigable, insurable, contractually limitable; none approaches FATAL. Gate lands SERIOUS-BUT-MANAGEABLE at its gentle end — a due-diligence checklist, not a tier penalty.
