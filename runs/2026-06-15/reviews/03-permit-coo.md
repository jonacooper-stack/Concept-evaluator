COO REVIEW — Permit & License Renewal Management for Multi-Location Operators

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is a genuinely operable, remote-first, back-office model — no trucks, no installs, no field visits; the repeated unit of work is filing a renewal and chasing a confirmation, all from a laptop. The honest tension is not "is this field ops" (it is not) but how thin the "thin managed service" actually stays: renewals across thousands of idiosyncratic city/county/state offices are heterogeneous, manual, and resist full automation, so the managed layer is real labor that scales roughly with filings, not with software. Two founders can run the software-plus-system-of-record spine and the first ~10–30 logos themselves; the question is whether the renewal-execution layer is staffed and priced so unit economics survive the human-in-the-loop work, and whether the per-jurisdiction "playbook library" can be built faster than the long tail of weird offices grows.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday, March 17, 12-month mark. ~25 paying operators, ~30 locations each (~750 locations), ~15–25 credentials each → ~13,000–18,000 permits in the system of record. Team: two founders + one licensing/filing specialist (hired ~month 7) + a part-time offshore data/VA contractor.
7:30am — Founder A opens the portfolio dashboard; the system has flagged everything inside 45/30/15-day windows. Today: 22 renewals due this week across 9 operators (health permits, business licenses, fire/occupancy, liquor/ABC, elevator/boiler, sign permits, weights-and-measures, food-handler certs). Software has pre-filled forms + a known playbook for 16; six are new jurisdictions needing a specialist to call the office, confirm form/fee/portal, build a new playbook entry.
8:30am — Specialist works the 16 known renewals. ~9 have an online portal: log in with stored credentials, confirm pre-filled data, pay the fee (or route a payment-authorization), submit, capture confirmation, attach receipt — ~12–20 min each when the portal cooperates. ~5 are mail-in/fax-only: generate form, get the client's signer (DocuSign), print/mail or fax, set a 10-day tickler — ~25–40 min plus follow-up. ~2 are "renew in person / appear before the board" — flag for the client, document that the local manager handles the appearance.
10:30am — The six new jurisdictions. Specialist calls City of X business-license office; third call, a clerk says the renewal moved portals last quarter + the fee rose $40. Specialist documents the new process, creates a reusable playbook entry (the asset that makes the next operator in that city cheap), files it. One county health office only takes renewals by appointment, booked three weeks out — escalate to the client now (deadline in 28 days). This is the moment the model earns its fee: a spreadsheet-and-office-manager would have discovered the backlog the day the permit lapsed.
12:30pm — Founder B runs an onboarding kickoff for a new 45-location fitness chain. The painful part is the initial inventory build: which of ~700+ credentials the chain should hold, where each lives, when each renews. The VA pulled what the client could hand over (a messy spreadsheet + a folder of PDFs); Founder B walks the ops lead through gaps: 60 locations missing a current fire occupancy cert, 30 with a business-license holder name that's a dissolved entity. Billable onboarding ($2K–$10K) and the stickiness engine.
2:00pm — Confirmation-chasing block. The VA works the tickler list: 18 previously-filed renewals awaiting government issuance. Two "pending" 6 weeks; one location's old permit expires in 9 days, the new one isn't issued — Founder A decides whether to escalate or advise the client to print the receipt-of-application as interim proof. The single most operationally important hour: the promise is "your location never goes dark," and the failure mode is a permit lapsing in the gap between "we filed on time" and "the government got around to issuing it."
4:00pm — Founder A reviews the week's exceptions + SLA board. Two clients got "renewal filed — confirmation pending" emails; one got a "need your signer's authorization to pay $1,140" request. No location currently dark. Playbook library two entries richer; new-jurisdiction backlog is the recurring constraint.
Where it breaks: (a) the long tail of mail/in-person/appointment-only jurisdictions that resist automation; (b) government-side latency between filing and issuance the company is accountable for but doesn't control; (c) the onboarding data build; (d) liability anxiety if a permit lapses on the company's watch. None are field-ops failures; they're software-coverage and content-maintenance failures the founders can systematize.

CAPACITY MATH
Ladder: ~$300K/founder by mo12 (~$600K combined), ~$500K+/founder by mo24.
Mo12 revenue: ~25 operators × ~30 locations = ~750 locations; subscription ~$120/location/month → $90,000/month = ~$1.08M ARR from subscription alone, plus per-filing + onboarding. $1.0–1.2M ARR is reasonable [ASSUMED 20–30 logos, ~30 loc/logo; verify with a 10-call discovery sprint — the $75–$250/loc/mo band is load-bearing]. Combined ~$600K founder income out of ~$1.0–1.2M revenue requires margin/opex that leave ~$600K to two founders; with a thin team (1 specialist ~$70–90K, 1–2 VAs ~$30–50K, modest software) plausible IF filing labor stays bounded. mo24 ($1M+/founder) needs ~2–2.5x the location base, supported by the land-and-expand dynamic.
Filing volume at mo12: ~15,000 active permits; avg renewal cycle ~1.3 yrs → ~11,500 filings/year ≈ ~960/month ≈ ~220/week ≈ ~44/business-day [ASSUMED cycle 1.0–2.0 yrs; verify by sampling 200 real permits — swings staffing ~2x]. ~70% managed → ~31/business-day managed filings + tracking-only on the rest.
Labor: blended ~22 min/managed filing (9 portal at ~15 min, 5 mail/fax at ~35 min, occasional new-jurisdiction builds 45–60 min, plus chase overhead) [ASSUMED 15–35 min; verify by time-tracking the first 300]. 31 × 22 min ≈ ~11.4 hrs/day filing labor + lumpy onboarding builds (~0.5–1.0 FTE during active onboarding) + chasing. So mo12: ~1.5 FTE filing/chasing (1 specialist + ~0.5–1.0 VA) + two founders supervising/selling/onboarding — a lean, affordable team. Operable. [31 filings/day × 22 min = 11.4 hr/day ≈ 1.4 FTE.]
Mo24 (~2x → ~30,000 permits, ~88 filings/day, ~62 managed): 62 × 22 min ≈ ~23 hrs/day ≈ ~3 FTE filing + onboarding + chasing → ~4–5 person ops team against ~$2.0–2.5M revenue. Still small, remote, affordable. First bottleneck = the licensing-specialist judgment function, not routine portal volume. Critical leverage: as the playbook library matures, the new-jurisdiction tax falls (each new operator in a known city is mostly pre-built), so per-filing minutes should decline — the flywheel, the assumption to watch [track "minutes per filing" monthly].
Conclusion: two founders + a sub-5-person remote team can carry mo12 and mo24. Labor scales sub-linearly to revenue only if the software genuinely deflects routine filings and the library compounds; if it stays a manual filing shop, it's operable but margin-capped. Either way NOT field ops, NOT a 50-person operation.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.1 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- True remote back-office: zero field ops, zero routing/drive-time, identical cost to serve a permit in any state — the lean, location-independent shape the founders want (~44 filings/day from one desk).
- Lean, affordable, hireable team carries the target: two founders + ~1 licensing specialist + 1–2 VAs covers mo12 (~1.4 FTE filing labor shown), scaling to a sub-5-person remote team at mo24; no scarce/credentialed labor.
- Naturally smoothed, sticky workload: staggered government renewal clocks level the queue year-round (no seasonality), while the hand-built system of record + onboarding inventory makes the relationship deeply sticky and the playbook library compounds into operational leverage.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- The "thin" managed service is thicker than it looks: heterogeneous long-tail jurisdictions (mail/fax/in-person/appointment-only) resist automation and keep filing labor closer to linear-with-volume, compressing margin unless the playbook library genuinely deflects routine work over time.
- Accountability for government-side latency the company does not control: the gap between "we filed on time" and "the office issued the permit" is where a location can still go dark, and the company owns that promise — a high-stakes QA + escalation burden on every filing.
- Onboarding data-build is labor-heavy and uncertain at the start: discovering which of 600–1,500 credentials a 40-location operator actually holds, where each lives, when it renews, is genuine investigative work that gates time-to-value and consumes specialist/VA hours before recurring revenue stabilizes.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Whether the renewal-execution layer stays bounded enough to keep this a software-margin business rather than a labor-margin filing shop — specifically the long-tail-plus-latency combination. The capacity math says two founders + a sub-5-person remote team can do the volume; what's uncertain is the minutes-per-filing trajectory. If the playbook library compounds and the software auto-prepares an ever-larger share of routine portal renewals, per-filing minutes fall, margins expand, and the bottleneck stays the small judgment-heavy specialist function — a great business. If the work stays dominated by idiosyncratic mail/fax/appointment jurisdictions and one-off "the office changed the process again" rebuilds, labor scales close to linearly and the business is operable but margin-capped, and worse, the company is contractually/reputationally on the hook for a permit lapsing in the issuance gap it can't fully control. That single dynamic — heterogeneous long-tail filings the company is accountable to close, against a promise of "no location ever goes dark" — is where two people's QA attention gets stretched and where unit economics are decided. Not a field-ops killer; a labor-linearity-and-liability question, answerable with data from the first ~300 real filings.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- The real measured distribution of renewal channels across a representative portfolio — what share renew via online portal (automatable) vs mail/fax (semi-manual) vs in-person/appointment-only (cannot be done remotely)? Pull 200 real permits from 3 prospect portfolios and classify each; this decides whether "thin managed service" holds.
- Average renewal cycle length and resulting steady-state filing volume per 1,000 permits, and minutes-of-labor per location per month? Time-track the first 300 filings (capacity swings ~2x on the assumed cycle).
- How is "no location goes dark" scoped in the contract relative to government-side latency you don't control — guarantee on-time filing (controllable) or continuous validity (partly uncontrollable), and what interim-proof/escalation playbook covers the issuance gap?
- For onboarding, how long/how many labor-hours to build a complete verified permit inventory for a 40-location operator, and is it recoverable in the $2K–$10K fee? Run two paid pilot onboardings.
- Will minutes-per-filing actually fall as the library grows (a real compounding flywheel), or is each new operator's footprint mostly new jurisdictions? Track "% of this operator's jurisdictions already in the library" at each new logo.

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Operationally a clean fit: remote-first back-office work, no field ops/routing/seasonality, a lean and hireable team, replaceable vendors, and shown capacity math letting two founders + a sub-5-person remote team carry both mo12 and mo24. The two real soft spots — long-tail jurisdiction heterogeneity (delivery repeatability) and the high stakes of the "never go dark" promise against government issuance latency (quality control) — are managed risks, not structural killers, and both are cheaply testable before scaling. Proceed and, in parallel, run the three measurement experiments (channel-distribution sample, first-300-filings time study, two paid pilot onboardings) so the staffing ratio and minutes-per-filing trajectory are grounded in data rather than the optimistic "thin" framing; if those come back portal-dominant + library-compounding, the operational profile is strong; if mail/fax/appointment-heavy, the fix is to price the managed tier higher or scope it tighter, not to change the fundamentally sound delivery model.
