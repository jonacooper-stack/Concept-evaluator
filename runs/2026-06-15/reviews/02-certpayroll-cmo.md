CMO REVIEW — Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is a genuinely sellable, well-shaped must-have with one of the cleanest forcing functions I've reviewed: on any Davis-Bacon or state-prevailing-wage job, a wrong or late weekly certified payroll means the contractor's progress payment is withheld, and repeat violations bring fines up to ~$13,508 per violation and debarment for up to three years — the buy is forced, weekly, recurring, and already budgeted against ~$217B/yr of covered federal/federally-assisted construction spend (DOL Fact Sheet #66; Workyard). Willingness to pay is already proven: eBacon lists at ~$1,000/mo, Miter at ~$50/mo + $6.50/employee, and Points North/LCPtracker sell custom-priced into this exact problem. The catch the founders must internalize: LCPtracker is paid by the prime/agency and is the mandated destination subs are forced to submit into — often free to the sub but clunky — so the wedge is not "replace the portal," it is "make feeding the portal painless, error-free, and done-for-you" for the lean sub back office. That reframing is the difference between a sharp wedge and a head-on collision with a category that already has modern, well-built players (Miter, eBacon).

POSITIONING DRAFT
For the contractor: "Certified payroll, done for you every week — so your progress payment never gets held up on a public job." For the prime/GC (secondary buyer + referral engine): "Every sub's certified payroll, clean and on time, before you have to chase it." Real edges because it anchors on the consequence the buyer already fears (held payment) and a job-of-getting-paid rather than a feature. The trap: "certified payroll software" is NOT ownable — eBacon, Points North, eMars, LCPtracker, Miter, CertifiedPayrollPro all say it. The defensible wedge in the language is "done-for-you / handled" + "so you get paid," aimed at the sub too small/lean to want another DIY portal. Position as "better certified-payroll software" and they lose; position as "we run your prevailing-wage paperwork so you don't lose a payment" and they win.

THE WEDGE
First 100 from three concrete, low-cost vectors, in priority order. (1) Prime-referral / pull-through. The prime is the guarantor of subs' prevailing wages and is on the hook if a sub's certified payroll is missing — so primes already chase subs weekly (AZDOT). A prime that adopts "we'll get your subs compliant" hands you a warm list of 10–40 subs per project; cheapest channel, compounds. (2) Trigger-event outbound around award notices. Public bid awards are public record (SAM.gov, state/municipal bid boards, Dodge feeds); a sub that just won a prevailing-wage job is in acute pain and reachable by name — targeted outbound + a "first certified payroll done free" offer; [ASSUMED] $150–$400 per qualified sub, verifiable with a 200-contact test. (3) Trade-association + niche content. NECA (118 chapters), MCAA (~2,700 firms), SMACNA (~3,500 firms), plus state SEO ("California DIR certified payroll help," "WH-347 done for you") where legacy vendors rank thin and rely on demo-gated funnels with no free trial. Blended CAC plausibly $300–$1,200 against $3,600–$14,400 first-year contract value + $500–$2,000 onboarding — LTV/CAC clears comfortably if retention holds. Real and founder-fit.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- LCPtracker — the agency/prime-side standard, the mandated submission portal primes/DOTs require subs to file into; paid by the prime, not chosen by the sub. You don't displace it, you make the sub's side painless and feed it cleanly. LCPcertified targets self-performing subs, but the brand is built for primes watching a hundred subs.
- eBacon — modern, construction-specific DIY platform (time + certified payroll + fringe), ~$1,000/mo entry. Best at full mid-market construction payroll. Well-built — a strong player, not sleepy — but DIY software, not done-for-you, and prices out the smallest subs.
- Miter — modern, VC-flavored construction payroll + prevailing wage, ~$50/mo + $6.50/employee, Procore-marketplace integrated. Clean UX, ERP integrations. Strong modern competitor; competes on software, not service.
- Points North (Certified Payroll Reporting) — multi-portal compatible (LCPtracker, eMars, TRS), ADP/Workday/Paychex integrations, offers managed services; old-line, demo-and-contract, no free trial. Beatable on marketing/packaging.
- eMars / eComply, Foundation Software, CertifiedPayrollPro ($49/mo) — eMars legacy compliance tool; Foundation construction accounting emitting WH-347; CertifiedPayrollPro a low-end modern SaaS undercutting the old guard.
- Encroachment watch: Procore — native Time Cards + certified-payroll/Davis-Bacon features + app marketplace; could reach down, but sells up-market to primes/GCs and leans on partners (Miter) for payroll depth — real but not imminent to the small-sub done-for-you niche.
Where is the air? In the small-to-mid specialty subcontractor (5–150), done-for-you "handled" tier — below eBacon's price/complexity, above spreadsheets, explicitly not asking a one-bookkeeper office to learn another portal. Modern players sell DIY software; legacy players sell to primes/agencies or via demo-gated funnels. A productized "we run it for you, every week, so you get paid" with sharp positioning + prime-referral distribution is genuine open air — but air in a crowded room with two fit competitors in it.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.8 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- Textbook forcing function tied directly to cash flow: no compliant certified payroll = withheld payment, with documented fines (up to ~$13,508/violation) and debarment (up to 3 yrs) — buyer forced, weekly, already budgeted.
- Willingness to pay proven at the concept's exact price points by named competitors (eBacon ~$1,000/mo, Miter ~$50/mo+$6.50/employee, CertifiedPayrollPro $49/mo, Points North/LCPtracker custom) — entering a market that already transacts.
- A distribution gift: the prime/GC is the contractual guarantor of the sub's prevailing wages and already chases subs weekly, making prime-referral pull-through a warm, compounding, low-CAC channel fitting the founders' relationship-led edge.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- The category has modern, well-built competitors already in it (Miter, eBacon) — not the "sleepy incumbents only" ideal; differentiation rests on packaging/service, copyable in ~6–12 months by a funded player.
- Positioning/word-collision risk: "certified payroll software" claimed by 6+ vendors; market on the feature rather than the "done-for-you, so you get paid" wedge and you become the seventh undifferentiated entry.
- The LCPtracker reality: the mandated portal is paid by the prime and supplied to the sub, so many subs already have a free (clunky) submission path — sell prep + accuracy + done-for-you, not portal access, or the buyer asks "why pay when the agency already gave me a login?"

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The most likely market-side killer is getting trapped in the "certified payroll software" feature war instead of owning the "done-for-you, so you never lose a payment" service wedge — squeezed from two sides. From above, eBacon and Miter are modern, construction-specific, well-funded-feeling DIY platforms that already generate WH-347s, validate prevailing wages, and push to state portals; out-feature them and you lose (the outspend/out-engineer fight the founder profile says to avoid). From below, the smallest subs may shrug "the agency already gave me an LCPtracker login for free" and not grasp that the portal doesn't do the prep, classification, fringe math, and error-catching that actually gets payments held up. The concept survives only if the founders relentlessly position on the managed outcome (we run it, we catch the errors, you get paid on time) and the channel (prime pull-through + award-trigger outbound) rather than software parity. If messaging drifts to "a better certified-payroll tool," the wedge collapses into a crowded, partially-modern field and CAC balloons. The good news: positioning + relationship-led distribution is exactly the founders' wheelhouse — but it is make-or-break.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- For the smallest subs handed a free agency LCPtracker login, what is the crisp, demonstrable reason to pay $300–$1,200/mo? (Hypothesis: the portal doesn't do prep/classification/fringe math/error-catching — prove with 8–10 sub interviews + a "held-payment story" case.)
- Will primes/GCs actively refer or co-sell this to subs (clean subs = prime gets paid faster), and on what terms (referral, reseller, prime-pays-for-subs)? Test with 5 prime conversations — the low-CAC thesis hinges on it.
- How defensible is the done-for-you service layer against Miter/eBacon adding a managed tier, and Procore reaching down via its marketplace — what accrues to you over 18 months (data, prime relationships, audit-trail history, multi-state determination library) a fast follower can't cheaply replicate?
- Which beachhead state(s) first — start where the pain + portal-clunkiness are worst and the consequence is sharpest (California DIR eCPR, plus NY/WA) — and does multi-state rule maintenance need a named prevailing-wage specialist on day one?
- Real blended CAC + onboarding effort per sub from a 200-contact outbound test + one NECA/MCAA/SMACNA chapter webinar — does it hold the LTV/CAC math at $300–$1,200/mo?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
A strong, evidenced concept with a documented must-have, proven willingness to pay at the concept's own price band, a fragmented reachable buyer, and a distribution channel (prime pull-through) most niches would envy — all squarely in the founders' positioning-and-relationship wheelhouse. GO, not slam-dunk, for one reason: unlike a purely sleepy market, this category already contains two modern, capable competitors (Miter, eBacon), so the win depends on executing the wedge, not the feature set. To convert GO into conviction: (1) lock positioning on "done-for-you, so you get paid" and refuse "better software"; (2) build GTM around prime-referral pull-through + public award-notice trigger outbound rather than generic content/paid; (3) pick a high-pain beachhead state (California DIR obvious first) and a named prevailing-wage specialist to scaffold the rules. Do those three and this is a defensible, recurring, capital-light business with a clean painkiller at its core.
