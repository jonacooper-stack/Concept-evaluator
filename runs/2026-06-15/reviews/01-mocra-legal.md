LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Cosmetics Regulatory Compliance Program (MoCRA)

Not formal legal advice. I am an experienced compliance operator with strong regulatory pattern-recognition, flagging issues the founders should brief real FDA/regulatory counsel on — not a licensed attorney resolving them.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ

This is a compliance-services business sitting on top of a brand-new, clearly-written federal statute (MoCRA, codified at FD&C Act §§ 604–607, 21 U.S.C. §§ 364–364h), and the single most important structural fact is the one the one-pager already nails: the founders are NOT the manufacturer and NOT the legal "responsible person." That means FDA's hard enforcement hammers — mandatory recall authority, facility-registration suspension, adulteration/misbranding liability — fall on the brand/manufacturer, not on the service vendor. The founders' own risk reduces to standard professional-services exposure: negligence/errors-and-omissions if they file something wrong, miss a 15-business-day adverse-event deadline, or whiff a safety-substantiation gap, plus ordinary B2B contract, data, and advertising-of-their-own-services concerns. There is no license to practice cosmetics compliance, no bar admission required to prepare FDA registrations, and the founders' SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR background is more than enough to scaffold this regime; the path is named, well-trodden, and bridgeable. The real residual risks are E&O liability concentration (single point of failure on a deadline-driven obligation), the unsettled-rules problem (GMP and several MoCRA pieces still being finalized), and the temptation to drift into giving legal/safety conclusions that look like unauthorized practice of law — all contractually and operationally manageable.

RISK-GATE RATING: SERIOUS BUT MANAGEABLE

Real E&O and accuracy-of-filing exposure plus an actively-evolving rule set, but the regime is federal, named, single-jurisdiction at the substance level, the founders are explicitly NOT the responsible person (heavy FDA enforcement liability lands on the client), and the whole posture is contractible and insurable via a standard tech-E&O / professional-liability policy. A due-diligence checklist, not a tier penalty — and the risk here is materially lower than for most regulated-services plays precisely because the founders sit beside, not inside, the regulated obligation.

REGULATORY MAP
- FDA — MoCRA (FD&C Act §§ 604–607; 21 U.S.C. §§ 364–364h), administered by CFSAN. The core forcing function, but for the founders it is the client's obligation they facilitate. Facility registration + biennial renewal; product/ingredient listing kept current; safety substantiation records; serious-adverse-event reporting within 15 business days with six-year retention; new fragrance-allergen labeling; forthcoming GMP regulations (statutory deadline already slipped). FDA does not license or accredit third-party filers — no gate to entry, but no safe-harbor shield; accuracy is on the founders by contract.
- FDA — Adulteration/Misbranding/Recall & Suspension authority (§§ 601–602; § 605 recall; § 607 suspension). The teeth, and they bite the brand/manufacturer. Relevant to founders only as the consequence the client is buying down.
- FTC — Section 5 (advertising of the founders' OWN service). Must not over-promise ("guarantees FDA approval" — FDA does not "approve" cosmetics). Ordinary marketing hygiene.
- Unauthorized Practice of Law (50-state patchwork). Preparing/submitting FDA registrations is administrative/regulatory-affairs work, not law practice (like customs brokers, tax preparers, patent agents, existing RA consultants). Giving a legal conclusion ("your label is legally compliant," "you are not liable") edges toward UPL. Manageable with scope discipline and disclaimers.
- Practice of "regulatory affairs" — NO licensing regime. No federal/state license to be a cosmetics regulatory consultant.
- Data privacy — CCPA/CPRA, other state laws, GDPR (only if EU brands/EU consumer data), and adverse-event PII. Adverse-event intake means consumer health-adjacent reports — sensitive but NOT HIPAA-covered (cosmetics brands are not covered entities; founders are not business associates). Ordinary-to-sensitive B2B SaaS data handling; six-year retention aligns with the regulation.
- Recordkeeping/evidentiary integrity. Founders store client safety dossiers and adverse-event records FDA can demand — data durability, audit trail, six-year retention are contractual obligations.
- Professional liability/E&O (insurance market). Standard tech-E&O/miscellaneous-professional-liability covers "we filed it wrong/missed a deadline." Readily insurable.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No occupational or industry license is required. Preparing/submitting FDA facility registrations and product listings on a client's behalf does not require bar admission, FDA accreditation, or any state credential — there is no licensing regime for cosmetics regulatory-affairs providers, exactly as there is none for the hourly consultants the founders replace. The only licensing-adjacent constraint is the soft UPL boundary: prepare/organize/submit/track compliance, but do not render legal opinions or "certify" legal compliance — held with contract scope and disclaimers, not a license. The named regulatory-affairs advisor supplies substantive judgment for safety-dossier review; the founders' SOC 2/HIPAA/GDPR background plus that advisor is a fully sufficient scaffold. Standard entity formation + home-jurisdiction business license is the entire footprint. Confidently: no special license needed.

LIABILITY POSTURE
The founders' exposure is professional-services E&O, concentrated rather than catastrophic. Dangerous fact pattern: a client suffers an FDA enforcement event (suspension halting sales, recall, adverse-event-reporting failure) and points at the founders' platform/service ("you were supposed to file the renewal / report within 15 business days / catch the label defect"). Because the obligation is deadline-driven and the founders run the workflow, a missed biennial renewal or blown 15-business-day clock is a plausible claim. Squarely insurable: tech-E&O/miscellaneous professional liability with adequate limits, plus cyber for the adverse-event PII store. Contained contractually with (a) scope schedule placing the legal responsible-person duty on the client, (b) client data-accuracy/cooperation covenants, (c) liability cap at fees paid, (d) not-legal-advice + no-FDA-guarantee disclaimers, (e) acknowledgment that final accountability remains with the client. Left over: reputational risk if a marquee client is suspended on the founders' watch, and residual judgment risk on safety-substantiation review (why the named advisor must own sign-off and E&O limits should be sized to it). Pre-entity personal exposure is the ordinary "form the LLC/corp before the first client" point. Net: a normal professional-services profile, made safer by the founders not being the responsible person.

- Unsettled/evolving rules (GMP + shifting FDA enforcement posture) — building against a moving target with an ongoing duty to track FDA rulemaking.
- UPL/scope-creep into legal or safety conclusions — the safety-substantiation review is the most opinion-laden deliverable and must be owned by the named advisor with disciplined disclaimers.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The issue most likely to seriously slow (not kill) this is professional-liability concentration on the deadline-and-judgment workflows the founders take over. MoCRA obligations are recurring and time-boxed — biennial renewals, continuously-current listings, adverse-event reports due within 15 business days with six-year retention — and the value prop is "we handle these for you." The founders voluntarily become the operational single point of failure on obligations whose failure mode for the client is severe (a suspended registration halts sales). When a client suffers an enforcement event, the founders are a natural target for an E&O claim — "you were retained precisely to prevent this" — even though final legal accountability remains with the client. Manageable, not fatal, because it is a well-understood, well-insured pattern, bought down with (1) tech-E&O/cyber sized to the worst plausible client, (2) a liability cap at fees paid + client-cooperation/data-accuracy covenant, (3) hard QA gates and redundant deadline-tracking so a single human miss can't cause a silent default, and (4) the named RA advisor owning safety-substantiation sign-off. The founders must treat E&O posture and deadline-integrity engineering as core product, not an afterthought.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
1. UPL boundary in key states (CA, NY, FL, TX): does preparing/submitting FDA cosmetics registrations and reviewing labels fall safely within non-lawyer regulatory-affairs practice, or do any deliverables cross into UPL? What scope/disclaimer language keeps us clear?
2. Responsibility allocation & E&O contract: draft a services agreement leaving the responsible-person duty with the client, capping liability at fees paid, imposing data-accuracy covenants, disclaiming legal advice and FDA-outcome guarantees — and test whether the cap survives a missed-statutory-deadline claim.
3. E&O/cyber insurance scope: what form and limits cover "we filed late/wrong and the client was suspended/recalled," and do they cover the adverse-event PII store? Close exclusions.
4. Adverse-event data handling: confirm not a HIPAA business associate; map CCPA/CPRA (and GDPR-if-EU) obligations; set a six-year retention/secure-disposal posture.
5. Submitting to FDA on a client's behalf: lawful basis, authentication, authorized-agent designation (power of attorney), and any FDA position on third-party submitters.
6. Advertising of our own service: review claims so we never imply FDA "approval"/certification/guarantee (FTC §5).
7. Rulemaking-tracking obligation: contractual + standard-of-care duty to keep clients current as FDA finalizes GMP and adjusts enforcement; limit liability for changes in law between renewals.
