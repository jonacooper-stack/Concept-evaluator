COO REVIEW — Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
Yes — two founders plus a lean remote team can operate this, and the operational shape is genuinely favorable. The unit of work is a recurring software-generated weekly artifact (the certified payroll report), not a truck roll or an install; once wage determinations and classification mappings are set up per contractor at onboarding, the steady-state weekly work is software-validated data plus a thin human exception-handling layer. The two real operational hazards are not field ops — they are (a) the genuinely high-judgment, specialist-heavy onboarding (mapping wage determinations, fringe-credit logic, apprenticeship ratios per state) and (b) the multi-jurisdiction "form/portal zoo" where every state agency has its own form, rules, and filing destination, so the product's coverage map IS the operational liability. Manage those two and this is a lean, remote, software-leveraged book of recurring revenue with strong retention mechanics.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday in March, ~12-month mark. [ASSUMED ~70 active accounts, range 50–90.] Public-works contractors run weekly payroll; the certified payroll is due ~7 days after the pay date, so Mon/Tue is the heavy filing window — busiest day, matters for capacity.
Morning: The system already ingested last week's payroll for integrated accounts (QuickBooks/Foundation/Sage 300 CRE/ADP); import accounts uploaded a payroll export Monday. Overnight the engine applied each project's wage determination by classification, computed prevailing wage + fringe per worker, validated apprenticeship ratios, generated the per-job WH-347 / per-state form (CA DIR XML for eCPR, NY, WA…). Dashboard shows per contractor: green (ready), yellow (flag), red (missing data/week).
The ops founder opens the exception queue. [ASSUMED 15–30% of accounts surface ≥1 exception/week.] Typical: a worker coded to a classification with no matching determination; fringe credits not reconciling; apprentice-to-journeyman ratio out of compliance; a project missing its current determination after an agency modification. Each is a 5–20 min task: message the bookkeeper, correct a mapping, or pull the updated determination and re-run. High-skill ones escalate to the named PW specialist.
Midday: Clean (green) accounts filed/pushed to the mandated destination — WH-347s to the prime/agency channel, CA reports to DIR eCPR, others to their portals or the prime's required format. Audit trail timestamps every submission. "Missing week" radar pings two contractors who haven't uploaded — ops nudges them; because a missing week stalls their progress payment, they respond fast (the forcing function does the founders' collections).
Afternoon: One new contractor mid-onboarding — the heavy lift, NOT self-serve at this stage. The specialist (or specialist-scaffolded founder) enumerates active public jobs, pulls correct determinations per job (by county, bid date, funding source — federal Davis-Bacon vs state vs both on the same job), and builds the classification-to-determination map. [ASSUMED 6–15 hours of skilled onboarding labor per contractor.] This is why the $500–$2,000 onboarding fee exists and should skew high for multi-state contractors.
Where it breaks: (1) a state without full form/portal support — a contractor wins a job there and the product can't file; (2) an agency changes its portal/format/rules mid-year and the integration silently breaks until caught; (3) a misclassification the software passed that the agency later rejects — a QC miss that erodes trust and creates rework. None are field-ops failures; they're software-coverage/content-maintenance failures the founders can systematize.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- True software leverage on the recurring unit: ~50,000 weekly reports/yr machine-generated + machine-validated, humans only on exceptions — delivery cost per artifact trends to zero, the opposite of a field-ops grind.
- Zero physical logistics, fully remote: no trucks/installs/routing/on-site visits in steady state — squarely the capital-light, remote-first shape the founders want; avoids the near-disqualifier entirely.
- The forcing function does the founders' worst ops chores: a missing/late/wrong certified payroll directly withholds the contractor's progress payment, so customers respond instantly to "missing week" nudges and exception requests — retention + data hygiene enforced by the customer's own cash-flow pain.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- The multi-jurisdiction form/portal zoo: every state regime has its own form/rules/fringe logic/filing destination, and they change; the product's value equals its coverage map — perpetual content/engineering burden, not a one-time build.
- Onboarding is specialist-heavy and the true throughput governor: ~6–15 skilled hours each; the named PW specialist is the single hire the whole model leans on.
- Silent-correctness risk: software can validate a report an agency later rejects (stale determination, edge case), producing rework and eroded trust with a buyer who adopted this to stop worrying about getting paid.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to bite is the cost and fragility of multi-jurisdiction rules-and-filing maintenance — the form/portal zoo. The promise is "filed correctly to the mandated destination, every week," and that destination, form, fringe math, and validation rules differ for federal Davis-Bacon and for each state (CA DIR eCPR, NY, WA, dozens more), with agencies periodically revising forms/portals/determinations. The leverage that makes the capacity math work — software generating 50,000 reports/yr unattended — is only as good as the underlying rules content and live portal connectors, and that content doesn't maintain itself: when a state changes its portal or DOL issues determination modifications, filings can silently fail or generate wrong until a human catches and patches it. So "national coverage" is an ongoing engineering-plus-domain liability, not a finished asset, and the realistic play is to dominate one or two high-volume jurisdictions first (federal + California is the obvious wedge) and expand deliberately, never over-promising states the product doesn't truly cover. Not a founder-fit problem (it's software + content the founders can systematize) and not field ops — but mismanaged, it converts a clean low-touch model into a brittle, support-eating one.

- Real onboarding labor profile: time the first 10–20 onboardings — skilled hours per contractor by job/state count, what fraction must be the specialist vs a trainable ops person, and does the $500–$2,000 fee cover it (may need to scale with job/state count)?
- Measured exception rate per contractor per week and support-ticket rate per account per month once live (the low-touch model hinges on these staying inside ~10–35% exceptions and ~0.3 tickets/account/month)?
- Where do payroll inputs come from for the target buyer — which payroll/accounting systems (QuickBooks, Foundation, Sage 300 CRE, ADP, Viewpoint) do the ~5–150-employee subs run, and is it integration or weekly import for each (determines ingestion engineering + weekly data friction)?
- Named PW specialist's actual availability/cost (employee, fractional, per-engagement) and a bench/backfill so the most specialized dependency isn't a one-person failure point?
