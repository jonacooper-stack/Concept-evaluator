CMO REVIEW — Cosmetics Regulatory Compliance Program (MoCRA)

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is a genuinely forced, recurring buy in a fast-growing, fragmented segment — that part is real and well-evidenced: a federal mandate, biennial renewals, continuous listing, and retailers (Sephora, Ulta, Target, Amazon) now asking for proof of listing at vendor onboarding. But MoCRA is not a sleepy, unserved market — it is a land-rush market that already has a clear 800-pound gorilla in Registrar Corp (30,000+ FDA customers, 1,000+ cosmetics clients, its own Cosmetri software) plus a dozen functioning competitors (REACH24H, Biorius, Obelis, Willow Glen, i3CGlobal, Cosmeservice, Gegahelix) and a pure-software cohort (SG Systems V5, QT9, TotalSDS, Valent). The positioning ("MoCRA, handled") is clean and the founders can absolutely out-market the sleepy à-la-carte filers — but the differentiation is a packaging/UX difference an incumbent can match in a quarter, the price point the concept assumes ($3K–$12K/yr) sits 3–8x above the visible transactional market ($399–$1,500/yr), and the buyer-of-record is awkwardly split between brand and contract manufacturer. Sellable and positionable: yes. Owning durable air against a funded, named incumbent who got there first: that is the open question.

POSITIONING DRAFT
"MoCRA, handled — the done-for-you FDA program for beauty brands: we register, list, build your safety files, and run your 15-day adverse-event reporting for one fixed annual price, so you never lose Sephora, Amazon, or a shipment over a compliance gap." Clean and edged, better than incumbents' feature-soup ("FDA Registration & U.S. Agent"). The problem is not the sentence — it is that the sentence is available to be copied verbatim by Registrar Corp tomorrow, because nothing in it is structurally yours. Excellent positioning of an undifferentiated underlying service.

THE WEDGE (three, ranked)
1. Contract-manufacturer / private-label co-packer channel partnerships (the sharpest wedge). One co-packer makes products for dozens of indie brands and is already the registered facility for them; the natural batch-distribution node, asked the MoCRA question by every client. Sign 10–20 co-packers as referral/white-label partners → acquire brands in batches. CAC ~[ASSUMED] $300–$800/acquired brand after partner rev-share; verify by closing 2–3 co-packer partners in 60 days. The single best reason this concept is fundable.
2. Trade shows where the buyer stands at a booth. Cosmoprof North America (~30K+ attendees, the dominant Americas B2B beauty show) + the indie-founder circuit. ~$5K–$15K/show [ASSUMED]; verify with one show + tracked leads.
3. SEO/content against high-intent MoCRA queries + the retailer-readiness moment ("MoCRA compliance," "do I need to register with FDA to sell on Amazon"). Incumbents' content is dry; a free "Are you MoCRA-compliant?" checklist is cheap compounding top-of-funnel.
Caveat: every channel is also visible to Registrar Corp — the wedge buys a head start and a positioning edge, not a structural lock.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- Registrar Corp — the gorilla to out-position. 30,000+ FDA customers, 1,000+ cosmetics clients, 20+ years, U.S.-agent infrastructure, already owns the software angle via Cosmetri. NOT a sleepy legacy consultancy — a functioning, well-positioned, multi-vertical FDA-compliance machine. Biggest dent in the "fragmented sleepy market" thesis.
- REACH24H / Biorius / Obelis.us — global regulatory houses (EU cosmetics origin) extended into MoCRA; out-credential the founders on safety science.
- Transactional filer cohort — Willow Glen, Cosmeservice, i3CGlobal, Gegahelix, Assurentry (~$399/yr U.S. agent), FDA Registration Assistance (~$1,449/yr facility + ~$799/listing), à-la-carte stacks (~$1,500). THESE are the sleepy, beatable, poorly-marketed players: cheap one-time filing, no program, ugly sites — the air you breathe.
- Pure-software/QMS — SG Systems V5, QT9, PSC, TotalSDS, Valent — aimed at manufacturers' quality systems, not done-for-you for a 10-person DTC brand.
- Law firms — Morgan Lewis, Wiley, B&D Law — expensive top end; don't want the $5K indie account.
Where the air is: the productized, fixed-price, ongoing-program middle for the indie-to-mid brand — above the cheap filers, below the law firms, packaged as a managed subscription. Real gap; threat is the gorilla can step into it.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.0 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- Textbook forced, recurring demand with a hard deadline cadence, reinforced by retailers (Sephora, Ulta, Target, Amazon) checking proof of listing at onboarding; 589,762 listings already filed proves the spend is live.
- A genuinely sharp, founder-fit wedge in the co-packer channel (one co-packer = dozens of brands), converting partnership/positioning strength into low-CAC compounding acquisition.
- Clean positioning into a real packaging gap (the indie-to-mid managed middle, above $1,500 filers, below law firms).

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- A capable, named incumbent already owns the category and the software angle (Registrar Corp + Cosmetri) — the opposite of the founders' ideal "sleepy incumbent" setup; "MoCRA, handled" is copyable by a fast follower with an installed base.
- Price-anchor compression: visible market clears at $399–$1,500/yr; concept assumes $3K–$12K/yr. If buyers anchor to "$399 to register," realized ACV and the income ladder shrink.
- Split, confused buyer-of-record: the brand (responsible person) owns listing + adverse-event reporting; the contract manufacturer owns facility registration. Selling one "done-for-you" package across two parties creates messaging friction.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The framing rests on "sleepy incumbents in a fragmented market," and the evidence only half-supports that. The cheap, ugly transactional filers are indeed sleepy and beatable — but sitting on top of them is Registrar Corp, a 20-year, 30,000-customer FDA-compliance company serving 1,000+ cosmetics clients, already owning compliance software (Cosmetri), already ranking for the queries, with U.S.-agent infrastructure. MoCRA is a 2023–2024 land-rush, not a forgotten backwater, so the most capable competitor is awake, funded, and ahead — and the founders' one edge (superb positioning) is exactly what that competitor can replicate in a quarter and push through a larger installed base. The differentiation is packaging, not structure; the only durable moat (switching cost on a brand's full FDA posture) accrues after you've won the account. The decisive question is not "can we position it?" (yes) but "can we acquire and lock a defensible base through the co-packer channel faster than Registrar Corp closes the fixed-price-program gap?"

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Will brands actually pay $3K–$12K/yr when visible filers charge $399–$1,500? What in the managed layer commands a 3–8x premium, and can you prove it with 5–10 paid LOIs before building? (Fake-door pricing page + 20 founder calls.)
- Will contract manufacturers/co-packers partner — or compete? Sign 2–3 co-packer partnership LOIs in 60 days.
- What is Registrar Corp's exact cosmetics offer, price, and renewal motion today, and where are they demonstrably weak for the indie segment (a named, ownable gap)?
- How do you sell one package across a split buyer-of-record — who signs, brand or co-packer, and how do you message "done-for-you" when each owns half the obligation?
- Does the retailer-onboarding trigger actually force the buy or just nudge it? Get on record from 3–5 buyers at Sephora/Ulta/Target/Amazon what compliance proof is required to be stocked.

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The demand is real and well-evidenced (9), positioning sharp (8), wedge genuinely founder-fit (8), GTM fits founders (8) — a legitimately attractive concept, not deflated. But held from a clean GO by two market facts the framing understates: a capable named incumbent (Registrar Corp) already owns the category and the software angle, and the assumed price sits well above the visible transactional market. To convert from "out-marketing $1,500 filers" into a defensible business, refine on three axes: (1) lead the entire GTM with the co-packer channel and lock 2–3 white-label partnerships first; (2) validate the $3K–$12K price with paid LOIs and pin the premium to the ongoing managed layer, not the commoditized one-time filing; (3) sharpen the wedge to a named segment where Registrar Corp is structurally weak (e.g., sub-50-SKU indie brands needing an ongoing program). Nail those and this is a strong B heading toward A; skip them and it's a well-marketed reseller of a commoditized filing in a market the leader already owns.
