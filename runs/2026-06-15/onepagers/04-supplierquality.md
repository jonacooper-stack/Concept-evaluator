CLEAN-ROOM ONE-PAGER — Supplier-Quality Document Management for Manufacturers

CUSTOMER
Small-to-mid manufacturers — "Tier-2" and "Tier-3" suppliers, job shops, and component
makers, typically 10–250 employees — that sell parts, components, sub-assemblies, or
processed materials to much larger manufacturers and OEMs ("primes") in automotive,
aerospace, defense, industrial equipment, and medical-device supply chains. The core buyer
is the quality manager, operations lead, or owner who is personally responsible for keeping
the company on the prime's approved-supplier list. These firms are numerous, regional, and
unglamorous; most run their quality paperwork on a mix of Excel, shared drives, paper
binders, and email, and many are too small to justify a heavy enterprise quality system.

THE MUST-HAVE / FORCING FUNCTION (contractual / counterparty requirement)
To win and keep a purchase order, the prime contractually REQUIRES its suppliers to produce
and maintain a stack of quality documentation, and to submit it on the prime's schedule and
into the prime's systems. The recurring obligations include: PPAP packages (Production Part
Approval Process — the multi-document submission a prime requires before a part may ship),
First Article Inspection Reports (FAIRs), material/mill certifications and Certificates of
Conformance with every lot, current ISO 9001 / IATF 16949 (automotive) / AS9100 (aerospace)
certificates, control plans, PFMEAs, calibration records, and timely responses to Supplier
Corrective Action Requests (SCARs) and to the prime's periodic supplier-quality audits and
scorecards. The forcing function is the customer relationship itself: if a required document
is missing, expired, or rejected, the prime will not release the purchase order, will issue
a chargeback for a line-down, will flag the supplier on its scorecard, or will drop the
supplier from the approved list entirely — losing not one PO but the entire account. The buy
is forced by the contract (you cannot ship without it), recurring (every new part, every
lot, every audit cycle, every certificate renewal), and already budgeted as a non-negotiable
cost of doing business with that customer. No supplier-quality manager needs to be convinced
the problem exists — keeping the documentation current and audit-ready is a standing source
of pain and headcount.

PRODUCT
A productized "supplier-quality documentation, handled" subscription: a system of record plus
a thin managed-service layer. The platform (a) holds every quality document, certificate, and
material cert in one place with expiration tracking and automatic alerts before anything
lapses; (b) provides guided, templated workflows to assemble the documents a prime requires —
a PPAP/FAIR package builder that walks the supplier through each required element; (c) helps
push the finished packages into the OEMs' supplier portals and keeps a submission/audit trail;
(d) tracks SCARs and corrective actions to closure; and (e) gives an audit-readiness dashboard
showing "every customer requirement met / what is expiring / what an auditor will ask for." A
managed-service layer (founder-led, scaffolded by a named quality-systems specialist) sets up
the supplier's document library and assembles the most complex PPAP packages. Software does the
high-volume tracking and templated assembly; the specialist handles the gnarly first-article
and audit-prep judgment.

REVENUE MODEL
Recurring SaaS subscription per supplier site, roughly $300–$1,500/month depending on the
number of customers/parts and the level of managed help, billed monthly or annually, plus a
one-time onboarding/setup fee (~$1,500–$5,000) to build the initial document library and
templates. Optional per-package fees for done-for-you PPAP/FAIR assembly. Revenue recurs for
as long as the supplier holds the account (i.e., continuously), and is sticky because the
document history, templates, and audit trail become the supplier's quality system of record.

POSITIONING
"Never lose a purchase order over missing paperwork." The supplier-quality document system of
record and done-for-you PPAP/certification management built for the small-to-mid manufacturer
that supplies demanding OEMs — replacing the binder, the shared drive, the frantic
pre-audit scramble, and the enterprise quality suites that are too heavy and too expensive for
a 50-person shop. It turns the standing risk of getting dropped from an approved-supplier list
into one dashboard and a reliable routine.
