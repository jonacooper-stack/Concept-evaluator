CLEAN-ROOM ONE-PAGER — Cosmetics Regulatory Compliance Program (MoCRA)

CUSTOMER
U.S. beauty and personal-care brands and the contract manufacturers / private-label
co-packers who make their products. The core buyer is the brand owner / "responsible
person" at an indie-to-mid-size cosmetics brand that sells more than ~$1M/yr of
cosmetics (above the federal small-business threshold, so the full regime applies) —
typically a DTC or retail skincare, color-cosmetics, haircare, or fragrance brand with
5–150 employees. Tens of thousands of such brands exist and the segment has grown fast
with the DTC/"clean beauty" boom. Secondary buyer: contract manufacturers who want to
offer compliance as a value-add to the brands they produce for.

THE MUST-HAVE / FORCING FUNCTION (regulatory mandate)
A 2022 federal law (the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act) for the first time
imposes mandatory, recurring FDA obligations on cosmetics companies, phased in from
2023–2024 and ongoing: (1) register every manufacturing/processing facility with FDA
and renew the registration every two years; (2) list every marketed cosmetic product
and its ingredients with FDA and keep listings current; (3) maintain adequate safety
substantiation records for each product; (4) receive and report serious adverse events
to FDA within 15 business days and retain records for six years; (5) meet new labeling
requirements and forthcoming Good Manufacturing Practice rules. FDA now has mandatory
recall authority and can suspend a facility's registration — which stops the brand from
distributing product. The buy is forced (it is federal law, not optional), recurring
(biennial renewals, continuous product listing as SKUs change, ongoing adverse-event
monitoring), and increasingly checked by retailers and marketplaces that ask brands to
prove FDA registration before they will stock them. If a brand ignores it, the fast,
concrete consequences are FDA enforcement, a registration suspension that halts sales,
and getting dropped by retail buyers who require proof of compliance.

PRODUCT
A productized "MoCRA compliance, done-for-you" subscription: a web platform plus a thin
managed-service layer. The platform (a) prepares and submits facility registrations and
product/ingredient listings to FDA's electronic submission system and tracks biennial
renewals on a compliance calendar; (b) generates and stores safety-substantiation
dossiers from guided templates mapped to each product's ingredients; (c) runs an
adverse-event intake form, triage, 15-business-day reporting workflow, and six-year
record retention; (d) reviews labels against the required elements and flags gaps; and
(e) gives the brand a single dashboard showing "you are compliant / here is what's due."
The managed-service layer (founder-led, scaffolded by a named regulatory-affairs advisor)
handles onboarding, the initial product-listing bulk load, and review of safety
dossiers. Software does the high-volume, repeatable submission and tracking work; the
expert handles judgment calls.

REVENUE MODEL
Annual subscription, tiered by number of SKUs and facilities: roughly $3,000–$12,000/yr
for a typical brand (small catalog at the low end, large catalog / multiple facilities
at the high end), plus a one-time onboarding fee of ~$1,000–$3,000 for the initial
registration and listing load. Optional add-on for managed adverse-event monitoring.
Revenue is recurring and renews with the regulation; switching away means re-papering a
brand's entire FDA compliance posture, so retention is expected to be high.

POSITIONING
"MoCRA compliance, handled." The done-for-you FDA registration, product-listing,
safety-substantiation, and adverse-event program built specifically for beauty brands —
so a founder stays FDA-legal and retail-ready without paying a regulatory consultant by
the hour or trying to decode the law themselves. The alternative today is expensive
hourly regulatory consultants and law firms, or brittle DIY spreadsheets; this replaces
both with a sharp, modern, fixed-price product aimed at brands the big consultancies
don't bother to serve well.
