CLEAN-ROOM ONE-PAGER — Permit & License Renewal Management for Multi-Location Operators

CUSTOMER
Businesses that operate multiple physical locations, each of which must hold and
continuously renew a stack of government permits and licenses to legally stay open.
The core buyer is the operations, compliance, or finance lead (or the owner) at a
multi-location operator with roughly 5–500 sites — for example restaurant and
food-service groups, fitness/gym chains, childcare and senior-care operators, urgent-care
and dental groups, auto-service and car-wash chains, convenience and liquor retailers,
and franchisees/franchisors. Each location carries many credentials: a general business
license, health permit, fire/occupancy permit, alarm permit, signage permit, weights-and-
measures, food-handler and manager certifications, liquor/tobacco licenses, elevator and
boiler certificates, and any profession-specific licenses — each issued by a different
city, county, or state office, each on its own renewal clock. A 40-location operator can
easily carry 600–1,500 individual permits. These operators are numerous and fragmented,
and most track this today in spreadsheets, a shared drive, or one overworked person's memory.

THE MUST-HAVE / FORCING FUNCTION (operational continuity / license to operate)
A permit or license is the legal right to operate a location. If one lapses, the
consequences are fast and concrete: the location can be cited, fined daily, fail an
inspection, be forced to stop serving (a restaurant that lets its health permit or liquor
license lapse cannot legally serve food or alcohol), or be shut down outright — and a
closed location bleeds revenue every day it is dark while also risking the brand. Renewals
are not optional, they recur on fixed government schedules (often annually or biennially,
staggered so that something is always coming due), and they are already a budgeted cost of
operating. The pain is purely operational continuity: nobody has to be convinced the
problem exists — every multi-location operator has a story about a near-miss or an actual
lapse that cost them a location's revenue, an emergency expedite fee, or an inspection
failure. The risk grows with every new location opened, and it is precisely the kind of
low-glamour, high-consequence back-office obligation that falls through the cracks of a
growing operator.

PRODUCT
A productized "every location stays open" subscription: a system of record plus a thin
managed-renewal service. The platform (a) inventories every permit and license across all
locations with its issuing authority, number, cost, and renewal date; (b) tracks every
renewal deadline on a single calendar and escalates well before due dates; (c) stores the
documents and proof-of-compliance for inspections and audits; (d) provides
jurisdiction-specific renewal checklists and pre-filled forms; and (e) shows a portfolio
dashboard — "every location current / what is expiring in the next 90 days / what is at
risk." A managed-service layer (founder-led, scaffolded by a licensing specialist and/or
filing partners) actually prepares and submits the renewals on the operator's behalf and
chases the confirmations. Software does the high-volume tracking and form generation; the
service handles the filings and the jurisdiction quirks.

REVENUE MODEL
Recurring subscription priced per location (and/or per permit volume): roughly
$75–$250 per location per month for the platform, plus per-filing fees for managed
renewals (or an all-in managed tier). A typical 40-location operator lands around
$3,000–$8,000/month. An onboarding fee (~$2,000–$10,000) covers the initial inventory and
data build for all existing permits. Revenue recurs as long as the operator runs locations,
grows automatically as they open new sites, and is sticky because the platform becomes the
system of record the operator cannot easily reconstruct elsewhere.

POSITIONING
"Never lose a location to a lapsed permit." The single system of record and done-for-you
renewal service for multi-location operators — replacing the spreadsheet, the shared drive,
the overworked office manager, and the expensive law firm or generalist filing service that
treats each renewal as a one-off. It turns a sprawling, invisible, high-consequence
back-office liability into one dashboard and a quiet monthly routine.
