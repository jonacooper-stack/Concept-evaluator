# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A recurring managed service plus system-of-record that keeps construction
contractors continuously "bondable" — maintaining the work-in-progress (WIP)
schedules, interim financial statements, cash-flow and backlog reporting, and
covenant documentation that surety underwriters require to grant and grow a
contractor's bonding capacity — and presenting them in the way that maximizes the
capacity the surety will extend, so the contractor can bid the bigger and the
public jobs that bonding gates. The service runs the recurring reporting cadence
to the contractor's surety and bond agent, and continuously manages the WIP and
financial picture that drives the bonding decision.

## The customer
Privately-owned construction contractors that must be bonded to win work: general
contractors and specialty trades (mechanical, electrical, concrete, sitework,
roofing) doing public, institutional, and larger private projects, typically
$5M–$150M in annual revenue. Bonding capacity — the aggregate and single-job bond
limit the surety will write — directly caps how much and how large a contractor
can bid, and therefore caps revenue and growth. The buyer is the owner, the CFO,
or the controller. They already work with a bond agent and a construction CPA,
but the WIP and interim reporting is typically done late, in spreadsheets, and
without an eye to what actually moves the underwriter — leaving bonding capacity
(and bids) on the table.

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- Surety underwriters REQUIRE recurring, current WIP schedules, interim financials,
  and covenant/backlog reporting to maintain and increase bonding capacity; stale,
  messy, or pessimistically-presented reporting directly limits the bond line.
- Bonding capacity is the hard ceiling on bidding: a contractor that cannot get a
  bond for a $20M public job simply cannot bid it — the lost backlog is large,
  immediate, and visible to the owner.
- The reporting recurs continuously — monthly/quarterly WIP, interim statements,
  annual reviews, and a fresh package every time the contractor wants a capacity
  increase or a large single-job bond — so the burden never ends.
- It is real money against growth: a contractor whose bonding capacity is $10M
  short of the job it wants is losing a specific, nameable project.

## The product
A fixed-scope recurring service on one delivery spine:
1. Onboarding: build the contractor's bonding-readiness system-of-record — WIP
   schedule, percentage-of-completion math, interim financials, backlog, cash
   flow, and the surety's specific requirements — and produce a clean,
   underwriter-optimized initial package.
2. A continuous WIP and bonding system-of-record the contractor's office actually
   runs on: job-cost and percentage-of-completion tracking, over/under-billings,
   backlog, covenant monitoring, and an accumulating, anonymized benchmark of what
   underwriting actually rewards (the financial ratios, WIP presentation, and
   disclosures that grow capacity) drawn across all clients.
3. Recurring managed cadence: keep the WIP and interim financials current and
   accurate every month/quarter, prepare and deliver the recurring reporting
   package to the surety and bond agent, flag covenant and ratio issues before the
   underwriter does, and assemble the package for every capacity-increase or
   large single-job bond request.
4. Capacity-growth work: identify and fix the specific WIP/financial-presentation
   issues that are suppressing the contractor's bond line.
The software runs the WIP/percentage-of-completion engine, the reporting calendar,
the covenant/ratio monitoring, and the benchmark; a named expert (a former surety
underwriter or construction CPA) applies the judgment on presentation and
capacity strategy. The contractor keeps its own bond agent and its own CPA (who
still issues reviewed/audited year-end statements); this service is the continuous
WIP-and-bonding-readiness layer between them. No field work.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription per contractor, scaled by the size of the bonding program
and job-cost volume (mid four to low five figures per month, i.e. low-to-mid five
figures per year), billed monthly, optionally with a component tied to documented
bonding-capacity increases. Revenue is structurally recurring because WIP and
interim reporting are perpetual, capacity reviews recur, and the system-of-record
becomes the contractor's operational backbone.

## Positioning
"Bond bigger. Your bonding capacity decides which jobs you can bid — and stale,
spreadsheet WIP is quietly capping it. We keep your WIP and financials underwriter-
ready every month and present them to grow your bond line, so you can bid the jobs
you're being shut out of." Sold against the contractor's overworked controller
doing WIP in Excel, the construction CPA who shows up quarterly, and the bond
agent who places the bond but does not manage the contractor's financial story.

## Go-to-market
Marketing/positioning-led demand generation into contractor owners/CFOs (content
on bonding capacity, WIP traps, and the financial moves that grow a bond line;
targeted outbound to growth-stage bonded contractors), plus founder-led close,
plus partnerships with bond agents and sureties who want their contractors better-
reported and more bondable (more capacity written = more premium) and with
construction-focused CPA firms. Bond agents are a strong referral channel because
a better-reported contractor is an easier, larger account for them.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders strong in marketing/positioning, software, finance, and
operating formal reporting programs, supported by a named surety-underwriting /
construction-accounting expert for bonding-presentation judgment. Remote-first, no
field operations.
