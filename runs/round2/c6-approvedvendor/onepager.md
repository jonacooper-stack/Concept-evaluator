# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A recurring managed service and system-of-record that keeps specialty-trade
service contractors continuously "approved-vendor ready" across all the national
property owners and facility managers they work for — maintaining their
certificates of insurance, additional-insured endorsements, W-9s, lien waivers,
safety statistics (EMR/OSHA logs), licenses, and background-check attestations,
and keeping each buyer's vendor-qualification portal current — so the contractor
never gets removed from a dispatch list, never has a work order held, and never
has an invoice blocked for a lapsed document.

## The customer
Privately-owned specialty-trade and facility-service contractors that depend on
national/regional property owners, REITs, retail chains, and third-party facility
managers for the bulk of their revenue: commercial HVAC/refrigeration, electrical,
plumbing, roofing, landscaping, janitorial, fire-and-life-safety, parking-lot and
signage service firms — typically $2M–$50M in revenue with work spread across
dozens of client locations. They live or die by staying on their customers'
approved-vendor and dispatch lists. The buyer is the owner, the controller, or the
operations manager. They already pay (to one or more vendor-credentialing portals,
and in lost time and blocked invoices); they have no one whose job is to keep it
all current.

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- National property owners and facility managers CONTRACTUALLY require their
  service vendors to maintain continuous, specific insurance limits, additional-
  insured and waiver-of-subrogation endorsements, current W-9s and lien waivers,
  EMR/safety thresholds, and license currency — verified through vendor-
  qualification portals — as a condition of receiving (and being paid for) work.
- A single lapsed COI or an expired endorsement gets the contractor flagged
  "non-compliant," which means work orders stop routing to them and, critically,
  invoices for completed work get HELD until the document is fixed — a direct,
  immediate cash hit.
- A growing contractor serves many such customers, each with its own portal,
  rules, and renewal cadence; the burden multiplies and recurs continuously as
  policies renew, jobs close, and new customers onboard.
- This is real money against payroll: a held invoice or a lost dispatch list is
  cash and revenue the owner feels immediately.

## The product
A fixed-scope recurring service on one delivery spine:
1. Onboarding: build the contractor's single "vendor passport" system-of-record —
   all insurance policies and endorsements, W-9, licenses, EMR, safety programs,
   and every buyer relationship and its specific requirements — and bring every
   buyer portal current.
2. A continuous portal that is the contractor's single source of truth: it tracks
   every document's expiry, every buyer's distinct requirements, and the live
   "compliant / at-risk" status per customer, and it accumulates a structured
   library of what each major property owner / facility manager actually requires
   (a dataset that makes onboarding the next contractor faster and the next
   requirement change easy to absorb).
3. Recurring managed cadence: monitor expirations, coordinate with the
   contractor's insurance agent to get renewed COIs and the exact endorsements
   each buyer demands, push updated documents into each buyer's portal, generate
   lien waivers per job, and resolve "non-compliant" flags before they hold an
   invoice.
4. Revenue-protection reporting: surface held/at-risk invoices and get them
   released.
The software runs the requirement library, expiry tracking, per-buyer status, and
document routing; humans handle the agent coordination, the endorsement chasing,
and the exception resolution. No field work. The contractor keeps its own
insurance agent and its customers; this service is the back-office that keeps the
contractor on every list.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription per contractor, scaled by the number of buyer
relationships and documents managed (typically low-to-mid five figures per year
for a contractor juggling many national accounts), billed annually or monthly in
advance, optionally with a performance component tied to released held invoices.
Revenue is structurally recurring because insurance renews annually, endorsements
must be reissued, lien waivers are per-job and continuous, and every new customer
the contractor wins adds a managed relationship.

## Positioning
"Stay on every list. Stop losing work and getting invoices held over a lapsed
certificate. We keep your insurance, endorsements, waivers, and every customer's
vendor portal current — so the work and the payments never stop." Sold against
the contractor's overworked office manager doing it in a spreadsheet, the
insurance agent who issues a COI but does not manage the buyers' portals, and the
buyer-side credentialing portals that police compliance but do nothing to help the
contractor achieve it.

## Go-to-market
Marketing/positioning-led demand generation into specialty-trade contractors
(content on getting off "non-compliant" status, endorsement traps, and held
invoices; targeted outbound to contractors who serve national accounts;
partnerships with insurance agents who write contractors' commercial policies and
want a value-add, and with trade associations), plus founder-led close. Insurance
agents are a natural referral channel because the service makes their COIs land
correctly and keeps their clients happy.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders strong in marketing/positioning, software, and operating
formal compliance/insurance programs, supported by a named commercial-insurance /
vendor-credentialing practitioner advisor (a former agency account manager or
vendor-compliance specialist) for endorsement and requirement judgment. Remote-
first, no field operations.
