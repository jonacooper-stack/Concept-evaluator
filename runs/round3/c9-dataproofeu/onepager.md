# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A recurring managed service that makes US-based small and mid-size B2B software
and services companies "EU-data-ready" so their deals stop stalling — standing in
as their required EU/UK data-protection representative, producing and keeping
current the data-processing agreements (DPAs), records of processing (ROPA),
subprocessor lists, transfer documentation (SCCs), and the security/privacy
questionnaire answers that their European and enterprise customers demand before
signing — and answering the recurring buyer due-diligence that arrives every
renewal. It is anchored by a shared, accumulating library of pre-cleared DPA
clauses, subprocessor assessments, and buyer-questionnaire answers that gets
faster and stronger as more customers join.

## The customer
US-based B2B SaaS and digital-services companies, roughly $2M–$50M revenue and
20–300 employees, that sell into the EU/UK or into large enterprises that impose
EU-grade data terms. The buyer is the founder, the head of sales/revenue, or a
general-counsel-of-one. They are NOT privacy specialists. They hit the wall the
moment a European customer (or a US enterprise's procurement/security team) sends
a DPA to sign, asks "who is your Article 27 EU representative?", demands a
subprocessor list and SCCs, or sends a 200-question security-and-privacy
questionnaire — and the deal sits frozen until it is answered.

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- EU/UK law (GDPR Article 27 / UK GDPR) REQUIRES many US companies that offer
  goods/services to, or monitor, EU/UK individuals to appoint a local data-
  protection representative — and European customers and partners increasingly
  refuse to sign until one is named and a compliant DPA is in place.
- The pain is ACUTE and revenue-blocking: a signed contract is held hostage to a
  DPA redline, a missing subprocessor disclosure, or an unanswered privacy
  questionnaire — the founder feels the stalled deal immediately.
- It RECURS continuously: every new EU/enterprise customer sends its own DPA and
  questionnaire, subprocessor lists change, SCCs and transfer rules shift, ROPA
  must be kept current, and the representative appointment is an ongoing
  obligation that must be maintained, not a one-time filing.
- It is budgeted as a cost of doing business: losing or delaying enterprise/EU
  deals is a direct, large revenue event, so the buyer will pay to unblock it.

## The product
A fixed-scope recurring service on one delivery spine, software-led:
1. Onboarding: appoint the company's EU/UK representative, build its ROPA,
   subprocessor register, standard DPA, and transfer documentation, and assemble
   its reusable security/privacy questionnaire answer set.
2. A shared, accumulating answer-and-clause network: a structured library of
   pre-negotiated DPA clause positions, vetted subprocessor assessments (the same
   subprocessors — AWS, Stripe, etc. — recur across every client), and
   buyer-questionnaire answers, so each new customer's DPA redline or
   questionnaire is answered faster and from a stronger, precedent-backed base.
   The network compounds: the more clients and the more buyer questionnaires that
   flow through, the larger and more authoritative the reusable answer set.
3. Recurring managed cadence: serve as the standing EU/UK representative (receive
   and triage data-subject and authority inquiries), keep ROPA and subprocessor
   lists current, and turn around each inbound customer DPA redline and security/
   privacy questionnaire on a fast SLA so deals never stall.
4. Deal-desk support: a fast lane for the sales team to get a DPA signed or a
   questionnaire returned within days.
The software runs the clause/answer library, the subprocessor register, the
questionnaire-response engine, and the representative-inbox workflow; humans apply
judgment on novel redlines. The client remains the controller/processor and signs
its own DPAs; this service prepares, represents, and responds. No legal opinions
are rendered as a law firm; genuinely novel legal questions are routed to the
client's counsel. Low-sensitivity data only (clause text, subprocessor lists,
questionnaire answers) — not the client's customer personal data.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription per client, tiered by company size and deal volume
(low-to-mid five figures per year), billed annually or monthly in advance, with
the EU/UK representative appointment, ongoing ROPA/subprocessor maintenance, and a
included allotment of DPA/questionnaire turnarounds, plus per-questionnaire
overage for high-volume sellers. Revenue is structurally recurring because the
representative obligation is continuous, buyer due-diligence recurs every renewal,
and the answer-library access is the reason to stay.

## Positioning
"Stop losing EU and enterprise deals to data terms. We are your EU
representative, we keep your DPA, subprocessor list, and privacy answers ready,
and we turn around every customer's data redline and security questionnaire in
days — so the contract gets signed." Sold against expensive privacy law firms
that bill hourly and slowly, standalone Article-27-representative box-tickers that
do nothing else, and the founder's own panicked weekend trying to answer a
procurement questionnaire.

## Go-to-market
Marketing/positioning-led demand generation into US B2B SaaS founders/revenue
leaders (content on "the DPA that's blocking your EU deal," Article 27, and
enterprise security questionnaires; targeted outbound to US SaaS companies
expanding into Europe), plus founder-led close, plus partnerships with the
fractional-GC, SOC 2, and sales-enablement ecosystems that touch the same buyer
at the same moment. The founders' hands-on GDPR/CCPA compliance-operator
background is the credibility wedge.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders with direct, hands-on experience running GDPR/CCPA and
SOC 2 compliance programs, strong in marketing/positioning and software,
supported by a named EU-qualified privacy practitioner (for the representative
function and novel-redline judgment) and EU-based points of presence for the
representative role. Remote-first, no field operations.
