# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A recurring managed service plus shared grantee due-diligence network that keeps
private foundations and donor-advised-fund sponsors continuously meeting their
mandatory grant-compliance obligations — running the required pre-grant
charity-status and OFAC/anti-terrorism checks, expenditure-responsibility
documentation for grants to non-public-charities, foreign-grantee equivalency
determinations, and the recurring grantee reporting that the IRS rules require —
and, critically, letting the same grantee be vetted ONCE and that verified
diligence reused by every foundation that funds it, instead of each foundation
re-running the same checks on the same nonprofits every year.

## The customer
Privately-run small and mid-size grantmaking organizations: independent and
family private foundations (roughly $5M–$500M in assets) and the sponsors and
program teams behind donor-advised funds and small community foundations. The
buyer is the foundation's executive director, program director, or grants
manager. They are legally required to perform pre-grant diligence on every
grantee, to exercise and document "expenditure responsibility" on grants to
organizations that are not US public charities, and to keep current
records — but they run it on spreadsheets, a part-time grants administrator, and
their accountant, and they live in fear of a grant that later turns out to fund
the wrong organization.

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- IRS rules MANDATE grant-compliance steps as a condition of the foundation's
  tax-exempt operation: pre-grant inquiry, OFAC/anti-terrorism screening, and —
  for grants to non-public-charities and foreign organizations — full
  expenditure-responsibility procedures (pre-grant inquiry, written grant
  agreement, separate-account tracking, and recurring grantee reports) or
  equivalency determinations. A self-dealing or improper grant triggers excise
  taxes and is a finding on the foundation's annual 990-PF.
- The pain is ACUTE and recurring: every grant cycle (often quarterly or
  continuous) forces fresh diligence on each grantee, expenditure-responsibility
  grantees must file recurring reports that the foundation must collect and
  document, and a single missed or botched check is a real, nameable compliance
  failure the ED is personally accountable for to the board.
- It is wildly duplicative: thousands of foundations fund the same well-known
  nonprofits and re-run identical charity-status, OFAC, and public-charity
  checks on each one, every year — pure repeated overhead.
- It is budgeted: grant compliance and grants-management are existing line items,
  and the cost of getting it wrong (excise tax, board embarrassment, a clawed-back
  or reputationally toxic grant) is far larger than the service fee.

## The product
A fixed-scope recurring service on one delivery spine, software-led:
1. Onboarding: set up the foundation's grant-compliance workflow and import its
   active grantee roster; run the current diligence (charity status via IRS data,
   OFAC/anti-terrorism screening, public-charity vs. expenditure-responsibility
   determination) and produce a clean compliance file per grantee.
2. The shared grantee due-diligence network (the moat): each grantee's verified
   diligence package — charity status, OFAC clearance, public-charity
   determination, equivalency where applicable — becomes a reusable record that
   any foundation in the network can rely on (with appropriate re-verification
   timestamps), so the same nonprofit is vetted once and reused many times. Because
   foundations overwhelmingly fund an overlapping universe of grantees, the
   network's coverage and freshness compound rapidly with every foundation and
   every grant added — genuine two-sided pull where more funders make the verified
   pool more valuable and more grantees get covered.
3. Recurring managed cadence: re-screen grantees on the required cadence, generate
   and track expenditure-responsibility grant agreements and the recurring grantee
   reports, refresh OFAC and charity-status checks, and keep the per-grant
   compliance file audit-ready for the 990-PF and the board.
4. Board/audit support: produce the grant-compliance documentation package the
   foundation's auditors, counsel, and board ask for each year.
The software runs the screening workflow, the IRS/OFAC data checks, the
expenditure-responsibility document generation and report tracking, and the
shared-record matching; humans handle the judgment calls (equivalency
determinations, ambiguous status, edge-case grantees). The foundation makes and
owns every grant decision and signs its own filings; this service performs and
documents the diligence the foundation directs. Low-sensitivity data only (public
charity-status data, grant records, organization-level screening) — no donor PII
beyond business contacts, no individual personal data of beneficiaries.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription per foundation, tiered by grant volume and the share of
expenditure-responsibility/foreign grants (mid four to low five figures per year),
billed annually in advance, with per-expenditure-responsibility-grant or
per-equivalency-determination overage for heavy international funders. Revenue is
structurally recurring because diligence must be refreshed every cycle,
expenditure-responsibility reports recur for years per grant, and the network
access is the reason to stay.

## Positioning
"Never let a bad grant onto your 990. We run every pre-grant check, handle your
expenditure-responsibility paperwork, and keep every grantee audit-ready — and
because we've already vetted the nonprofits you fund, your diligence takes minutes
instead of weeks." Sold against the foundation's overworked grants manager doing
it in spreadsheets, the foundation's law firm billing hourly for
expenditure-responsibility and equivalency work, and legacy grants-management
software that tracks grants but does not perform the diligence.

## Go-to-market
Marketing/positioning-led demand generation into foundation EDs/program officers
and grants managers (content on expenditure responsibility, OFAC grant screening,
equivalency determinations, and 990-PF compliance; targeted outbound to private
foundations identifiable from public 990-PF data; presence in regional grantmaker
associations and philanthropy-infrastructure groups), plus founder-led close, plus
partnerships with the fiscal sponsors, community foundations, and philanthropy
advisors who serve the same buyers. Because the network's value rises with funder
density, early concentration within a region or a grantmaker association
accelerates coverage.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders strong in marketing/positioning, software, and operating
formal compliance programs, supported by a named nonprofit/foundation-tax
practitioner (a former foundation grants-compliance specialist or nonprofit
attorney) for the equivalency and expenditure-responsibility judgment. Remote-
first, no field operations.
