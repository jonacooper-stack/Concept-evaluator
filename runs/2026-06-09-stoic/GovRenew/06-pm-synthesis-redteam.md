# SYNTHESIS — GovRenew
Date: 2026-06-09
Council members reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO

## ONE-PARAGRAPH PM READ
GovRenew is a real market-pulled must-have wrapped around a commercially fragile anchor and a legally dangerous differentiator. The pain is genuine and revenue-gating: a lapsed SAM.gov registration or a botched 8(a)/HUBZone/WOSB/SDVOSB recertification locks a set-aside firm out of awards and payments; the buyer list with renewal dates is public and free; the build is a boring 6–9 week CRUD-plus-scheduler wedge; delivery is remote and capital-light — squarely in the founders' lane. But two facts are load-bearing and both cut against it. First, the headline credential (SAM renewal) is free and self-service, and the GovCon community is openly hostile to paying for it, so the entire ARPU thesis depends on an unproven reframe to "set-aside eligibility insurance." Second, the very thing that justifies the premium tier — a non-attorney specialist reviewing eligibility before attestation — imports the single most-prosecuted fraud regime in federal procurement (False Claims Act / 18 U.S.C. 1001) plus 50-state UPL exposure, both fraud-excluded by E&O and reaching founders personally. The mechanical service is a clean GO; the concept as scoped is a REFINE, because its differentiator and its pricing power are in direct tension — the part that justifies the price is the part that can sink the founders.

## TOP 5 RISKS (RANKED)
1. FCA / 18 U.S.C. 1001 / UPL exposure from the eligibility-attestation layer (Legal) — the only catastrophic, uninsurable, founder-personal risk; a single blessed front-fraud file converts a clean subscription into a fraud co-defendant. Reduce: re-scope to process/document/deadline service that does NOT opine on eligibility; route eligibility-legal questions to a retained attorney; client-screening; disciplined marketing.
2. WTP collapse against the FREE SAM.gov substitute (CFO+CMO) — ARPU may compress to ~$3,500. Reduce: 500-record outbound test + 50 priced conversations leading with certification-loss, on the process-only framing.
3. First-renewal retention / "got-organized" churn (CFO) — LTV-dependent; graduation from 8(a) churns. Reduce: cohort the first 20 renewals; stickiness via the held archive + multi-credential bundling.
4. Catastrophic-failure asymmetry + client-data dependence (COO) — a $6K fee insures six-figure damage triggered partly outside GovRenew's control. Reduce: client-response SLAs with carve-outs, 60–90 day buffers, E&O, specialist sign-off gate.
5. Thin defensibility / copyability (CMO scored 5) — copyable in months. Reduce: speed-to-brand, archive switching cost, locked APEX/PTAC referral channels.

## TOP 3 STRENGTHS
- The qualified buyer list AND the buying trigger are both public and free (SAM.gov, DSBS, VetCert) — an almost unfair, perfectly-timed outbound wedge (CMO 9).
- Capital-light, negative-working-capital, non-seasonal, remote, easy-build (CFO 9/9, COO 9, CTO 9).
- A genuine open competitive seam: no incumbent owns the productized, fixed-fee, recurring multi-credential lifecycle for the small set-aside firm.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
Can the business charge a four-figure recurring fee that holds at ~$6K ARPU WITHOUT performing the eligibility-review function that creates the FCA/UPL exposure? The CMO says the premium reframe to "set-aside eligibility insurance" is what lifts ARPU off the free-SAM floor; Legal says that same eligibility-vouching is precisely what must be removed to avoid becoming a fraud co-defendant. If a process-only service can still command ~$6K on reliability + archive + never-lapse insurance, it's a GO; if the only thing justifying the premium over free-DIY is someone vouching "you qualify," the safe version is a low-ARPU commodity and the lucrative version is uninsurable.
