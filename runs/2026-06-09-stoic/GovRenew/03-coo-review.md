COO REVIEW — GovRenew

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is one of the more operable concepts I've seen for two software-capable founders. The unit of work is a recurring administrative compliance task — renew SAM.gov, file SBA recerts, update reps-and-certs, archive records — mostly document collection, form completion, and deadline tracking, all remote, no inventory, no trucks, no field visits, and naturally distributed across the calendar by each entity's SAM anniversary rather than spiking seasonally. The genuine tension is that the founders are NOT GovCon specialists and the eligibility-review piece (size standards, 8(a) economic-disadvantage attestations, HUBZone principal-office tests) is where a wrong answer creates real client liability, so the whole model leans on one named compliance specialist whose judgment does not scale by software alone. Whether two founders plus that specialist can carry the recurring per-client data-gathering load at a few hundred clients is the question; my read is yes for a lifestyle-to-strong-income business, with the specialist as the first and most important bottleneck.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday in March, ~12-month mark, [ASSUMED] ~120 active clients (verify against pipeline). The deadline engine flags the week's queue overnight: 4 SAM registrations expiring within 45 days, 1 WOSB annual attestation due, 1 HUBZone recert window opening, 2 entity-validation tickets stuck in SAM's "legal business name doesn't match IRS/D&B" purgatory. Founder A (ops/software) works the dashboard: the system auto-generated document-request checklists for the 4 SAM renewals and emailed each client a secure upload link for updated ownership, NAICS, financials, address changes. Two clients uploaded; two haven't responded in a week so the system queued a second nudge and Founder A calls to unstick them — this client-chasing is the real recurring labor, not the form-filling. Mid-morning Founder A is inside SAM.gov for a ready client, working the renewal screens and rep-and-cert questionnaire, copying validated data from the archived record so it isn't re-keyed. The entity-validation tickets are the landmine: one client changed registered agent and SAM rejects the entity match → back-and-forth with the Federal Service Desk that can take days to weeks, entirely outside GovRenew's control; Founder A documents it, sets expectations, escalates. The named specialist (contractor or part-time W-2) spends the afternoon on the two liability items: confirming the WOSB owner still holds 51% and unconditional control before attestation, and assessing whether the HUBZone client still meets the 35%-employees-in-a-HUBZone test + principal-office requirement — judgment work, the part clients actually pay for. Founder B (marketing/sales) is off the delivery line running outbound to firms scraped from SAM expiring in 60–90 days plus a PTAC/APEX call. Where the day breaks: (1) a client who went dark and whose SAM lapses anyway — GovRenew owns the optics even though the client withheld data; (2) an eligibility edge case the specialist isn't sure about, which may need outside counsel; (3) SAM/SBA portal behavior changing or going down, stalling the queue.

CAPACITY MATH
Ladder: ~$300K/founder mo 12 (~$600K combined), ~$500K/founder mo 24 (~$1M combined). Scored vs the ladder, not "$2M".
Revenue: blended ARPU [ASSUMED] ~$6,000/yr (verify via 20 discovery calls). ~$600K revenue ≈ 100 clients; ~$1M ≈ 167 clients (REVENUE, not take-home).
Per-client annual labor [ASSUMED, verify with a 10-client time-and-motion log]:
- SAM renewal + rep-and-cert upkeep: ~3–5 hrs incl. client data-chasing.
- One SBA annual attestation or recert: ~2–6 hrs (8(a) annual review and HUBZone recert heavier; WOSB attestation lighter).
- State/local registrations: ~1–3 hrs.
- Eligibility review by specialist: ~1–2 hrs/client/yr (set-aside firms).
- Monitoring / ad-hoc change events: ~1–2 hrs.
≈ ~10 hrs/client/yr blended, of which ~1.5 hrs specialist-judgment, ~8.5 hrs founder/coordinator.
At 167 clients (~$1M/mo-24): total = 1,670 hrs/yr. Specialist-judgment = 167 × 1.5 = ~250 hrs/yr — well under one FTE, so ONE specialist comfortably covers eligibility review at this scale (specialist is NOT the ceiling until several hundred clients). Execution/coordination = 167 × 8.5 = ~1,420 hrs/yr ≈ 0.9 FTE → one founder part-time + one offshore/junior coordinator; the second founder stays on marketing/sales.
Load distribution: anniversary-dated renewals spread the work ~1,670/50 ≈ 33 delivery hrs/week at 167 clients — smooth, not a Q1 cliff. The model's best operational feature.
Honest hedge: 167 small clients at $6K is a LOT of small relationships, and revenue ≠ income — at ~60–65% net margin after specialist/coordinator/tooling, ~$1M revenue nets the founders meaningfully under the combined ~$1M income target, so hitting the mo-24 founder-income rung likely requires higher ARPU (more $10–15K multi-cert clients) or more clients. Operations can carry it; the income target is the strain.

3. Logistics tractability: 9 — Fully remote, zero field ops/routing/inventory; portal-and-document work from anywhere in the US — exactly the non-field-ops shape the founders require.

5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: 6 — Rides federal portals whose UI/validation rules/policies change unilaterally (the 2022 SAM Unique Entity ID transition is precedent); can't switch providers, but the underlying compliance obligation persists, so demand is durable even when the workflow churns.
6. Hiring feasibility: 6 — Core dependency is ONE credible GovCon eligibility specialist (ex-PTAC counselor, ex-SBA, GovCon compliance consultant); the pool is real but not deep, and a bad hire here is a liability event. Coordinator roles easy to staff offshore/junior.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.9 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Non-seasonal, distributed renewal load: anniversary-dated SAM renewals spread the work across all 50 weeks (~33 delivery hrs/wk at 167 clients), eliminating the seasonal-spike staffing problem that plagues tax/compliance services.
- Pure remote, capital-light delivery: no trucks/inventory/MOQs/field visits; the entire unit of work is portal-and-document based, matching the founders' non-field-ops preference (logistics scored 9).
- Standardized, trainable playbook with a contained specialist bottleneck: only ~1.5 of ~10 hrs/client/yr is true judgment work, so one named specialist covers eligibility review well past 167 clients while juniors handle the repeatable 85%.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Catastrophic-failure asymmetry: one missed deadline or botched recert can drop a client out of their set-aside program — a $6K fee can cause six-figure client damage, inviting blame, churn, and liability even when the client withheld data.
- Client-data dependency / "the client went dark" problem: the recurring labor is chasing clients for data; a non-responsive client can cause a lapse the market attributes to GovRenew, with no operational lever to force a response.
- Single-specialist key-person risk: the eligibility moat lives in one named practitioner's head; if they leave or err, delivery quality and the value proposition collapse until replaced from a shallow pool.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The asymmetry between a small recurring fee and a catastrophic, partly-uncontrollable failure mode. GovRenew charges ~$6K/year to manage credentials whose lapse can cost a client a $400K award, ejection from an 8(a)/HUBZone program, or a frozen pipeline. Worse, the trigger is frequently outside GovRenew's control: a client who ignores three data-request nudges, a SAM entity-validation match the Federal Service Desk sits on for weeks, or a borderline eligibility judgment that turns out wrong under a size protest. When the bad outcome happens, the client doesn't parse fault — they blame the service whose promise was "never lose a contract to a lapsed registration again," and in a tight referral-driven GovCon community a couple of public failures poison acquisition. Not a reason it can't be two people — it operationally can — but the founders must engineer hard guardrails (documented client-response SLAs with explicit "we cannot file without your data by date X" outs, 60–90-day early-start buffers, E&O insurance, a specialist sign-off gate on every attestation) BEFORE scaling, or a single bad recert becomes a reputation/liability event disproportionate to the fee.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the real per-client annual labor by credential type? Time-and-motion log the first 10 clients — if heavy 8(a)/HUBZone clients run 20+ hours, the FTE math and margins change materially.
- Who is the named specialist, on what terms, and what's the backup plan if they leave? A single point of failure here is unacceptable past a few dozen clients.
- What is the contractual/operational protocol when a client goes dark or a SAM entity-validation stalls at the Federal Service Desk? Show the SLA language, early-start buffer, and "client failed to provide data" carve-out.
- What is the actual blended ARPU and tier mix you can close, and does the client count reconcile with the founder-income ladder? At $6K you need ~167 clients for ~$1M revenue, which nets below the combined ~$1M mo-24 rung after costs — does the plan rely on higher-tier multi-cert clients?

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with operational guardrails to lock before scaling)
Operationally a clean fit: remote, capital-light, non-seasonal, no field ops, a standardized trainable playbook, and a bridgeable expertise barrier (GovCon eligibility) via one named specialist whose judgment-hours stay well under one FTE even at 167 clients. The capacity math closes for the revenue side with ~3.x FTE and a smoothly distributed weekly load. Two reservations, not throughput: (1) the catastrophic-failure asymmetry + client-data dependence, contained with response SLAs, 60–90-day buffers, E&O, and a specialist sign-off gate; (2) the founder-income rung, where operations can carry the clients but $6K ARPU implies a large book of small relationships and likely requires an up-tier toward multi-cert $10–15K clients. Designable controls, not dealbreakers. Lock the specialist arrangement, data-response contract terms, and a time-and-motion validation of the 10-hour assumption, and this is operable by two founders plus a lean team.
