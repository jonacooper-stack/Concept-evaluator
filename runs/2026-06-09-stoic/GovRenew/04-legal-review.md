LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — GovRenew

Not formal legal advice. I am an experienced operator with strong regulatory pattern-recognition, not your retained attorney. The items below are issues to brief real counsel on — not legal conclusions.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
GovRenew is a clean-looking B2B compliance-ops subscription sitting on top of a genuinely dangerous substrate. The mechanical 80% — SAM.gov annual entity renewal, deadline calendars, rep-and-cert upkeep, state vendor registrations, document assembly and archiving — is low-regulation, insurable, clickwrap-able paperwork. But the differentiating 20% — "a named GovCon compliance specialist reviews size-standard and set-aside eligibility (ownership, control, size recalculation) before attestation" — straddles two serious landmines simultaneously: (1) preparing or reviewing SBA set-aside eligibility certifications (8(a)/HUBZone/WOSB/EDWOSB/SDVOSB and size status) that the contractor then certifies to the government, which carries direct False Claims Act and 18 U.S.C. exposure if the certification is false; and (2) advising on whether a firm legally qualifies (ownership/control tests under 13 CFR), which can constitute the unauthorized practice of law in most states if done by non-attorneys giving a legal opinion. The mechanical product is a GO; the eligibility-attestation product as scoped is a REFINE — the firm must be repositioned as a process/document-management service that does not opine on or attest to eligibility, with genuine legal determinations routed to retained counsel.

REGULATORY MAP
- False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733) — federal, DOJ-enforced (civil) plus relator/qui tam suits. The central regime. Set-aside fraud (certifying SDVOSB/8(a)/HUBZone/WOSB when not qualified) is among the most actively prosecuted procurement-fraud categories. Treble damages + per-claim penalties (~$13,946–$27,894 each, inflation-adjusted). A service that prepares or reviews the eligibility basis can be drawn in under conspiracy/causing-a-false-claim theories. High burden, existential if it lands.
- 18 U.S.C. § 1001 (false statements) and § 287 (false claims) — criminal exposure for false statements to a federal agency; SAM reps-and-certs and SBA attestations are made under penalty of perjury. A preparer who knowingly assists a false attestation has criminal exposure.
- SBA program regulations — 13 CFR Part 121 (size), Part 124 (8(a) BD), Part 125 (SDVOSB/VOSB + SBA's VetCert), Part 126 (HUBZone), Part 127 (WOSB/EDWOSB) — regulator is the SBA, with VA-origin SDVOSB authority consolidated under VetCert (eff. Jan 2023). Clerical filing against these is fine; rendering the legal determination of whether the ownership/control structure qualifies is the danger zone.
- GSA / SAM.gov entity registration (FAR Subpart 4.11, 2 CFR Part 25) — annual renewal, entity validation. Acting as an authorized agent filing on a client's behalf is standard/low-risk; published "third parties charge for free services" warnings mean disclosure discipline matters.
- FTC Act § 5 / GSA advisories on SAM.gov third-party assistance — GSA explicitly warns that SAM registration is free and third parties charge for it. A paid service must avoid any implication of government affiliation; deceptive-affiliation claims invite FTC/state-AG scrutiny. Low burden if messaging is disciplined.
- Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) — state-by-state (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 6125–6126; analogues in all 50 states) — applying the legal ownership/control tests of 13 CFR to a specific client's facts and advising "you qualify" is arguably the practice of law. Non-attorney GovCon consultants commonly do adjacent work, but the line between "process assistance" and "legal opinion on eligibility" is exactly where UPL complaints arise. Moderate-to-high burden.
- State/local vendor registration regimes — 50-state patchwork plus localities; purely clerical, low individual burden but additive.
- Data privacy — CCPA/CPRA + state analogues; no HIPAA/biometric/children's data — data held is business-entity registration data, ownership/financials, possibly owner SSNs/EINs and veteran-status/disability documentation (SDVOSB). Sensitive enough for real safeguards, no HIPAA; GLBA not directly triggered. Moderate-low.
- Auto-renewal / subscription law — FTC ROSCA/Click-to-Cancel + state ARLs — applies to the recurring subscription; low burden if built in, softened by B2B context.
- Professional liability / E&O environment — not a regulator, but the liability spine.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No occupational or industry license is required to file SAM registrations, assemble recertification packages, or maintain a compliance calendar — GovCon consultants do this routinely without licensure, and there is no federal "registered agent for SBA" credential analogous to an enrolled agent before the IRS. At the clerical/process layer: confidently none beyond a standard business license and entity formation. The hard caveat is the eligibility-review layer: there is no license that authorizes a non-attorney to render a legal eligibility opinion, and the absence of a license is not permission — it is exactly what makes UPL the exposure. The "named GovCon compliance specialist" is not a licensed gatekeeper; their attestation review is only safe if scoped as fact-gathering/process and not legal conclusion. If the founders want to actually opine on eligibility, the person who can lawfully do so is a licensed attorney (ideally a government-contracts attorney), retained or partnered, not merely a "compliance specialist." Founders are not attorneys; that gap is bridgeable via a real attorney relationship but not via a non-attorney specialist alone.

LIABILITY POSTURE
The entity (formed before first client) + a solid professional liability / E&O policy + general liability cover the foreseeable mechanical failures: a missed renewal deadline causes a contractor to lose award eligibility or payment, and the client sues for the lost contract — real, quantifiable, insurable damages, with a tight engagement agreement (limitation-of-liability cap at fees paid, scope-of-services exclusion, explicit "client is solely responsible for the truth of all certifications and for legal eligibility determinations" disclaimer) handling most of it. What insurance and contract terms do NOT reliably handle is the FCA/criminal layer: E&O typically excludes fraud and intentional acts, and if GovRenew's specialist reviewed and blessed an eligibility basis that turns out false (a rent-a-vet SDVOSB or a pass-through 8(a) front), the firm can be named as having "caused" a false claim, and no LLC veil protects an individual from personal criminal exposure under 18 U.S.C. § 1001/§ 287 for knowing assistance. That is the uninsurable, potentially personal, potentially existential residue. The mitigation is structural, not contractual: do not opine on eligibility; document that the client and their counsel made every eligibility determination; refuse files that smell like front fraud.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The False Claims Act, paired with criminal false-statement statutes, applied to the eligibility-attestation product. Set-aside fraud — a firm certifying SDVOSB/8(a)/HUBZone/WOSB or small-business size status it does not meet — is one of the most aggressively prosecuted categories of procurement fraud, driven by DOJ and a robust qui tam relator bar, and SBA's recent consolidation of SDVOSB certification under VetCert signals tightening scrutiny of ownership and control. GovRenew's scoped differentiator is that "a named GovCon compliance specialist reviews size-standard and set-aside eligibility... before attestation." The moment that review function blesses an eligibility basis — recalculates a size standard, opines that an ownership/control structure satisfies 13 CFR Part 125 or 124 — and the client certifies to the government on that basis, GovRenew has potentially "caused" the certification within 31 U.S.C. § 3729, and if any GovRenew person knew or should have known the structure was a front, individual criminal exposure under § 1001/§ 287 attaches. E&O excludes fraud, the LLC veil does not shield individuals from criminal liability, and the firm's own marketing ("we keep your set-aside certifications current and audit-ready") could be read as standing behind the eligibility itself. This single risk separates a 6.0-average tidy business from a potentially uninsurable, founder-personal-exposure business — and it is entirely a function of how the eligibility-review layer is scoped.

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The mechanical, deadline-driven renewal business is a legitimate GO on its own — a real recurring must-have (a lapse is directly revenue-gating), unlicensed knowledge work, clean data/IP posture, foreseeable failure mode (a missed deadline) squarely E&O-insurable with a fee-capped engagement agreement. The reason the overall recommendation is REFINE is that the concept's stated differentiator — a non-attorney specialist reviewing and attesting to set-aside eligibility under 13 CFR — imports the single most prosecuted fraud regime in federal procurement (FCA / 18 U.S.C. § 1001) plus 50-state UPL exposure, both fraud-excluded and potentially personal. The structural fix is precise and preserves the business: (1) reposition GovRenew as a process, document-assembly, and deadline-management service that explicitly does NOT opine on or attest to eligibility — the client and their own counsel make every eligibility determination, in writing; (2) route any genuine eligibility-legal question to a retained government-contracts attorney (a referral/partner channel, not the in-house "compliance specialist"), converting a liability into a revenue partnership; (3) implement client-screening to decline files that look like fronts; (4) discipline the marketing so it never implies GovRenew vouches for eligibility or carries government affiliation. With those changes the FCA/UPL channel is largely engineered out and the business reverts toward the strong mechanical-service profile; without them, the firm is one bad client away from being a fraud co-defendant.
