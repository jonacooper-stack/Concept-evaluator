ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil at small scale with very attractive working-capital and margin characteristics: annual-prepaid subscriptions, near-zero startup capital, a software-leveraged service spine. The central tension is willingness-to-pay against a free, self-service DIY substitute (SAM.gov registration costs $0; certify.SBA.gov is free), which pressures my [ASSUMED] blended ARPU of ~$6,000/yr and threatens churn after the first renewal once the customer realizes the work, while annoying, is finite and public. The income ladder ($300K/founder by mo 12) is reachable only at roughly 100+ paying firms at that ARPU — plausible given a public buyer list of tens of thousands of set-aside firms, but it lives or dies on whether small contractors will pay four figures recurring for something they perceive as a one-time chore.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Negative working-capital, annual-prepaid model with near-zero startup cash: customer pays a year upfront before the specialist touches the file; growth self-funds and launch costs are founder time.
- Structurally high gross margin (~75% modeled: $6,000 ARPU vs ~$400–$1,500 loaded annual labor) because software amortizes the recurring work an hourly consultant re-bills.
- Public, countable, renewing buyer universe (SBA DSBS 100K+ registrants; set-aside firms in the tens of thousands), so 270 customers is <1% penetration and renewal dates are knowable for free outbound timing.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ARPU/WTP collapse against a FREE DIY substitute: SAM.gov and certify.SBA.gov are $0 and self-service, so $6,000 ARPU may compress to $2,500–$3,500, roughly doubling the customer count needed and putting the mo-12 rung out of reach.
- Retention/churn after first renewal: a contractor who pays once, watches the work get done, and perceives a finite annual chore may not renew at the rate the LTV (and mo-24 ladder) depend on.
- Acquisition cost into a fragmented long tail: reaching thousands of tiny, dispersed set-aside firms one at a time via SEO/outbound may carry higher blended CAC and longer payback than assumed.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Willingness-to-pay against a zero-cost substitute. The entire model rests on small contractors paying ~$6,000/yr in perpetuity to never personally touch a renewal the government lets them do for free. The fear-of-lapse pitch is real and revenue-gating, but it's felt acutely only around the once-a-year deadline, and a rational small contractor may decide one afternoon of annoyance per year beats a four-figure recurring subscription. If true blended ARPU lands at $3,500 instead of $6,000 AND first-renewal retention is ~70% rather than 90%+, the customer count required for the mo-12 rung nearly doubles (~300+ firms) while LTV shrinks — and a two-founder-plus-one-specialist team cannot acquire and service that many tiny accounts fast enough. The business doesn't fail to exist; it fails to clear the income target, sliding from a $500K/founder asset to a $150K/founder lifestyle service.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the demonstrated willingness-to-pay and tier mix? Put three published prices ($3K/$6K/$12K) in front of 25 real set-aside firms and report close rate + chosen tier.
- What is the first-renewal retention rate, and what makes a customer renew rather than treat year one as a one-time fix? Cohort the first 20.
- What is the true blended CAC and payback to reach a fragmented long tail via SEO/outbound/PTAC, and how many net new firms/month can two founders + one specialist actually acquire AND onboard without margin erosion?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The financial architecture is genuinely strong — negative working capital, ~75% gross margin, near-zero startup cash, a public renewing buyer base, a recurring spine that fits the income ladder by month 24. What keeps it from an unqualified GO is that the two numbers the model turns on — blended ARPU and first-renewal retention — are entirely [ASSUMED] and sit directly opposite a free, self-service substitute. To GO: (1) validate 25 real firms will pay a published four-figure annual fee at a blended ~$6,000 (or re-anchor the model); (2) show first-renewal retention above ~85%; (3) demonstrate a blended CAC with payback under ~9 months. Clear those and the math supports ~$500K/founder by mo 24; fail them and this is a sub-$200K/founder lifestyle service.
