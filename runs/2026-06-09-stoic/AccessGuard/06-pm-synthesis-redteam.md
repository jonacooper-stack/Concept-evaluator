# SYNTHESIS — AccessGuard
Date: 2026-06-09
Council members reviewed: CFO, CMO, COO, Legal/Regulatory, CTO

## ONE-PARAGRAPH PM READ
AccessGuard is one of the better-shaped bootstrapped concepts this council has seen on the dimensions the founders prize most: the buy is legally forced by an ADA demand letter, the customer is literally enumerable from public PACER dockets, the spend is already budgeted (cheaper than repeat $5K-$25K settlements), the stack is boring-and-proven, and the GTM maps cleanly onto the founders' marketing-plus-compliance-operator edge with no field ops. It is genuinely strong at the top of the funnel. But two structural facts keep it out of clean-GO territory: (1) the recurring thesis is unproven — the acute trigger that makes acquisition easy is the same thing that may make renewal hard once the lawsuit settles, and no cohort exists; and (2) AudioEye (NASDAQ: AYE) is a public, funded, litigation-focused incumbent in nearly the identical lane, exactly the "well-funded incumbent" the objectives tell us to avoid. The defensible part of the product (manual expert audit + legal-grade attestation) is non-scaling human labor on a thin hiring pool. This is a REFINE that is close to GO — the path runs through proving retention and out-positioning AudioEye, not structural redesign.

## WHERE THE EXPERTS AGREE
- The wedge and the forced buy are excellent and real (enumerable buyers from public dockets; legally-forced, budgeted spend; national/year-round; no field ops). Evidenced: ~4,000+ federal filings/yr (Seyfarth/UsableNet); accessiBe $1M FTC settlement Jan 2025.
- The recurring/retention thesis is the make-or-break unknown — CFO #1 risk, CMO #1 risk, and COO capacity logic all converge independently. Maximum signal.
- The defensible value is human, not software, and does not scale linearly — COO + CTO agree: automated scanning catches ~30-40% of WCAG AA; the part that defends a client is skilled labor at ~5-9 hrs/client/month on a thin pool.
- Self-inflicted-but-controllable legal risk — Legal + CMO agree the FTC §5 overclaiming and UPL drift are real but within the founders' control.

## TOP 5 RISKS (RANKED)
1. Retention collapse after the fear window (CFO+CMO) — no cohort; if first-renewal is 60-70% vs 85-90%, LTV halves. Reduce via cohort test + "living attestation" reframe.
2. Funded incumbent in the same lane — AudioEye (CMO) — copyable differentiation; doc-01 constraint #5. Reduce via proven out-positioning into the just-sued segment.
3. Automation-vs-legal-defense gap (CTO+COO) — scanning catches ~30-40%; selling a green dashboard as "provably accessible" creates a dismantlable paper trail.
4. Remediation-labor bottleneck vs spiky active-litigation onboarding (COO) — legal-grade deliverable (QC 4) can't be surged/shipped sloppy.
5. FTC §5 overclaiming + UPL drift (Legal) — the most persuasive message is the illegal one.

## TOP 3 STRENGTHS
- A legally-forced, already-budgeted painkiller with a buyer list that is literal public record — the best fit to doc-01's must-have spine.
- Capital-light, negative-working-capital, revenue-precedes-build economics (CFO 9/9; CTO ~8-12 dev-weeks).
- Precise founder-edge fit (marketing into a distrust category, software build, compliance-operator/SOC2 attestation instinct, regulated close); no license required.

## THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION
What is the real first-renewal rate once the triggering lawsuit is resolved? It governs the recurring dimension, the income upper rung, the exit multiple, and whether this is a durable subscription or a project business in a subscription costume.
