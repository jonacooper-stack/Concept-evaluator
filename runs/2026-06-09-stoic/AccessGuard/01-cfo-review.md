CFO REVIEW — AccessGuard

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil unusually well for a bootstrapped recurring service: an enumerable, acutely-paining buyer (a company holding a demand letter), an already-budgeted spend (they are choosing between paying you ~$30K/yr or paying $5K-$25K per settlement repeatedly), and software-leveraged delivery that should hold a 65-78% gross margin. The income ladder is reachable with a defensibly small customer count (~50-80 accounts gets each founder past $300K). The two financial fragilities I cannot wave away are (1) retention after the acute fear fades — once a sued company is conformant and quiet for 18 months, the renewal logic weakens; and (2) the manual-audit component, which is the part of COGS most likely to scale margins downward if it cannot be productized. This is a GO with conditions, not a clean GO.

THE MATH
Target (per the ladder, NOT the legacy "$2M/24mo"): ~$300K income/founder by month 12, ~$500K+/founder by month 24. I score dimension 6 against this ladder and relabel it accordingly.

Two founders, bootstrapped. Income to founders = revenue × margin − non-founder costs (the founders take what's left after a lean cost base; in the first 24 months there is little or no payroll beyond contracted auditors).

- Blended ARPU: [ASSUMED] $2,500/mo = $30K/yr (founder's stated blend). Range $18K-$48K/yr. Verify cheaply by pricing 10 inbound just-sued prospects at three tiers and watching where they sign.
- Gross margin: [ASSUMED] 70% (range 65-78%) after crawler infra (cheap) + the manual expert-audit labor (the expensive part). Verify by timing one real audit-and-remediation cycle and costing the contractor hours.

Month 12 target — combined founder income ~$600K ($300K each):
Assume in year 1 the founders ARE the delivery + sales engine, so "income" ≈ gross profit minus modest tooling/contract-audit overflow. To clear ~$600K combined gross profit at 70% margin needs ~$857K ARR.
$857K ÷ $30K ARPU = ~29 accounts. Call it ~30 paying accounts by month 12.
Sanity: 30 annual contracts in 12 months from a channel of just-sued companies + defense-firm referrals + vertical outbound = ~2.5 closes/month. Plausible for two strong closers against an acute, time-boxed pain. [ASSUMED] close rate on a just-sued inbound lead 15-30%.

Month 24 target — combined ~$1.0M+ ($500K each):
At 70% margin, ~$1.0M GP needs ~$1.43M ARR = ~48 accounts at $30K. But by month 24 there is real COGS payroll (auditors/remediation staff) and probably 1-2 hires, so to net $500K/founder you realistically want ~$1.8-2.2M ARR = ~60-75 accounts. ~60-75 annual contracts in 24 months is roughly 2.5-3 net adds/month sustained — demanding but not heroic given the inbound enumerability of the market.

Market sizing (bottoms-up, not TAM theater):
ADA Title III web-accessibility filings run ~4,000-5,000/yr (UsableNet has reported ~4,000+ federal filings annually for several years), heavily concentrated in NY/FL/CA, PLUS a far larger, unrecorded volume of pre-suit demand letters (commonly cited at 5-15x filings). Even taking just filings, ~4,500/yr × (the mid-market slice that can afford $30K/yr, say 25%) = ~1,100 acutely-paining new buyers/year, before counting the much larger "watched a peer get hit" prevention segment. Capturing 60-75 of ~1,100+ acute buyers/year is ~5-7% of one year's fresh inflow — comfortably within reach for two founders. The obtainable market is not the constraint.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.6 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Negative working-capital cycle: annual contracts billed upfront against costs incurred over the year — the customer finances the founders' growth.
- Forced, already-budgeted, painkiller spend: the buyer is choosing AccessGuard over repeated $5K-$25K settlements, so the purchase is a cost-avoidance decision, not discretionary — minimal "convince them they have a problem" selling.
- Low capital to first revenue with a sub-100-account path to the income ladder: ~30 accounts to clear ~$300K/founder, no inventory or fixed-asset drag.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Retention decay after the fear window: the acute pain is the demand letter; 18 months of quiet may erode willingness to renew at $30K/yr, directly attacking LTV and the dimension-4 economics.
- Margin compression from manual audits/remediation: the non-software COGS is the part that scales with headcount and can drag 70% toward the low 60s if not productized.
- Channel/regulatory correlation: revenue is tied to the persistence of ADA web-accessibility litigation pressure; a defendant-favorable circuit split, federal rulemaking that clarifies safe harbor, or a drop in serial-plaintiff filings would suppress both new sales AND renewals simultaneously.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Retention is the number that decides whether this is a great business or a churny one. The entire model's LTV — and therefore the dimension-4 unit economics and the dimension-10 exit multiple — rests on the claim that monitoring, drift-remediation, and attestation renewal are "continuous" enough to justify a recurring $30K/yr after the acute lawsuit has been resolved and the site has been quiet for a year. The honest risk is that a meaningful fraction of accounts treat AccessGuard like a fire extinguisher: buy it during the emergency, downgrade or cancel once they feel safe, and self-insure on the bet that lightning won't strike twice. If first-renewal retention comes in at, say, 60-70% rather than 85-90%, LTV roughly halves, payback math still works but the income ladder slows, and the clean-recurring-revenue exit story degrades toward a services multiple. The mitigants are real (sites genuinely change weekly; a lapse in documentation is exactly what a serial plaintiff exploits; the "keep the receipts" framing is renewal-friendly), but I have no measured renewal cohort, and I will not assume one.

Track the first 10-15 acute (just-sued) signups and report month-12 renewal vs. month-3 cancellation; if you cannot yet, what reframing (e.g., bundling the attestation as the renewable asset) makes non-renewal feel reckless to the buyer?
- How many billable hours does one real manual-audit + remediation cycle take, and at what contractor cost? This is the single number that sets gross margin; I have it as [ASSUMED] 70% (range 65-78%) and need it measured on at least one live account.
- What is true CAC by channel — defense-firm referral vs. just-sued outbound vs. SEO — and which channel actually converts the acute buyer? I've assumed CAC $4K-$9K; verify by running 30 outbound contacts to scraped Title III defendants and one referral pilot with a single defense firm, and report cost-per-closed-contract.

RECOMMENDATION: GO
The financial architecture is among the better bootstrapped-service shapes I've reviewed: capital-light, negative working capital, an enumerable acute buyer choosing you over recurring settlement cash, and a sub-100-account path to the founder-income ladder. It is a GO, not a clean GO, because two assumed numbers carry the whole case: first-renewal retention and audit-labor hours. If the founders can show (a) first-renewal retention plausibly at 80%+ via reframing the attestation as the renewable asset, and (b) measured audit COGS that keeps gross margin at 70%+, this moves to an unqualified GO.
