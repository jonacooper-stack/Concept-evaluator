# AccessGuard — Clean-Room One-Pager

## What it is
A productized, recurring managed service that keeps a company's public-facing
website conformant to web-accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA) and produces
the documentation a business uses to deflect ADA Title III web-accessibility
demand letters and lawsuits.

## The customer
Mid-market US companies that run high-traffic, transactional public websites in
the verticals plaintiffs target most: e-commerce retailers (~$5M–$100M revenue),
multi-location restaurant/hospitality groups, healthcare and dental practice
groups, financial-services and insurance agencies, and hotel/booking sites.
Primary entry segment: companies that have just received an ADA web-accessibility
demand letter or been named in a Title III suit (acute, bleeding, time-boxed
pain) and companies in high-risk verticals that have watched a peer get hit.

## The pain
Serial plaintiff firms run automated scans against thousands of sites and mail
settlement demand letters (commonly $5K–$25K to settle) or file Title III suits;
filings have run in the multiple thousands per year and concentrate in NY, FL,
and CA (CA Unruh Act adds statutory damages). A one-time audit-and-fix does not
durably solve it: sites change weekly (new products, CMS updates, third-party
widgets, marketing pages), so conformance drifts and the company is exposed
again. The buy is effectively forced once a demand letter arrives, recurring
because the site is a living target, and budgeted (it is cheaper than repeated
settlements). Many companies bought "accessibility overlay" widgets (accessiBe,
UserWay, AudioEye) that plaintiffs now specifically target — and the FTC has
penalized overlay marketing claims — leaving buyers burned and confused.

## The product / service
1. **Continuous monitoring** — a recurring automated crawl of the live site
   against WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA, augmented by periodic manual expert audits of key
   templates and user flows (checkout, booking, forms), flagging new violations
   as the site changes.
2. **Remediation** — prioritized fixes delivered either as code/PRs to the
   client's dev team or implemented directly on common CMS/e-commerce platforms
   (Shopify, WordPress/WooCommerce, etc.); not a JavaScript overlay.
3. **Defensible documentation** — a maintained accessibility conformance report
   (VPAT-style), a published accessibility statement, and a dated remediation
   audit trail demonstrating ongoing good-faith conformance — the file a
   defense attorney uses to argue mootness/good faith.
4. **Demand-letter rapid response** — a packaged evidence file and remediation
   sprint when a client receives a letter.

## Revenue model
Recurring monthly subscription on an annual contract, tiered by site
size/complexity: [ASSUMED] ~$1,500/mo (small site) to ~$4,000/mo (large
multi-template site), with a higher-priced "active litigation" onboarding tier.
Recurring because monitoring + drift-remediation + attestation renewal are
continuous. [ASSUMED] target blended ARPU ~$2,500/mo ($30K/yr).

## Positioning
"Stop paying ADA demand-letter settlements every quarter. We keep your site
provably accessible — and keep the receipts — so your lawyer has a case the day a
letter arrives." Explicitly the credible, evidence-based alternative to discredited
overlay widgets.

## Competitive landscape (stated neutrally)
The field includes specialist accessibility firms (Level Access/eSSENTIAL, Deque),
overlay vendors (accessiBe, UserWay, AudioEye — some public/funded, and reputation-
damaged among plaintiffs), boutique consultancies that do one-time audits, and
general web agencies that dabble. There is no single dominant productized
*recurring managed* offering aimed at the just-sued mid-market buyer; the space is
a mix of expensive enterprise consultancies, discredited cheap widgets, and
one-shot audits.
