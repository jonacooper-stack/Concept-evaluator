# ChemSDS — Clean-Room One-Pager

## What it is
A productized, recurring managed service that keeps a chemical-handling company's
Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and hazard labels authored, current, and compliant with
OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard (29 CFR 1910.1200, updated 2024 to align with
GHS Revision 7), and hosts the SDS-access portal those companies are legally
required to provide. Software does the hazard classification, 16-section document
authoring, label generation, versioning, and the access portal; an expert
chemist/toxicologist reviews and signs off on classifications.

## The customer
Small-to-mid US chemical formulators, blenders, importers, and specialty
manufacturers — makers of cleaning/janitorial products, coatings/paints/adhesives,
lubricants and oils, specialty and agricultural chemicals, and industrial supplies
— typically with ~50 to a few-thousand hazardous SKUs and NO staffed regulatory/EHS
department. They are too small for the enterprise EHS platforms and too busy to
hand-author SDS in-house.

## The pain
OSHA's 2024 HazCom update (final rule May 2024, aligned to GHS Rev 7) forces a wave
of re-authored SDS and relabeled products on staggered, hard compliance deadlines —
roughly January 2026 for substances and July 2027 for mixtures. Beyond that
one-time wave, the need is permanently recurring: any formulation change, new
hazard data, new ingredient, or new product requires an updated 16-section SDS and
label; downstream customers (big-box retailers, distributors, industrial
procurement) refuse to stock or buy a product without a current SDS on file; and
OSHA can cite missing/outdated SDS or non-compliant labels (HazCom is perennially
among OSHA's most-cited standards). The catalog is a living thing, so compliance
drifts continuously.

## The product / service
1. **SDS authoring & maintenance** — GHS hazard classification of each product,
   authoring of the compliant 16-section SDS, and re-authoring when formulations,
   ingredients, or rules change (including the 2026/2027 HazCom updates).
2. **Label generation** — GHS-compliant labels (pictograms, signal words, hazard/
   precautionary statements) generated from the same classification data.
3. **Hosted SDS-access portal** — a branded library so the company's employees and
   downstream customers can pull current SDS on demand (satisfying the
   right-to-know access obligation), with version history.
4. **Chemical-inventory & reporting support** — maintained chemical inventory and
   help with EPCRA Tier II / SARA 312 reporting where applicable.

## Revenue model
Recurring monthly subscription tiered by SDS/SKU volume: [ASSUMED] ~$500/mo (small,
<50 SDS) to ~$5,000/mo (mid, several hundred SDS), plus per-new-SDS authoring fees
for net-new products. [ASSUMED] blended ARPU ~$1,800/mo (~$21.6K/yr). Recurring
because maintenance, re-authoring on regulatory/formulation changes, and portal
hosting are continuous.

## Positioning
"Your chemical catalog's compliance department, as a subscription. Current SDS and
labels for every product, the access portal your customers demand, and the
2026/2027 OSHA deadlines handled — without hiring a regulatory team."

## Competitive landscape (stated neutrally)
The EHS/SDS field includes large platforms (VelocityEHS/MSDSonline, Sphera, Verisk
3E, SiteHawk, ChemTel) and boutique SDS-authoring consultancies that bill per
document. The large platforms target enterprise (expensive, complex, often
self-service authoring tools rather than done-for-you); the boutiques author SDS
one at a time with little recurring managed model or modern portal. The small/mid
formulator who wants done-for-you authoring + maintenance + a customer-facing
portal at an affordable recurring price sits between them.

## Why these founders
- Marketing/positioning to reach a fragmented, unglamorous base of small formulators
  that nobody markets to well.
- Software to build the classification/authoring/versioning engine and the
  customer-facing SDS portal.
- Compliance-operator instinct to run the regulatory program and the deadline machine.
- The GHS hazard-classification expertise is a real but bridgeable moat: the GHS
  criteria are codified rules, and a named chemist/toxicologist (hire or partner)
  signs off on classifications — keeping lazy competition out while remaining
  reachable for adaptive founders.

## Acquisition motion
Buyers are enumerable from EPA TSCA and state chemical registries, specialty-chemical
and janitorial/sanitation supplier directories, and trade associations; outbound +
content/SEO on "OSHA HazCom 2024 SDS deadline / GHS Rev 7 relabeling"; partnerships
with chemical distributors and industry groups that require current SDS from their
suppliers (a referral channel where the distributor pushes its small suppliers to
get compliant).
