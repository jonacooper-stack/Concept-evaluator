CFO REVIEW — ChemSDS

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This is a financially attractive shape: a recurring, software-leveraged compliance service with a regulatory deadline forcing the buy, very high theoretical gross margin (software does the work, a chemist signs off), and near-zero startup capital. The numbers pencil to the income ladder at a customer count that is achievable in a market of thousands of small formulators. The two real financial fragilities are (1) the chemist/toxicologist sign-off is a human cost center that, if mishandled, caps gross margin and slows throughput, and (2) the headline regulatory wave (substances Jan 2026, mixtures July 2027) is a one-time demand spike layered on a genuinely recurring base — the recurring base is what must carry the model after the wave passes. Subject to verifying ARPU and churn, this is one of the better-penciling concepts of its type.

- High, durable gross margin (70–80% [ASSUMED]) because software does classification/authoring/portal and the chemist is a thin review layer.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ARPU and churn are both [ASSUMED]; if ARPU lands at $1,200/mo and churn at 12%, the customer count to hit the ladder roughly doubles and payback stretches.
- The chemist sign-off is the throughput and margin governor — under-resource it and onboarding caps; over-resource it and net margin compresses below 45%.
- Demand-timing risk: the Jan 2026 / July 2027 wave is one-time; if the recurring maintenance base is thinner than assumed, revenue is front-loaded rather than annuity-like.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single financial issue most likely to hurt this is misjudging the recurring base versus the deadline wave. The 2024 HazCom update creates a large, time-boxed surge of re-authoring/relabeling (substances ~Jan 2026, mixtures ~July 2027), but that surge is one-time per SKU. The annuity thesis rests on "the catalog is a living thing." If the typical small formulator's catalog churns slowly (a janitorial blender selling the same 60 SKUs for years), then after the wave the recurring component collapses toward just portal hosting, which alone can't justify $1,800/mo. The model would then resemble a consultancy with a lumpy project pipeline rather than high-multiple recurring SaaS, and the income ladder (especially the growing month-24+ figure) would not hold without constantly replacing churned wave-customers. Verifiable before betting on it.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the true recurring re-authoring frequency for a representative ~60–300 SKU formulator absent a rule change, and does that + portal hosting justify $1,800/mo on its own (does the model survive after the 2026/2027 wave)?
- What is realized ARPU and price acceptance? Run 10–20 priced discovery calls; what % accept $1,500–$2,500/mo, and how many SDS does a paying account carry?
- What is chemist economics? Time one chemist through 20 real classifications post-software-pre-classification: minutes-per-SDS and sign-offs-per-day set true gross margin and the step-cost cadence.
