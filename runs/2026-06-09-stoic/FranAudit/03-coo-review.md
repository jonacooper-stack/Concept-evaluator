COO REVIEW — FranAudit

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is a document-and-deadline managed service: assemble a 23-item FDD, coordinate CPA-audited financials, file/renew across ~13 registration states plus notice states, and clear examiner comment letters — with a partnered franchise attorney providing the legal sign-off. The delivery unit is a paperwork-and-coordination cycle, not a truck or a field visit, operationally ideal for two remote generalists. The two real operational hazards: (1) the Dec-31 fiscal-year-end clustering, which jams most renewals into one ~120-day March-to-April window, and (2) the attorney sign-off being the throughput bottleneck and a single-point partner dependency. Both manageable but must be designed for from day one, not discovered in March.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Month 12, March 2027 — pointedly the worst possible Tuesday because it sits inside the renewal-cluster window. Say ~25 franchisor clients signed in year one [ASSUMED 15–35; verify against actual sales velocity]. Of those, ~18 have a Dec-31 FYE (the dominant franchisor FYE), so their annual FDD update is due within 120 days — by ~April 30. So in March ~18 renewal cycles are simultaneously live.
Founder A (ops) opens the compliance-calendar dashboard: 18 renewals in flight at different stages. Client #1's CPA delivered audited financials, so software auto-populates Item 21 and flags the three changed items (Item 20 outlet table, Item 3 litigation, Item 19 FPR). Founder A runs assembly, produces a redline, routes to the attorney's queue. Client #5's CPA is late — the nightmare, because the 120-day clock does not stop for a slow CPA; Founder A chases the CPA and documents the delay is client-side. Client #9 got a CA DFPI comment letter requiring a revised Item 19; turn around, re-route to attorney, re-file. Three new clients need prior-year FDDs ingested.
Founder B (sales) is mostly NOT selling — March is seasonally wrong — so doing filing-clerk work: submitting registrations through each state's portal (CA DFPI online, others paper/PDF/check), tracking fees, logging confirmations. By 11am the bottleneck is visible: the attorney can review only so many FDDs/day, and 18 redlines plus comment-letter responses stack up. If the attorney is a part-time solo, March breaks. The founders' work is repeatable and software-supported; the legal review is the constraint, and the CPA dependency is the wildcard that can blow a client's statutory deadline through no fault of FranAudit.

Throughput vs the March cluster. Work per client per cycle:

The true throughput governor.

- The partnered franchise attorney is a near bet-the-business single dependency for legal sign-off and itself a March throughput bottleneck.
- Client-sourced CPA audited financials arrive late and outside FranAudit's control, yet the 120-day statutory clock doesn't stop — deadline-miss exposure FranAudit can be blamed for.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The seasonality-plus-attorney-bottleneck combination is what makes me uncertain two people can do this without structural redesign. Because franchisors overwhelmingly share a December 31 FYE, the 120-day deadline lands the majority of the book in the same March–April window every year — the capacity math shows ~42 of 60 clients compressing ~1,260 ops hours into ~8 weeks against ~800 available founder hours, a ~460-hour gap. That same window overloads the one partnered attorney who must sign off on every FDD, and collides with late-arriving client CPA financials that can blow a statutory deadline the founders cannot control. A smooth-looking recurring-revenue line masks a violently peaky labor curve where eleven months are manageable and one quarter is a fire drill in which a single missed filing means a client legally cannot sell franchises — a reputation-ending failure mode. Solvable (named attorney panel, Q4 prep head-start, deliberately recruiting non-Dec-FYE clients, early ops hires), but it must be engineered before the first March, not discovered during it.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the attorney arrangement — one named solo or a panel of 2–3 — and how many FDD reviews plus comment-letter responses can that capacity absorb in the March–April cluster, contractually guaranteed?
- How do you de-risk the CPA dependency: contractually require audited financials by a fixed date, build a CPA referral panel, or accept deadline-miss liability — and who is on the hook if a client misses its statutory deadline because its CPA was late?
- What is the realistic distribution of fiscal-year-ends in your target base, and your concrete plan to smooth the cluster (multi-year engagements with Q4 prep starts, intentional non-Dec-FYE recruitment, or seasonal ops labor)?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally a far better-shaped concept than most: remote, capital-light, no field ops, a codified-template deliverable, a public-registry pipeline. Not a GO purely on operations because of three coupled, unaddressed friction points: the Dec-31 FYE seasonality spike, the single-attorney throughput-and-dependency risk, and the client-CPA-timing exposure. Specific simplifications: (1) lock a named franchise-attorney panel (2–3) with volume-priced review capacity; (2) build cluster-smoothing into the sales motion and engagement terms (Q4 prep starts, deliberate non-Dec-FYE mix, planned ops hiring at ~30+ clients); (3) contractually fence the CPA-timing liability so a late client CPA is never FranAudit's deadline miss. Solve those three and this moves to GO.
