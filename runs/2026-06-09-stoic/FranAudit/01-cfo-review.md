CFO REVIEW — FranAudit

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil unusually well for a bootstrap: a legally mandated, deadline-driven, annually recurring buy with a public list of named prospects (state franchise registries), priced at $8K–$30K/yr against incumbent law-firm bills of $20K–$50K+, delivered as software + a partnered attorney rather than a billable-hour shop. Gross margin should sit in the 60–75% range because the irreducible cost is attorney sign-off and CPA-coordinated audited financials, neither of which the founders can fully productize away. The dominant financial risk is not demand — demand is forced by 16 CFR Part 436 — but the cost and bottleneck of the partnered attorney, which is both a margin drag and a liability that, if it breaks, breaks the whole offering. On the income ladder, ~$300K/founder by month 12 is reachable with ~55–65 customers; ~$500K/founder by month 24 with ~150 customers, inside the addressable base.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.4 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Forced, annual, deadline-driven demand (FTC 120-day update + ~13-state renewals) against a free, enumerable prospect list with known renewal dates — you can predict exactly when each named buyer must buy.
- Negative-to-neutral working capital: annual subscription upfront, state fees passed through, no inventory/deposits.
- Strong unit economics under conservative assumptions: $14K ARPU × 65% GM vs $2–4K CAC → sub-6-month payback, high LTV/CAC, structurally low churn (legally mandatory buy).

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Attorney economics: partnered-attorney sign-off is both the margin cap (keeps GM <80%) and a single-point cost dependency; high-end legal cost compresses GM toward 55% and the mo-12 rung slips.
- ARPU realization: if most register in only 1–3 states, real ARPU may be $8–10K, pushing the income-ladder customer count up 40–60%.
- Audited-financials dependency: every FDD needs CPA-produced audited financials the franchisor supplies; late/messy financials stall engagements and push revenue recognition.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The financial spine is the spread between what customers pay ($8K–$30K) and delivery cost, and the largest least-controllable cost is the partnered franchise attorney who must review/sign the FDD's legal disclosure content. This is not engineer-able away — the value prop and regulatory defensibility both depend on a real attorney standing behind the disclosure. If that attorney charges near what a small franchise firm would (the very benchmark FranAudit undercuts), the margin advantage collapses toward 50–55% GM, turning comfortable mo-24 math into a grind and making the mo-12 $300K/founder rung unreachable without far higher volume. Worse, it is concentration risk: a single attorney's capacity, pricing renegotiation, or departure throttles the entire book. The whole case rests on securing volume-priced legal sign-off (a fixed low per-FDD rate, ideally a second attorney for redundancy) before scaling.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the actual fixed per-FDD/per-renewal cost for attorney sign-off at volume, in writing from at least two attorneys for redundancy? This determines whether GM is 55% or 75%.
- What is the real distribution of registration-state counts, and therefore true blended ARPU — $14K or closer to $8–10K? (Verifiable from CA DFPI/IL/MN registries + FDD samples.)
- How do you handle the audited-financials dependency — contractual delivery date, a CPA panel, or taking coordination liability — and what does it do to timeline and revenue recognition?
- What is demonstrated outbound conversion against the enumerable renewal-dated list, and what CAC does that imply founder-led vs paid?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Clears my bar as a financially sound bootstrap candidate: demand legally forced, annually recurring, tied to a free list of named buyers with known deadlines; favorable working capital; strong unit economics under conservative assumptions; income ladder reachable at ~6% penetration. GO rather than strong GO only because two assumptions are load-bearing and unverified — the volume attorney sign-off cost and true blended ARPU. Lock a written fixed per-FDD legal rate from two attorneys and confirm ARPU from public registries → high-conviction GO; if attorney cost comes back near law-firm hourly economics, revisit pricing tiers before scaling.
