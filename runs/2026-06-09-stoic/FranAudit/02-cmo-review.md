CMO REVIEW — FranAudit

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is a genuinely sellable concept built on top of a hard, public, annual, revenue-gating deadline — almost the platonic painkiller. Under the FTC Franchise Rule, a franchisor literally cannot sell a franchise with a stale FDD, and in the ~13 registration states a lapsed registration shuts off new-unit sales in that state; that is not a vitamin, it is the franchisor's growth oxygen. The marketing problem is not whether the pain is real (it is, and already paid for, typically to a franchise law firm) but whether a software-leveraged fixed-fee challenger can credibly capture the "renewal" portion of legal spend without the buyer fearing they are trading their lawyer for a project-management tool. The positioning, the wedge (public state registries listing every franchisor and its renewal date), and the channel partners (CPAs who do the audited financials, franchise brokers) are unusually concrete — closer to the CMMC archetype than 90% of what I see.

POSITIONING DRAFT
"Your FDD, renewed and filed in every registration state, on time, every year — for one predictable annual fee instead of an unpredictable law-firm hourly bill. You keep selling franchises; we keep you compliant and the examiners satisfied." Real edges: names the deliverable, the cadence, the pain (predictable vs hourly), the outcome. Not mush. The one soft edge is "compliance" — for a legal deliverable, buyers ask "who is actually on the hook," so the positioning must front-load the named partnered franchise attorney to avoid sounding like a paralegal service charging lawyer money.

THE WEDGE
Unusually clean. Every registered franchisor and its registration/renewal date is public record: California DFPI's DocQNet franchise search, Washington DFI, Minnesota, Wisconsin, and the other registration states publish searchable registries, and FDD repositories exist. The founders can build a literally enumerable list of every franchisor with a known renewal deadline and run timed outbound 90–120 days before each fiscal-year-end +120-day FDD deadline. Near-zero to assemble (public data scraping + a structured calendar); a "your CA renewal is due [date] — here's a fixed quote" sequence is the highest-intent outbound imaginable. Layer on: (a) CPA partnerships (the auditor producing the required financials is the natural referrer); (b) franchise-development consultancies/brokers (FranNet, FranChoice-type, IFA ecosystem); (c) SEO/content on "FDD renewal deadline," "franchise registration states," "[state] franchise renewal." First 100: timed outbound off public registries. First 1,000: registries + CPA/consultant referral + SEO.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
(1) Franchise law firms — dominant incumbents who own FDD work because it is legal work: Lathrop GPM, DLA Piper's franchise group, Lewitt Hackman, Gardner & Mendoza, and a long tail of boutiques. Best at legal substance + the relationship; weakest at productization, fixed pricing, software, proactive deadline management — the sleepy hourly-billing incumbents the founders are built to out-position. (2) FDD-prep/franchise-development consultancies (iFranchise Group, MSA Worldwide) — many partner with attorneys but skew to the launch event, not the recurring renewal. (3) FRANdata — data/benchmarking, not a fixed-fee renewal managed service. (4) DIY/templates + the franchisor's outside general counsel. (5) Legal-ops/contract-management generalist SaaS — not franchise-specific. Where is the air? Nobody has shown up with a productized, software-run, fixed-fee, attorney-backed recurring renewal-and-filing service aimed at the 1–100-unit franchisor who finds the big firm's $20K–$50K+ annual cycle painful. Real gap; incumbents are slow to close it because hourly billing is more profitable than productizing themselves out of margin.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.7 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- A hard, public, annual, revenue-gating deadline (FTC 120-day FDD update + ~13-state renewals) — a true painkiller already paid for to law firms.
- The single cleanest buyer list I've scored: every franchisor and its renewal date is public record (CA DFPI DocQNet, WA DFI, MN, WI), enabling near-free, timed, highest-intent outbound.
- A natural warm-referral channel built into the deliverable — the CPA who already produces the required audited financials, plus franchise brokers/consultants in the IFA ecosystem.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- "Trading my lawyer for a project manager" objection — disclosure content is legal work with rescission/liability consequences; if named-attorney backing isn't front and center, the fixed-fee pitch reads as a cheaper, riskier paralegal service.
- The total market is finite — on the order of [ASSUMED] 3,000–4,000 active US franchisors, sub-100-unit a subset; SAM is real but not vast, capping the ceiling without expanding scope.
- Incumbent law firms can defensively launch a "flat-fee renewal package" using the founders' own positioning the moment it works, since the legal substance is theirs to keep.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The credibility-of-the-fixed-fee-legal-deliverable problem: the customer is buying peace of mind on a document whose errors can trigger franchisee rescission claims and state-examiner enforcement, and the buyer's instinct is that "real" protection comes from "their lawyer." If FranAudit is perceived as a software vendor and project manager that merely rents an attorney's signature, sophisticated franchisors and their general counsel may distrust it on the highest-stakes filings and keep the work at a full-service firm — leaving FranAudit with only the smallest, most price-sensitive accounts who churn or self-serve. The entire pitch hinges on making the partnered franchise attorney feel as accountable and present as the franchisor's own counsel while still delivering the fixed-fee, software-run experience; if that trust transfer fails, the positioning collapses into "cheap legal," the worst place to be in a liability-laden category.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Who is the named, credentialed partnered franchise attorney (and their malpractice/E&O coverage), and how is their accountability presented so this reads as "attorney-backed compliance," not "paralegal service with a rubber-stamp"?
- What is the realistic addressable count — 1–100-unit, multi-state-registered, no-in-house-counsel franchisors (verify by counting active registrants across CA DFPI, WA, MN, WI) — and does it support the income ladder at $8K–$30K ACV?
- What is the true annual cost a sub-100-unit franchisor pays today for the renewal cycle (not launch), and will they perceive $8K–$30K as a clear saving? (Verify with 10 franchisor calls + 5 franchise-CPA calls.)
- Can the software-run multi-state filing workflow handle the heterogeneity of ~13 states' portals/fees/comment-letter formats at fixed price without margin eaten by manual filing labor — which states are realistically automatable in v1?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
One of the stronger concepts I've reviewed against the must-have spine: a mandate-forced, recurring, already-budgeted buy; sleepy hourly-billing incumbents structurally disinclined to productize; a near-free, enumerable, high-intent buyer list from public registries; warm referral channels built into the deliverable; clean fixed-fee positioning. Mirrors the CMMC archetype almost beat for beat. Nail before scaling spend: (1) the trust-transfer to the named attorney so the fixed fee reads as protection, and (2) a hard count of the addressable base to confirm headroom. Both validation steps, not positioning flaws — advances as GO, with named-attorney accountability messaging the single most important thing to get right in v1 collateral.
