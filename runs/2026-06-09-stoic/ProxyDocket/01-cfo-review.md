ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil better than most concepts I see: a software-leveraged, recurring, mandatory-compliance managed service with high gross margin and an annual-billing cash cycle that self-funds. The economics work IF blended ARPU holds near $25K and the named-specialist-review labor doesn't balloon into a per-customer consulting drag — that is the swing factor between a 75% software-margin business and a 45% advisory business. The acute "audit notice / VDA cleanup" entry segment is real, urgent, already-budgeted money (companies pay Big-4 and Ryan hourly for exactly this today), but it is lumpy project revenue that converts to recurring only if the founders deliberately wire the annual subscription onto the back of the cleanup engagement. Path to ~$300K/founder by month 12 is plausible but not trivial; ~$500K+/founder by month 24 is very reachable on conservative math.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Favorable cash cycle: annual upfront subscription billed ahead of the fall reporting/remittance cycle; cash-positive on each new logo, postage as pass-through.
- Mandatory, recurring, already-budgeted demand: all 50 states require annual report/remit/due-diligence with penalties; companies already pay Ryan/True Partners/Big-4 hourly — the money exists in the wrong (expensive, non-recurring) bucket.
- Clean exit/financeability: Sovos's acquisition of an unclaimed-property software business is a named recent comp.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Margin dilution from services creep: if specialist review + VDA/audit-defense scale linearly with customers, blended GM slides from ~75% toward ~50% and the income-ladder headcount math gets much harder.
- ARPU unvalidated: the model rests on [ASSUMED] $25K; if mid-market holders anchor to a $5K–$10K self-service-software price (UPExchange/Sovos), the customer count roughly doubles.
- Lumpy, project-concentrated early revenue: the acute audit/VDA segment is easiest to sell but one-time; if it doesn't convert to the annual subscription, year-one revenue is a few large engagements (concentration + churn-cliff risk).

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single financial issue most likely to kill the upside is margin compression via services creep masquerading as a SaaS business. Unclaimed property is deceptively case-specific: dormancy periods, exemptions, B2B and de minimis rules, gift-card treatment, and securities/dividend handling vary by state and property type, and edge cases are the norm for the multi-state mid-market holders being targeted. If each customer requires meaningful named-specialist hours every cycle — and audit-defense/VDA engagements always do — the model is not a 75%-margin software service two founders plus a fractional team can run; it is a 45–55%-margin boutique advisory practice gated by hiring credentialed UP specialists, the very labor constraint the productization is meant to escape. That converts a leverageable recurring business into a linear consultancy, blows out the headcount needed for the income ladder, and erodes the clean recurring asset that makes it financeable. The thesis lives or dies on how much the rules engine genuinely automates versus how much the specialist must touch per account.

- What share of acute audit/VDA project clients convert to the recurring subscription, and at what attach rate? (Structure the first 5 cleanups with a built-in renewal clause; survey 10 recent Delaware-VDA filers.)
