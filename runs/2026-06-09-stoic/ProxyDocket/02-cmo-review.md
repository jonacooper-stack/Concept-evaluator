CMO REVIEW — ProxyDocket

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
This is a structurally attractive concept that hits the founders' archetype dead-center: a 50-state regulatory mandate forces a recurring, deadline-driven, budgeted buy; the incumbents are a sleepy split between expensive Big-4/Ryan advisors and self-service software (Sovos, UPExchange); and nobody owns the productized, fixed-fee, done-for-you mid-market lane. The marketing wedge is unusually clean because there are named, dreaded enforcement triggers — Kelmar audit notices, Delaware VDA letters, M&A diligence flags — that convert a deferred obligation into an acute, budget-loosening event you can target with precision. The honest caveat, and it is the whole question, is that for the broad base of holders unclaimed-property compliance is a "should-comply" obligation that finance teams rationally defer until an audit makes it real; the painkiller is sharp and immediate for the audit/VDA/IPO segment but latent and soft for everyone else, which splits the market into a hot, easy-to-sell minority and a large, hard-to-sell, education-heavy majority.

POSITIONING DRAFT
For the audit/VDA/IPO entry segment the positioning writes itself: "Unclaimed-property compliance, handled in every state, every year — for a fixed annual fee, not a contingency consultant's percentage — so the next Kelmar audit finds a clean, defensible file instead of a ten-year liability." Real edges: it names the enemy (Kelmar's contingency math), the dread (ten-year lookback), and the relief (fixed fee, done-for-you, every state). For the cold mid-market holder with no audit pending, the same sentence weakens to "you have a compliance gap you don't feel yet," which is a vitamin pitch and much harder. Excellent where demand is acute, mush where demand is latent.

THE WEDGE
The first 100 customers come from the acute-trigger segment, and the wedge is specific and cheap. (1) BOFU SEO/content on terms a panicked controller types after a notice: "Kelmar audit notice," "Delaware unclaimed property VDA," "escheatment reporting deadline [state]," "NAUPA format" — low-volume, ultra-high-intent terms big advisors don't rank for; [ASSUMED] $3K–$8K/mo content + specialist review can own this SERP in 6–9 months (verify by pulling volume/difficulty in Ahrefs/SEMrush for $100–$200). (2) Referral from M&A diligence advisors, audit-defense law firms, and accounting firms who surface the exposure but don't want the grinding annual remittance work — warm hand-offs at near-zero CAC. (3) Targeted outbound to Delaware-incorporated mid-market companies (Delaware incorporation is the strongest predictor of Kelmar exposure), filterable from public incorporation data + Apollo/ZoomInfo at [ASSUMED] $200–$500/mo. The wedge is real; the risk is that the acute segment, while easy to sell, may be a smaller annuity than the recurring-subscription thesis needs — many audit/VDA engagements are project-shaped one-and-done cleanups.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
Sovos (acquired the escheatment products) — best at enterprise self-service reporting software; strong tech, a tool you operate, not done-for-you. Eagle Technology Management / UPExchange — best at low-cost self-service NAUPA report generation; cheap, widely used by DIY holders, dated experience, no managed service. Ryan, True Partners, Big-4 abandoned-and-unclaimed-property groups — best at high-end audit defense + VDA for the largest holders, hourly at advisor rates; ignore the mid-market because the hours don't justify it. Kelmar Associates — best at enforcement; the contingency auditor that creates demand, effectively ProxyDocket's chief sales engine, not a competitor. Georgeson/Keane — outsourced UP processing for the very largest holders, enterprise-only. The air is clear: the mid-market holder too small for Ryan's hourly model and unwilling to operate Sovos/UPExchange has no productized, fixed-fee, done-for-you option. Differentiation (fixed fee vs contingency/hourly, managed vs self-service, mid-market vs enterprise) is structural, though a software incumbent (Sovos) could add a managed-service layer in 12–18 months.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.3 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- A named, dreaded enforcement engine (Kelmar contingency audits, Delaware VDA, M&A flags) that converts latent obligation into acute, budget-loosening events targetable with precision BOFU SEO + referral.
- A genuine, founder-doc-prized competitive gap: productized fixed-fee done-for-you mid-market managed service between self-service tools (Sovos, UPExchange) and hourly enterprise advisors (Ryan, Big-4).
- GTM (positioning-led TOFU + founder-led close into CFOs/controllers) maps to the founders' edge with no field ops and a bridgeable expertise moat.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- Latent demand for the cold base: for holders with no pending audit, this is a "should-comply" item finance teams defer; selling them is education-heavy, slow, vitamin-like — the recurring thesis may rest on a hot minority while the majority resists.
- The acute segment skews project-shaped: audit-defense and VDA cleanups are often one-and-done, threatening the recurring spine unless cleanups reliably convert into ongoing annual subscriptions.
- A funded software incumbent (Sovos) or an advisory firm building a SMB tier could add managed service in 12–18 months and out-distribute on existing customer bases.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The demand-bifurcation trap. The pitch sells beautifully to the narrow slice facing an active Kelmar notice, a Delaware VDA decision, or IPO/M&A diligence — but that slice is a finite annual flow, and its engagements are often project-shaped cleanups, not recurring subscriptions. The recurring annuity the business needs lives in the much larger base of holders with no acute trigger, and for that base unclaimed-property compliance is a textbook latent, deferrable obligation: real on paper, ignorable in practice until an auditor shows up. Converting cold holders requires manufacturing urgency they don't yet feel — precisely the "educate them into believing they have the problem" motion 01-objectives.md warns against. If the acute segment is too small to sustain the recurring thesis and the cold segment too vitamin-like to close affordably, the LTV/CAC blend degrades and the business becomes a steady stream of one-time cleanups. The mitigation — converting every cleanup into a mandatory ongoing managed subscription — is the make-or-break product and pricing decision.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What share of revenue and logos comes from acute-trigger buyers (audit/VDA/IPO) vs cold preventive buyers in year one, and what is the actual conversion from one-time cleanup into a recurring subscription?
- What is the genuine annual volume of high-intent search and identifiable acute triggers — how many Kelmar notices and Delaware VDA entrants per year, how many Delaware-incorporated mid-market holders fit — so we can size whether the hot segment alone hits the income ladder?
- For cold holders, what is the cost and cycle length to close with no pending audit, and is there a credible non-education trigger (a renewal moment, a recurring deadline-calendar product, a board/audit-committee mandate) that makes the buy feel forced?

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with one positioning condition)
Clears the bar honestly at 7.3 and fits the archetype better than most: a regulatory mandate forces a recurring budgeted buy, incumbents are a beatable split of expensive advisors + DIY tools, the wedge is named and cheap, GTM maps to the founders' edge. The condition is positioning/product discipline: lead with the acute segment (Kelmar/VDA/IPO) as the entry vector — never lead with cold preventive sales (the vitamin trap) — and architect pricing so every audit-defense or VDA cleanup mandatorily lands the client into the recurring annual managed-service subscription. Win on "we hold your defensible file and run every state's deadline so the next audit is a non-event," using switching cost on the multi-year archive as the retention moat. If cleanups reliably convert to subscriptions and the acute-trigger flow is large enough to seed the base, this is a strong build.
