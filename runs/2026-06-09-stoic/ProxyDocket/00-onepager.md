# ProxyDocket — Clean-Room One-Pager

## What it is
A productized, recurring managed service that handles a company's multi-state
unclaimed-property (escheatment) compliance: identifying reportable property,
running the legally required owner due-diligence mailings, generating each state's
report in the required format, remitting on each state's deadline, and maintaining
the audit-defense file. Software runs the property-identification rules, the
50-state deadline/format engine, the due-diligence mailing workflow, and the
records archive; a named unclaimed-property specialist reviews edge cases and
exemptions.

## The customer
US companies with multi-state unclaimed-property exposure and no dedicated
unclaimed-property staff — mid-market and larger companies across retail,
financial services, insurance, healthcare, manufacturing, and tech that accumulate
unclaimed obligations (uncashed payroll and vendor checks, customer credits and
refunds, rebates, gift-card balances, securities/dividends). Two acute entry
segments: (1) companies that have just received a state audit notice or a
contingency-auditor (e.g., Kelmar) engagement letter, and (2) companies in
pre-IPO or M&A diligence where unremitted escheatment liability is a flagged
exposure that must be cleaned up.

## The pain
All 50 states require holders of unclaimed property to perform owner due-diligence,
report, and remit annually, on staggered state deadlines (a large cluster falls in
the fall reporting cycle), with interest and penalties for non-compliance and no
statute of limitations protection without filing. States enforce aggressively
through contingency-fee third-party auditors (Kelmar dominates, especially for
Delaware-incorporated companies), and a multi-state audit can reach back 10–15
years and produce seven-figure assessments using estimation when records are
missing. Most finance teams under-comply because the work is tedious, multi-state,
and easy to defer — until an audit notice makes it acute. The obligation recurs
every year and grows with the company.

## The product / service
1. **Property identification & exemption analysis** — ingest AP/AR/payroll/GL data,
   identify reportable property by type and dormancy period, apply state-specific
   exemptions and B2B/de minimis rules.
2. **Due-diligence mailings** — generate and send the statutorily required
   owner-outreach letters within each state's window, track responses, reunite
   owners where possible (which reduces the remittance).
3. **Multi-state reporting & remittance** — produce each state's NAUPA-format
   report and remittance package, file on each deadline, track confirmations.
4. **Audit-defense file & VDA support** — maintain a dated, defensible records
   archive and support voluntary-disclosure-agreement (VDA) filings (e.g.,
   Delaware's VDA program) for companies cleaning up back exposure.

## Competitive landscape (stated neutrally)
The space includes escheatment software (Sovos, which acquired unclaimed-property
software; Eagle Technology Management's UPExchange), large advisory/consulting
practices (Ryan, True Partners, Big-4 abandoned-and-unclaimed-property groups —
expensive, hourly, audit-defense-oriented), and the contingency auditors (Kelmar)
on the enforcement side. The large advisors target the biggest holders at high
hourly rates; the software tools are largely self-service. There is no dominant
productized, fixed-fee, done-for-you recurring managed service aimed at the
mid-market holder who can't staff this and can't afford Big-4 hours.
