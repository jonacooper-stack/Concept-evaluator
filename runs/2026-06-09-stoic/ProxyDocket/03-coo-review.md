COO REVIEW — ProxyDocket

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
ProxyDocket is operationally one of the cleaner managed-compliance shapes I have reviewed: 100% remote, no physical supply chain, no field ops, no trucks/installs, and the deliverable is a data-driven document workflow (identify reportable property, mail due-diligence letters, file NAUPA-format reports, remit, archive). Two software-and-compliance-literate founders plus a named unclaimed-property (UP) specialist can genuinely operate this. The two operational hazards that decide whether it stays lean or collapses are (1) per-client data ingestion from messy AP/AR/payroll/GL systems — a recurring, unstandardized, judgment-heavy onboarding task that does NOT compress into a one-click import — and (2) the brutal seasonal spike around Oct 31–Nov 1, where roughly half the country's deadlines stack into a few weeks. The model is operable, but the spike and the ingestion-cleanliness problem mean the "software does it all" framing is partly aspirational; the real unit of work is data wrangling plus specialist judgment, not button-clicking.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Mid-March, ~12-month mark. [ASSUMED] ~25 recurring clients at ~$25K blended ARPU (~$625K ARR) plus 2–3 active projects (audit defense / Delaware VDA). March is deliberately quieter — the fall filing wave is over; spring is for onboarding, VDA projects, and the slow first-pass data ingestion that feeds the fall reports.
Founder A (compliance-operator) starts on a new client onboarding: a 600-employee multi-state retailer that signed in February after a state inquiry letter. Onboarding = get read access to (or flat-file exports from) AP, payroll, AR/credit-balance ledger, GL; then reconcile what "property" exists — uncashed vendor/payroll checks, customer credit balances, gift-card remainders, rebates. This is the hard part. The AP export has 40,000 line items, inconsistent vendor names, no clean "outstanding/uncashed" flag, a slow finance team. Founder A spends ~3 hours on back-and-forth: which GL accounts map to which property type, what's voided, what's a true outstanding liability. The rules engine applies dormancy + exemption logic ONCE the data is clean — but getting it clean is human, iterative, client-dependent.
Founder B (software/marketing) splits the day: ~2 hours maintaining the 50-state deadline/format/dormancy rules engine (a state changed a dormancy period or NAUPA layout — happens piecemeal every year), ~3 hours outbound to Delaware-incorporated controllers + two audit-defense law firms about referral flow. No one else to sell, so founder-led sales competes for delivery hours.
The named UP specialist (fractional, [ASSUMED] ~$120–180/hr) is engaged ~2 hours on an edge case: B2B credit balances with state-varying B2B exemptions; the specialist confirms which balances are exempt where. Exactly the judgment that can't be fully automated and is the firm's defensibility.
Where it breaks: nothing catastrophic, because March is calm — and that is the point. The stress test is late October, not March. Move this 25-client book to Oct 25: every client needs due-diligence mailings done (states require letters 60–120 days pre-filing, so front-loaded into summer), reports generated/reconciled, remittances wired by Oct 31–Nov 1 — that is when two founders + a fractional specialist risk being underwater.

CAPACITY MATH
Per-client annual effort [ASSUMED, verifiable by timing first 5 onboardings + first filing cycle]:
- Initial onboarding/ingestion (year 1 only): ~15–30 hrs (data-cleanliness-driven). Midpoint 20.
- Annual recurring cycle (steady state): due-diligence mailing prep/list (~3–5 hrs), report generation/reconciliation across reporting states (~4–8 hrs), remittance + archive (~2–3 hrs), ad-hoc support (~2–4 hrs) ≈ ~14 hrs/client/year, bulk in summer–fall.
- Specialist edge-case review: ~2–4 hrs/client/year. Take 3.
At 25 clients steady-state: generalist/ops 25×14 = 350 hrs/yr ≈ 7 hrs/wk; specialist 25×3 = 75 hrs/yr; plus onboarding ~20 new/yr × 20 hrs = 400 hrs ≈ 8 hrs/wk. ~15 hrs/wk delivery across two founders — trivially absorbable alongside sales/product. THIS is why the annual average looks fine.
Fall-spike stress test (the real constraint): [ASSUMED] ~55% of recurring hours + due-diligence prep concentrate into an ~8-week window = ~193 hrs in 8 weeks = ~24 hrs/wk pure delivery during the spike, plus in-flight onboarding and compressed specialist load (~40+ specialist hrs same window). Two founders can absorb 24 hrs/wk in the spike but with little room for lumpy high-value VDA/audit projects or sales; scales badly — at 60 clients the spike delivery alone is ~58 hrs/wk (~1.5 FTE crammed into 8 weeks), forcing a seasonal hire or second specialist.
Income-ladder check (vs 01-objectives ladder): mo12 ~$300K/founder = ~$600K pair → at ~$25K ARPU and [ASSUMED] 70–80% GM, to net ~$600K likely need ~$850K–$1.0M ARR ≈ 34–40 clients OR a mix of higher-ticket VDA/audit projects ($30K–$150K) to bridge year one — plausible but NOT a layup on subscription alone. mo24 ~$500K/founder = ~$1.0M pair → ~$1.4–1.6M ARR ≈ 55–65 clients; at 60 the fall spike is a real staffing event, so mo24 requires a seasonal ops hire (~$70–110K loaded UP-literate analyst) + a second fractional specialist, or aggressive ingestion automation.

SCORES (1–10)
1. 2. Supply chain resilience: 8 — Essentially no physical supply chain; inputs are client data + cloud/print-mail vendors, multi-sourced/replaceable. Due-diligence mailings run through commodity print-and-mail APIs (Lob, PostGrid, or a local mail house — ≥3 substitutable), no MOQs/deposits/FX.
3. Logistics tractability: 9 — Fully remote, no field visits/routing/installs; the only "logistics" is outbound postal mail via a vendor API — structurally the opposite of field ops.
4. Customer-ops scalability: 5 — Recurring annual cycle with low inter-cycle support is favorable, but onboarding is high-touch (15–30 hrs/client, data-cleanliness-dependent) and the fall spike concentrates support; neither self-serve nor smoothly low-touch.
5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: 8 — No bet-the-business platform: cloud, a print-mail API, and the public NAUPA spec are the inputs, all replaceable; the one real dependency is the named UP specialist (key-person), staffable with a second contractor.
6. Hiring feasibility: 6 — The named UP specialist exists as a fractional role (UP consultants from Ryan/True Partners/Big-4 alumni and Sovos/UPExchange ecosystems), but it is a thin specialized pool and a second specialist for spike/redundancy is not abundant; generalist ingestion analysts are easy to hire.
7. Throughput capacity at target: 6 — Annual-average load easily handled by two founders + fractional specialist (~15 hrs/wk at 25 clients), but the Oct 31–Nov 1 spike (~24 hrs/wk at 25, ~58 at 60) is the binding constraint and forces seasonal staffing before mo24 scale.

Quality control simplicity: 6 — The rules engine standardizes format/deadline/dormancy logic, but correctness depends on data completeness + exemption judgment, and the failure mode is severe (a missed deadline/under-reporting exposes the CLIENT to interest/penalties and the firm to liability), so QC requires specialist sign-off and can't be fully mechanized.
9. Geographic / seasonality risk: 4 — National and weather-neutral, but the fall deadline cluster around Oct 31–Nov 1 is a structural, unavoidable seasonality spike baked into state law; demand/work is genuinely peaky — the single most operationally awkward attribute.

Tooling maturity available: 6 — Cloud, print-mail APIs, CRM, billing off-the-shelf, and reference points exist (Sovos, Eagle/UPExchange), but the core 50-state deadline/format/dormancy/exemption rules engine + ingestion-normalization layer must be BUILT and continuously maintained as states change rules — real ongoing engineering.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.4 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Zero field ops, zero physical supply chain, fully remote: a document/data workflow with mailings via a vendor API — the capital-light, remote-first model the founders want (8–9 on supply chain/logistics).
- Recurring annual cycle with low inter-cycle support and a real software leverage point (the rules engine): annual-average delivery at 25 clients is only ~15 hrs/week across two founders.
- Few, replaceable vendor dependencies, no bet-the-business platform; the only key-person risk (the UP specialist) is staffable with a second contractor.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- The Oct 31–Nov 1 deadline cluster: a hard, law-imposed seasonal spike concentrating ~half the year's delivery into ~8 weeks, forcing seasonal hiring/second specialist before mo24 scale (dim 9 = 4, dim 7 = 6).
- Per-client data ingestion is bespoke, high-touch (15–30 hrs/client), judgment-heavy; the real unit of work, and it does not compress into self-serve, capping repeatability and customer-ops scalability.
- Severe failure mode: a missed deadline or mis-classified exemption exposes the client to penalties and the firm to liability, so QC requires specialist sign-off every cycle — tethering throughput to a thin specialist pool.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The seasonality spike is the operational issue most likely to make me say "I'm not sure two people can do this" — specifically at scale, not at 25 clients. By state law a large block of deadlines clusters around Oct 31–Nov 1, with the due-diligence mailings that must precede them front-loaded into summer. The firm cannot smooth its workload the way a normal SaaS can; the calendar is dictated by 50 statutes. At 25 clients this is uncomfortable but survivable (~24 delivery hrs/wk in the window). At the 55–65 clients the mo24 ladder requires, the spike delivery balloons to roughly a 1.5-FTE crammed into eight weeks, simultaneously demanding more specialist review hours from a thin pool — and a single botched fall cycle (a missed remittance, an under-reported property type surfacing in a later audit) is exactly the failure that destroys the "clean, defensible file" trust the whole business is sold on. It does not break in March; it breaks in the third week of October of the year they over-sell relative to staffed capacity. The mitigation is real and known (seasonal analyst hires, a second fractional specialist, staggering smaller-state filings, controlled onboarding ramp) but must be designed in deliberately, and it caps how "two-founder lean" this stays as it scales.

(Build a deadline calendar across a sample 25-client book; sum hours by week.)
- The named UP specialist is your defensibility AND your key-person risk — is this a contracted fractional engagement with defined SLA and fall-spike capacity, and do you have a second identified specialist for redundancy? (Get a signed fractional engagement + one named backup before launch.)
- How current/maintainable is the 50-state rules engine — who owns tracking statutory changes to dormancy periods, exemptions, NAUPA layouts each year, and how many hours does maintenance consume? (Reconcile your engine against one commercial source like Sovos/UPExchange for 5 states; time the diff.)

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally a fundamentally sound, remote-first, capital-light managed service with no field ops and a genuine software leverage point — closer to the desired archetype than most. Not a GO yet only because two specific mechanics are unproven and load-bearing: (1) the bespoke, high-touch per-client ingestion that is the true unit of work, and (2) the law-imposed Oct 31–Nov 1 seasonality spike that dictates staffing and caps leanness at the scale the ladder requires. Before GO: standardize onboarding into a documented, analyst-runnable ingestion playbook with a target hours ceiling per client; pre-design the fall-spike staffing model (seasonal analyst + second fractional specialist + deliberate filing-sequence staggering) rather than hand-waving it; and secure a contracted named specialist plus a redundant backup so QC isn't a single-person bottleneck during peak weeks. Resolve those three and this moves to GO.
