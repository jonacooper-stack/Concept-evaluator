# Clean-Room One-Pager — Independent Claims Adjuster Workspace

## The customer
Independent property/casualty insurance claims adjusters and the small independent adjusting
(IA) firms that employ them — the contractors that insurance carriers hire to inspect and
document claims (roof/storm damage, water, fire, catastrophe deployments) when the carrier's
own staff is overloaded. The U.S. has tens of thousands of licensed independent adjusters and
several thousand IA firms, most of them solo operators or shops of 2–25 adjusters. The buyer
is the independent adjuster or the firm owner — someone who is paid per claim, lives out of a
truck during catastrophe season, and personally eats the cost of every hour wasted on
paperwork. They have budget authority over their own tools and feel the pain directly.

## The problem
An independent adjuster's job is a documentation marathon: per claim they shoot dozens to
hundreds of photos, take measurements and moisture readings, write scope notes, fill carrier-
specific forms, and assemble a complete claim file that must satisfy a specific carrier's
requirements and survive later scrutiny. Today this is stitched together across a phone
camera, a notes app, email, spreadsheets, a separate estimating package, and the carrier's
portal. Photos get mislabeled or lost, files are assembled late at night in a hotel, and a
file that comes back for missing documentation costs unpaid rework and delays payment. During
catastrophe deployments the volume spikes and the mess compounds. The work repeats on every
single claim, forever — so the pain is chronic and per-transaction, not one-time.

## The product / service
A mobile-first adjuster workspace that organizes the entire claim file from first photo to
final submission:
- On-site capture: structured photo collection with automatic labeling/tagging by area and
  damage type, voice-to-text scope notes, measurement and moisture-reading logging, all
  organized per claim and synced to the cloud.
- File assembly: auto-generates a clean, carrier-ready claim package (photo report, narrative,
  documentation checklist) in the format common carriers expect, flagging missing items before
  submission so files don't bounce back.
- Workflow: a simple pipeline of every open claim with status, deadlines, and a record of what
  was sent where — replacing the spreadsheet-and-email sprawl.
- Optional integrations/exports to the common estimating tools and carrier portals adjusters
  already use, rather than trying to replace the estimating engine itself.

## The revenue model
Recurring subscription. Two tiers: a per-adjuster monthly seat (roughly $75–$200/seat/month)
for solo and small-firm adjusters, and firm plans for IA shops (a few hundred to low-thousands
per month depending on adjuster count), billed annually or monthly. Revenue is repeating and
seat-based, expanding as a firm adds adjusters or as solo adjusters convert to annual plans.
An optional per-claim usage add-on (a few dollars per assembled file) captures catastrophe-
season volume spikes without raising base price.

## Positioning
"Every claim file, court-ready and carrier-ready, before you leave the site." Positioned
against the status quo of phone-plus-email-plus-spreadsheet duct tape, and against heavyweight
carrier-side claims-management platforms that are bought by carriers for staff adjusters, not
by the independent adjuster who actually owns their own tooling. The wedge is being the tool
built for the independent adjuster's real on-site workflow — fast capture, automatic
organization, no-bounce-back files — rather than a back-office system of record imposed from
above.

## How it's delivered and grown
The product is standard mobile + web SaaS. Early customers come through independent-adjuster
communities, licensing/continuing-education channels, catastrophe-adjuster networks and rosters,
trade associations, and word of mouth among adjusters who deploy together during storm season.
The founders bring direct insurance-industry domain knowledge to the product design, carrier-
format requirements, and the positioning, and run a content-and-community go-to-market aimed at
the independent-adjuster audience.
