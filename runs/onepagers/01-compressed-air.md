# Clean-Room One-Pager — Compressed Air Optimization Service

## The customer
Small and mid-size industrial plants that run on compressed air: machine shops,
automotive parts manufacturers, plastics/injection molders, food and beverage processors,
and packaging operations. Typical site has 1–10 air compressors (50–500 HP total) running
one to three shifts. The buyer is the plant manager, operations manager, or facilities/
energy manager — someone with a maintenance budget and direct authority over utility and
downtime costs. These are unglamorous, regionally dispersed businesses; most do not employ
a dedicated energy engineer.

## The problem
Compressed air is the most expensive utility per unit of useful work in a plant, and the
single largest source of waste in most facilities is air leaks — at fittings, hoses,
quick-disconnects, drains, and aging pipe. Industry field studies put leak losses at
roughly 20–30% of total compressed-air energy in an unmanaged plant. Leaks are invisible
and inaudible over ambient noise, so they go unfixed; plants compensate by running extra
compressors, which burns electricity 24/7 and accelerates equipment wear. Most plants have
never had a systematic leak survey, or had one once years ago and never repeated it. Leaks
regenerate continuously as equipment vibrates and ages, so a one-time fix decays within
12–18 months — which makes this a recurring problem, not a one-time project.

## The product / service
A productized "compressed-air optimization" program delivered on a recurring cadence:
- An ultrasonic leak-detection survey of the plant (handheld acoustic imaging devices make
  leaks visible/audible), tagging every leak with location, estimated CFM loss, and
  estimated annual dollar cost.
- A written report quantifying total leak load, prioritized fix list, and projected energy
  $ savings, plus optional coordination/verification of repairs.
- Quarterly or semi-annual re-surveys to catch newly formed leaks and document sustained
  savings — the recurring core.
- A simple customer dashboard tracking leaks found/fixed, CFM recovered, and cumulative
  dollar and kWh savings over time, suitable for the plant's own reporting and for utility
  energy-rebate paperwork.

## The revenue model
Recurring service contract, billed annually or quarterly, in the range of roughly
$8,000–$30,000 per plant per year depending on plant size and survey cadence. The pitch is
ROI-anchored: a typical mid-size plant wastes tens of thousands of dollars a year on leaks,
so an ongoing program that recovers most of it pays for itself several times over. An
optional shared-savings component (a percentage of verified, metered energy reduction) can
be offered to lower the buyer's perceived risk. Many electric utilities offer rebates for
compressed-air efficiency work, which can subsidize the customer's cost and shorten the
sales cycle.

## Positioning
"We find and keep finding the money leaking out of your plant's air system." Positioned
against the status quo of doing nothing, against one-and-done consultants who survey once
and leave, and against compressor OEMs/dealers whose incentive is to sell more hardware
rather than reduce air demand. The wedge is the recurring, measured, vendor-neutral program
with documented savings — turning an occasional, ignored maintenance task into a managed
subscription with proof of ROI.

## How it's delivered and grown
Founders learn the survey methodology (a learnable hands-on skill, not a licensed trade),
buy or lease acoustic imaging equipment, and personally run early surveys to build a
reference base of documented savings. As contracts grow, trained technicians run routine
surveys on a regional routing model while the founders handle sales, partnerships (utility
energy programs, regional manufacturers' associations), and the software/reporting layer.
