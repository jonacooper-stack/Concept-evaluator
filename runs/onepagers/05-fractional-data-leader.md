# Clean-Room One-Pager — Fractional Data & Analytics Leader for Mid-Market Companies

## The customer
Profitable, non-tech mid-market companies — roughly 50 to 500 employees, $10M–$150M revenue —
in unglamorous sectors like distribution, manufacturing, logistics, multi-location services,
healthcare services, construction, and specialty retail. These companies have outgrown
spreadsheets and run on a tangle of disconnected systems (an ERP, a CRM, a payroll system,
point-of-sale, spreadsheets everywhere), but they are too small to justify a $250K/year VP of
Data or a full in-house analytics team. The buyer is the CEO, COO, or CFO — an owner-operator
or senior executive who is drowning in conflicting reports, can't get a straight answer to
basic questions ("which customers actually make us money?"), and is making seven-figure
decisions on gut and stale spreadsheets.

## The problem
Mid-market operators have plenty of data and almost no usable intelligence. Numbers don't tie
out between systems, every department reports its own version of the truth, the monthly close
is a manual fire drill, and nobody owns turning raw operational data into decisions. Hiring is
the obvious fix and the obvious trap: a full-time analytics leader is expensive, hard to find,
hard to evaluate, and often overkill for a company that needs direction and a few good
dashboards more than a standing team. So the work doesn't get done — the CEO keeps flying
partially blind, initiatives stall for lack of a number, and the company leaves margin and
growth on the table because no one translates its data into a plan.

## The product / service
A fractional data & analytics leader engagement — an outsourced "head of data" delivered as an
ongoing monthly retainer:
- A standing senior advisor (the founders) who acts as the company's part-time data/analytics
  leader: sets the roadmap, defines the metrics that matter, and owns the translation of
  business questions into answers.
- A connected reporting foundation: wiring the company's existing systems into a single source
  of truth and a small set of executive dashboards (revenue, margin by customer/product,
  cash, operations KPIs) that finally tie out — built on modern off-the-shelf data tooling,
  not a custom platform.
- A monthly operating cadence: a recurring executive review where the advisor walks leadership
  through what the numbers say, what changed, and what to do about it — plus ad-hoc analysis on
  the questions that come up between meetings.
- Light hands-on build and vendor management: standing up and maintaining the dashboards and
  pipelines, and managing or directing any cheaper offshore/contract analysts as the account
  grows, so the client gets outcomes without hiring a team.

## The revenue model
A monthly retainer per client, roughly $5,000–$12,000 per month depending on scope and company
size, on an ongoing engagement (not a fixed-end project). Revenue is recurring and sticky:
once the dashboards are embedded in how leadership runs the monthly business review and the
advisor is the trusted owner of "the numbers," the engagement renews indefinitely and tends to
expand. A modest one-time setup/onboarding fee covers the initial system-wiring sprint. A
two-person team can carry a portfolio of retainers directly, then add junior/contract analysts
to extend capacity per account.

## Positioning
"Your outsourced head of data — executive-grade analytics leadership, without the $250K hire."
Positioned against the three real alternatives: doing nothing (flying blind), hiring a
full-time data leader the company can't really justify or evaluate, and one-off BI consulting
projects that deliver a dashboard and disappear. The wedge is ongoing leadership plus a living
reporting foundation — a retained relationship that owns the outcome month after month, rather
than a project that ends or a hire that's a year-long bet.

## How it's delivered and grown
Delivery uses modern, mostly-managed data tooling (cloud warehouse, off-the-shelf pipeline
connectors, a BI/dashboard layer) so the build is configuration and integration, not custom
engineering; the high-value work is the judgment — knowing which metrics matter and translating
them into decisions. Clients are reached through the founders' executive and operator networks,
referrals from accountants/fractional-CFOs/consultants who serve the same mid-market buyer,
industry and owner peer groups (e.g., Vistage-style CEO groups), and targeted outbound to
sectors the founders know. The founders deliver the senior advisory and relationship, build the
reporting foundation, and add contract analyst capacity as the portfolio grows.
