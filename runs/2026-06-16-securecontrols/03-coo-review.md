# COO REVIEW — SecureControls

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ

Yes — this is operable by two founders plus a lean team, and it is squarely the kind of remote-first, software-leveraged, recurring-service operation the founders are built for. The unit of work is knowledge work (gap assessment, SSP/POA&M authorship, SPRS calculation, questionnaire responses, quarterly evidence refresh) delivered entirely over the wire from a multi-tenant system-of-record — no trucks, no installs, no on-site visits, no field crews. The two real operational hazards are (1) front-loaded onboarding (40–80 hours per new customer) being the true throughput throttle until it is templatized, and (2) the credentialed-reviewer sign-off gate becoming a single-point-of-judgment bottleneck — both of which the concept names openly and both of which are manageable, not structural. The "artifact-only, never CUI" architectural choice is the single smartest operational decision in the brief: it strips out the heaviest liability and infrastructure burden (enclaves, CUI custody, MSSP-grade SOC obligations) and keeps the delivery surface to documents and judgment, which is exactly where these founders' SOC 2 / HIPAA program-running muscle transfers.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH

Picture month 12: ~28 active subscribers, two founders, one compliance analyst (hired month 4–6), and a fractional credentialed RP/CCP on a retainer. It's a Tuesday in March — quarterly cadence season, so this is a representative-to-busy day, not a quiet one.

Morning. The recurring-cadence scheduler has already queued the day's work. Across 28 accounts the quarterly review cadence spreads roughly 7 accounts per month (28 ÷ 4), so on any given week ~1.5–2 accounts are mid-quarterly-cycle; today the analyst is working evidence refresh on two of them — pulling updated screenshots/config exports the customer uploaded to their tenant of the system-of-record, checking that the 110 control objectives still map to current evidence, and flagging two controls where evidence has gone stale (an expired vulnerability-scan artifact, a lapsed access-review attestation). She drafts the POA&M update and the SPRS-worksheet delta but does NOT post anything — that's gated.

Mid-morning. Founder A (delivery/program lead) is 3 weeks into a new-customer onboarding — the heavy unit. This single engagement is the day's center of gravity. Today's onboarding block is authoring the baseline SSP narrative for ~20 of the 110 controls and reconciling the customer's self-reported implementation against what their MSP actually has configured. This is the 40–80-hour task spread across the 4–8 week window; call it ~10–14 focused hours this week on this one account. A second prospect's free AI-assisted readiness assessment finished overnight; Founder A spends 30 minutes sanity-checking the auto-generated 15–25 page gap report before it goes out as a lead magnet (the AI does the heavy lifting; a human eyeballs it so nothing embarrassing ships).

Midday. An inbound prime supplier-security questionnaire lands from an existing subscriber's customer (the prime) — 60-odd questions, due in 5 business days. This is a turnkey deliverable: the questionnaire-response engine auto-answers ~70–80% from the maintained evidence and the growing answer library; the analyst reviews the auto-answers and hand-writes the ~15 that are account-specific. Total human time ~2–3 hours instead of the 1–2 days an unequipped subcontractor would burn. This is the operational flywheel — the answer library compounds, so questionnaire #50 is far cheaper than questionnaire #1.

Afternoon — the gate. The fractional RP/CCP has a standing 2-hour review block. Everything SPRS-affecting funnels here: the two quarterly SPRS-worksheet deltas, the new customer's first baseline SPRS calculation isn't ready yet (still in onboarding), but a prior account's re-posting package is. The practitioner signs off, the analyst posts the SPRS score to SPRS and files the evidence package. Nothing reaches the customer's attestation or a prime's inbox without this signature. On a Tuesday in March this gate is comfortably within a 2-hour fractional block; the question (correctly flagged in the brief) is what this looks like at 55+ accounts, which is why a second qualified reviewer is scheduled before ~30 customers.

Founder B (marketing/close) is off the delivery critical path entirely today — running a mock-review call with a prospect from an APEX/MEP event, nurturing a prime supplier-development referral relationship, and publishing SEO content on the Nov 10, 2026 CMMC Level 2 deadline. That functional split — one founder owns delivery, one owns demand — is what keeps two people from colliding.

Where it breaks if it breaks. If two new customers close in the same fortnight, onboarding hours (40–80 each) collide on Founder A while quarterly cadence keeps grinding underneath — that is the pinch point, and it is a SCHEDULING/sequencing problem (stagger onboarding starts, cap concurrent onboards to 1–2 until analyst #2 lands), not a structural one. The second break point is the practitioner gate if volume spikes; the fix is already in the plan (second reviewer before 30 accounts).

CAPACITY MATH

Show the work. Two distinct workloads: steady-state subscription maintenance, and front-loaded onboarding.

Steady-state maintenance (the brief states ~30–35 expert/analyst hours per subscriber per year):
- At month 12, 28 accounts × 32.5 hrs (midpoint) = ~910 maintenance hours/year ≈ ~17.5 hrs/week.
- At month 24, 55 accounts × 32.5 hrs = ~1,788 maintenance hours/year ≈ ~34 hrs/week. (Brief says ~1,800–1,900 hrs/yr ≈ ~2.5 delivery FTEs — confirmed; my arithmetic lands at the low end of their stated band, which is the honest read.)

Onboarding (the heavy unit, ~40–80 hrs per new customer; midpoint ~60 hrs):
- To reach 28 active by mo12 (net of any churn) ≈ ~30 onboards in year one. 30 × 60 hrs = ~1,800 hrs of onboarding across the year ≈ ~35 hrs/week of onboarding labor in year one — front-loaded and lumpy, not smooth. THIS is the binding constraint in year one, not maintenance.
- Year-one total delivery load: ~910 maintenance + ~1,800 onboarding = ~2,710 hrs ≈ ~52 hrs/week of delivery work across the year. Two founders (even at, say, 60% of their time on delivery vs. 40% on sales/admin = ~2 × ~25 delivery hrs = ~50 hrs) plus a part-loaded analyst from month 4–6 covers this — but it is TIGHT and assumes onboards are staggered. The fractional practitioner is a reviewer (a few hours/week of gate time), not a producer, so they don't relieve production load. [ASSUMED: ~50% of net new onboards templatized enough by H2 to drop toward the 40-hr end — verify by tracking actual onboarding hours per cohort in the system-of-record.]

Month 24 target (55 accounts, team of 2 founders + 2 analysts + fractional practitioner):
- Maintenance: ~1,788 hrs/yr.
- Onboarding to grow 28 → 55 net (~30–35 gross onboards in year two): ~33 × 50 hrs (templated midpoint, lower than year one) = ~1,650 hrs/yr.
- Total year-two delivery: ~1,788 + 1,650 = ~3,438 hrs ≈ ~66 hrs/week, divided across 2 founders (partial) + 2 analysts (full) ≈ 3.0–3.5 delivery FTEs. With 2 analysts at ~1,800 productive hrs each = 3,600 hrs of analyst capacity ALONE, plus founder hours, the headcount covers the load with margin. Confirmed: 2 founders + 2 analysts + fractional practitioner is sufficient for 55 accounts, and the stated ~2.5 delivery FTEs figure refers to STEADY-STATE maintenance only — the brief is internally consistent if you read it that way (maintenance ≈ 2.5 FTE; onboarding adds the rest).

The gate math:
- Practitioner sign-off applies to every SPRS-affecting judgment + SSP/POA&M. At 55 accounts, quarterly reviews = 55 × 4 = 220 SPRS-affecting events/year ≈ ~4–5 review events/week, plus ~33 onboarding baseline sign-offs/year (~0.6/week). At ~30–45 min of senior review per event, that's ~3–5 senior review hours/week — a fractional practitioner can carry this, and the planned SECOND reviewer before 30 accounts removes the single-point-of-failure. This is the correct operational instinct.

Bottom line: the capacity math closes. The constraint is onboarding hours (front-loaded, lumpy) in year one and the review gate at scale — both named, both staffable, neither structural.

- High-touch, anxious customer base under a hard deadline: compliance buyers facing Nov-2026 CMMC Level 2 will generate non-self-serve support and "will we pass?" pressure, plus expectation-management risk on C3PAO-assessment-prep "within defined limits" — scope discipline and a CS function (month 18+) are essential to keep support load from swamping two founders.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The one that makes me pause is onboarding throughput colliding with the quality gate during the growth ramp — specifically, the year-one window where the model must simultaneously (a) absorb ~30 front-loaded onboards at 40–80 hours each, (b) keep quarterly cadence current on a growing installed base, and (c) funnel every SPRS-affecting judgment through a single fractional credentialed reviewer — all on two founders plus one analyst who isn't hired until month 4–6. The capacity math closes ON PAPER (~52 delivery hrs/week in year one across two founders + a part-loaded analyst), but it closes only if three things hold: onboards are staggered rather than lumpy, the templatization curve actually drives per-onboard hours down on schedule, and the practitioner gate doesn't become a queue. If onboarding hours run hot (toward 80, not 40) because the analog SOC 2 instinct under-models the DIB-specific 800-171 nuance the founders openly lack, OR if reviewer #2 is slow to recruit, the bottleneck isn't a missed quarter of growth — it's a degraded or late SPRS posting, which in this market is the one failure that actually hurts a customer (a frozen purchase order). It is manageable and the brief names every piece of the fix (price onboarding to loaded cost, cap concurrent onboards, second reviewer before 30, templatize relentlessly), but it is the axis where two people are most likely to feel genuinely underwater before the third and fourth hires land.
