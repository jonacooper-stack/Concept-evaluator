# CTO REVIEW — SecureControls

ONE-PARAGRAPH TECHNICAL READ
Technically, this is one of the cleaner bootstrap builds I see: the defining architectural decision — artifact-only, raw CUI excluded by design — strips out the single hardest and most expensive technical obligation in this space (CUI custody, GovCloud/FedRAMP-aligned hosting, CMMC-on-your-own-infra) and leaves a multi-tenant compliance system-of-record that is, at its core, a structured-data CRUD app with a scheduler, a document/evidence store, and an LLM-assisted survey-and-questionnaire layer. None of the hard parts are research problems; everything on the critical path is boring, proven, off-the-shelf tech (Postgres, a standard web framework, object storage, a managed auth provider, Stripe, a hosted LLM API). The ~8–12 dev-week estimate for the system-of-record is credible and arguably conservative, and the founders' explicit plan to earn revenue on document-delivered onboarding before the portal is finished is exactly the right sequencing for a bootstrap. The two real technical watch-items are (1) the AI-assisted SPRS estimate must be positioned as decision-support with a credentialed human gate — never an authoritative score — because a wrong posted SPRS score has contractual consequences, and (2) the "raw-CUI-never-touches-us" boundary is as much a policy/contract/training problem as a technical control, and a single leaked drawing in an evidence upload undermines the whole risk story.

ARCHITECTURE SKETCH
- Frontend: A standard multi-tenant SPA — React/Next.js (or Remix) on Vercel/Netlify. Per-tenant control dashboard against the 110 NIST 800-171 requirements, SSP/POA&M views, evidence library, SPRS worksheet, questionnaire workspace. No exotic UI; the complexity is in data modeling the control framework, not in rendering it.
- Backend: A single application service (Node/TypeScript or Python/Django/FastAPI) — REST/GraphQL API, role- and tenant-scoped. This is a structured-data application: the "domain model" is the 110 control objectives, their assessment objectives, mappings, statuses, and evidence linkage. That model is the actual product IP, not the framework.
- Data store: Managed Postgres (RDS/Supabase/Neon) with strict per-tenant row-level isolation; object storage (S3) for evidence artifacts and generated reports (SSP/POA&M/SPRS package PDFs). A versioned, append-only audit log table is mandatory, not optional, given this is a compliance product whose own credibility depends on traceability.
- Integrations (thin): Stripe for fixed-price onboarding + tiered subscription billing; transactional email (Postmark/SES) for the cadence engine; a hosted LLM (OpenAI/Anthropic/Azure OpenAI) for survey follow-ups and questionnaire drafting; document generation (server-side templating to PDF/DOCX) for the deliverables. SPRS itself is a government portal — assume NO API; posting is a human action by the credentialed RP/CCP, so this is a "generate-the-package-and-track-the-window" workflow, not a system integration.
- Async / background: The recurring-cadence engine — a scheduler (a job runner like a cron worker, BullMQ, Temporal, or even Postgres-backed pg-cron/Graphile Worker) firing quarterly review tasks, evidence-refresh reminders, reassessment dates, SPRS re-posting windows, renewal alerts. This is the second-most-important piece of custom code after the control data model, and it is well-trodden territory.
- ML/AI: Bounded and non-critical-path by design — LLM does (a) assessment follow-up questioning and mapping answers to control objectives, (b) questionnaire-answer drafting from a maintained answer library (a retrieval/RAG pattern over the firm's own evidence and a curated answer corpus). The SPRS *estimate* is deterministic arithmetic over the 110 controls (the DoD scoring methodology is a published, fixed-weight rubric), so it should be a coded scoring function — NOT an LLM guess — with the LLM only helping gather inputs. That distinction is where complexity and liability hide.
- Auth: Buy it. A managed IdP (Auth0/Clerk/WorkOS/Cognito) with SSO/SAML readiness for larger accounts, enforced MFA, and tenant-scoped RBAC. Rolling your own auth here would be malpractice given the buyer is a security-conscious population.
- Hosting: Ordinary commercial cloud (the firm's own infra never holds CUI, so NO GovCloud requirement for the core product — this is the whole point and it is correct). Stay in a single mainstream provider; FedRAMP/GovCloud only re-enters if/when the gated Phase 2 enclave business is pursued.
- Where the real complexity lives: (1) faithfully encoding the 110 controls / assessment objectives and the SPRS scoring methodology so the artifacts are defensible; (2) the technical control that blocks raw-CUI upload while still allowing legitimate evidence — a genuine false-positive/false-negative design problem; (3) keeping the multi-tenant isolation airtight for a buyer who will literally audit you. Everything else is assembly.

BUILD PLAN TO REVENUE-EARNING MVP
Total to a portal-backed MVP: ~8–14 dev-weeks of one developer, which tracks with the founders' 8–12 estimate. Critically, revenue does not wait for the portal — the onboarding deliverables (gap assessment, SSP, POA&M, SPRS package) can be produced with templated documents + spreadsheets + a doc generator from week 0, so first dollars can land in weeks, with the portal hardening the recurring-cadence margin afterward. Chunks in order:
1. Revenue-now, no-portal layer (~1–2 weeks, partly non-dev): Templated SSP/POA&M/SPRS-worksheet documents, the readiness-assessment survey as a structured form (Typeform/Tally + a coded SPRS scoring spreadsheet), and a doc-generation step. This earns onboarding revenue before any portal exists. The AI follow-ups can start as a human-in-the-loop prompt workflow, not a feature.
2. Core system-of-record (~4–6 weeks): Multi-tenant data model for the 110 controls, statuses, evidence linkage, SSP/POA&M as structured records, the SPRS scoring function (deterministic), managed auth, RBAC, audit log, evidence object storage with the raw-CUI-block control. This is the heart and the moat-in-software.
3. Recurring-cadence engine (~2–3 weeks): Scheduler + task generation + email reminders + reassessment/re-posting windows + renewal alerts. This is what converts the subscription from "consultant remembers to email you" into a scalable, low-touch product.
4. Questionnaire-response engine + answer library (~2–3 weeks): Answer-library data model, RAG/retrieval over evidence + curated answers, LLM-assisted draft generation, human review/approve flow. Highest-leverage feature for the prime-questionnaire pain; can ship as v1.5.
5. AI-assisted assessment polish + reporting (~1–2 weeks): LLM follow-up questioning wired into the survey, the 15–25 page report generator (the lead magnet). The free assessment is the top-of-funnel growth engine, so it earns its place early, but the *manual* version can run before it is automated.
Punt-to-v2 candidates: the questionnaire RAG engine (do it semi-manually first), SSO/SAML (only larger accounts need it), any analytics/benchmarking, and absolutely the entire Phase 2 GovCloud enclave (correctly already gated out).

CRITICAL ASSUMPTIONS
1. ASSUMED — The SPRS scoring methodology and the 110 control objectives can be encoded as a deterministic, defensible function. The DoD SPRS scoring template is a published, fixed-weight rubric (110 controls, specified point deductions), so this is encodable rather than discretionary. Verify cheaply: have the RP/CCP advisor reproduce 3–5 real customer SPRS scores by hand and reconcile them against the coded function before any score is posted. If the human and the code disagree, the human wins and the code is wrong — design for that.
2. ASSUMED — The LLM is reliable enough for *assistance* (drafting follow-up questions, drafting questionnaire answers from a known corpus) but is NOT the system of authority for any posted/attested artifact. This is the safe and correct framing; the risk only appears if the founders let the AI output flow to SPRS or to a prime without the credentialed human gate. Verify: red-team 20 questionnaire items and 10 assessment flows for hallucinated controls/answers; measure the human-correction rate; require sign-off on anything customer- or government-facing regardless of confidence.
3. ASSUMED [must verify, this is the load-bearing one] — A technical control can reliably block raw-CUI upload to the platform while still permitting legitimate compliance evidence (screenshots, configs, attestations). This is a fuzzy classification problem with no clean technical bright line — a network-diagram drawing or a CUI-marked document can look like "evidence." A purely technical filter will have false negatives. Verify before launch: design the boundary as defense-in-depth — contractual prohibition + customer training + structured evidence types (prefer attestations and config exports over free-form file upload) + CUI-marking/keyword detection + size/type gating + a documented "this is not a CUI repository" UX — and accept that the contract and training, not a magic filter, are the primary control. Pressure-test it with the legal/security advisor.
4. ASSUMED — SPRS has no usable public API for posting/reading, so posting is a human RP/CCP action. This is almost certainly true (SPRS is a CAC/PIV-gated government portal). The plan already treats posting as a human step, so this is fine; verify only that nothing in the roadmap secretly depends on programmatic SPRS access.
5. ASSUMED — The founders can stand up the firm's own SOC 2 Type II within ~9–12 months using their own prior SOC 2 experience, without expensive outside help. Their stated multiple-end-to-end-SOC-2-cycle background makes this credible (this is genuinely their wheelhouse), and the artifact-only design keeps the audit scope small. Verify: scope the SOC 2 boundary in month 1, pick the auditor and the evidence-automation tool early, and don't let the firm's own posture lag the moment a buyer's questionnaire asks for it.

INFORMATION SECURITY POSTURE
Data collected: compliance artifacts and attestation evidence for many defense subcontractors — SSPs, POA&Ms, control-status records, configuration screenshots, questionnaire answers, SPRS worksheets — plus customer-org contact/PII and billing data. Explicitly NOT collected: the customers' raw CUI (that is the architectural promise and the central risk-reduction lever). Where it lives: mainstream commercial cloud (no GovCloud needed for the core, because no CUI) — managed Postgres + S3-class object storage, single mainstream provider. Threat model: this firm is a concentrated, high-value target — it aggregates the security *weaknesses* (POA&Ms, gaps, control deficiencies) of thousands of defense suppliers in one place; a breach exposes not raw CUI but a roadmap of where every customer is weak, which is itself sensitive and reputationally fatal. The buyer is security-literate and will audit the firm. Controls needed at launch (non-negotiable, not "later"): managed IdP with enforced MFA; tenant-scoped RBAC with strict per-tenant data isolation (row-level security + tested authorization, since cross-tenant leakage is the worst-case failure); encryption at rest and in transit (table stakes, provider-default plus app-level for the most sensitive fields); centralized secrets management (no secrets in code/env files — use a vault/parameter store); comprehensive, immutable audit logging (mandatory for a compliance product's own credibility and for any SOC 2); least-privilege access to production with logged, ideally just-in-time access; backups with tested restore; a written incident-response plan from day one (a compliance vendor with no IR plan is indefensible). The raw-CUI-block control is itself a security control and must be treated as such — defense-in-depth (contract + training + structured evidence + detection), not a single filter. SOC 2 implications: the founders have run multiple SOC 2 Type II cycles end-to-end, so SOC 2-attainable is not aspirational here — it is squarely in their demonstrated competence, and the artifact-only scope keeps the audit boundary small. The honest gap is timing: buyers may ask for the firm's SOC 2 before the 9–12-month plan delivers it, so a Type I early and a clear "in-progress" attestation bridge that window. PII handling is modest (business contacts, not consumer data), which keeps privacy exposure low.

AVERAGE SCORE: 8.3 / 10

TOP 3 TECHNICAL STRENGTHS
- The artifact-only / never-CUI architectural decision is the single best technical choice in the concept: it deletes the hardest and most expensive obligation in the entire DIB-compliance space (CUI custody, GovCloud/FedRAMP-aligned hosting, CMMC scope on the firm's own infra), letting the founders ship on ordinary commercial cloud while shrinking breach exposure and audit scope.
- A genuinely thin custom wedge over bought commodity infrastructure — auth, billing, email, hosting, doc-gen, and the LLM are all off-the-shelf; the only real code is the control data model and the cadence engine, which is the textbook capital-light bootstrap shape and supports the ~8–12 dev-week estimate.
- Revenue precedes the portal: because onboarding deliverables are documents, the founders can charge for onboarding within weeks using templates + a coded SPRS scoring sheet, then let the software harden the recurring-subscription margin — eliminating the "12 months of building before a dollar" failure mode the bootstrap can't survive.

TOP 3 TECHNICAL RISKS
- The raw-CUI-block boundary is more policy/contract/training than clean technical control; a fuzzy classification problem (a CUI-marked drawing can masquerade as "evidence") means a purely technical filter will have false negatives, and one leaked CUI artifact would collapse the entire risk-reduction story the architecture is built on.
- AI overreach on attested artifacts: if the LLM's SPRS estimate or questionnaire answers ever reach SPRS or a prime without the credentialed human gate, a single wrong posted score has direct contractual consequences (a supplier frozen out of POs) and converts a software bug into a customer-business-impacting liability.
- Concentration-target threat model: the firm aggregates the documented weaknesses (POA&Ms, gaps) of thousands of defense suppliers, making it a high-value breach target whose compromise is reputationally fatal even though it holds no raw CUI — and the firm's own SOC 2 lags revenue by a planned 9–12 months, creating a window where security-literate buyers may demand proof the firm cannot yet show.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The most likely thing to hurt this business technically is not a build failure — the build is sound — it is the integrity of the artifact-only boundary, because that one decision is load-bearing for the entire cost structure, security posture, and legal positioning of the company. The architecture's whole advantage (commercial cloud, no GovCloud, small audit scope, shrunken breach exposure) depends on the firm credibly never holding raw CUI. But "evidence" and "CUI" are not cleanly separable at the file level: a configuration screenshot can contain a CUI marking, a network diagram is both legitimate SSP evidence and potentially CUI, and a customer under deadline pressure will absolutely try to upload whatever is convenient. A single technical filter cannot guarantee this boundary; it must be enforced as defense-in-depth — contractual prohibition, customer training, a UX that steers toward structured attestations and config exports rather than free-form file dumps, CUI-marking/keyword detection, and type/size gating — and even then the residual risk is real. If a CUI document lands in the platform and is later discovered (e.g., during an incident or a customer's own assessment), the firm has inadvertently taken on exactly the CUI-custody and CMMC-hosting obligations it designed itself to avoid, on infrastructure that was never built for it — simultaneously a security exposure, a contractual breach, and an existential blow to the firm's credibility with a buyer population that is, by definition, paying it to handle compliance correctly. This is manageable, but it must be treated as a first-class design and operational problem from day one, not a checkbox.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Exactly how is the raw-CUI-block enforced, and what is the layered control set (contractual language + training + structured evidence types + detection + type/size gating)? What is your tested false-negative plan when — not if — a customer uploads a CUI-marked file anyway, and how is such an event detected, quarantined, and remediated?
- Is the SPRS score a coded deterministic function over the published 110-control rubric (with the LLM only gathering inputs), and what is the mandatory human-sign-off workflow that guarantees no AI-generated number or answer ever reaches SPRS or a prime without the credentialed RP/CCP approving it? What is the audit trail proving who approved what before posting?
- What is the concrete plan and timeline for the firm's OWN security posture, given buyers will audit you and the SOC 2 Type II is 9–12 months out — specifically, will you stand up a SOC 2 Type I or an equivalent bridge attestation early, enforce MFA/RBAC/immutable-audit-logging/secrets-vault/IR-plan from launch, and how do you answer a prospect who demands proof of your posture in month 2?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Technically this is a strong, well-scoped, genuinely buildable bootstrap with the rare property that its single defining architectural choice (artifact-only, never CUI) simultaneously lowers build cost, shrinks the security/audit surface, and dodges the most expensive obligation in the category — and it is backed by a credible ~8–12 dev-week estimate over a thin custom wedge atop bought commodity infrastructure, with revenue starting on documents before the portal ships. The founders' demonstrated multiple-SOC-2-Type-II background makes the firm's own security posture a realistic in-house deliverable rather than an aspiration, and nothing on the critical path is a research problem or a brittle single-vendor bet. The two things to get right are not build problems but discipline problems: enforce the raw-CUI boundary as rigorous defense-in-depth (contract + training + structured evidence + detection, not a single filter), and hard-gate every AI-produced, attestation-bound artifact behind a credentialed human before it touches SPRS or a prime. De-risking spikes to run before heavy investment: (1) have the RP/CCP reproduce 3–5 real SPRS scores by hand and reconcile against the coded scoring function; (2) red-team 20–30 questionnaire/assessment outputs for AI hallucination and measure the human-correction rate; (3) prototype and pressure-test the raw-CUI-block control with the legal/security advisor against realistic "messy upload" scenarios; (4) scope and start the firm's own SOC 2 boundary in month 1 so posture never lags a buyer's questionnaire.
