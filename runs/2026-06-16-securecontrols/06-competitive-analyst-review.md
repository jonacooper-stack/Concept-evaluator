## ONE-PARAGRAPH COMPETITIVE READ

Two marketing-strong, regulated-compliance-fluent founders can plausibly win a defensible slice of this market, but the lane is narrower and filling faster than the one-pager admits — the central risk is not sleepy boutiques, it's that the founders' exact thesis ("done-for-you, fixed-price, recurring managed CMMC compliance for the small sub") is already being productized by defense MSPs: OSIbeyond launched a near-identical subscription "Compliance-as-a-Service" in April 2026 (BusinessWire/GovConWire), and Summit 7, Core Business Solutions (CORE Vault), Cuick Trac, Abacode, VSO and others all sell managed-compliance subscriptions, most bundling the secure environment the founders deliberately omit. The genuine, defensible air is real but specific: an artifact-only, environment-agnostic, operated-not-tooled offering for the 15-80 person sub that won't run a Vanta dashboard (Vanta is self-serve — "implementation is on you," per its own customers) and is too small for Summit 7's enterprise motion — and it rides a once-in-a-decade forcing function (CMMC Level 2 in new contracts from Nov 10, 2026, with <1% of the DIB currently certified and an 18-30 month assessor backlog). It is winnable, but it is a B-grade competitive position, not an A — the differentiation is a service-delivery choice rather than a structural moat, several well-run players are converging on it now, and the founders enter with zero DIB track record against incumbents whose entire credibility is DIB track record.

## COMPETITOR TEARDOWN

**1. OSIbeyond — "Compliance-as-a-Service (CaaS)" — the most dangerous direct competitor.** A Maryland-based MSP that is itself CMMC Level 2 certified and, in April 2026, launched a subscription, fully-managed CMMC compliance offering with no upfront implementation cost and a "predictable monthly subscription," explicitly targeting "small and mid-sized defense contractors that need a practical path to sustained compliance" (BusinessWire 2026-04-29; GovConWire; AIThority). This is the SecureControls thesis, shipped, by an incumbent that already holds the certification SecureControls' founders lack. The one differentiator: OSIbeyond bundles IT + security operations + environment ("manages the environment, IT, security operations, and compliance as one integrated system"), i.e., it is the heavier MSSP model SecureControls explicitly defers to Phase 2 — so OSIbeyond is more expensive and more entangled, leaving room for an artifact-only, lighter-weight alternative. But it proves the category is being defined right now by credible operators. **DANGEROUS** (as a category-definer and credibility benchmark), though beatable on price/scope for the very small, environment-light sub. *Sources: businesswire.com, govconwire.com, aithority.com.*

**2. Summit 7 — the 800-lb gorilla of the DIB.** Self-described and widely regarded as the #1 MSP/MSSP for DoD contractors, "largest team of certified experts in the DIB," 8+ years in GCC High, and — critically — just won a U.S. Army NCODE contract (effective May 15, 2026) as the only AOS-G partner, explicitly to solve "the price barrier NIST 800-171 and CMMC pose to small businesses with 2-10 employees," via a Managed CUI Enclave (Summit7.us; PRNewswire 2026). This is a DANGEROUS, well-funded, aggressive, government-endorsed incumbent now moving down-market into the exact small-sub segment SecureControls targets — the single most important competitive fact in this review. They bundle the enclave (heavier), so the artifact-only lane is technically distinct, but Summit 7 has the brand, the certs, the assessor relationships, and now a federal distribution channel the founders cannot match. **DANGEROUS.** *Sources: summit7.us, prnewswire.com.*

**3. Cuick Trac (Beryllium InfoSec) — funded, modern, enclave-led managed compliance.** A privately-hosted managed GCC-High enclave + "turnkey compliance solution," covers ~80% of Level 2 out of the box, deploys in ~14 days, achieved its own CMMC Level 2 cert, and took outside investment from BEMA Capital (cuicktrac.com; einpresswire). Modern site, clear productization, FedRAMP-Moderate-Equivalent. Enclave-centric (heavier than SecureControls), but a credible, capitalized, modern competitor — the opposite of "sleepy." **DANGEROUS-LEANING / capable.** *Sources: cuicktrac.com, clutch.co, einpresswire.com.*

**4. Core Business Solutions (CORE Vault / coremanaged.com) — productized managed CMMC for SMB.** Long-established small-business compliance firm (also ISO/SOC), offers CORE Vault, a managed, pre-configured environment in the customer's own AWS GovCloud account, plus tiered Level 1-3 managed services. Productized, SMB-focused, the direct profile competitor — again enclave/environment-inclusive. Competent and established, neither bleeding-edge nor sleepy. **BEATABLE-to-capable.** *Sources: coremanaged.com, thecoresolution.com.*

**5. The boutique RPO / GRC-consultant field (Centre Technologies, CyberSecOp, E-N Computers, Petronella, MAD Security, NeoSystems, VC3, Abacode, VSO, Moonshot Solutions, hundreds more).** This is the fragmented long tail the founders' thesis targets — ~387 RPOs and ~2,000 Registered Practitioners in the CyberAB Marketplace (Secureframe ecosystem data, March 2026). Many are exactly as described: project-based gap-assessments billed hourly, then gone, leaving the sub to maintain SPRS alone. Marketing posture is genuinely uneven and old-school across much of the tail. BUT a non-trivial and growing subset (VC3, Abacode, VSO, Moonshot) now explicitly advertise ongoing "managed compliance" with recurring evidence collection and audit-readiness — the founders' differentiator is being adopted by the smarter consultants. The tail is **SLEEPY/BEATABLE in bulk; the leading ~10-15% are awake and converging.** *Sources: cyberab marketplace via secureframe.com, petronellatech.com, abacode.com, vso-inc.com, moonshot-solutions.com.*

**6. Vanta & Drata — the horizontal GRC platforms (substitute + reach-down risk).** Vanta: $4.15B valuation, $504M raised, ~16,000 customers (April 2026), ~$19K ACV (CNBC, Sacra, Bloomberg). Drata: ~$2B valuation, $328M raised, ~$100M ARR, ~7,000 customers (Sacra, getlatka, Crunchbase). Both publish NIST 800-171 / CMMC control mappings, SSP/POA&M management, and continuous monitoring. The wedge thesis is corroborated by their own customers: with Vanta "implementation is on you" and reviewers report being "left managing our SOC audit manually" (Drata's own comparison page; multiple review summaries). These are tools, not operators — a 30-person machine shop with one outsourced MSP will not run them unaided. This is the founders' strongest, evidenced opening: SecureControls can run on top of a customer's Vanta/Drata. Reach-down risk is real but slow (see war-game). **DANGEROUS as platforms, but structurally adjacent — they sell software, not the done-for-you service.** *Sources: cnbc.com, sacra.com, bloomberg.com, drata.com, getlatka.com.*

**7. DIY compliance software (FutureFeed, ComplyUp, Totem, Kiteworks).** The price floor and the "just do it ourselves" substitute. FutureFeed: $198/$498/$798 per month flat-rate for CMMC tiers (futurefeed.co, Capterra) — and notably runs a "Powered-by-FutureFeed" partner program that lets consultants like SecureControls white-label it, which is an opportunity, not just a threat. ComplyUp generates SPRS scores and SSP exports; Totem sells small-business GRC software + training; Kiteworks is a FedRAMP-authorized CUI data platform (~90% of Level 2). These define what a self-service shop pays (~$2.4K-$9.6K/yr in software) and frame SecureControls' price premium as "you pay us 3-5x the tool cost so you don't have to operate it." **BEATABLE as substitutes (they require an operator the customer doesn't have); usable as SecureControls' own delivery rail.** *Sources: futurefeed.co, capterra.com, complyup.com, totem.tech, kiteworks.com.*

**8. C3PAOs / CCAs — not competitors, but the market's defining bottleneck.** 103 authorized C3PAOs and ~748-759 CCAs as of March 2026, against an estimated 2,000-3,000 assessors needed to process 80,000 Level 2 certs; <1% of the DIB certified; ~178 new Level 2 certs issued in March 2026; wait times projected at 18-30 months by late 2026 (VC3, Coalfire, MyCMMC, planetsecurity). A different, regulated business (they grade; SecureControls prepares). This is pure demand tailwind and a referral surface — a structural PLUS for any well-run prep/maintenance provider. *Sources: vc3.com, coalfirefederal.com, secureframe.com, blog.planetsecurity.net.*

## MARKET STRUCTURE

The market is a barbell: a highly fragmented long tail under a small number of well-capitalized, modernizing leaders. The CyberAB Marketplace lists ~5,732 active entries / ~3,607 unique entities, ~387 RPOs, ~2,000 Registered Practitioners (Secureframe, March 2026) — no single provider owns more than a sliver of the readiness-services market, which is exactly the fragmentation the founders' edge is built for. There is no "Salesforce of CMMC managed compliance." That is the good news, and it is genuine.

The bad news qualifying it: the modern, productized, recurring-subscription end of this market — which is specifically where SecureControls plays — is NOT fragmented and sleepy; it is actively consolidating around credible operators in real time. Summit 7 (now government-channel-endorsed via NCODE), Cuick Trac (VC-backed), Core Business Solutions, OSIbeyond (April-2026 CaaS launch), and a dozen awake RPOs are all converging on "fixed-price recurring managed CMMC for the SMB" in the same 18-month window. PE/roll-up energy is flowing into MSSPs broadly, and the DIB-MSP space is an obvious roll-up target. So the founders enter a fragmented market whose most attractive sub-segment is filling fastest — the one-pager's own phrase, "real but filling," is accurate and, if anything, slightly understated on velocity. Net structural read: fragmented enough to enter, consolidating enough that timing and a sharp, narrow position matter more than the one-pager implies.

## WEDGE & DIFFERENTIATION

The wedge is real, evidenced, and defensible — but it is a positioning/delivery choice, not a structural moat, and it is narrow. Three things make it genuine:

1. Artifact-only / environment-agnostic. Nearly every credible managed competitor (Summit 7, Cuick Trac, Core Vault, OSIbeyond) leads with an enclave/GCC-High environment — heavier, pricier, stickier, but also a bigger commitment. SecureControls' choice to *not* hold CUI and to run on top of whatever environment the customer has (including a partner's enclave or a customer's Vanta) is a legitimately differentiated, lower-friction, lower-liability position for the sub that handles CUI lightly via documents/email/drawings. This is structural-ish: it changes the cost base, the sales cycle, and the liability posture.

2. Operated, not tooled, for the truly small sub. Vanta/Drata's self-serve gap is documented by their own customers. The 15-80 person shop with one MSP and no security staff is genuinely orphaned between "buy software you can't operate" and "buy Summit 7's enterprise enclave you can't afford." That orphan is the customer, and it's real.

3. Recurring maintenance as the product, not the leftover. The dominant boutique failure mode (one-time project, then gone) is well-attested; making quarterly SPRS/SSP/POA&M upkeep the core subscription is the right product shape, and pricing benchmarks support it (managed SSP maintenance $500-$2,000/mo + POA&M $300-$1,500/mo per VSO/industry data — SecureControls' $1,500-$3,500/mo sits squarely and defensibly inside this).

The honest limit: none of the three is hard to copy in 90 days. "We do it for you, fixed price, on top of your environment" is a go-to-market and packaging decision an awake RPO or OSIbeyond can announce next quarter — several effectively already have. The differentiation is durable through execution and brand, not through a technical or regulatory barrier the founders own. It is a 6/10-to-7/10 wedge: clearly better than "me-too," clearly short of "uncopyable."

## DEFENSIBILITY & SWITCHING COSTS

Switching costs build meaningfully with traction, which is the best defensive news here. Once SecureControls holds a customer's living SSP, POA&M history, evidence library, SPRS calculation methodology, and the relationship with that sub's primes' supplier-security teams, ripping it out mid-cycle — especially in the run-up to a C3PAO assessment or a prime questionnaire deadline — is painful and risky for the customer. Compliance continuity is genuinely sticky: nobody wants to re-document their environment and reset their evidence trail to save a few hundred dollars a month, and a stale SPRS score freezes purchase orders, so the cost of a botched switch is concrete and immediate. Annual subscriptions with quarterly deliverables further dampen churn. Expect high gross retention (low-to-mid 90s%) once a customer is past first assessment — comparable to other compliance-continuity businesses.

The caveats: (a) lock-in is moderate, not fortress-grade — the artifact set is portable enough that a determined competitor (or the customer's own MSP deciding to in-source it) can migrate it; the founders deliberately don't hold the CUI or the environment, which is great for liability but means they hold *less* of the stickiest asset than enclave players do. (b) Defensibility is relationship- and reputation-led, and the founders start with zero DIB reputation — the moat has to be *built* customer-by-customer, design-partner-by-design-partner, with a borrowed RP/CCP and advisor for initial credibility. So: real, accreting, but earned slowly and shallower than an enclave incumbent's.

## COMPETITIVE-RESPONSE WAR-GAME

At $1-3M revenue (~40-115 accounts), here is what the field does:

- The awake RPOs and OSIbeyond-type MSPs: copy the packaging. "Fixed-price recurring managed compliance, artifact-only option" becomes a standard SKU. This is the most likely response and the one already in motion — it doesn't kill SecureControls, but it compresses the differentiation to brand, content, and service quality, exactly the founders' strength, so they survive it if they've built a marketing/brand lead. Manageable, but it caps pricing power over time.

- Summit 7 / Cuick Trac (the dangerous, funded incumbents): mostly ignore SecureControls at $1-3M — too small to notice, and they're chasing enclave ACVs 3-5x higher. The danger is indirect: their NCODE/government channels and brand vacuum up the most attractive small accounts at the top of SecureControls' range before SecureControls reaches them. They don't price-war (different cost base); they out-credential. Survivable if SecureControls stays in the artifact-only, environment-light niche they don't want.

- Vanta / Drata (reach-down): low-to-moderate reach-down risk on a 2-4 quarter horizon, rising over 2-3 years. They already publish CMMC mappings, but their model is self-serve software, not done-for-you operations, and CMMC's CUI/GCC-High/attestation specifics and tiny-customer service intensity are a poor fit for their PLG engine and ~$19K-ACV unit economics. More likely they enable the niche (partner/agency programs, as FutureFeed already does) than absorb it. The realistic threat is a managed-services partner ecosystem forming on top of Vanta where SecureControls is one of many agencies — which is survivable but commoditizing. Rate reach-down: MODERATE, slow.

- The wildcard — the customer's own MSP in-sourcing it. The generalist/defense MSP already inside the account is the quietest threat: as CMMC managed-compliance becomes a checkbox SKU (and FutureFeed/Vanta make the tooling turnkey), the incumbent MSP can bolt it on and undercut on bundling. This is a real, ongoing margin and retention pressure, not a single event.

Net war-game read: no single move kills the position; the cumulative risk is slow commoditization of the packaging by a converging field, which pushes the contest onto marketing, brand, and service — the founders' home turf — but also caps the "open lane" premium the one-pager assumes. The concept survives competitive response; it does not enjoy a quiet decade.

## TOP 3 COMPETITIVE STRENGTHS

- An extraordinary, hard-sourced forcing-function tailwind with structural under-supply: <1% of ~80,000 in-scope firms certified, mandatory L2 in new contracts from Nov 10, 2026, and an assessor bottleneck (103 C3PAOs / ~748 CCAs vs. 2,000-3,000 needed; 18-30 month waits). Demand is forced, recurring, and arriving on a clock — the single best thing about this concept competitively.
- A genuinely fragmented overall market (~3,607 entities, ~387 RPOs, no category king) with a documented, evidenced service gap — Vanta/Drata are self-serve ("implementation is on you," per their own customers) and the enclave players are too heavy/expensive for the 15-80 person, CUI-light sub — leaving a real orphan customer the founders' positioning fits.
- Pricing power and stickiness are benchmark-supported: managed SSP+POA&M retainers already command $800-$3,500/mo and Vanta lands $10-80K/yr, validating the $18K-$42K ACV; once the living SSP/evidence/SPRS methodology is held, retention is high because a botched switch can freeze the customer's SPRS score and purchase orders.

## TOP 3 COMPETITIVE RISKS

- The thesis is being productized in real time by credible operators — OSIbeyond's April-2026 subscription "CaaS," plus Summit 7 (now Army-NCODE-endorsed, moving to 2-10-employee shops), Cuick Trac (VC-backed), Core Vault, and a dozen awake RPOs — so the "open lane" is filling fast and the founders are not first.
- The differentiation is a packaging/delivery choice with no owned barrier — "done-for-you, fixed-price, artifact-only, on-top-of-your-environment" is copyable in ~90 days, and the smarter consultants and MSPs are copying the recurring-managed model now; durability rests on brand/execution, not a moat.
- Founder credibility gap into a credibility-led market — incumbents' entire value proposition is DIB track record, certs, and prime/assessor relationships; the founders have none yet and must rent them (RP/CCP, advisor, RPO designation, design partners), which is bridgeable but a real cold-start disadvantage exactly where buyers are most risk-averse.

## BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The competitive dynamic most likely to cap (not instantly kill) this concept is convergence-driven commoditization of the exact wedge by an awake, credentialed field — crystallized by OSIbeyond's April-2026 "Compliance-as-a-Service" launch and Summit 7's NCODE-fueled push down-market. The one-pager's strategic bet is that "no existing brand owns this lane for the ~30-person shop." That was likely true 18 months ago; the live evidence says the lane is being claimed right now by operators who hold the one asset the founders most conspicuously lack — a CMMC Level 2 certification of their own and a DIB reputation. OSIbeyond shipped the same subscription model with no upfront cost; Summit 7 has a U.S. Army channel explicitly aimed at the smallest contractors; Cuick Trac is capitalized. None of them will price-war SecureControls into the ground (their cost bases and ACV targets differ), and the artifact-only, environment-light niche is a real corner they don't prioritize — which is why this is a cap, not a kill. But it means the "clear air" premium the financial model assumes will erode toward a marketing-and-brand contest faster than the plan anticipates, and the founders must win that contest from a standing start against names buyers already trust. The concept's survival is fine; its *easy* version is gone.

## QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE

1. What is your answer to OSIbeyond, specifically? They shipped your subscription model in April 2026 from a position of holding the CMMC L2 cert you don't. Why does the artifact-only, environment-agnostic version win the 15-80 person sub against a same-priced "we manage everything including the environment" CaaS — and can you prove customers prefer to keep their environment rather than hand it over? (Test: put both offers in front of 15 design-partner prospects.)
2. How do you neutralize Summit 7's NCODE/government channel at the top of your range? Their Army-endorsed, small-business-targeted Managed CUI Enclave directly courts your most attractive accounts. Which sub profiles are structurally *unreachable* by an enclave bundle (and therefore yours to keep), and how big is that defensible sub-segment really — closer to your 8,000-15,000 SAM, or a much smaller slice?
3. Without a DIB track record or an in-house assessor, how do you win the first 20 logos against incumbents whose whole pitch is track record? Name the specific RP/CCP, the senior DoD advisor, and the prime supplier-development relationships you can stand up in 90 days — and whether you'll white-label an existing rail (FutureFeed runs a "Powered-by" partner program; building on it would cut your cold-start and engineering risk) or build your own system-of-record.
4. What stops the customer's existing MSP from in-sourcing this as a bundled SKU and undercutting you? As Vanta/FutureFeed make the tooling turnkey, the MSP already in the account is your quietest churn threat — what makes your operated service durably better than their bolt-on?

## RECOMMENDATION: REFINE (lean GO)

This is a fundamentally sound competitive entry on a rare, forced, recurring-demand tailwind into a fragmented market — it clears the bar to pursue — but it should NOT be pursued on the one-pager's premise of an unclaimed open lane, because the live evidence shows the lane is being claimed now. Refine the position to where the air is actually, durably open: narrow hard onto the artifact-only, environment-agnostic, "operated-not-tooled" sub that handles CUI lightly and refuses both the self-serve dashboard and the full enclave — and make the wedge *explicitly* "the alternative to handing your environment to an MSSP," turning Summit 7/OSIbeyond/Cuick Trac's enclave-bundling into your contrast rather than your competition. Lead the brand with the founders' real edge (marketing/content/positioning velocity into an SEO-starved, trust-led niche) and rent DIB credibility fast (RP/CCP, advisor, RPO designation, 3-5 paid design partners, possibly a "Powered-by-FutureFeed" rail to cut build risk). Win on the timing, the founders' marketing engine, and a sharply-drawn niche the funded incumbents don't want — not on the assumption that nobody else has noticed the opportunity, because the funded incumbents demonstrably have.
