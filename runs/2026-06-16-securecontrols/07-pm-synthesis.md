## STEP 3 — MANDATORY TWO-DIRECTIONAL RED-TEAM

I distrust this packet's near-unanimity (five GOs and one REFINE-lean-GO; five of six expert averages between 7.5 and 8.3). I pressure the highest sub-scores down and the deflated ones up, and only move a number where the evidence forces it.

### (a) DOWN — take the highest sub-scores and argue each is one point too high

**Down-challenge 1 — Dimension 1 (Must-have), scored 9. Argue 8.** The *certification* trigger doesn't bite until Nov 10, 2026 and phases through 2027–2028; CMMC dates have slipped repeatedly. Is this "forced today" or "forced soon, maybe later"?
**Verdict: does NOT survive — the 9 holds.** The DFARS 7012/7019/7020 obligation (110 controls, maintained SPRS score, SSP/POA&M) is *already in force today*, independent of the CMMC certification timeline, and a stale/blank SPRS score *already* freezes purchase orders today. The CMMC date is an accelerant on top of a forcing function that already exists. Two independent web-enabled reviewers landed demand at 9. The 9 is earned.

**Down-challenge 2 — Dimension 6 (Capital efficiency), scored 9 (CFO's three 9s). Argue 8.** The negative-working-capital story assumes advance payment and full in-year onboarding cash; a small shop may resist a $26K annual prepay, or onboarding cost may stick at $8–12K.
**Verdict: does NOT survive — the 9 holds.** Even at $8–12K onboarding cost, the onboarding *fee* ($9.5–15K) still covers loaded cost and clears CAC, so the business self-funds from customer #1; templating affects *margin*, not *capital adequacy*. Launches on $30–60K with no pre-revenue capex (artifact-only deletes the GovCloud/enclave capex). Corroborated by CFO and CTO. Survives.

**Down-challenge 3 — Dimension 7 (Operational tractability), scored 9 (COO logistics 10). Argue 8.** The COO's own "biggest single risk" says the team will feel "genuinely underwater" on onboarding throughput and the reviewer gate.
**Verdict: PARTIALLY survives — holds at 9.** Dimension 7's rubric is specifically the field-ops question (lean/remote/low-touch vs. trucks/installs/field visits); on that axis this is a 9-to-10 profile. The onboarding-throughput throttle is real but is captured in dimensions 3 and 5 and the risk register; double-counting it here would penalize the same risk twice. Holds at 9; the throughput risk is recorded against dims 3/5 and in TOP 5 RISKS.

**Down-challenge 4 — Dimension 9 (Scalability), scored 8. Argue 7.** CFO flags a key-person/credential dependency (RP/CCP gate) a buyer would diligence; Competitive Analyst notes lock-in is moderate and portable.
**Verdict: does NOT survive — the 8 holds.** Dimension 9 measures lifestyle-OR-scale-OR-sellable vs. inherently capped. This business has all options: run lean at 55 accounts, scale to 80–100+, add gated Phase 2, or sell a recurring-ARR book to an active acquirer class. The credential dependency is a diligence item, not a growth cap.

**Net of the DOWN pass: no dimension lowered. Every high score tested survives on evidence.** I resisted manufacturing a trivial cut for "balance."

### (b) UP — where an expert clearly deflated a well-evidenced dimension out of timidity

**Up-challenge 1 — Competition (dim 2) at 7. Should it be higher?** The artifact-only / never-CUI structure is praised by four reviewers as a genuine structural difference the strongest incumbents "cannot claim without abandoning infrastructure revenue"; Legal and CTO call it "the single best decision."
**Verdict: does NOT move up — holds at 7.** The most important call in the red-team. The differentiation is real and the incumbents-can't-copy point is true *for the enclave players*. But it is NOT unavailable to the field that matters most: an awake RPO or an OSIbeyond-style MSP can announce an artifact-only SKU next quarter with no infrastructure to abandon — and the evidence is several already have (durability 5, copyable in ~90 days). The four reviewers praise the architecture as a *liability and cost* decision (credited in dims 1, 4, 6 and the legal gate), not as a competitive *moat*. 7 is honest — neither the "clear air" the one-pager implied (8) nor the deflated 6.

**Up-challenge 2 — Recurring revenue (dim 4) at 8. Was it deflated?** Permanent obligation + sticky system-of-record + advance billing → arguably a 9.
**Verdict: does NOT move up — holds at 8.** The obligation is permanent but the *vendor relationship* is not: lock-in is "moderate, not fortress-grade" precisely because the firm deliberately doesn't hold the CUI/environment, and the customer's own MSP can in-source the artifact layer. Permanent demand + moderate vendor lock-in = a strong 8, not a 9.

**Up-challenge 3 — Founder fit (dim 8) at 8. Deflated by the no-DIB gap?** The gap is named and explicitly bridgeable; the demonstrated SOC 2 + regulated-sales fit is an unusually exact match.
**Verdict: does NOT move up — holds at 8 (closest call to a 9).** The fit is genuinely strong and the barrier bridgeable, but the bridge is real work *not yet built* (RP/CCP, DoD advisor, prime relationships all to-be-recruited), and three reviewers flag the DIB cold-start as a live risk to the close rate. An 8 — "squarely in the wheelhouse, barrier bridgeable" — is right; a 9 would presume the bridge is already crossed.

**Net of the UP pass: no dimension raised.** I genuinely looked for a deflated, well-evidenced strength; the closest candidates are correctly scored because in both cases the strength is real but the competitive-durability / bridge-is-built claim is not yet earned. The packet is calibrated honestly in both directions.
