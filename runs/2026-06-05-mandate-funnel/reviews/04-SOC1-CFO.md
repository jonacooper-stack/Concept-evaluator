# CFO REVIEW — SOC 1 Readiness Managed Service

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil well; structurally the strongest archetype on the table. SOC 1 is a hard contractual gate — large enterprise customers won't sign or renew without a current report — so the buy is mandatory, annual, budgeted, bought without internal selling. That produces durable recurring revenue at software-leveraged margins, and the income ladder is reachable at modest, conservative customer counts. Two fragilities: (a) onboarding and evidence-review labor that, if it stays human-heavy, compresses margin and caps how many accounts two founders carry; (b) a real but bridgeable risk that the buyer bundles SOC 1 readiness with the same vendor doing their SOC 2, squeezing a SOC-1-only specialist.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.9 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Negative working-capital cycle: annual subscription + upfront onboarding fee — customer pays before the bulk of specialist labor; growth self-funds, no AR drag/inventory/deposits.
- Contractually forced recurring revenue: the report must be renewed every year for as long as enterprise customers require it — renewal driven by the customer's own contracts, underwriting retention and margin durability.
- Software-leveraged margin at the top of the compliance-readiness band: $30K-class ACV vs $2.5K-$6.5K steady-state COGS → ~80%+ blended gross margin once tooling carries routine collection.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Onboarding/evidence-review labor: if specialist hours/account stay high (first-year 60-120 [ASSUMED]) and don't fall at renewal, margin compresses and founders' carrying capacity caps account count below the ladder.
- Bundling/substitution pressure: if a generalist platform or the CPA firm bundles SOC 1 readiness into a broader deal, a SOC-1-only specialist faces price compression at renewal.
- Outcome-coupled liability: revenue and retention hinge on accounts actually passing — a qualified or late report (partly outside the founders' control, since the CPA issues it) threatens that account's renewal and word-of-mouth.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Labor intensity per account in the onboarding year and whether it truly amortizes at renewal. The margin and account-carrying capacity rest on software doing the heavy lifting while a specialist only reviews quality and quarterbacks the CPA. SOC 1 is controls over financial reporting — evidence is judgment-laden (control-objective scoping, management's description, walkthroughs, sampling), harder to automate than a security-checklist SOC 2. If first-year engagements run 100+ specialist hours and renewals don't drop sharply, COGS climbs toward $10K-$15K/account, blended margin slips from ~80% toward ~50-60%, two founders plus a trained specialist can only carry 25-35 accounts before quality breaks — slowing the climb to mo-24 and forcing earlier margin-diluting hires of people trusted with financial-controls work. The whole thesis hinges on software genuinely converting expert hours into routine collection; that's the number to prove first.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- How many specialist hours does a first-year SOC 1 readiness engagement consume, and how much does it drop at renewal? Time-box your first three onboardings to the hour.
- Real recurring ACV + onboarding fee a CFO/controller commits to against the contract gate, and does it hold at renewal once the program "feels easy"? Run 5 priced proposals before you build.
- How big is the genuinely contract-forced subset (firms whose enterprise customers mandate a SOC 1, not SOC 2)? Build the bottoms-up list and count it — that subset is your real SOM.
- What happens to revenue/reputation when a client gets a qualified or late report from the CPA you shepherded — contractual posture on outcomes you don't fully control?
- Can you defend ACV when the buyer also needs SOC 2 and a bundled vendor offers "both for one price" — is the wedge SOC-1-only depth, or do you need to offer SOC 2 alongside?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Financials are sound and the structure matches the strongest archetype: a mandatory, recurring, budgeted, contractually forced buy delivered as a productized, software-leveraged, marketing-led managed service, with negative working capital, ~80% blended margin, and a conservative path to the ladder at ~24 accounts (mo12) and ~40-45 (mo24). The founders' SOC 2 Type II / compliance-operator background and regulated-enterprise deal-closing make the expertise barrier bridgeable. GO rather than refine because demand is genuinely market-pulled and the income math is conservative; the open risks (onboarding labor amortization, bundling/substitution, outcome coupling) are diligence items to resolve in the first three engagements, not structural defects. Prove the per-account labor curve and renewal-ACV durability early and this clears comfortably.
