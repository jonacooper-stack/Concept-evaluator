# LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — LIHTC (Section 42) Compliance Managed Service

Not formal legal advice. Operator pattern-recognition flagging issues for retained counsel.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
A B2B managed-service play where the customer's industry is heavily regulated (IRC Section 42 / LIHTC) but the service provider's own regulatory footprint is unexpectedly light: the business needs no occupational license, the data it touches is sensitive PII but not HIPAA/biometric, and the contracting motion is standard B2B. The defining feature is not licensing or privacy — it is liability transfer and professional-services exposure. The service prepares income certifications and files compliance reports whose errors can trigger IRS credit recapture, an event the one-pager calls "existential." That creates a real E&O and contractual-indemnity problem, manageable with disclaimers, scoped contracts, and E&O insurance, but the single thing that defines this concept's risk profile. Net: lower regulatory burden than it appears, concentrated liability that must be papered carefully from day one.

REGULATORY MAP
- IRC Section 42 / 26 U.S.C. §42 + Treasury Reg §1.42-5 (compliance monitoring) + IRS Form 8823 Guide. Governs the customer's obligations, not a license on the provider. No IRS registration/enrollment/license required to prepare certifications for an owner. Moderate-to-high competence burden, near-zero licensing burden.
- State Housing Finance Agencies (CA TCAC, NY HCR, TX TDHCA, FL FHFC, ~50+ allocating agencies). Each sets its own QAP, compliance manual, reporting formats, recert rules, MOR procedures under §1.42-5. Regulate the property, not the vendor — no HFA vendor license, but operational burden multiplies per state. High operational-variance, low licensing.
- HUD Handbook 4350.3 / HOTMA income-determination rules. Many LIHTC properties layer HUD-program rules (Section 8, HOME, PBV) on top of §42; HOTMA materially changed income/asset calc recently. A "monitor closely" item — the rules the service implements are actively changing, raising error risk (but it's the very pain being sold).
- IRS Circular 230 (practice before the IRS) — likely NOT triggered but must be confirmed. Preparing certifications/HFA reports is generally property-compliance work, not tax-return preparation or representation. Service disclaims tax/legal advice. Confirm not inadvertently engaged in tax-return prep (PTIN) or UPL. Probable low burden; verify before launch.
- State data-privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA + VA/CO/CT/TX wave). Service holds tenant PII: SSNs, income docs, bank statements, tax returns — sensitive but not special-category federal regime. As a B2B service provider/processor on the owner's behalf, largely DPA obligations rather than consumer-facing duties. Moderate, well-understood.
- State data-breach notification (all 50) + likely contractual security requirements. Holding SSNs/financial docs puts the service in breach-notification territory; real but standard, addressed with SOC 2-style controls + WISP (e.g., MA 201 CMR 17.00).
- FTC Act §5 (deceptive advertising). "Never trigger a recapture" is a near-guarantee of an outcome the service does not fully control (depends on tenant truthfulness, owner conduct, HFA/IRS action). Advertising-substantiation exposure; soften or it invites FTC + breach-of-warranty theories.
- No FTC ROSCA / state auto-renewal consumer law exposure — B2B subscription to business entities. Near-zero burden.

LICENSING SUMMARY
The service itself requires NO occupational or industry license in any jurisdiction. There is no federal or state "LIHTC compliance provider" license; HFAs regulate the property and owner, not the third-party preparer. The relevant credentials (NCHM's HCCP, SHCM, Spectrum's C3P) are voluntary market-signaling certifications, not legal prerequisites — bridgeable via a named credentialed hire or trained specialist, exactly as the one-pager contemplates. The only licensing needed is the ordinary stack: LLC/entity registration, home-state business license, foreign-entity qualification where nexus. Caveat: confirm with counsel that certification-preparation work does not cross into IRS tax-return preparation (PTIN) or unauthorized practice of law/accountancy. On current facts that line is not crossed, but it must be confirmed.

LIABILITY POSTURE
The heart of the concept. The service performs/reviews income certifications and files reports whose defects can trigger IRS credit recapture — existential, capable of triggering investor penalties and partnership default. When a deal goes bad, the owner looks at whoever did the certification work. Three layers, all achievable: (1) Contractual — MSA that defines scope narrowly ("we prepare based on tenant-provided documentation; owner is the responsible party to the HFA and IRS"), disclaims tax/legal advice, caps liability at fees paid (or a multiple), disclaims consequential damages including recapture, places burden of tenant-document accuracy on owner/tenant. The one-pager already gestures at this — a strong signal. (2) Insurance — professional liability/E&O is the load-bearing policy, plus cyber/data-breach (SSN/financial-doc holdings), plus GL. E&O is insurable, premiums scale with credits-at-risk; underwriters want documented review procedures. (3) Entity — properly formed LLC/corp from day one. Residual after all three: damages-cap-busting arguments (gross negligence, willful misconduct) and reputational risk. Concentrated and real but the ordinary professional-services posture of an accounting/compliance firm — manageable, not fatal.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The professional-liability/E&O exposure created by selling near-responsibility for an "existential" financial outcome the service does not fully control. The value prop is "protect your tax credits — never trigger a recapture," and recapture under §42 can run into hundreds of thousands or millions per property and cascade into investor penalties and partnership default. When recapture occurs — even one caused by tenant fraud, owner conduct, or an HFA judgment call rather than the service's error — the damaged owner and its investor will look hard at the entity that prepared and filed the certifications, and the marketing promise is Exhibit A. Liability caps, consequential-damages waivers, "owner is the responsible party" language, and E&O all blunt this, but none eliminate the gross-negligence/willful-misconduct carve-out that survives caps, and none stop a lawsuit being filed or a reputation damaged. The asymmetry is brutal: the service collects a low-five-figure per-property subscription while standing adjacent to a six- or seven-figure downside per claim. Survivable — accounting/compliance firms live in this posture daily — but it makes disciplined scope language, conservative marketing, documented review procedures, and adequately-sized E&O non-optional, and is why this dimension scores 5 not 8.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
1. Does preparing tenant income certifications and filing HFA reports on the owner's behalf cross into (a) IRS tax-return preparation requiring a PTIN, (b) practice before the IRS under Circular 230, or (c) UPL/public accountancy in any state? What scope language keeps us on the compliance-services side?
2. What is the enforceable ceiling on liability limitation for work that can contribute to recapture — will a cap-at-fees and consequential-damages waiver hold against a sophisticated owner, and what gross-negligence carve-outs will courts read in?
3. How must "never trigger a recapture" / "protect your tax credits" be qualified to survive FTC §5 substantiation and avoid an implied warranty/guarantee?
4. Given custody of SSNs/tax returns/bank statements, which state privacy and data-security regimes (CCPA/CPRA service-provider terms, MA 201 CMR 17.00 WISP, NY SHIELD, the breach-notification overlay) impose affirmative obligations, and what minimum security program + DPA template do we need?
5. What E&O/cyber limits and terms will an underwriter offer for this risk profile, and what documented review/QA procedures will they require as a condition of coverage?
6. As we add states, what foreign-qualification, tax-nexus, and remote-employee payroll registrations trigger, and do any state HFAs impose registration/approval on third-party compliance vendors acting on owners' behalf?

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with mandatory pre-launch legal hardening)
From a purely legal/regulatory standpoint an attractive structure: the regulated party is the customer, not the business, so the concept harvests the urgency of a heavy mandate (§42, §1.42-5, state HFA monitoring) without itself needing a license, and avoids the consumer-protection and field-operations traps entirely. The risk is not regulatory gatekeeping — it is concentrated professional-liability exposure plus sensitive-PII custody, both the ordinary insurable contract-able posture of a compliance/accounting firm. Two pre-launch musts: (1) a properly scoped MSA with liability caps, consequential-damages waivers, "owner is the responsible party" language, and a DPA; (2) adequately-sized E&O and cyber insurance bound before the first client file. The one structural refinement to press: discipline the marketing — change "never trigger a recapture" to a process-and-diligence promise ("rigorous, continuous, audit-ready compliance"), removing both the FTC §5 and implied-warranty edges at no cost to the value proposition. With those, the legal/regulatory profile is clearly acceptable for a bootstrapped two-founder team.
