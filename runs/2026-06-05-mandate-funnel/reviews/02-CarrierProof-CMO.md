# CMO REVIEW — CarrierProof (Appointment-Maintenance Managed Service for Insurance Agencies)

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
A real, recurring, deadline-dense pain with a direct dollar line: a lapsed E&O policy or missed carrier attestation can suspend an appointment and freeze commissions on a whole book — agency principals understand that consequence without education, the mark of a painkiller. The catch: this is a crowded, named-incumbent compliance category (AgentSync, Vertafore Sircon, NIPR/PDB, agency-management compliance modules), and the buyer is fragmented and notoriously slow-spending. The wedge is plausible and the positioning sharp, but the open question is whether independent agencies feel this acutely enough to write a recurring check before the lapse, or shrug it off as "my office manager handles the spreadsheet" until it bites.

POSITIONING DRAFT
"We make sure your agency never loses a carrier appointment — or the commissions tied to it — to a missed license, lapsed E&O, or unreturned attestation. You keep selling; we keep you appointed." Clean, edged, aimed at the agency principal with a specific dollar consequence (lost commission). The loss-aversion frame is the strongest available angle and survives the "would a customer repeat this back?" test.

THE WEDGE
First 100 from a narrow named-channel attack, not paid ads: (1) Big "I" (IIABA) and its 50 state associations run relentless conference/webinar/newsletter calendars; sponsoring/speaking at a handful of state chapters + NetVU/Vertafore user community costs ~$3K–$10K/event [ASSUMED; verify via 2-3 association rate cards], directly in front of principals who already buy compliance services. (2) Cluster groups/aggregator networks (SIAA, Smart Choice, ISU, Keystone) aggregate hundreds of small agencies under one umbrella and negotiate vendor deals — landing ONE cluster endorsement reaches dozens at near-zero marginal CAC. (3) Targeted outbound/LinkedIn to principals/ops managers with a free "appointment-risk audit" opener (inventory carriers/licenses/E&O dates, show gaps) — content-plus-audit motion fitting positioning-strong founders; the free audit doubles as paid onboarding lead-in. CAC via association/cluster channels plausibly low; cold outbound to skeptical small agencies higher and slower. Leans heavily on landing 1–2 cluster/association relationships early.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- AgentSync — best at producer licensing/appointment compliance via modern API into NIPR; well-funded (raised $100M+ at $1.2B valuation 2021), strong-tech, sells primarily to carriers and large MGAs/distributors. The dangerous name. NOT sleepy. But sweet spot is carrier/large-distributor side, leaving possible air downmarket.
- Vertafore Sircon — license/appointment transaction processing many carriers/agencies already touch; old-line, broad, not productized as a hand-holding managed service for small agencies. Sleepy-ish but entrenched.
- NIPR/PDB — the underlying license-status data source; not a competitor but the rail everyone integrates against.
- Traditional licensing back-office / agency-management consultants — the "sleepy incumbents," manual, no software, no marketing. Genuinely beatable.
- Vertafore AMS360 / Applied Epic / EZLynx — agency-management systems with bundled compliance modules; the "good enough, already paid for it" alternative.
Air is downmarket, in the 3–100-producer independent agency AgentSync largely ignores and Sircon serves only as a transaction utility, packaged as done-for-you managed service. Risk: "managed service" is exactly the manual-labor exposure the founders want to avoid, and AgentSync or an AMS vendor could move downmarket with self-serve at a fraction of the price.

SCORES (1–10)
1. Positioning clarity: 8 — "Never lose a carrier or its commissions to a missed attestation" is a sharp, loss-framed claim tied to a named dollar consequence (suspended appointment = frozen commission book); edges, not mush.
2. Real differentiation: 6 — The managed-service "we file it for you" layer is real but copyable; AgentSync or Sircon could bolt on a concierge tier in <1 year, and generic "modern + service" framing caps this.
3. Wedge to first 1,000 customers: 7 — Cluster groups (SIAA, Smart Choice) and state IIABA chapters give named low-CAC aggregation points; an endorsed-vendor deal reaches dozens at once, though it depends on winning 1–2 relationships.
4. GTM motion fit: 7 — Marketing-led + founder-led close via associations/clusters + free appointment-risk audit fits versatile, marketing-strong founders and a relationship-driven buyer.
5. Distribution channel realism: 7 — IIABA chapters, NetVU/user communities, cluster networks are real, affordable ($3K–$10K/event [ASSUMED]), scalable; not reliant on fantasy paid scale.
6. CAC realism: 6 — Association/cluster CAC plausibly low, but small agencies are slow, skeptical buyers; cold-outbound CAC and sales cycles run longer than founders assume, and ACV may be modest.
7. Brand / story compounding: 6 — "The people who keep you appointed" can compound via association trust and word-of-mouth, but it's a quiet-trust brand, not viral; every regional cluster is still a fresh relationship.
8. Competitive air: 6 — Real air downmarket, but the category is named and partly occupied: AgentSync (funded, strong-tech, $1.2B valuation 2021) and Vertafore Sircon already own the compliance-data layer, so the air is a niche between them, not open field.
9. Defensibility: 6 — Switching costs build once you're system of record for every appointment/license/E&O, audit-ready files create lock-in, but no network effect or proprietary data moat early; NIPR data available to all.
10. Customer truth: 5 — Pain is logically real and well-articulated, but no evidence the founders have talked to principals or measured how acutely the pain is felt BEFORE a lapse; reasoned, not validated.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.4 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- A genuine painkiller with a direct dollar line: missed attestation/lapsed E&O → suspended appointment → frozen commissions; principals grasp stakes without education.
- Named, low-CAC aggregation channels exist: cluster groups (SIAA, Smart Choice, ISU, Keystone) and state IIABA chapters deliver dozens of agencies per relationship.
- Recurring by structure: licenses, E&O, carrier attestations all renew on rolling deadlines — inherently a retainer.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- A well-funded strong-tech incumbent owns the data layer: AgentSync ($100M+ raised, $1.2B valuation 2021) and Vertafore Sircon could move downmarket with self-serve at a fraction of a managed-service price.
- "Pain felt only after the lapse": small agencies may believe their office manager + AMS module already covers this, making the sale a convince-them-of-the-risk (vitamin) motion.
- The "we file the attestation for you" layer is labor exposure and copyable; differentiation may erode to price.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
A perception-of-urgency gap at the moment of purchase. The consequence (a frozen book) is severe and real but also rare/tail-risk — most agencies have never actually lost an appointment to a missed attestation, so they rationalize that their spreadsheet + AMS reminders + a diligent office manager are "good enough." That turns a logically must-have product into a sale where you must first convince the principal the risk is live — the vitamin-selling motion the founders are trying to avoid. Compounded by a small, cash-conscious, relationship-driven buyer that moves slowly and resists new recurring fees. If pain is felt only after a lapse, the best customers are the already-burned reactive segment, and the broader market requires expensive education. The thesis hinges on whether "appointment risk" is a felt, budgeted fear or a theoretical one — the one-pager hasn't demonstrated which.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- Interview 15–20 principals/ops managers: how many have ever had an appointment suspended over a missed attestation/lapse, how many pay anyone for this today vs in-house, and what would they pay annually?
- Realistic ACV/agency, blended CAC and cycle length through cluster/association vs cold outbound — does LTV/CAC clear with the slow skeptical small-agency buyer?
- Can you land an endorsed-vendor relationship with one cluster network or state IIABA chapter in 90 days, and what do they require (rev share, your own E&O, references)?
- How do you stop AgentSync or a Vertafore AMS module adding a "compliance concierge" tier downmarket and undercutting you — durable reason an agency stays?
- Where does the "managed service" labor live — productized/software-leveraged enough to stay capital-light, or a manual back-office op as you scale carriers × states × producers?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Positioning is sharp and recurring structure genuine, but two things must be proven. First, customer truth: talk to 15–20 principals and measure whether appointment risk is felt/budgeted or theoretical — if theoretical, this is a vitamin dressed as a painkiller. Second, sharpen the wedge from "managed service" to a single undeniable productized entry offer — lead with a continuously-monitored "Appointment Risk Score / never-lapse guarantee" so differentiation is a software-driven outcome, not a copyable concierge, sidestepping the manual-labor trap. Lock in one cluster/association endorsement as the proof channel before scaling outbound. If interviews show acutely-felt pain and a cluster will endorse, moves toward GO; if pain only registers post-lapse, reposition or drop.
