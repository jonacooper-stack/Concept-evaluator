# COO REVIEW — CarrierProof (Appointment-Maintenance Managed Service for Insurance Agencies)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
A remote, document-and-deadline managed service riding a software spine — structurally close to a compliance-ops business, no trucks/installs/field visits. The unit of work: "track a renewal deadline, collect evidence, fill out and submit a carrier attestation on the agency's behalf, keep an audit-ready file." Real, recurring, labor-bearing — fundamentally a back-office processing operation where humans complete carrier portal forms and chase documents. Two founders plus a small remote ops team can run it, but the throughput driver is "attestations/renewals processed per month," scaling with carriers × producers × states — large and lumpy. The make-or-break question is how much of each attestation is standardized/templated vs bespoke per-carrier portal labor, because that ratio decides whether this is high-margin software-leveraged or a creeping per-agency labor sink.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday, March 10, 12-month mark. ~70 agency clients [ASSUMED 60–80]. Founder A in the deadline dashboard: engine flags 41 items due in 30 days — 14 producer-license renewals (several multi-state), 9 carrier attestations, 6 E&O renewals, 5 cyber attestations, 7 carrier certifications/audit responses; 18 due this week. Two remote ops specialists ("appointment coordinators") each own half the book. Coordinator 1: logs into three carrier portals to confirm license-renewal status vs state DOI/NIPR feed, emails two principals for a missing E&O dec page, starts a carrier attestation requiring principal e-signature + three uploads. Coordinator 2: works a carrier audit request (proof of AML training for all 9 producers at one agency) — discovers two never completed it, escalates to the agency (the service can't manufacture training that didn't happen), logs the gap. Founder A handles two "fire" events: one agency's E&O is 6 days from lapse and unbound — the value is the alarm fired in time, but binding is the agency's broker's job, so Founder A's role is escalation/documentation. Founder B is on sales (three discovery calls + onboarding two new clients — each onboarding a multi-week inventory of every appointment/producer/license/E&O/attestation, the messiest phase). Where it breaks: a carrier rolls out a NEW attestation to all appointed agencies on short notice — the same novel form for 40+ of 70 clients in a two-week window with no template — the single most important capacity risk to design for.

CAPACITY MATH
Target: ~$600K founder income mo12 → ~$900K-1.1M revenue after small ops payroll + tooling [ASSUMED]. Pricing ~$1,200/mo (~$14,400/yr)/agency [ASSUMED $800–$2,000/mo; verify w/ 10 pricing convos vs what they pay licensing back-office shops today]. $1.0M ARR ÷ $14,400 ≈ 70 agencies (matches walkthrough).
Throughput: per agency ~12 appointments, ~12 license renewals/yr (8 producers × 3 states, staggered 2-yr), ~12 carrier attestations/yr, ~1 E&O, ~2 cyber/AML, plus ad-hoc → ~27-30 events/agency/yr [ASSUMED 20–40; verify by inventorying 5 real agencies]. 70 × 30 = 2,100/yr ≈ 175/month ≈ 40/week ≈ 8/business-day.
Labor/event: templated attestation ~20–30 min; license confirmation ~10–15 min; novel/bespoke form or audit ~60–120 min. Blended ~35 min [ASSUMED; verify timing 50 events]. 175 × 35 min ≈ 102 hrs/month ≈ 24 hrs/week steady-state. One FT coordinator (~130 productive hrs/mo) covers steady state; a second absorbs surges/onboarding/audit spikes → ~1.5–2 coordinators + founders.
Hidden load is ONBOARDING: ~8–20 hrs/agency [ASSUMED; verify w/ 3 end-to-end]. 0→70 in year one (~6 net new/mo) → ~84 hrs/mo ≈ ~20 hrs/week ≈ another half-to-full coordinator during growth. Realistic mo-12 team: 2 founders + ~2 remote coordinators. Bottleneck lands first on onboarding throughput and surge events, not routine steady-state.

SCORES (1–10)
1. Delivery clarity & repeatability: 6 — Deadline-tracking/evidence-collection spine is standardizable, but the attestation work is per-carrier-portal and partly bespoke; no single documented unit covers a fragmented ever-changing carrier-form landscape.
2. Supply chain resilience: 6 — "Supply chain" is data feeds (NIPR/PDB, state DOI lookups) + carrier portals; NIPR is effectively single-source for authoritative license data and carrier portals are unstandardized and outside the service's control.
3. Logistics tractability: 9 — Fully remote, document-and-portal based, zero physical movement, no routing/install/weather; unit of work is "log into a portal and submit a form," geography-neutral, runs from anywhere.
4. Customer-ops scalability: 6 — Steady-state moderate-touch (175 events/mo across 70 agencies manageable), but onboarding is high-touch/labor-dense (~8–20 hrs/agency) and audit/escalation events are unpredictable spikes preventing true low-touch scaling.
5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: 6 — Dependent on NIPR/state license data + dozens of carrier portals whose terms/forms/access change unilaterally; no single platform bet-the-business, but the aggregate "carriers can change requirements anytime" is the operational weather.
6. Hiring feasibility: 8 — First hire is a remote "appointment coordinator" (back-office insurance-ops/compliance-admin), abundant/affordable pool ($45K–$65K/yr remote [ASSUMED]), trainable on a documented playbook, not a scarce credentialed specialist.
7. Throughput capacity at target: 8 — ~175 events/mo at ~35 min ≈ ~102 hrs/mo steady-state, coverable by ~1.5–2 coordinators + founders; two founders + ~2 coordinators can credibly hit ~$1M ARR/70 agencies at mo12.
8. Quality control simplicity: 5 — A missed deadline or wrong attestation is high-consequence (agency can lose an appointment + commissions), so error tolerance is low while the work is human-keyed across heterogeneous carrier forms — low tolerance + manual variance makes QC genuinely demanding.
9. Geographic / seasonality risk: 7 — National, year-round, weather-neutral; some lumpiness (renewal/attestation cycles cluster at calendar-quarter and policy-anniversary), but the book diversifies peaks across many agencies/carriers.
10. Tooling maturity available: 6 — CRM, doc storage, e-sign, deadline engine buildable/buyable off-the-shelf, but reliable license-status monitoring and carrier-portal automation are custom builds; the differentiated tooling must be built.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.7 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Genuinely remote and field-ops-free: entire unit of work is portal-and-document, no trucks/installs/site visits, geography-neutral (dim 3 = 9).
- Affordable, abundant first hire: a remote appointment-coordinator from a deep insurance back-office pool at ~$45K–$65K, trainable on a playbook (dim 6 = 8).
- Throughput at target achievable by a tiny team: ~175 events/mo at ~35 min ≈ ~102 hrs/mo, covered by ~1.5–2 coordinators + two founders (dim 7 = 8).

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Onboarding is the real labor sink: ~8–20 hrs/agency of messy inventory-and-cleanup; during 0-to-70 growth it can consume an extra half-to-full coordinator, throttling growth.
- Carrier-driven surge events: a carrier pushes a NEW attestation to all appointed agencies at once → the same novel un-templated form for dozens of clients in a tight window — an unpredictable spike the lean team can't pre-staff.
- Low-error-tolerance, high-consequence QC: a single missed deadline/botched attestation can cost an agency a carrier appointment + commissions, yet the work is human-keyed across non-standard portals — severe failure mode, not easily engineered away.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The collision of low error tolerance with non-standardized, human-keyed carrier work, amplified by surge events. The promise is "never lose a carrier to a missed attestation," so a single dropped deadline isn't refund-and-move-on — it can freeze new business and threaten commissions on an existing book, the catastrophic reputation-ending failure a two-person shop can't absorb at the moment of growth. Each carrier's portal/form/requirement is its own unstandardized thing, so the work resists full automation; consistency depends on disciplined humans following a playbook across dozens of heterogeneous changing systems — and the worst load arrives when a carrier issues a new requirement to everyone at once. The worry isn't "can two people do steady-state?" (math says yes) but "can two people + two coordinators guarantee zero misses across 70 agencies' high-stakes deadlines when a carrier drops an industry-wide surprise in a two-week window?" Mitigation exists (buffer capacity, conservative deadline padding, escalation putting binding/training back on the agency, E&O on the service) but must be designed deliberately — the blast radius of a single quiet failure is disproportionate.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What fraction of carrier attestations can be templated/reused vs completed bespoke each cycle? Time 50 real attestations across the top 15 carriers for actual blended minutes-per-event — the whole margin story turns on this.
- How heavy is onboarding really? Run three full end-to-end onboardings; if 20+ hrs/agency it caps growth rate and may need a separate onboarding tier/role.
- Where does liability sit on a missed deadline? Nail the contractual liability cap, service-level E&O, and exact line where responsibility transfers back to the agency (binding E&O, completing training).
- Is authoritative license data accessible at acceptable cost/terms (NIPR/PDB)? Can carrier-portal status be monitored reliably without violating portal ToU?
- What is the surge plan when a carrier issues an industry-wide requirement (cross-trained bench, fractional contractors, prioritized triage)?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The right shape — remote, document-based, no field ops, abundant affordable labor, steady-state throughput two founders + ~2 coordinators can carry to ~$1M ARR/70 agencies by mo12. Held back from clean GO by three unquantified unknowns that each move margin/risk: (1) template-vs-bespoke ratio of attestation work, (2) true cost of onboarding, (3) liability posture on a missed high-consequence deadline. Before GO: publish a tiered fixed-scope service definition bounding what's included (bespoke audit responses priced as add-ons); price onboarding as a separate real fee reflecting measured hours; design a deliberate surge/overflow mechanism + contractual liability cap + service-level E&O. With those resolved, moves to GO; without them, the low-error-tolerance manual core could quietly break a two-person team at the moment it's trying to grow.
