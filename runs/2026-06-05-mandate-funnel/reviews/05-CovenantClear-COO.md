# COO REVIEW — CovenantClear (Managed Loan-Covenant Compliance for Lower-Middle-Market Borrowers)

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
A remote, software-leveraged, recurring managed service with a clean delivery shape: per borrower, parse the loan agreement once into a covenant register, then run a recurring quarterly (sometimes monthly) close-to-certificate workflow culminating in one reviewed document. No trucks, no installs, no field visits, no physical supply chain, no geographic density problem — the unit of work is a spreadsheet model and a PDF certificate delivered by email. The real operational risk is not logistics; it is the per-borrower variability of loan-agreement parsing and the concentrated, deadline-bunched, judgment-heavy review work that lands on a scarce, expensive finance specialist during quarter-close windows. Two founders plus a small bench of finance reviewers can plausibly operate this, but throughput is gated by review labor at predictable calendar peaks, and onboarding (loan-agreement abstraction) is the bespoke, hard-to-standardize front door.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday, second week of March, 12-month mark. March is a peak: the close window for December-31 FYE borrowers delivering Q4 and annual compliance certificates (many due 45-90 days after period end) — one of the heaviest weeks of the year. ~80 active borrower clients on quarterly plans; the December-FYE cohort (~55%, ~44 borrowers) has certificates and annual packages due late Feb through March. By month 12 the founders have hired 1-2 finance specialists and split sales, onboarding oversight, exception-handling. A founder opens the in-house deadline-tracker: 11 certificates due to lenders within 7 days, 4 due Thursday. For 3 of the 11 the financials are late/incomplete → a founder/specialist chases the client's bookkeeper ("we need your final December trial balance to compute fixed-charge coverage; the certificate is due to First National Thursday") — the single most common friction, outside the company's control. For the 8 with clean data, the specialist pulls financials into the pre-built model, the engine computes leverage, fixed-charge coverage, min EBITDA, tangible net worth, and flags cushion. On two, computed FCCR comes in at 1.18x against a 1.20x covenant — a projected breach. The high-stakes moment: a founder spends 60-90 min/client reviewing add-back definitions (can a one-time legal settlement be added back to EBITDA per the credit agreement?), recomputing, and deciding whether to (a) certify a tighter-but-compliant number with defensible add-backs, or (b) call the client: "you'll trip FCCR — cure it on the balance sheet before close, or get ahead of it with your lender and request a waiver." That conversation is the whole value proposition and requires genuine credit/finance judgment. Meanwhile the specialist finishes the 8 clean certificates (final human review: does the format match this lender's requirement? schedule references right? signature block?), converts to lender-ready PDF, sends to the client's CFO to sign and forward. The thing that breaks on a bad Tuesday: a missed deadline because a client delivered their trial balance two days late and a specialist was already saturated — exactly the failure the service is sold to prevent, so SLAs and buffer staffing around quarter-close are existential.

CAPACITY MATH
Target ladder: ~$600K combined mo12; ~$1.0M+ mo24. Pricing [ASSUMED] ~$1,500-$3,500/month per borrower + one-time onboarding $3,000-$8,000; model blended $2,000/month = $24K ARR.
Revenue sizing: mo12 ~$600K income → to net after a small reviewer bench/tooling, gross recurring ~$1.0M-$1.3M → ~45-55 active borrowers (used 80 as upper-bound peak; ~50-60 conservative center). mo24 ~$1.0M+ income → ~$2.0M-$2.6M gross → ~85-110 borrowers; call it ~100.
Delivery labor per borrower-quarter: data intake + chase ~0.5-1.5 hrs; run models + validate ~1-2 hrs; specialist review + lender-format check ~0.5-1 hr; breach-risk advisory (~20% of borrower-quarters) +1.5-2 hrs. Blended ~2.5-4 hrs, ~3 hrs average [ASSUMED; verify by timing first 10 cycles]. At 100 borrowers, quarterly recurring = 300 review-hrs/quarter, but bunched: ~55% Dec-FYE compresses ~55×3=165 hrs into an 8-week window ≈ ~21 hrs/week peak. One FT specialist supplies ~30-35 productive hrs/week → ~1.5-2 specialists + founder overflow on breach calls. Annualized 100×4×3 = 1,200 review-hrs/yr ≈ ~0.7 FTE pure review, but peaks force staffing to ~2 FTE.
Onboarding (lumpier): abstracting a loan agreement into a register + building the model is ~6-12 hrs/borrower [ASSUMED]. Growing ~25→100 in year 2 = ~75×9 = ~675 hrs ≈ ~13 hrs/week steady, ~0.4-0.5 FTE, spiky around wins. Onboarding is where standardization is hardest — every credit agreement is drafted differently.
Headcount verdict: at ~100-borrower scale, ~2 founders + ~2 finance specialists + ~1 onboarding/ops analyst = ~5 people. Clears the "two founders + lean team" test. The throughput ceiling is review-hours at quarter-close, manageable with modest staffing.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.7 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Zero field/logistics footprint: the deliverable is a PDF sent remotely, geographically unconstrained and operationally identical at 10 or 500 clients — no routing/trucks/installs (scored 9).
- Genuinely recurring, calendar-forced cadence: covenant certificates recur every quarter (monthly for some borrowing-base lines) for the life of the loan, giving a predictable repeatable rhythm.
- Lean staffing fits the income ladder: at the ~100-borrower / ~$2M-gross mo24 scale, ~1,200 annual review-hours implies ~2 finance-specialist FTE plus an onboarding analyst — a ~5-person org two founders can run.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Quarter-close bunching: ~55% December-FYE concentration compresses the bulk of the year's certificate work into an 8-week window (~21 peak review-hrs/week at 100 clients), forcing peak-staffing and making a single saturated week a missed-deadline event.
- Client data latency: every quarterly cycle depends on the borrower delivering clean financials on time; late/messy trial balances (chased manually each quarter) are the most common friction, outside the company's control yet inside its SLA.
- High-stakes QC with severe failure mode: a single wrong ratio or misread add-back can certify a false-compliant number or miss a real breach, calling a client's loan and exposing the company to a liability claim — every certificate needs skilled human sign-off, permanently capping automation.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Not capacity — the hour math is comfortable — but the irreducible, high-judgment review labor colliding with a zero-tolerance failure mode at the exact moment of peak load. The service sells a promise — "never trip a covenant" — it cannot fully control, because the certificate depends on the client's own (often late, often messy) financials and on subtle credit-agreement interpretation (which add-backs are permitted, how a specific lender defines fixed-charge coverage). When 50-plus December-FYE borrowers come due in the same March weeks, a single overloaded specialist who misreads one EBITDA add-back or lets one Thursday deadline slip can trigger a real technical default for a client — a catastrophic, reputation-ending, potentially litigated outcome, not a refundable callback. The work can never be deskilled into cheap, fully-automated throughput; every certificate needs a competent finance reviewer's sign-off, the reviewers are a non-commodity hire, and the founders are the backstop on every projected-breach call during the busiest weeks. Operable, but it lives or dies on reviewer quality and disciplined peak-season staffing buffers.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- How standardized can loan-agreement abstraction actually be? Pull 10 real lower-middle-market credit agreements and measure: how many covenant types, definitions, add-back schedules recur vs one-off? If onboarding stays ~6-12 hrs and never compresses, it becomes the growth-gating bottleneck — what's the real number after the first 10?
- What does the company deliver vs advise, and where does liability sit? Do you certify the numbers, or does the client's officer sign? If a certified number is challenged or a deadline missed on your watch, what's the contractual liability cap, and does it survive a called loan? (Shapes QC cost and insurance.)
- How do you handle the quarter-close peak without over-hiring for the trough? Fractional/contract reviewer bench, deadline-staggering across lender-required dates, or a cap on December-FYE concentration to flatten the curve?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally a strong concept by this venture's standards: remote, capital-light, no field ops, genuinely recurring on a forced calendar cadence, and the capacity math clears the ladder with a ~5-person org by mo24. Not a GO yet for two fixable reasons (operating-model design, not business-model change). First, the front door — loan-agreement abstraction — is the bespoke, hardest-to-standardize step; the founders must prove (with 10 real agreements timed) that it compresses with reusable templates rather than staying a 9-hour artisanal task per client, or onboarding throttles growth. Second, the delivery engine must be explicitly designed around the quarter-close peak and the zero-tolerance failure mode: a defined reviewer-sign-off protocol, a contracted fractional reviewer bench for Feb-March, hard SLAs on client-data deadlines, and a clear liability posture. Make those two operational simplifications concrete and this moves to GO; the underlying delivery shape is sound and well-suited to two founders plus a lean finance bench.
