# CFO REVIEW — CoverHold (Insurer-Attestation Management for Property Owners)

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil if — and only if — the founders can prove that property owners will pay a recurring subscription for what is, at its core, a deadline-tracking and document-assembly service, because the failure mode (lost coverage = lender default) is genuinely existential and the buyer is genuinely too overloaded to do it themselves. The economics are attractive on paper: software-leveraged, remote, high gross margin, recurring, with a compliance specialist as the only per-account human cost. The fragility lives in three places: (1) ARPU is unproven and likely modest ($150–$600/property/month band, not enterprise pricing), so the founder-income ladder depends on property COUNT; (2) it is partly a "panic during the hard market" buy whose urgency softens if the property-insurance market loosens; and (3) there is real seasonal/episodic demand — requirements cluster around renewal and inspection. On absolute merit, a good model with a credible path to the ladder, not a rare one.

Customers for ~$1.5M ARR: Path A portfolio accounts @ ~$96K = ~16 accounts; Path B small owners @ $36K (10-property) = ~42 accounts; Path C per-property long tail = ~420 properties.
Capacity/margin governor: compliance specialist at ~$70–95K loaded reviews [ASSUMED] 60–120 active properties. At $3,600/property/yr and ~80 properties/specialist → ~$288K revenue vs ~$85K labor → ~70% gross margin on the delivery line. Real but NOT 90%.
Mo-12 rung (~$600K): ~$700–850K ARR ≈ 8–10 portfolio accounts OR ~200 properties.
Conclusion: ladder reachable at modest customer counts; binding question is ARPU realism and whether subscription survives contact with buyers.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.4 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Capital-light and fast to cash: low-five-figure launch, onboarding fees upfront, annual agreements — self-funds growth without a raise (9/8/8).
- Reachable income ladder at low customer counts: ~16 portfolio accounts @ ~$96K ARR clears ~$1.5M ARR → ~$500K+/founder by mo24 without heroic scale.
- Clean recurring asset: contracted, software-leveraged compliance revenue in an insurance niche is financeable and acquirable.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ARPU/willingness-to-pay entirely [ASSUMED]: if owners value it at $50–100/property/month not $300, customer-count requirements triple and the ladder slips.
- Demand is hard-market-cyclical and episodic; "always-on subscription" framing may revert to one-time project fees.
- Revenue concentration on the portfolio path: ~16 accounts means one defection (or in-housing) is a single-to-double-digit revenue event.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single financial issue most likely to kill this is that the work is "obvious once shown" and therefore subscription-fragile: the buyer paying $96K/year to never miss a deadline may, after one or two renewal cycles, conclude that requirement volume is low enough that a part-time internal admin plus a $30/month task tracker replaces the service — collapsing it from a defensible recurring managed service into a one-time backlog-cleanup project that does not recur. The deadline engine and packaged carrier-ready response must be genuinely hard to replicate (carrier-specific formatting, evidence standards, consequence of error) for the price to hold; if it's just reminders and a folder, gross margin and retention both erode and the income ladder never compounds.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What is the real, paid ARPU? Sell 10 paid pilots, publish a price sheet — $150, $300, or $600/property/month, and do portfolio buyers accept per-property pricing or demand a flat cap?
- What is annual logo retention after the first renewal cycle, and what fraction try to bring it in-house once backlog is cleared?
- How seasonal/episodic is requirement volume per account, and how much of revenue is non-recurring onboarding vs true recurring base?
- How concentrated will the book be — % of ARR in top 5 accounts at mo24 — and is the small-owner long tail acquirable at LTV/CAC > 3?
- Does urgency/pricing power survive a softening insurance market, or is this a 2–4 year hard-market window?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Financial skeleton sound — capital-light, recurring, high-margin, reachable ladder at modest counts, clean transferable asset, founders with direct insurance-deal and compliance-operator experience. Not yet GO because the two load-bearing numbers (real paid ARPU, post-onboarding retention) are entirely assumed and the worst case (one-time cleanup masquerading as subscription, in a cyclical window) guts both gross margin and the recurring base. To GO: (1) close 8–10 paid accounts, report actual ARPU + price sheet; (2) show first-renewal retention ≥85% with anti-insourcing lock-in; (3) show recurring revenue exceeds onboarding fees by a healthy multiple. Land those and this is a GO at 7.5+.
