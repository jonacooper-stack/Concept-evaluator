# CMO REVIEW — LIHTC (Section 42) Compliance Managed Service

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
A real painkiller in a real market, and "never trigger a recapture" is one of the sharpest fear-based hooks in any compliance category — Form 8823 and credit recapture are existential, deadline-driven, already budgeted. But the market is smaller and slower-moving than it first appears: the buyer is a conservative, relationship-driven affordable-housing operator who already has incumbent vendors (RealPage/Spectrum, Yardi Affordable, ProLink, Nan McKay, US Housing Consultants, Costello), and the actual workflow software is largely owned by RealPage and Yardi already. The wedge — "managed service" owning the continuous operation rather than selling software or periodic training — is genuinely under-served, but selling it means displacing trusted, embedded compliance staff and consultants in a market where switching is scary precisely BECAUSE the pain is existential. Winnable through state-association events and referral, but sales-led and trust-gated, not a fast marketing flywheel; credential dependency (a named credentialed LIHTC specialist) is real.

POSITIONING DRAFT
"We run your LIHTC compliance end-to-end — every income certification, every recert, every annual owner cert and state filing — so your tax credits never get recaptured." Clean, specific, edged, aimed at a specific buyer (asset manager / compliance director / small-operator principal). Differentiated from the incumbent "we'll review your files and train your staff" because it promises to OWN the operation and outcome. Weakness: every LIHTC vendor already says "protect your credits / avoid 8823," so the fear hook is table stakes; the edge is "run" — fully managed, outcome-owned — and marketing must make THAT the headline.

THE WEDGE
First 100 from three concrete places, not SEO/paid: (1) State HFA compliance-training circuit + trade associations — NCSHA annual conference, state AHMA chapters, NAHMA. Booth + speaking slot ~[ASSUMED $3K–$10K/event; verify via 2026 AHMA sponsor prospectus]; rooms full of exactly the compliance directors and small-operator principals who are the buyer. (2) Referral from LIHTC syndicators/investors (Boston Financial, Hunt Capital Partners, RBC Community Investments, Raymond James Affordable Housing) — the investor side is terrified of recapture and will push a struggling GP toward a compliance fixer; warm intros are the highest-quality channel. (3) The remediation wedge baked into the model: the one-time pre-audit file-cleanup fee is the perfect land motion — find operators with a looming MOR or recent 8823, fix the deficiency for a fixed fee, convert to recurring. Verify channels cheaply: pull HUD/state 8823 + inspection-failure data and the HUD LIHTC database (~50,000+ properties); cold-test 20 small operators with the remediation offer.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
Three buckets. (1) Compliance software with embedded certification engines: RealPage (OneSite Affordable / Spectrum WinTen lineage) and Yardi Affordable/Voyager — own the system-of-record for much of professionally managed LIHTC; well-funded strong-tech incumbents. Best at software rails but they do NOT operate compliance for you. (2) LIHTC compliance consultancies/training shops: US Housing Consultants, Costello Compliance, Nan McKay, Spectrum Enterprises, ProLink Solutions, Zeffert — best at periodic file audits, training (TCS/SHCM/HCCP), pre-MOR reviews. Exactly the "sleepy periodic-review" incumbents, beatable on the managed-service axis. (3) The owner's own thin in-house compliance team — the real default to displace. Air is in the gap between (1) and (2): nobody productizes the CONTINUOUS operation — "we are your compliance department," software-leveraged, fixed scope, published pricing, audit-ready file maintained for you. Danger: RealPage/Yardi could add a managed-service layer to existing rails in a quarter or two; US Housing Consultants/Costello already have credentialed people and relationships — their only deficiency is software and packaging, both copyable.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.5 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- True painkiller: Section 42 recapture is existential, work is deadline-driven and unforgiving, certifications recur annually for the full 15-yr period — already-budgeted, must-have.
- The remediation-to-recurring land motion (fixed-fee pre-audit cleanup → convert to managed subscription) is a genuinely smart, specific, low-friction entry wedge into a fear-driven buyer.
- The "we run it, we own the outcome" framing is a clean differentiation vs sleepy periodic-review incumbents, and the syndicator-referral channel (Boston Financial, Hunt Capital, RBC) is a high-quality fear-aligned distribution path.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- Differentiation is a service-model/packaging difference, not structural — Costello, US Housing Consultants, Zeffert already employ credentialed specialists and trusted relationships and can copy the managed tier; RealPage/Yardi own the engine and could add managed service.
- A conservative, trust-gated displacement sale into a risk-averse buyer who fears change BECAUSE the pain is existential — long cycles, founder-heavy closing, slow logo accumulation.
- Credential dependency is real: a fully managed certification service must stand behind credentialed work (HCCP/SHCM/TCS), so founders can't run delivery on versatility alone — they must hire/partner a named LIHTC specialist, and the brand promise sits on that hire.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
This is a copyable service wrapped around someone else's rails, sold into a buyer who is hard to switch. Defensibility is thin: the certification-engine software is owned by RealPage and Yardi, and the credentialed-labor-plus-relationships half is owned by Costello, US Housing Consultants, Nan McKay, Spectrum, Zeffert — so the one novel element, the productized continuous managed operation, is a packaging move an incumbent consultancy with existing credentialed staff and client trust can replicate within a year of seeing it work. Worse, the very severity that makes the positioning sing makes the buyer terrified to hand existential compliance to an unproven new vendor; "never trigger a recapture" cuts both ways — the safest-feeling choice for a risk-averse compliance director is to stay with the consultant they know. If the founders can't show fast that the remediation wedge converts and that a credentialed delivery lead is locked in, this becomes a slow sales-heavy grind in a market the big software players and established consultants can squeeze from both sides.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- Who is the named credentialed LIHTC lead (HCCP/SHCM/TCS) standing behind every certification, and how do you scale that credentialed-review capacity without it becoming a per-unit bottleneck? The brand promise lives or dies on this person.
- Realistic annual price per property and close rate/cycle on the remediation-to-recurring motion? Have you run the remediation offer past 15-20 small operators and gotten a price and a yes?
- When this works, what stops Costello/US Housing Consultants — who already have the credentialed people and relationships — from launching the identical managed tier, and RealPage/Yardi from adding managed service to the engine clients already use? Name the specific switching cost beyond "we hold your files."

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Pain is real and the hook strong, but as drawn this is a 6.5: a copyable, credential-dependent managed service squeezed between well-funded software incumbents (RealPage, Yardi) and embedded credentialed consultancies (Costello, US Housing Consultants, Nan McKay), sold into a slow risk-averse trust-gated buyer. To GO: (1) lead marketing with "we ARE your compliance department" (outcome-owned, fixed-scope, published-price) and make remediation-to-recurring the explicit tip of the spear; (2) lock and name the credentialed delivery lead before going to market; (3) build a real switching cost beyond holding files — own the syndicator/investor relationship so YOU become the channel investors push GPs toward (distribution + a moat sleepy consultants can't replicate). Without those, differentiation is packaging and defensibility too thin for the slow displacement sale.
