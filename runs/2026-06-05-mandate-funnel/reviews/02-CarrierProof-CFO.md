# CFO REVIEW — CarrierProof (Appointment-Maintenance Managed Service for Insurance Agencies)

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil to a real, durable, high-margin recurring business and the income-ladder target is reachable, though not trivially: a software-leveraged compliance managed service with a forced-renewal cash dynamic, low startup capital, and 75-85% gross margins once the document workflow is templatized. The fragility is not the unit economics — it is whether the pain is acute enough at the small-agency end to command a real annual retainer (vs a $50/month tool) and whether the managed-service labor scales sub-linearly or quietly turns into a head-count business. At a defensible ~$6-12K/agency/year blended ACV, ~$300K/founder by mo12 needs ~100-130 paying agencies, ~$500K by mo24 needs ~330 — aggressive but not heroic in a market of tens of thousands of independent agencies; gated by sales velocity, not margin or capital.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.8 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Negative working-capital, self-funding cash dynamic (annual retainer upfront, labor across the year).
- SaaS-like gross margin (73-91%) on a software-leveraged spine, near-zero capital to launch.
- Carrier-mandated, deadline-driven demand spread across hundreds of small agencies — low concentration, painkiller buy.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ACV compression at the small-agency end: a 3-5 producer shop may buy a $600/year tracker instead of a $6-12K retainer, collapsing the math toward low-ACV SMB SaaS.
- Service-labor creep: if attestation portals resist templating, the managed service becomes a head-count business and margin erodes toward 50%.
- Sales-throughput gating: ~100-150 agencies by mo12 needs ~10-15 net new/month; slower close misses the rung even with fine long-run economics.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
ACV compression driven by willingness-to-pay at the small end. The model rests on ~$8K blended retainer, but the most numerous prospects (3-15 producer agencies) are the most price-sensitive and likely to perceive a "spreadsheet-replacement tool" worth a few hundred dollars a month. If real-world quoting clears at $2-3K instead of $8K, mo12 revenue need of ~$1.2M requires 400-600 agencies instead of 150, CAC payback stretches, and the ladder slips a year. Mitigant within founder control: anchor price to avoided loss (a suspended appointment freezes new business and jeopardizes commissions worth tens-to-hundreds of thousands) and aim up-market at larger agencies/MGAs where $15-30K ACV is defensible. Until tested against live buyers, ACV is the assumption everything stands on.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- What annual price clears with live buyers at three sizes (5-producer, 25-producer, MGA) — retainer ($6K+) or tool ($600-1,200)? Run 15-20 price-probe calls.
- How many carrier attestation workflows can be templated vs bespoke each cycle, and true service-hours/agency/year? Time-study first 10-20 onboarded agencies.
- Realistic founder-led close rate and cycle length, and can it deliver ~10-15 net new agencies/month? Is there a Big "I"/state-association/carrier-referral channel compressing CAC?

RECOMMENDATION: GO (conditional on ACV validation)
The financial architecture is strong and squarely in the founders' lane: capital-light, negative working capital, SaaS-like margins, carrier-forced recurring demand, low customer concentration, fragmented ~36,000-agency market served by sleepy back-office shops — the CMMC-archetype shape, with founders' insurance-sales + compliance-operator background bridging the domain. GO rather than reflexive REFINE because nothing in the economics is broken; the two open variables (small-end ACV, attestation-labor templatability) are cheaply testable throughput/pricing questions. Harden to unqualified GO with: (1) live quoting confirming blended ACV ≥ ~$6K, ideally an up-market $15-30K tier; (2) attestation workflow templatable enough to keep margin north of 70% as agency count scales. If price clears at a few hundred a month and attestations prove irreducibly manual, flips to REFINE (re-anchor up-market).
