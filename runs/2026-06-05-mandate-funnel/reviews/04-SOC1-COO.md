# COO REVIEW — SOC 1 Readiness Managed Service

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
A remote-first, software-leveraged managed service with zero physical logistics, zero field ops, zero installs — the delivery unit is evidence collection, control review, and audit-firm quarterbacking, all over screen-share, email, and a controls register. Operable by two founders plus a small specialist team. The genuine friction is not trucks or routing; it is (a) the depth of judgment required to scope financial-reporting control objectives correctly per client, harder to standardize than the security-control checklists that automate SOC 2, and (b) the concentrated, deadline-driven labor spike when multiple clients' audit windows and fieldwork periods overlap. The work is real but knowledge-bound, and the bottleneck is qualified controls-review labor, not capital or systems.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Month 12, ~30 clients [ASSUMED 25-35]. March matters: a large share of SOC 1 reports are calendar/fiscal-year with audit fieldwork landing in Q1, and Type II reports cover an operating-effectiveness window — so March is a peak season with multiple clients simultaneously in fieldwork or closing their evidence window. The founders wake to a controls-register dashboard across 30 clients: which evidence requests are outstanding, which controls are at risk of an exception, which CPA-firm fieldwork engagements are live. Say 8 in active fieldwork/final close (peak cohort), 12 in steady-state continuous-readiness, 10 in early build/off-cycle. Founder A as CPA-firm quarterback: a 9:00 call where the auditor flags that Client #14's change-management sample shows two production deployments with no approval ticket — a real gap (a developer hot-fixed outside the workflow). Now a control-exception conversation — qualified opinion, or remediate-and-retest within the window? The high-stakes judgment moment, undelegatable to junior staff or software. The controls specialist spends the day on evidence-quality review for the 8 peak clients: confirming each artifact actually demonstrates the control (a screenshot of a reconciliation ≠ a signed-off reconciliation with reviewer initials and date), kicking back deficient evidence. ~25-40 controls per SOC 1 engagement [ASSUMED 20-50; verify with sample report ToCs]. Founder B runs onboarding for two new clients: scoping control objectives for a payroll-administration client (payroll accuracy, tax remittance, GL feeds?), drafting management's description, standing up the evidence workflow — the most consultative, least-templatized work, partly bespoke per vertical. Where it breaks: if three peak clients hit fieldwork problems in the same week, both founders get pulled into exception firefighting and steady-state cadence for the other 22 slips; the failure shows up as a late/qualified report three months later — exactly what the service sold against.

CAPACITY MATH
Target ladder: ~$600K combined mo12; ~$1M+ mo24. Pricing [ASSUMED] ~$30K-$60K/org + first-year $15K-$40K onboarding (a single hourly-consultant SOC 1 scramble runs $40K-$100K, so a $40K productized annual fee is credible); blended ~$45K ACV.
Mo-12 ~$600K income on ~55-65% margin → revenue ~$700K-$900K → at $45K ACV, ~16-20 clients; use ~20.
Labor/client/yr: first-year build 40-80 hrs; steady-state continuous readiness ~60-100 hrs/yr; CPA quarterbacking 20-40 hrs in the window. Blended mature ~90-140 → ~110 hrs/yr/client. 20 × 110 = ~2,200 hrs/yr. A FT controls pro delivers ~1,400-1,600 productive hrs/yr → ~1.4-1.6 FTE delivery. Two founders + one specialist = ~2.5 delivery FTE, comfortably covers 20 on average — BUT load isn't flat; if 60% cluster fieldwork into one quarter, peak-quarter weekly load is 2-3x average, making the first specialist hire non-optional before mo12.
First hire: controls/audit-readiness specialist (ex-Big-4 or regional SOC senior, or internal-audit/SOX analyst), loaded ~$110K-$160K [ASSUMED] — the critical hire and a thinner pool than SOC 2 reviewers.
Mo-24 ~$1M+ income → revenue ~$1.4M-$1.8M ≈ 30-40 clients → ~2.5-3 delivery FTE beyond founders (2-3 specialists). A normal small-services curve; bottleneck stays the supply of qualified specialists, not systems/capital. Software (register, evidence-request automation, reminders, package assembly) lets one specialist cover more clients — the whole margin thesis.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.6 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Pure remote, capital-light, non-field-ops delivery — no logistics/installs/routing/geographic constraint; entire unit of work is evidence orchestration and audit shepherding over screen-share.
- Resilient, substitutable supply chain — deliberately offloads report issuance to a fragmented pool of interchangeable CPA firms; no single-source vendor, no platform the business bets its life on, no input with lead times/deposits.
- Software leverage on the routine 70% — evidence requests, reminders, register maintenance, package assembly are automatable, letting one specialist cover many clients and keeping clients-per-FTE (and margin) climbing as the product matures.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Thin specialist labor pool — competent controls-over-financial-reporting reviewers who can also manage CPA fieldwork are scarcer and pricier than SOC 2 security reviewers; the whole model is gated on this hire.
- Seasonal fieldwork clustering — Q1/audit-window concentration creates a 2-3x peak-quarter labor spike, forcing staffing for the peak (idle-ish off-quarters) or risk late/qualified reports during the crunch.
- Delayed, high-consequence failure mode — a missed control or weak evidence surfaces as a qualified/late SOC 1 months later, exactly the catastrophic outcome the service is sold to prevent, turning one slip into a reference-pipeline and reputational hit.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The combination of judgment-bound delivery and a thin specialist labor pool colliding with seasonal peaks. Unlike SOC 2 — where much security-control evidence can be mechanically collected and the reviewer pool is large and growing — SOC 1 turns on controls over financial reporting, where scoping and exception management require genuine accounting/audit judgment the founders (strong SOC 2 operators) can scaffold but don't natively own, and where qualified hires are a smaller, more expensive pool. When that scarce judgment is the bottleneck AND demand concentrates into a Q1 fieldwork peak, the business cannot throw cheap fast-trained labor at a crunch the way a checklist service can. If the founders cannot recruit and retain even one strong controls specialist by ~15-20 clients, growth halts at their personal hours, peak-quarter quality slips, and a single qualified report at a referenceable client undermines the core promise. The model is operable, but its ceiling is set by how scaffold-able and hire-able the financial-controls judgment turns out to be.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- How many distinct control objectives and evidence artifacts does a typical SME SOC 1 engagement carry, and how many hours does a first-year build vs steady-state year truly consume? (3 sample report ToCs from friendly auditors + time the first 3 onboardings.)
- How concentrated is the fieldwork calendar across the realistic client mix — what fraction cluster into one quarter, and can you smooth it by recruiting off-cycle (non-calendar-fiscal-year) clients?
- Where does the first controls specialist come from, fully-loaded cost, and can you recruit one (or advisor + junior reviewer) — i.e., is the judgment scaffold-able with your SOC 2 background + one named practitioner, or does it require a partner-level CPA you can't afford?
- Concrete playbook when an auditor flags a control exception mid-fieldwork — can you drive remediate-and-retest within the window, or risk owning blame for a qualified report you couldn't prevent?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally sound in its bones — remote, capital-light, no field ops, resilient supply chain, real recurring demand tied to a contractual gate — and it clears the ladder on conservative math (~20 clients mo12, ~35 mo24). Not a clean GO yet because three operational unknowns sit on the critical path: the true per-client hour load (drives capacity and margin), the recruitability of the scarce financial-controls specialist (the single gating hire), and seasonality concentration (staff for peak or average). Before GO: (1) productize/templatize the front-end scoping by vertical (separate playbooks for payroll admin, loan servicing, lockbox/payment, claims processing) so the bespoke build collapses into standardized variants and reduces per-engagement senior judgment; (2) deliberately diversify the client fiscal-year mix to flatten the Q1 peak; (3) line up a named controls/audit advisor or first-hire candidate before scaling past founder capacity. Nail those three and this moves to GO.
