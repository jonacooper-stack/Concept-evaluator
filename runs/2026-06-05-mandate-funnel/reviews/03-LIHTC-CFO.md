# CFO REVIEW — LIHTC (Section 42) Compliance Managed Service

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
Financially attractive: a recurring, annually-billed managed service tied to a catastrophic-if-failed compliance obligation running a 15-year compliance period (30 yrs total under most extended-use agreements), sold to a buyer who already budgets it. Margins on a software-leveraged remote document service should be high (60-75% after specialist labor), working capital favorable if billed annually upfront, income ladder reachable. Two worries: (a) labor intensity — a "credentialed LIHTC specialist reviews certifications" is the COGS engine; if review can't be heavily automated this drifts toward staffing margins; (b) trust/switching friction — owners hand over an existential function, so sales cycles and onboarding may be long, slowing the ramp.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.3 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Existential, recurring, already-budgeted pain with a 15-30 year obligation horizon: recapture under Form 8823 is catastrophic, bought without internal selling, renewed annually for the property's compliance life — structurally high retention.
- Favorable cash dynamics: annual-upfront billing, list-able buyers, founder-led acquisition, payback <6mo — growth largely self-funding with minimal capital.
- Clean, financeable, diversified revenue: many small/mid owners (low concentration), vendor-held system of record (high switching cost), multi-year contracted book — a sellable asset.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Margin compression from irreducible review labor: if a specialist must personally review every certification, COGS scales near-linearly and GM sits at 60-70%, capping income and scaling.
- Slow customer ramp from trust/onboarding friction: existential handoff + per-property system-of-record takeover → long cycles, mo12 income at the bottom rung.
- Catastrophic-error liability priced in: "never trigger recapture" means a single material miss creates outsized E&O exposure — a real recurring cost line not yet in the math.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The review-labor coefficient — how many minutes of credentialed-specialist time each certification/filing genuinely requires, and how many a specialist can safely handle per year. The entire margin/scaling/income story rests on software absorbing the bulk while the human only "reviews." If Section 42 certifications stay judgment-heavy (third-party income verification, asset imputation, student-status edge cases, complex households), a specialist covers far fewer properties, GM lands 55-65% not 70%+, the per-specialist cap throttles the ramp, and scaling stays flat — a margin-respectable but throughput-capped compliance staffing shop. Still clears the ladder at a smaller property count but forecloses upside and makes it hire-to-grow. Cheap experiment: run 25-50 real certifications end-to-end and measure actual review minutes/cert and properties-per-specialist at acceptable error rates.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- True credentialed-review labor per certification and per property-year → how many properties can one specialist safely cover? (Sets GM, scaling, income ceiling.)
- What do small/mid owners pay today (outside consultants + internal staff cost), and willingness to pay for a vendor that OWNS the operation? (Sets ARPU.)
- Sales cycle + onboarding for an existential handoff: meetings-to-close, time-to-first-revenue, specialist-hours to take over the file per property? (Sets ramp.)
- Liability/E&O posture and annual cost given "never trigger recapture," and how loaded into per-property pricing?
