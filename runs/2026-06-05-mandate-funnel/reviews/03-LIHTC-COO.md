# COO REVIEW — LIHTC (Section 42) Compliance Managed Service

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
A remote, document-based managed-service business with no trucks, installs, or field crew — physical inspections are explicitly excluded, the right call, keeping founders out of a field-ops model. The unit of work is a credentialed compliance specialist reviewing tenant income certifications and assembling/filing state-agency reports, leveraged by software that handles calculations, deadline tracking, document assembly. The hazard is not logistics — it is that the core deliverable is high-skill, judgment-heavy, liability-bearing certification review on an unforgiving deadline-driven calendar, where one wrong income calc on one unit can mean an 8823 and credit recapture. Throughput is a function of how many certs a credentialed reviewer can clear per hour and how much judgment software can absorb — and the credentialed-specialist labor pool is the bottleneck, not the software. Two founders can run this only if they bridge the LIHTC credential gap (a named specialist hire/partner) early, because they personally cannot sign off on certs on day one.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday in March, ~12-month mark. ~25 properties averaging ~60 LIHTC units (~1,500 units), one founder on GTM/ops, the other on product/software, plus one credentialed LIHTC specialist and one document-intake coordinator. March matters: many state agencies cluster annual owner-certification (AOC) deadlines in Q1 [ASSUMED; verify via 8-10 target-state HFA calendars]. Morning: intake coordinator opens the queue — three management companies uploaded move-in packets and recert docs (applications, pay stubs, TIC worksheets, asset verifications, student-status declarations). Software OCR'd and pre-populated income lines, flagged a household at $41,200 computed vs $40,440 limit — a recapture-threatening fail. Coordinator routes flagged certs to the specialist; clean ones queue for lighter review. Midday: the specialist is the chokepoint — annualizing irregular income, self-employed documentation, a household member who turned 18, the student rule, unit-transfer rules, adequacy of third-party verification. A careful move-in cert review with imperfect docs is realistically 30-60 min; a clean recert 15-20. Meanwhile a founder is on a call with a panicking new prospect whose state agency just scheduled an MOR in six weeks with messy files — the remediation upsell, but a one-time burst of file-rebuilding. Afternoon: the AOC crunch — for 9 properties due this month, software assembles the owner certification and unit-status report, but the specialist (or a founder under review) must verify set-aside ratios, rent limits vs current published limits, and the vacant-unit/next-available-unit rule before signing into the HFA portal. Each state's portal/forms differ (CA CTCAC, TX TDHCA, NY HCR). Where it breaks: a single missed deadline or math error on a signed owner certification is exactly the failure the product promises to prevent — the team cannot move fast and break things. Day ends ~9 filings + ~30-40 cert reviews, specialist as the hard constraint, founders doing sales and overflow review they're only marginally qualified for.

CAPACITY MATH
Target: ~$600K combined mo12; ~$1.0M+ mo24. Pricing [ASSUMED] ~$8,000/property/yr for ~60 units (~$130/unit/yr; range $5-15K; verify vs sleepy-consultant fees $1,500-5,000 for periodic reviews + internal staff cost). GM [ASSUMED 55-65%]. Revenue for ~$1.0M income ≈ $1.7-2.0M → ~210-250 properties at $8K.
Delivery-capacity check (binding unit = credentialed cert review): 220 properties × ~60 units = ~13,200 units. Events/unit/yr ≈ ~1 recert + ~0.2 move-in (turnover ~15-30% [ASSUMED]) ≈ 1.2 → ~15,800 certs/yr ≈ ~300/week. Time/cert under software leverage [ASSUMED ~12 min blended] → 300 × 12 = 3,600 min/wk ≈ 60 reviewer-hrs/wk. At ~30 productive review hrs/wk/specialist → ~2 FT specialists for cert review, plus ~220 AOCs (~4-5/wk) and audit/MOR spikes → ~3-4 specialists + 1-2 coordinators + 2 founders = ~6-8 people, tractable, NOT field ops. Holds IF the 12-min/cert assumption holds. If true review is 25-30 min (LIHTC income annualization resists automation), staffing doubles to 6-8 specialists, margins compress, the per-founder ladder strains. This single number is load-bearing. Verify cheaply: time a credentialed specialist reviewing 20 real certs with/without a software-prepped worksheet — one afternoon.

SCORES (1–10)
1. Delivery clarity & repeatability: 6 — Scope is productizable (system of record, deadline engine, report assembly standardized), but the core cert-review step is judgment-laden and varies per state and per messy file — not "easily trained" like a checklist.
2. Supply chain resilience: 8 — Inputs are software (cloud, OCR/doc-parsing, e-filing portals) and labor; no MOQs/deposits/FX/single physical vendor — the only hard supply is credentialed specialists, with multiple sourcing paths (hire, contract, train experienced affordable-housing staff).
3. Logistics tractability: 9 — Fully remote, document-based, physical inspections explicitly excluded; no trucks/install windows/drive times/regional density — work moves as files over a portal, served identically in Newark or Fresno.
4. Customer-ops scalability: 6 — Recurring deliverable is moderately high-touch (continuous cert review, deadline ownership, audit support), not self-serve; onboarding ingests/validates an existing tenant-file backlog, heavy at the front; support load ongoing.
5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: 7 — Commodity cloud + ~50 state HFA e-filing portals; portals are an annoyance (each different, formats change) but no single private platform can cut off the business; the agency dependency is structural to the market.
6. Hiring feasibility: 5 — The first and most important hire is a credentialed/experienced LIHTC specialist (HCCP/SHCM/C3P), a real but tight and not-cheap pool (~$70-110K loaded [ASSUMED]); founders cannot do this work themselves at start, so hiring is a gating constraint — squarely mediocre.
7. Throughput capacity at target: 6 — ~3-4 specialists + intake support can serve ~220 properties at target (achievable), BUT the whole figure rests on the unverified ~12-min/cert assumption; if 25-30 min, staffing doubles and the ladder strains.
8. Quality control simplicity: 4 — The soft spot: the deliverable is unforgiving and zero-defect by nature (one wrong income calc on one unit can trigger an 8823 and recapture), so QC must be near-perfect on judgment work, rework/liability cost is high, consistency across multiple specialists and 50 state rulebooks is hard to standardize.
9. Geographic / seasonality risk: 6 — National, weather-neutral (good), but real seasonality: AOCs and many state filings cluster Q1/spring, a predictable March crunch the small team must staff for peak, plus lumpy MOR/audit spikes.
10. Tooling maturity available: 4 — Off-the-shelf does NOT cover the core: existing LIHTC software (Yardi/RealPage modules, ProLink, Spectrum/MRI) is property-management-centric, not a productized managed-service spine, so founders must build the deadline engine, cert-calc logic against current published per-state limits, and report assembly — a substantial correctness-critical build.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.1 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Truly capital-light and field-free: remote, document-based, physical inspections explicitly out of scope — no trucks/installs/drive times/regional density (logistics 9).
- Clean supply chain and platform-risk profile: zero physical inputs, no MOQs/deposits/FX, dependency spread across commodity cloud + ~50 public state portals rather than one private platform (supply 8).
- Capacity scales to the income ladder with a small team: ~3-4 credentialed specialists + intake support serve ~220 properties at ~$1.7-2.0M revenue — a team of ~6-8, not 50, not field ops.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Zero-defect QC on judgment work: the promise is "never trigger a recapture," yet the core review is human-judgment-heavy across 50 state rulebooks, so a single miss is a liability event, not a refund (QC 4).
- Credentialed-specialist hiring as the throughput gate: founders cannot sign certs on day one, the HCCP/SHCM pool is tight and ~$70-110K loaded, every revenue increment needs another scarce specialist (hiring 5).
- Correctness-critical software must be built, not bought: the cert-calc and deadline engine against current published per-state limits is a real build the founders own, and a bug propagates the exact failure the service exists to prevent (tooling 4).

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The collision between a zero-defect promise and a judgment-heavy, scarce-labor delivery model. Unlike most managed services where a mistake means a redo or a credit, here a mistake on one income certification on one unit can surface in a state MOR, generate an IRS Form 8823, and put a client's previously claimed credits into recapture — existential for the owner and a potential professional-liability claim against the service that positioned itself as "protect your tax credits." QC cannot be "good enough"; it must approach zero-defect on work that resists standardization (annualizing irregular income, self-employment, student rules, set-aside math, per-state variation). Holding that bar needs experienced credentialed reviewers and double-review on high-risk certs, which inflates labor cost per cert, attacks the ~12-min/cert assumption the capacity and margin math depend on, and makes founders — who can't perform the credentialed sign-off early — dependent on hiring/retaining scarce specialists from the first dollar of revenue. If per-cert time is double the assumption and the founders carry E&O for recapture, both the throughput ladder and the gross margin that funds $500K/founder come under pressure simultaneously.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- Real per-cert credentialed-review time with software leverage — ~12 min or ~30? (Time a specialist on 20 real certs with/without a software-prepped worksheet; one afternoon. Sizes the whole staffing/margin model.)
- Who carries liability when a cert you reviewed leads to an 8823 and recapture, what does LIHTC E&O cost/cover, does the contract cap liability, and will investor-backed owners accept that cap?
- Can you hire/retain credentialed specialists (HCCP/SHCM/C3P) at a loaded cost preserving margin, and how many are available?
- How concentrated are AOC/state-filing deadlines, and can a lean team absorb the Q1/spring peak without overstaffing year-round or missing deadlines?
- How much front-loaded backlog work does onboarding each property require (validating a messy existing system of record before certifying), and is it priced as a one-time fee or eating margin?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
A legitimately attractive shape — remote, capital-light, field-free, clean supply chain, recurring by construction, scales to the income ladder with ~6-8 people not 50. Not disqualified on tractability. But three realities keep it out of GO: (1) the deliverable is effectively zero-defect with catastrophic liability-bearing failure modes, scored against judgment work that resists standardization; (2) throughput and margin both hinge on a single unverified number — credentialed minutes per cert — and on hiring scarce specialists the founders cannot substitute for early; (3) the correctness-critical calc/deadline software must be built and kept perfectly current against per-state published limits. To GO: a timed per-cert benchmark proving software leverage lands review near ~12 min; a named credentialed practitioner committed as reviewer-of-record and recruiting anchor before scale; a defined liability posture (contractual cap + E&O) buyers accept; and a deliberately narrowed launch (3-5 target states first, not 50) so the QC and software-build surface is bounded while the model is proven.
