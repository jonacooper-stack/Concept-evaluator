# CFO REVIEW — CovenantClear (Managed Loan-Covenant Compliance for Lower-Middle-Market Borrowers)

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil cleanly and the margin profile is excellent: a recurring, software-leveraged finance-knowledge service with near-zero COGS beyond a specialist's review hours, billed annually against a deadline-driven, high-consequence obligation the buyer already lives with every quarter. The model self-funds (annual upfront billing, no inventory, no AR float), and the path to the income ladder is reachable at modest customer counts. Two soft spots: (1) ARPU uncertainty — covenant compliance is a "we should fix this" pain buyers historically tolerate by leaning on their CPA, so willingness-to-pay for a standalone recurring line item decides whether this is a $6K/yr or $25K/yr product; and (2) acquisition cost into a fragmented, hard-to-target set of private mid-market CFOs with no clean channel and a long, episodic buying trigger (you mostly buy this right after a scare).

THE MATH
Target ladder: ~$600K combined ARR mo12; ~$1.0M+ mo24. Needed ARR ≈ income ÷ 0.75.
ARPU [ASSUMED] $8K-$18K/yr, midpoint $12K. A single waiver/amendment negotiation routinely costs $15K-$50K in legal/advisory + default-rate interest, so a $4K/yr floor is trivially justified. Verify: 20 outreach calls at three price points ($500/$1,000/$1,500/quarter).
Onboarding [ASSUMED] $2.5K-$7.5K one-time. Gross margin: specialist 2-5 hrs/quarter (~$60-90/hr loaded) = ~$480-$1,800/yr/borrower vs $12K ARPU → ~85-96% margin. Verify by timing first 10 cycles.
Customers at $12K ARPU, 75% margin-to-income: mo12 ~$600K income → ~$800K ARR → ~67 borrowers; mo24 ~$1.0M+ → ~$1.33M ARR → ~111 borrowers.
Labor capacity: at ~3 hrs/quarter, 111 borrowers = ~333 review-hrs/quarter = ~26 hrs/week — inside one specialist + founders. Binding constraint is acquisition, not delivery.
Market: 200K+ US private companies $5M-$100M revenue; conservatively 1-in-3 carry covenant debt → tens of thousands of borrowers; 111 is ~0.2%. Depth is not the risk.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.9 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Negative working capital and self-funding growth: annual-upfront billing against quarterly delivery — customers pay before the specialist's labor is incurred.
- Software-leveraged ~85-96% gross margin [ASSUMED, cheaply verifiable]: the calc engine and register are built once and reused; per-borrower marginal cost is a few review-hours per quarter.
- Low customer count to clear the ladder: ~67 borrowers (mo12) / ~111 (mo24) at a defensible $12K ARPU against a pool of tens of thousands.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ARPU/willingness-to-pay unproven for a standalone recurring line: buyers absorb this inside the CPA relationship and may anchor to "my accountant already does this," compressing price toward $4K and tripling the customer count/CAC needed.
- CAC into an ungated, hard-to-target audience with an episodic trigger: no clean list/channel, and the strongest buying moment (post-scare) is exactly when you're not in the room.
- Churn-on-payoff and liability tail: revenue ends when the loan is refinanced/paid off; a missed deadline or miscalculated ratio contributing to a called loan is a high-severity E&O exposure raising insurance cost.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
ARPU collapse driven by the buyer's existing substitute. Unlike a hard regulatory mandate where the buy is non-negotiable, covenant compliance is something every borrower is technically already "handling" — usually by having the outside CPA bang out the certificate each quarter, imperfectly. The pain is severe in tail events but invisible and tolerated in the median quarter — precisely the profile of a problem buyers under-price until it bites. If the market anchors to "a bit more than my accountant charges" rather than "a fraction of what one waiver costs me," ARPU lands near $4K-$5K instead of $12K, and the model still works but demands ~250-330 borrowers for the mo24 rung — roughly tripling acquisition burden in the model's weakest channel. The whole case turns on selling this as catastrophe insurance (priced against the $15K-$50K waiver) rather than bookkeeping (priced against the CPA's hourly) — a positioning-and-pricing question to answer with real paid pilots before scaling.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER
- At what quarterly price do real lower-middle-market CFOs say yes vs "my CPA already does this"? Run 20 paid-pilot offers at $500/$1,000/$1,500 and report the conversion curve.
- Actual CAC and sales-cycle length given no clean target list? Test two channels (lender/turnaround-advisor referral; direct outbound to recently-covenanted borrowers) over 90 days.
- Real per-certificate review labor and E&O/liability posture? Time the first 10 cycles; get an insurance quote for a service whose error could contribute to a called loan.

The financial architecture is genuinely strong — negative working capital, ~85-96% margin, self-funding growth, modest customer counts to clear the ladder, a clean recurring/acquirable asset — and nothing trips a hard constraint. Short of unqualified GO only because the two numbers that govern whether this hits the ladder at 67 vs 250 customers — ARPU/willingness-to-pay and CAC into an ungated episodically-triggered audience — are both [ASSUMED]. To GO: (1) validate $1,000+/quarter ARPU with 5-10 real paid pilots priced against waiver cost, not the CPA bill; (2) demonstrate one repeatable channel (likely lender/turnaround-advisor referral, which also solves post-scare timing) with CAC under ~$5K; (3) confirm per-certificate labor and obtain an E&O quote so net margin is real. Clear those three and this is a high-conviction GO on the financials.
