# LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — CarrierProof (Appointment-Maintenance Managed Service for Insurance Agencies)

Not formal legal advice. Operator pattern-recognition flagging issues for retained counsel.

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
A B2B managed service adjacent to one of the most heavily regulated distribution channels (insurance), but carefully scoped to avoid the activities that trigger the heaviest regulation. It does not sell, solicit, negotiate, or service insurance, does not hold premium funds, does not give legal advice; it administers compliance paperwork (licenses, E&O, attestations) on the agency's behalf. That scoping is the whole ballgame: done as described, it looks like administrative/back-office outsourcing plus software — lightly regulated. Two live landmines: (1) the data is licensee/producer PII and carrier-confidential compliance data, pulling in state insurance data-security laws and GLBA-adjacent obligations; (2) the service submits attestations/certifications to carriers and regulators on the agency's behalf — if a submission is wrong, late, or fraudulent, the service is in the causal chain of a failure that can cost the agency its book. An E&O/professional-liability problem, not a licensing problem — insurable and contractually allocable, but the thing to get right.

REGULATORY MAP
- State insurance producer-licensing law (each state DOI; NY DFS, CA DOI, TX TDI; NAIC model acts). Governs who may "sell, solicit, or negotiate" insurance. The service does NONE of these; it administers compliance about producers' licenses. As scoped, not a producer, no producer license. Burden: low — only if the scope line holds. The most important regime to confirm; misclassification is fatal.
- State insurance data security laws (NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law, 20+ states — NY DFS 23 NYCRR 500, Ohio R.C. 3965, SC). Applies to "licensees" handling nonpublic info; a third-party vendor is reached via the licensee's vendor-oversight obligations → the service will be contractually required to maintain a WISP, breach notification, audit cooperation. Burden: moderate, and a SELLING POINT (helps the agency satisfy its own vendor-management duty).
- GLBA Safeguards Rule + Privacy Rule (FTC). Agencies are "financial institutions" handling consumers' NPI; a service provider touching that NPI inherits Safeguards-Rule obligations flowed down by contract (amended Rule, 2023, requires service-provider oversight). Burden: moderate; standard WISP, encryption, access controls, MFA.
- State data-breach notification (all 50 states; Cal. Civ. Code 1798.82). Notification duties on breach, generally flowing to the data owner with vendor bound to notify/cooperate. Burden: low-moderate.
- CCPA/CPRA + other state privacy. Largely B2B/employee-data; mostly agency/producer business data, not high-volume consumer data; GLBA-regulated data partially exempt. Burden: low.
- FTC Act §5 / state UDAP. "Never lose a carrier to a missed attestation" is a performance promise; a miss makes the claim Exhibit A. Burden: low if softened and disclaimed; real if an absolute guarantee.
- Unauthorized practice of law (state bar). Completing/submitting attestations and "responding to audit requests on the agency's behalf" sits near the UPL line if it crosses into interpreting legal obligations or representing the agency in a proceeding. One-pager disclaims legal advice. Burden: low if factual/administrative.
- Power-of-attorney / agency-authorization. Submitting "on the agency's behalf with authorization" requires clean documented authorization (limited POA) per carrier/regulator portal. Burden: low, contractual.
- TCPA / CAN-SPAM. Outbound marketing to agencies — standard B2B rules. Burden: low.

LICENSING SUMMARY
As scoped, NO specialized insurance license. The service does not sell/solicit/negotiate/service insurance, bind coverage, handle premium/claims funds, or act as a TPA (state TPA model act attaches to administering claims, collecting premium, adjusting benefits — none done here). It is not a "producer" (no producer acts). What it needs is mundane: business entity registration, business license where required, possible registration as a service provider on specific carrier/state portals to submit on the agency's behalf. Watch item: a few states regulate "insurance consultants" broadly — counsel should confirm the administrative-only scope doesn't trip a consultant-licensing definition in an outlier state. Net: confidently low licensing burden, contingent on a clean scope opinion.

LIABILITY POSTURE
Core liability is professional/E&O: the job is to prevent a missed deadline, and the worst case is precisely that it misses one and the agency loses an appointment and a commission stream — a foreseeable, quantifiable economic loss directly attributable to the service's failure. Handled three ways: (1) Professional/tech-E&O insurance — fully insurable at modest premiums (remote, document-based, no bodily-injury/property exposure); (2) Contractual limitation of liability — cap at fees paid, disclaim consequential/lost-commission damages, frame as "best-efforts administrative support, agency remains responsible for its own compliance" (enforceable in B2B; essential because uncapped "lost book of business" exposure is uninsurable in practice); (3) Clear responsibility allocation — the agency, not the service, is the licensee of record and bears ultimate regulatory responsibility. Secondary exposure is data security (breach of producer/consumer NPI), handled by cyber/tech-E&O + WISP + flow-down clauses. Founder personal exposure is the standard pre-entity gap — form an entity before the first customer; no field-ops/bodily-injury exposure. Residual: reputational + marketing-claim/UDAP if absolute guarantees are made.

2. Licensing requirements: 7 — As scoped, no producer or TPA license required (performs none of the licensable acts defined under state producer and NAIC TPA model acts); only residual is confirming no outlier-state "insurance consultant" definition is tripped.
3. Data privacy exposure: 6 — Producer/consumer NPI subject to FTC GLBA Safeguards Rule (amended 2023, service-provider oversight) and state insurance data-security laws (NY DFS 23 NYCRR 500); manageable with a WISP + encryption, but special-category financial data, not minimal PII — honestly middling.
4. Employment & contractor risk: 8 — Remote, document-based knowledge work by W-2 staff or true 1099 specialists, no in-home/field workers; no multi-state field-worker misclassification or workers'-comp exposure; only ordinary remote-multistate payroll registration.

6. IP cleanliness: 7 — Original software and managed-service IP, no single restricted/proprietary API dependency; main third-party touchpoints are carrier/regulator portals (ToU compliance) and possible NIPR/PDB license-data feeds (licensable data sources, not IP traps).
7. Contract simplicity: 7 — Repeatable annual managed-service agreement (master terms + order form) with limitation-of-liability and authorization-to-act clause; heavier than clickwrap due to POA/authorization and infosec flow-down, but a single standardized template, not negotiated-MSA-per-deal.
8. Liability exposure: 7 — Worst case (missed deadline → lost appointment/commission) is foreseeable economic loss, but no bodily-injury/property risk, insurable via tech/professional-E&O at modest premiums, and contractually cappable at fees-paid with consequential damages waived under standard enforceable B2B law.
9. Jurisdictional simplicity: 5 — Agencies write in multiple states and the compliance obligations the service tracks are inherently 50-state (producer licensing varies per state per line), so the operational regulatory surface multiplies; the service's OWN footprint is more contained but cannot escape touching all-state rules — genuinely mediocre.
10. Regulatory trajectory: 7 — Insurance compliance is TIGHTENING (NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law spreading, carrier attestation requirements increasing, license-monitoring scrutiny rising), raising customer pain and demand; a tailwind even as it adds the service's own compliance work.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.8 / 10

TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY STRENGTHS
- Deliberately scoped OUTSIDE the licensable perimeter — no selling/soliciting/negotiating/servicing/claims-administration/premium-handling — sidestepping state producer and NAIC TPA licensing that would be a 50-state cost cliff.
- Pure B2B with no consumer subscriptions and no field/in-home labor — zeroes out two nastiest traps simultaneously: 50-state consumer auto-renewal law and multi-state field-worker misclassification/workers'-comp.
- Core liability is a clean, insurable, contractually-cappable professional-E&O exposure with no bodily-injury/property dimension.

TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY RISKS
- Scope creep across the producer line: advising on which appointment to keep, interpreting carrier requirements, or representing the agency in a proceeding risks recharacterization as a producer act or UPL.
- Causal-chain E&O on a missed deadline: the value prop is preventing the loss it could itself cause; a single missed attestation costing an appointment is a large, foreseeable, emotionally charged claim.
- Handling producer/consumer NPI under the FTC GLBA Safeguards Rule and state insurance data-security laws (NY DFS 23 NYCRR 500 + spreading NAIC model), where a breach triggers notification + indemnity exposure.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The gap between the marketed promise and the contractual/insurable reality on a missed deadline. "Keep every appointment and every commission — never lose a carrier to a missed attestation" sells an absolute outcome, but the service operates in a 50-state, dozens-of-carriers, rolling-deadline environment where some misses are statistically inevitable, and when one occurs the harm (suspended/terminated appointment freezing new business and jeopardizing commissions on an existing book) is large, foreseeable, directly traceable to the service's core function, and exactly the kind of loss an agency principal will litigate over emotionally. An absolute marketing guarantee becomes both a UDAP/FTC-§5 problem and the plaintiff's centerpiece for arguing the liability cap should not be enforced. Fix is structural and within founder control: pair softened qualified marketing ("dramatically reduce" not "never") with a tight enforceable B2B contract that caps liability at fees paid, waives consequential/lost-commission damages, and states the agency remains the responsible licensee — backed by professional/tech-E&O sized to the cap. Get that triad right and the risk is ordinary cost-of-doing-business; get it wrong and a single bad miss + an aggressive claim could produce an uninsured "lost book of business" judgment dwarfing annual revenue.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
- Scope opinion: does preparing, completing, and submitting carrier compliance attestations and responding to carrier audit requests on the agency's behalf constitute "selling/soliciting/negotiating/servicing" insurance under target-state producer-licensing statutes, or administering claims/premium under any state's TPA model act? Where is the line, and does any state's "insurance consultant" definition reach us?
- UPL line: at what point does completing attestations or responding to audit requests become UPL, and how do we keep the service factual/administrative and disclaimed?
- Data-security obligations: what flow-down attaches under the amended FTC GLBA Safeguards Rule and state insurance data-security laws, what must our WISP contain, and what breach-notification/cooperation duties go in the customer contract?
- Limitation-of-liability enforceability: will a fees-paid cap and consequential/lost-commission waiver hold against a "lost book of business" claim in our governing-law state, and how do we draft the responsibility-allocation language?
- Authorization mechanics: what limited POA/authorization do we need per agency, and which carrier/state portals require their own registration before we may submit on a client's behalf?
- Marketing-claim review: pressure-test the advertising against FTC §5 and state UDAP so positioning isn't a guarantee we can be held to.

RECOMMENDATION: GO
As scoped, a favorable legal posture for a bootstrapped two-founder venture: deliberately outside the insurance licensing perimeter (no producer/TPA license if scope holds), avoids the two worst small-business traps (consumer auto-renewal law, field-worker misclassification) by being pure B2B and remote/document-based, and carries a core liability that is insurable and contractually cappable. The regulatory tailwind (tightening insurance data-security and carrier-attestation requirements under the spreading NAIC model and NY DFS 23 NYCRR 500) increases demand rather than threatening the service. GO conditional on three pre-launch actions, all within founder control and none capital-intensive: (1) written scope opinion confirming the administrative-only model doesn't trip producer/TPA/consultant licensing in launch states and stays clear of UPL; (2) the liability triad (qualified marketing, fees-paid/consequential-waiver B2B contract with clean responsibility allocation, professional/tech-E&O + cyber insurance); (3) a GLBA/state-insurance-data-security-compliant WISP before touching client NPI. No structural change required; the risks are knowable paperwork and discipline, not landmines.
