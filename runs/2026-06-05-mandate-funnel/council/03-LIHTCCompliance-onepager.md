# Business Concept One-Pager

## Customer
Owners and third-party property managers of affordable-housing properties financed with federal Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC, IRC Section 42) — typically small and mid-size owners and management companies operating 5 to 150 tax-credit properties. The buyer is the owner's asset manager, the management company's compliance director, or the principal of a small affordable-housing operator.

## The problem
LIHTC properties must comply continuously with Section 42 rules to keep their tax credits. Every tenant must be income-certified at move-in and re-certified on schedule; the property must maintain set-aside ratios, rent limits, and student-status rules; and the owner must file annual owner certifications and detailed reports to the state housing finance agency. If a property falls out of compliance — a botched income certification, a missed re-certification, an over-income household, a late or wrong annual report — the state agency reports it to the IRS on Form 8823, and the IRS can **recapture** previously claimed tax credits and disallow future ones. For an investor-backed deal, credit recapture is an existential financial event that can trigger penalties to the investor and default under the partnership agreement. The certification and reporting work is technical, deadline-driven, voluminous, and unforgiving, and it must be done correctly on every unit for the full 15-year compliance period and beyond.

## The product
A productized, recurring LIHTC-compliance managed service. For each property the service: maintains the tenant-file system of record; performs and reviews income certifications and re-certifications against current limits; tracks set-aside, rent-limit, and student-rule compliance unit by unit; runs the deadline engine for every recurring certification and the annual owner certification; assembles and files the state-agency reports; and maintains an audit-ready file for state inspections (MOR/physical-and-file audits). Software handles the calculations, deadline tracking, document intake, and report assembly; a credentialed LIHTC-compliance specialist reviews certifications and filings. Delivery is remote and document-based; the service does not perform the property's physical inspections.

## Revenue model
Per-property annual subscription (tiered by unit count), billed as a recurring managed service, with per-unit certification volume as a pricing input. Optional one-time fee to remediate a property with existing file deficiencies before a state audit. Revenue recurs because certifications, re-certifications, and annual filings repeat every year for the property's entire compliance period.

## Positioning
Sold as "protect your tax credits — never trigger a recapture." Incumbents are sleepy LIHTC consultants who perform periodic manual file reviews and training but do not own the continuous certification-and-filing operation, plus the owner's own thinly staffed compliance team. The offering is a modern, productized, software-leveraged managed service with published scope and pricing, aimed at a fragmented owner/manager market that the large affordable-housing syndicators do not service directly and that no dominant software vendor owns. The wedge is the severity and certainty of the pain: recapture is catastrophic, the rules are unforgiving, and competent continuous compliance is scarce.
