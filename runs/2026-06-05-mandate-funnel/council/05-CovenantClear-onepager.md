# Business Concept One-Pager

## Customer
Lower-middle-market companies that carry bank term loans, asset-based lines, or SBA loans with financial covenants — typically privately held businesses with $5M to $100M in revenue across manufacturing, distribution, healthcare services, and similar sectors. The buyer is the company's CFO, controller, or owner-operator; in many of these firms there is no full finance team.

## The problem
Commercial loan agreements carry financial covenants and reporting obligations: the borrower must deliver, on a fixed schedule (usually quarterly and annually), a compliance certificate showing it has met covenant thresholds (leverage ratio, fixed-charge coverage, minimum EBITDA, tangible net worth), plus borrowing-base certificates, financial statements, and other deliverables. Missing a reporting deadline, or delivering a certificate that shows a covenant breach, is a **technical default** — even when the business is healthy — which gives the lender the right to call the loan, freeze the line, impose default-rate interest, or force a costly waiver/amendment. Many lower-middle-market borrowers lack the finance staff to track covenant definitions, calculate the ratios correctly, anticipate a breach before it happens, and produce lender-ready certificates on time. The work is technical, recurring, and high-stakes: a tripped covenant can put the company's financing — and survival — at risk.

## The product
A productized, recurring covenant-compliance managed service. For each borrower the service: parses the loan agreement to build a structured register of every covenant, definition, threshold, and reporting deadline; maintains the calculation models for each covenant ratio; runs a quarterly (and as-needed) close-to-certificate workflow that pulls the borrower's financials, computes the ratios, flags any cushion erosion or projected breach early, and produces the lender-ready compliance certificate and borrowing-base report; and tracks delivery to the lender against the deadline. When a breach is approaching, the service alerts the borrower with time to act (raise EBITDA add-backs, manage the balance sheet, or proactively approach the lender for a waiver). Software runs the covenant register, the calculation engine, and the deadline tracker; a finance specialist reviews each certificate before delivery. Delivery is remote.

## Revenue model
Quarterly recurring subscription per borrower (tiered by number and complexity of covenants and facilities), billed as a managed service with an annual agreement; optional onboarding fee to abstract the loan agreement and build the covenant register and models. Revenue recurs because covenant certificates and borrowing-base reports are due every quarter (and monthly for some lines) for the life of the loan.

## Positioning
Sold as "never trip a covenant — keep your financing safe." Incumbents are the borrower's overworked outside CPA firm (which is focused on tax and the annual review, not real-time covenant management) and generalist fractional-CFO consultants who do it manually and episodically. The offering is a modern, productized, software-leveraged managed service with published scope and pricing, aimed at a large, fragmented set of lower-middle-market borrowers that no software vendor specifically serves and that the banks themselves will not help (the lender is the counterparty). The wedge is the direct, severe consequence — a tripped covenant can call the loan — paired with how poorly equipped these borrowers are to manage it themselves.
