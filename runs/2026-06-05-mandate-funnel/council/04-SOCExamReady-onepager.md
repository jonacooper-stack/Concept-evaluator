# Business Concept One-Pager

## Customer
Small and mid-size "service organizations" whose software or operations sit inside their customers' financial-reporting processes — niche vertical SaaS vendors, claims processors, payroll and benefits administrators, loan servicers, lockbox/payment processors, and similar B2B firms — typically 20 to 500 employees. The buyer is the company's CFO, controller, head of security/compliance, or COO.

## The problem
When a company's service affects its customers' financial statements, those enterprise customers' auditors require a SOC 1 (SSAE 18) report — an independent attestation on the design and operating effectiveness of the service organization's controls over financial reporting. Without a current SOC 1, the enterprise customer's own audit is impaired, so large customers contractually require the vendor to produce a SOC 1 every year and will not sign or renew without it. Producing a SOC 1 means defining the control objectives, building and operating the control evidence over a months-long audit window, and managing the relationship with an external CPA audit firm that issues the report. Most small service organizations are unprepared: they scramble each year, struggle to assemble evidence, fail control tests, and risk losing or delaying enterprise contracts when the report is late or qualified. SOC 1 is specifically about controls over financial reporting and is distinct from the security-focused SOC 2.

## The product
A productized, recurring "SOC 1 readiness and evidence-management" managed service. The service: scopes the relevant control objectives for the organization's financial-reporting role; designs the control set; stands up an evidence-collection workflow; operates a continuous readiness program through the audit window (collecting, organizing, and reviewing control evidence on a schedule); prepares management's description of the system; and acts as the organization's quarterback with the external CPA audit firm through fieldwork to a clean report. Software runs the control register, evidence requests, reminders, and audit-package assembly; a controls specialist reviews evidence quality and readiness. The service does not issue the SOC 1 itself (an independent CPA firm does that); it makes the organization continuously ready and shepherds the engagement.

## Revenue model
Annual subscription per organization (tiered by scope/complexity and number of control objectives), billed as a recurring readiness-and-evidence managed service, plus an onboarding fee for the first-year program build. Revenue recurs because a SOC 1 must be renewed every year for as long as the enterprise customers require it, and the evidence program must run continuously.

## Positioning
Sold as "pass your SOC 1 every year and never lose a deal over it." Incumbents are traditional CPA firms (which audit but do not run the client's year-round readiness) and generalist hourly compliance consultants. Mainstream compliance-automation platforms focus on the security-oriented SOC 2 and do not specifically serve the financial-controls SOC 1 readiness need. The offering is a modern, productized, marketing-led managed service aimed at a fragmented set of niche service organizations for whom the SOC 1 is a hard contractual gate to enterprise revenue. The wedge is that the report is contractually mandatory, recurring, and budgeted, and the readiness work is exactly what these firms cannot do well on their own.
