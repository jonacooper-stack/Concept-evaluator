# Business Concept One-Pager

## Customer
Owners and third-party managers of "habitational" and small-commercial real estate — apartment buildings, multifamily portfolios, mobile-home parks, HOAs, strip retail, and light commercial — typically holding 5 to 200 properties. The buyer is the owner's risk manager, the property-management company's compliance lead, or the principal of a small/mid-size property-management firm.

## The problem
Property insurers, in a hard market, are increasingly conditioning coverage and renewal on documented "loss-control" corrective actions. After an inspection or underwriting review, the carrier issues recommendations and requirements — update the roof documentation, prove the electrical panel was remediated, supply sprinkler/fire-system certificates, document a water-mitigation plan, confirm a missing handrail was fixed — and gives the insured a deadline to submit evidence. If the insured does not respond with acceptable documentation by the deadline, the carrier can decline to renew, raise the premium materially, or cancel mid-term. In a market where replacement coverage is scarce and expensive, losing the policy is an existential event for the property's financing and operations (lenders require coverage). Owners and managers routinely miss these deadlines or submit inadequate evidence because the work is administrative, scattered across properties, and nobody owns it.

## The product
A productized, recurring "insurance-attestation management" managed service. For each property/portfolio the service: ingests the carrier's loss-control letters and requirement lists; builds a tracked register of every open requirement and its deadline; collects the required evidence from the owner/manager (documents, invoices, photos, vendor certificates) through a guided digital workflow; packages it into a carrier-ready response; submits/tracks it to closure; and maintains an always-current evidence file for the next renewal. Software runs the deadline engine, document intake, reminders, and response assembly; a compliance specialist reviews each response package before it goes to the carrier. **This is document-and-evidence work performed remotely — it does not include on-site physical inspections or repairs.** The actual repairs are done by the owner's own vendors; the service manages the requirement, the evidence, and the attestation.

## Revenue model
Monthly subscription per property or per portfolio (tiered by number of properties and open-requirement volume), billed as a recurring managed service with an annual agreement. Optional one-time onboarding fee to clear an existing backlog of open requirements. Revenue recurs because loss-control requirements and renewals recur every policy period and the evidence file must be continuously maintained.

## Positioning
Sold as "never lose your coverage to a missed insurer requirement." The incumbents are traditional loss-control/inspection field firms (which perform the inspection but do not own the owner's ongoing paperwork and evidence follow-through) and the owner's own overworked back office. The offering is a modern, marketing-led, productized service with published scope and pricing aimed at a fragmented segment that no software vendor currently serves and that large brokers treat as an afterthought. The wedge is the hard-market urgency: owners are actively losing or repricing coverage right now for failing to document corrective actions.
