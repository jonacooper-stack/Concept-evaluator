# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A productized, recurring managed service that runs the federally-mandated
drug-and-alcohol testing program for small DOT-regulated employers — operating
their random-testing consortium pool, scheduling and tracking required tests,
managing the chain of paperwork (chain-of-custody, MRO results, refusals,
return-to-duty), keeping each driver/employee's compliance file current, and
producing the annual MIS report and the audit binder the auditors demand — so
the employer passes its DOT audit and keeps operating.

## The customer
Small DOT-regulated employers OUTSIDE long-haul trucking, where the category is
under-served: short-haul and local motor carriers, school-bus and motorcoach
operators, transit agencies and paratransit contractors, pipeline operators,
aviation maintenance/charter operations, and maritime/transit employers — plus
the owner-operators and very small fleets (2–50 safety-sensitive employees) that
fall under FMCSA, FTA, FAA, PHMSA, or FRA testing rules. They employ
safety-sensitive workers and are legally required to maintain a compliant
drug-and-alcohol testing program, but they are too small to have an in-house
compliance department and currently rely on a patchwork of local clinics and
sleepy regional "third-party administrators."

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- Federal regulation (49 CFR Part 40 plus the modal rules — FMCSA Part 382, FTA
  Part 655, FAA Part 120, PHMSA, FRA) REQUIRES these employers to maintain a
  drug-and-alcohol testing program: pre-employment testing, random testing at
  federally-set annual rates, post-accident, reasonable-suspicion, and
  return-to-duty testing, plus an annual MIS data report.
- Random testing must run continuously on a defined cadence — the pool is drawn
  every quarter, every year, forever. This is intrinsically recurring, not a
  one-time setup.
- A DOT audit (and FMCSA's new entrant safety audit, and post-accident
  investigations) checks this program first; gaps mean fines, a failed audit, a
  downgraded safety rating, or being placed out of service — existential for an
  operator whose authority to run depends on it.
- The employer already pays for this; it is a budgeted line. The question is only
  who administers it, and the incumbents are weak.

## The product
A fixed-scope, published-price recurring service on one delivery spine:
1. Onboarding: enroll the employer and its safety-sensitive roster into a managed
   random-testing consortium pool, set up its policy, designated-employer-
   representative support, and supervisor reasonable-suspicion training records.
2. A compliance system-of-record portal holding the roster, the random selection
   history, every test result and chain-of-custody, MRO verifications, refusals,
   and return-to-duty documentation, with a live "audit-ready" status.
3. Recurring managed cadence: run the random draws on the federal schedule,
   dispatch testing notifications, coordinate collection sites and the Medical
   Review Officer (both third-party networks — the labs, collectors, and MRO do
   the physical/clinical work), track completion, chase missing results, and flag
   compliance gaps before they become violations.
4. Audit support and the annual MIS report: produce the MIS submission and a
   complete audit binder, and stand with the employer through a DOT audit.
The software runs the random-selection engine, the testing-cadence tracking, the
document system-of-record, and the MIS/report generation; humans handle the DER
support, the judgment calls, and the audit-facing reassurance. No collections,
no lab work, no clinical work — those are done by the existing collection-site,
laboratory, and MRO networks the service coordinates.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription per employer, priced per safety-sensitive employee in the
pool, with a per-test pass-through for the actual collections/lab/MRO fees.
Indicative pricing: a modest setup fee, then an annual managed-program
subscription that scales with headcount (small fleets in the low-to-mid four
figures/year; larger small-employers into five figures), billed annually or
monthly in advance, plus per-test fees. Revenue is structurally recurring: the
random program never stops, the roster turns over (new hires must be enrolled and
pre-employment tested), and leaving mid-year breaks the audit trail.

## Positioning
"Pass your DOT audit. We run your entire drug-and-alcohol testing program —
random pool, every test, every form, your MIS report — so an auditor never finds
a gap. One predictable price per driver." Sold against sleepy regional
third-party administrators with dated portals and clipboard processes, local
occupational clinics that only do the collection and hand back paper, and the
do-it-yourself owner who keeps results in a folder and fails the audit.

## Go-to-market
Marketing/positioning-led demand generation into small safety-sensitive
employers (content on DOT audit prep, new-entrant safety audits, random-rate
changes, and Clearinghouse rules; presence at trade groups for school-bus,
motorcoach, transit, pipeline, and aviation operators; targeted outbound to new
entrants who just got operating authority and must stand up a program; insurance
agents and safety consultants as referral partners). Founder-led close. Insurers
and the operators' own associations are referral channels because non-compliance
is also an insurance and liability problem.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders strong in marketing/positioning, software, and running
formal compliance programs, supported by a named DOT drug-and-alcohol program
expert / certified DER-trainer advisor and contracted relationships with
established collection-site, laboratory, and Medical Review Officer networks.
Remote-first, no field or clinical operations.
