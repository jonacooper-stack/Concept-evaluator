# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A productized, recurring managed-compliance service that keeps small defense
subcontractors continuously compliant with the NIST SP 800-171 security
requirements their prime contractors flow down to them — and keeps their SPRS
(Supplier Performance Risk System) score current and defensible — so they can
keep winning and holding DoD-related purchase orders.

## The customer
Small and mid-size manufacturers and service firms (roughly 10–200 employees)
that sit in the defense supply chain as second- and third-tier suppliers to
larger primes (e.g., machine shops, specialty fabricators, electronics
assemblers, engineering services). They handle Controlled Unclassified
Information (CUI) or Federal Contract Information (FCI) and are contractually
obligated under DFARS 252.204-7012 / 7019 / 7020 to implement 800-171 controls,
post and maintain an SPRS score, and respond to prime supplier-security
questionnaires. They typically have no internal security or compliance staff —
the owner, an office manager, or a part-time IT person currently "owns" this.

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- A prime will not issue or renew a purchase order to a supplier whose SPRS
  score is missing, stale, or too low. The requirement is contractual and
  deadline-driven, and it recurs every time a contract is renewed or a new prime
  onboards the supplier.
- 800-171 has 110 controls; maintaining them is continuous (policies, evidence,
  plan-of-action milestones, annual reassessment, incident reporting), not a
  one-time project.
- Getting it wrong carries False Claims Act exposure (DOJ has pursued
  cybersecurity-attestation cases), loss of contracts, and failed prime audits.
- The buyer has a real budget line for this because losing a prime relationship
  is an existential revenue event.

## The product
A fixed-scope, published-price recurring service with one delivery spine:
1. Onboarding assessment against the 110 controls, gap list, and a remediation
   plan written for non-experts.
2. A lightweight evidence/system-of-record portal where the supplier's policies,
   evidence artifacts, POA&M items, and SPRS inputs live and are kept current.
3. Quarterly managed cadence: control reviews, evidence refresh, SPRS score
   recalculation and re-posting, and turnkey responses to prime
   supplier-security questionnaires as they arrive.
4. Audit-/assessment-support when a prime or a third-party assessor comes
   knocking.
The software does the high-volume, repeatable work (control tracking, evidence
reminders, questionnaire auto-fill, reporting); humans do the judgment and the
prime-facing reassurance.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription. Indicative pricing: a one-time onboarding/assessment fee
in the low-to-mid four figures, then an annual managed-service subscription in
the mid four to low five figures per supplier per year depending on size and CUI
scope, billed annually or quarterly in advance. Revenue is >90% recurring after
onboarding; retention is structurally high because the obligation never ends and
switching mid-cycle risks the SPRS posture.

## Positioning
"We keep your defense contracts safe. Stay prime-ready and audit-ready on
800-171 and SPRS — done for you, every quarter, for a fixed price." Sold against
two stale alternatives: expensive hourly cybersecurity/GRC consultants who do a
one-time project and disappear, and generalist IT/MSP shops that treat security
paperwork as a side task and miss the defense-specific requirements.

## Go-to-market
Marketing- and positioning-led demand generation (content on DFARS/SPRS/CUI
deadlines, targeted outbound to suppliers named in prime supplier lists, presence
in defense-manufacturing associations and prime supplier-day events), with
founder-led closing. Primes themselves are a referral channel because they want
their suppliers compliant.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders with a compliance-operator background (hands-on
experience running formal security/compliance programs such as SOC 2 cycles) and
a track record selling into regulated enterprises, supported by a named
800-171/CMMC practitioner advisor for deep technical attestation judgment.
Remote-first, no field operations.
