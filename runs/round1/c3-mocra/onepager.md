# Concept One-Pager

## What it is
A productized, recurring managed-compliance service that keeps independent and
emerging cosmetics brands continuously compliant with the FDA's Modernization of
Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA) — handling facility registration, product
listing, safety-substantiation recordkeeping, responsible-person labeling
obligations, adverse-event recordkeeping, and the ongoing updates each of those
requires — so the brand can legally keep its products on shelf and in retail/
marketplace distribution.

## The customer
Independent, founder-led cosmetics and personal-care brands doing roughly
$1M–$50M in revenue: indie color-cosmetics and skincare lines, "clean beauty"
brands, contract-manufactured private-label lines, and fast-growing DTC/Amazon
beauty brands. There are thousands of these. They are run by founders and small
marketing teams; they outsource manufacturing to contract manufacturers but
remain the legally responsible party. They have no regulatory-affairs staff and
were, until MoCRA, almost entirely unregulated at the federal level — so the new
obligations landed on people with zero compliance muscle.

## The pain (why it's a must-have)
- MoCRA (enacted Dec 2022, core obligations phased in through 2024–2025) for the
  first time requires cosmetics facilities to register with FDA, requires every
  marketed cosmetic product to be listed with FDA, and requires the "responsible
  person" to maintain adequate substantiation of safety and to keep adverse-event
  records — with FDA now holding mandatory recall authority and the ability to
  suspend a facility registration.
- Retailers and marketplaces (Sephora, Ulta, Target, Amazon) increasingly demand
  proof of MoCRA compliance as a condition of shelf space and listing — turning a
  federal rule into a commercial gating requirement.
- Registrations and listings must be kept current (new products, reformulations,
  facility changes, biennial renewals), so it recurs; it is not a one-time filing.
- The penalty for getting it wrong is losing distribution and risking FDA
  enforcement — existential for a brand whose whole value is being on shelf.

## The product
A fixed-scope, published-price recurring service on one delivery spine:
1. Onboarding: register the brand's facilities (and coordinate with its contract
   manufacturers), list all current products with FDA, and assemble the
   safety-substantiation and labeling file.
2. A compliance system-of-record portal holding the brand's facility
   registrations, product list, ingredient/formulation records,
   safety-substantiation documents, labeling reviews, and adverse-event log.
3. Recurring managed cadence: list new and reformulated products, maintain
   registrations and biennial renewals, review labels against MoCRA/FDA labeling
   requirements, monitor for fragrance-allergen and other rule updates, and keep
   the adverse-event recordkeeping current.
4. Retail/marketplace support: produce the compliance documentation package
   retailers and Amazon request as a condition of distribution.
Software does the registration/listing workflow, renewal tracking, label-review
checklists, and document storage; humans handle judgment (safety substantiation
adequacy, label interpretation) and brand-facing guidance. No lab testing — the
brand's contract manufacturer and testing labs produce the underlying data.

## Revenue model
Recurring subscription per brand, tiered by SKU count and number of facilities.
Indicative pricing: a one-time onboarding fee in the mid four figures (register
facilities, list the existing catalog, build the file), then an annual
subscription in the mid four to low five figures per brand per year, with
per-SKU add-ons for high-catalog brands. Billed annually in advance. Highly
recurring: the catalog keeps changing, renewals recur, and the system-of-record
creates switching cost.

## Positioning
"Stay on shelf. We keep your cosmetics brand fully MoCRA-compliant — every
registration, listing, label, and safety file current — so a retailer audit or
an FDA letter never pulls your products. One predictable annual fee." Sold
against reactive FDA/regulatory attorneys who bill hourly only when something
breaks, generalist contract manufacturers who "handle some of it" inconsistently,
and brands' own overwhelmed founders doing it themselves in spreadsheets.

## Go-to-market
Marketing/positioning-led demand generation into the beauty-founder community
(content on MoCRA deadlines and retailer requirements, presence at beauty-industry
and indie-brand trade shows like Cosmoprof and Indie Beauty events, partnerships
with contract manufacturers and beauty-incubators/accelerators that want their
brands compliant, targeted outbound to brands newly hitting retail), with
founder-led close. Contract manufacturers and retailer-onboarding teams are
referral channels.

## Who runs it
Two generalist founders strong in marketing/positioning, software, and running
formal compliance programs, supported by a named cosmetics-regulatory practitioner
advisor (a cosmetic chemist or FDA cosmetics-regulatory specialist) for safety/
labeling judgment. Remote-first, no field or lab operations.
