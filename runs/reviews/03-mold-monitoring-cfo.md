CFO REVIEW — White-Label Moisture & Mold Monitoring for Restoration Firms

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The recurring-revenue spine is real and the per-home subscription model is genuinely attractive, but the hardware reality wrecks the bootstrap economics: this is a connected-hardware business with cellular/wifi sensors, certification, firmware, and per-device replacement that "low-cost wireless sensors" papers over, and the founders are selling through contractors who must do the install and absorb churn. The $2M founder-income math is reachable on paper (roughly 6,500–9,000 monitored homes at a $25–$30 net-to-us monthly margin), but getting there in 24 months requires both signing dozens of contractors AND those contractors actually pushing installs into homes — a two-stage adoption funnel where you control neither the install nor the homeowner relationship. The model can work as a durable lifestyle business, but the 24-month timeline and the hardware capital are where it bends.

Homes needed: at $22 net, $2.5M ARR / ($22×12) ≈ 9,470 homes; at $30 net ≈ 6,945. Order of magnitude 7,000–9,500 active monitored homes.

At [ASSUMED] 40 homes/contractor avg, 7,000–9,500 homes ≈ 175–240 signed-and-active contractors. A LOT of channel partners to recruit and keep pushing in 24 months for two founders.

SCORES (1–10)
1. Startup capital efficiency: 4 — Connected-hardware: sensor inventory + firmware/certification + IoT cloud build means low-five-to-six figures before meaningful revenue, not the low-four-figure SaaS ideal; pass-through fees soften but don't eliminate the inventory cash cycle.
2. Working-capital profile: 5 — Subscription bills monthly (not annual-upfront), and hardware must be bought/shipped before reimbursement, so cash gets tied up in inventory float ahead of collection.
3. Cash flow self-funding: 5 — Each new home requires hardware cash out front; growth consumes inventory cash, partly offset if activation fees cover sensor cost, but monthly billing means slow recovery.
4. Unit economics quality: 6 — At [ASSUMED] $22/home/mo, ~70% margin, ~2-month hardware payback, LTV/CAC healthy IF homes stay 24+ months, but CAC = contractor-acquisition cost spread thinly and is unproven.
5. Gross margin: 6 — Estimated 55–80% (midpoint ~70%) after cellular data, cloud, support; respectable but capped below pure-SaaS by connectivity/device support, and compresses at the $10 price floor.
6. Path to $2M in 24mo: 4 — Requires ~7,000–9,500 active homes via ~175–240 active contractors in 24 months — a two-stage funnel (sign contractor, then contractor installs) the founders only half-control; achievable economics, heroic adoption velocity.
7. Realistic obtainable market: 7 — Tens of thousands of U.S. restoration/waterproofing contractors (IICRC certifies a large installer base; SERVPRO/Roto-Rooter number thousands of locations) × hundreds of homes each = a multi-hundred-thousand-home pool, far larger than the ~9,000 needed.
8. Financial risk concentration: 5 — Per-home revenue is diversified, but you depend on a modest number of contractor channel partners; losing a franchise group or a few top contractors removes a large slice, and the homeowner relationship is owned by the contractor.
9. Scaling economics: 6 — Software/cloud margins improve with scale, but per-home hardware, connectivity, and field-support costs scale roughly linearly and replacement/RMA grows with the install base.
10. Financeability / exit optionality: 7 — Recurring per-home subscription with a contractor channel is a clean transferable asset for a PropTech/insurtech or restoration-franchise acquirer IF net revenue retention is demonstrated.

AVERAGE SCORE: 5.5 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Genuine recurring, per-home, seat-like revenue that expands automatically as each contractor adds new jobs.
- Large serviceable market: tens of thousands of contractors each owning hundreds of past homeowners; ~9,000 homes is a small fraction.
- Healthy steady-state unit economics (~$22/home/mo at ~70% margin, ~2-month hardware payback) and a clean transferable asset.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Two-stage adoption funnel: founders must recruit contractors AND get them to actually install; you control neither the install nor the homeowner relationship.
- Hardware capital and inventory cash cycle: ~$1M+ of sensors in the field at scale, bought/shipped before reimbursement, plus firmware/certification/RMA.
- 24-month timeline mismatch: ~175–240 active contractors averaging ~40 homes each is a steep channel build for two founders.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely killer is adoption velocity through a channel the founders do not control. This is a B2B2C model where the contractor is simultaneously buyer, installer, brand, and owner of the homeowner relationship — which sounds like leverage but is dependence. Revenue depends on signing enough contractors and each contractor diligently attaching monitoring to enough homes, pricing it, handling the install, and not churning. A contractor whose core business is lumpy emergency project work has every incentive to treat a $20/month recurring add-on as an afterthought. If average homes-per-contractor lands at 10 instead of 40, the founders need ~700–950 contractors instead of ~200 — a recruitment burden that does not close in 24 months for two people. The economics per home are fine; the business dies if the contractors don't push, and the white-label design that wins the contractor's trust also strips the founders of any direct demand-generation lever.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the realistic attach rate — of a contractor's next 50 completed jobs, how many go onto monitoring, and what does the contractor charge the homeowner vs pay you? (Sets whether you need 200 or 800 contractors.)
- What is the all-in landed hardware cost per home, and who funds that cash outlay at install — contractor, homeowner, or you?
- What is monthly home-level churn and contractor-level churn, and what happens to stranded hardware/revenue when a contractor drops the program?
- What connectivity model — homeowner wifi (free but unreliable/support-heavy) or cellular ($1–$4/device/mo margin drag) — and how does it change gross margin at 1,000 vs 9,000 devices?
- How long to recruit, onboard, and get the first install live with a new contractor, and how many can two founders activate per month?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
