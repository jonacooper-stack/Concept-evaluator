COO REVIEW — Independent Claims Adjuster Workspace

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
This is a mobile + web SaaS with essentially no physical delivery, no field labor, no inventory, and no install crew — the operational profile that two founders can actually run lean. The "unit of work" delivered is software access plus light onboarding and support, not a truck rolling to a site, which removes the entire class of logistics, routing, weather, and headcount-scaling problems that kill most field-ops concepts. The real operational risks here are software-shaped: the carrier-format mapping engine that has to stay accurate as carriers change requirements, a support load that spikes hard during catastrophe (CAT) season exactly when adjusters are most stressed and least patient, and cloud sync reliability when a user is uploading 200 photos from a hotel parking lot on a weak cell signal. None of these require more hands so much as more engineering hardening and a disciplined support process — which is where the founder skill gap (engineering ~3-4/10) lands heaviest.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Picture the 12-month mark. March is shoulder season into early storm season, so volume is real but not yet peak CAT. Say the company has roughly 600 paying seats [ASSUMED: see capacity math] across solo adjusters and a handful of small IA firms. On this Tuesday the two founders are not delivering anything per-customer physically — zero installs, zero site visits, zero shipments. The work is: (1) Support tickets. An adjuster in Oklahoma is on a roof, the app is choking on a 180-photo upload because cell signal is bad, and he is furious because he is paid per claim and this is costing him daylight. He emails support and also posts in the Facebook adjuster group the founders monitor. (2) A firm owner with 12 adjusters wants three new seats added and a quick training call for a new hire — a 20-minute Zoom and a billing change. (3) Two trial users from a CE webinar last week need onboarding nudges or they churn before assembling a file. (4) A carrier quietly changed its photo-report cover-sheet requirement, three adjusters noticed their files looked slightly off, and now the founders must update the "carrier-ready" template before word spreads that the tool produces stale files. Mike runs the community, onboarding calls, content, and firm relationships; Jon runs product fixes, template/format updates, and billing/ops tooling. The thing that breaks first is upload-sync reliability under bad connectivity; the thing that breaks slowest but most dangerously is template drift — a "carrier-ready" promise that silently goes stale. With 600 seats a realistic ticket volume is manageable by two people, but by peak CAT season they will need a part-time support contractor.

CAPACITY MATH (showing the work)
Target: $2M to each founder ≈ ~$4M distributable. For a bootstrapped 2-person SaaS, gross margins are high (~80%+), but founders also reinvest; sizing to throw off ~$4M to founders realistically means ARR ~$5M–$6M after costs. I size to ~$5M ARR.
- Blended price: tiers $75-$200/seat/mo solo, "few hundred to low-thousands" firms. Take blended ~$130/seat/mo = $1,560/seat/yr [ASSUMED; verify with pricing test on 50 adjusters].
- Seats for $5M ARR: $5,000,000 ÷ $1,560 ≈ 3,200 seats.
- Pool plausibility: if active pool is ~30,000-50,000 working IAs [ASSUMED; verify vs NAIC/state license counts + IA firm rosters], 3,200 seats is ~6-10% penetration. Ambitious but not absurd for a category-defining tool. Reaching it in 24 months is the aggressive part (CMO/CFO question more than COO).
Support/throughput at 3,200 seats:
- Low-touch SaaS support: ~0.3-0.5 tickets/seat/month [ASSUMED prosumer SaaS range]. At 3,200 × 0.4 = ~1,280 tickets/month ≈ ~64/business day.
- At ~12 min average handle time: 64 × 12 = ~13 support-hours/day ≈ ~1.6 FTE of pure support.
- CAT-season spike: 2-3x for several weeks; 64 × 2.5 = ~160 tickets/day ≈ ~32 support-hours/day ≈ ~4 FTE for a 2-4 week burst.
Conclusion: steady-state support needs ~1.5-2 dedicated support people; peak CAT ~4. Two founders cannot personally carry steady-state support at $5M ARR while doing product, format maintenance, sales, and community — they need ~2 support hires + seasonal contractors. Still a very lean org (founders + ~2-3 support/CS + eventually 1 real engineer), well within "two founders + small team." Bottleneck is not headcount-prohibitive; it is the seasonality spike and the need for a real engineer to harden sync.
Onboarding: ~15-20 min/seat, ~150 seats/month at full ramp = ~40-50 hours/month — one CS person's part-time load, largely deferrable to self-serve.

5. Vendor/partner dependency risk: 7 — No single platform owns the business; cloud and STT replaceable. Mild exposure to carrier portal/estimating export formats founders don't control, but the product avoids replacing the estimating engine, limiting that bet.
6. Hiring feasibility: 7 — Needed hires are common-market: remote SaaS support/CS reps (abundant, ~$45-60k or seasonal contractors) and eventually one experienced engineer to harden sync — the engineer is the only tight-market hire; founders cite strong recruiting networks.

9. Geographic / seasonality risk: 4 — The genuine operational wart. Demand and load are sharply tied to CAT/storm season: revenue, usage, support, and churn risk all peak together and trough in quiet months. National coverage helps, but the business is structurally weather-and-season-bound.
10. Tooling maturity available: 7 — Most stack off-the-shelf (cloud storage, sync frameworks, STT, Stripe, support desk, CRM). Custom build is the capture/organization app and the carrier-format template engine — buildable but the format engine is genuinely custom and must be maintained.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.0 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- No physical logistics whatsoever: zero installs/trucks/routing/crew — software delivered identically to all 50 states at near-zero marginal effort (true 10 on logistics).
- Lean, common-market staffing: at ~3,200 seats / ~$5M ARR the steady-state need is ~1.5-2 remote support FTE plus founders — no specialized labor pool, seasonal spikes coverable by contractors.
- Replaceable, cheap supply chain: cloud + STT + billing are multi-sourced commodities with no MOQs/deposits/FX — no single vendor can hold the business hostage.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Severe seasonality: usage, revenue, support load, and churn risk all peak together during CAT season and trough off-season, forcing flex-staffing and lumpy operations the founders dislike running.
- Carrier-format maintenance treadmill: "carrier-ready, no-bounce-back" must stay true as multiple carriers quietly change requirements — a perpetual QC obligation that, if it slips, silently breaks the core value prop.
- Sync/upload reliability under bad connectivity: the on-roof, weak-signal, 200-photo upload is the operational moment that makes or breaks daily trust; hardening it needs a real engineer the founders don't yet have.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The operational issue most likely to hurt this is the carrier-format maintenance treadmill colliding with the seasonality spike. The entire wedge is "court-ready and carrier-ready before you leave the site" — a promise the assembled file will not bounce back. But carriers are many, each with idiosyncratic and shifting requirements, and they change those requirements without notifying a third-party vendor. That means the founders are signing up for an open-ended, never-finished QC obligation: every format the product claims to support is a liability that can silently go stale and produce a non-compliant file that costs the adjuster real money and destroys the exact trust the product is sold on. This is not a headcount problem (two founders plus a couple support reps can run day-to-day); it is a quality-and-domain-maintenance problem that gets worst precisely during CAT season, when file volume is highest, adjusters most stressed, and the cost of a bounced file most acute. I'm reasonably confident two people can operate support and onboarding; I'm less confident two people — one the only product builder, prototype-grade — can keep dozens of carrier-format outputs verifiably current while also shipping a reliable sync engine. If they instead position the output as a strong, editable draft rather than a guaranteed compliant file, this risk shrinks dramatically — but it softens the marketing promise.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- How many distinct carrier formats must you support to be credible at launch, who maintains them, and how do you learn within days that a carrier changed a requirement (rather than when a file bounces)? Is "carrier-ready" a guarantee or an explicitly-labeled draft?
- What is the realistic upload/sync failure rate on weak cellular today, and do you have an engineer capable of hardening offline-first capture and resumable large-batch uploads — or is that the hire you concede you'll need, and when?
- What is your CAT-season ops plan: how do you staff a 2.5-4x support spike for a few weeks (seasonal contractors? on-call founders?), and what happens to churn in quiet months when adjusters aren't deployed and may pause/cancel?
- What is the real blended price and seat count you're underwriting — is ~3,200 paying seats (~6-10% of active U.S. IAs) realistic in 24 months, validated with a paid pilot rather than an assumed number?
