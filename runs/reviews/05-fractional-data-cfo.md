CFO REVIEW — Fractional Data & Analytics Leader for Mid-Market Companies

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The unit economics of a fractional advisory retainer are genuinely excellent — near-zero startup capital, customer pays monthly with no inventory or AR float, and 70-85% gross margins on founder-delivered work. The fatal problem is not margin, it is the capacity ceiling. This is a personal-services business where the two founders' own senior judgment IS the product, and a $2M-each ($4M total) income target requires either an impossible number of high-touch retainers per founder or a leveraged delivery model that contradicts the entire "trusted senior advisor owns the numbers" value proposition. The math gets to a comfortable $500K-$1M each as a lifestyle practice; it does not credibly get to $2M each in 24 months without becoming a different, lower-margin, harder-to-staff company.

THE MATH
Target: ~$4M combined distributable founder income/year within 24 months.
ARPU: $5K-$12K/month; blended midpoint $8K/mo = $96K/year/client [ASSUMED; verify with 5 scoped proposals].
Step 1 — Revenue needed: net margin to founders ~55-65% after tooling/contract analysts/overhead [ASSUMED]. To distribute $4M: $4M/0.60 ≈ $6.7M/year revenue.
Step 2 — Client count: $6.7M/$96K ≈ 70 active retainers.
Step 3 — Can two founders carry 70? A single senior advisor can personally own ~8-12 such relationships before the "trusted owner of the numbers" promise degrades [ASSUMED 8-12; verify by tracking hours/account]. Two founders = ~16-24 retainers personally anchored. At $96K each = $1.5M-$2.3M revenue → ~$0.9M-$1.4M combined founder income ($450K-$700K each). A great lifestyle practice, ~1/3 of the goal.
Step 4 — Leverage gap: reaching 70 retainers requires offloading delivery to contract/offshore analysts with founders supervising. But the dashboards/pipelines are the commodity layer; the senior judgment is what the CEO buys. Founders spread across 35 accounts each dilutes the relationship promise, raises churn, and rebuilds the exact "BI project that delivers a dashboard and disappears" the positioning attacks.
Step 5 — Realistic path: 20 retainers × $96K = $1.92M × 60% ≈ $1.15M combined ≈ $575K each in 24 months — credible. The $2M-each target is ~3.5x that and needs a delivery-leverage step-change this model doesn't naturally provide.
Working capital: trivially positive — monthly retainer in advance + setup fee, near-zero delivery-cash outlay beyond contractor wages. A genuine strength.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.4 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Near-zero startup capital and negative working capital: billed monthly in advance + setup fee, off-the-shelf tooling, no inventory — cash-flow positive from the first client.
- High gross margin on founder-delivered work (~80%) with sticky, indefinitely-renewing recurring revenue once dashboards are embedded in the monthly business review.
- Market depth is not the limiter: 200K+ U.S. mid-market firms means demand vastly exceeds what two founders can serve.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Capacity-ceiling cap on income: the product is the founders' own senior hours, so combined income realistically tops out ~$1.1M-$1.4M ($550K-$700K each), ~1/3 of target, in 24 months.
- Anti-leverage scaling: the only way to break the ceiling (push delivery to contract analysts) dilutes the senior-advisor relationship that is the entire differentiator.
- Low transfer/exit value: a two-person personality-dependent advisory book trades at a low services multiple and is hard to pass to children or sell.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single financial issue most likely to defeat this against its stated goal is the irreducible coupling of revenue to scarce senior-judgment hours. Every dollar of high-margin revenue is purchased with a unit of the founders' personal attention, and there are only ~3,000-4,000 productive senior hours across the two of them per year. The value proposition is explicitly that a trusted senior person OWNS the numbers and walks leadership through them monthly — that promise is the moat and the pricing power, and it is precisely the part that cannot be delegated to a $30/hour offshore analyst. So the business faces a structural fork: stay high-touch and high-margin but capped at ~$0.6M each, or scale headcount/leverage and watch both differentiation and per-account margin erode toward commodity BI-shop economics (where Analytics8, 2nd Watch, or any regional BI consultancy already compete on price). Neither fork reaches $2M each in 24 months. The numbers produce an excellent lifestyle business and a poor match for the specific income target.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- How many high-touch retainers can ONE founder personally anchor at promised quality? If under 12, the $2M-each target is mechanically unreachable without a leveraged delivery model — what is that model, and what does it do to margin and churn?
- What is the real blended net margin after a fully-staffed account (contract analysts, tooling, supervision)? Build the P&L for one $10K/month account at full delivery load — does it hold 55%+?
- What is the actual CAC and sales-cycle length to a CEO/COO/CFO buyer, and how many founder-hours per close — does that cannibalize the delivery hours the same founders owe existing clients?
- How concentrated is the early book, and what is observed churn when a sponsoring CEO leaves or a recession triggers cost cuts?
- Is there a productized layer (repeatable dashboard/data-foundation product, per-seat subscription) that could carry revenue independent of founder hours — and if so, why isn't THAT the business?
