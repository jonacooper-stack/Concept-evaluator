CFO REVIEW — Independent Claims Adjuster Workspace

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
This is a textbook bootstrap-friendly vertical SaaS shape: high gross margin, recurring seat revenue, near-zero working capital, and a buyer who personally controls budget and feels per-transaction pain. The numbers can pencil to a lifestyle-grade business, but the $2M-combined-founder-income-in-24-months target is the hard part — it requires roughly 1,500–2,000 paying seats at ~$120/mo average, which against a U.S. base of perhaps 30,000–40,000 active independent adjusters is a real penetration ask (4–6% of the entire licensed universe inside two years) for a content-and-community GTM with no inside sales muscle. The economics are good; the velocity needed to hit $2M net in 24 months is where the fragility lives, and seasonal/CAT-driven churn is the second crack.

THE MATH
Target: ~$2M combined distributable founder income/year within 24 months.
Step 1 — Revenue needed. A two-person bootstrapped SaaS with high gross margin and a lean team can plausibly distribute 45–60% of revenue as founder income once at scale [ASSUMED 40–60%; verify vs actual hosting + 1–2 support/eng hires]. To net $2M distributable, gross revenue ~$3.3M–$4.4M ARR. Working figure ~$3.5M ARR.
Step 2 — ARPU. Stated $75–$200/seat/mo solo, firm plans a few hundred to low-thousands/mo. Blended realized after discounting and solo weighting [ASSUMED ~$110–$130/seat/mo → ~$1,440/seat/yr; verify with a 50-customer pricing test]. Use $120/mo = $1,440/yr.
Step 3 — Seats required. $3.5M ÷ $1,440 ≈ 2,430 seats. At heavier firm mix (~$1,800/seat/yr) ≈ 1,950 seats. Honest range ~1,950–2,430 paying seats inside 24 months.
Step 4 — Market reality. Actively-working independent (non-staff) adjusters [ASSUMED 30,000–45,000; verify via state rosters + IA firm directories]. ~2,000+ seats = ~5–7% of the entire active universe paying within 24 months — achievable for a loved tool, not a layup for a two-founder content motion with no field sales.
Step 5 — CAC and payback. Community/content CAC [ASSUMED $150–$500/seat; verify with $5–10k pilot]. At $1,440/yr and ~80% GM (~$1,150 gross profit/yr), payback <6 months even at high CAC. LTV at 80% margin, [ASSUMED] 3-yr life ≈ $3,450; LTV/CAC ≈ 7–23x. Unit economics strong; constraint is volume and retention, not per-unit profit.
Verdict: revenue model supports a strong lifestyle business comfortably; $2M-in-24-months reachable only at the optimistic end of penetration and ARPU simultaneously.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Near-perfect bootstrap capital and working-capital profile: prototype-able by founders, no inventory/deposits, customer pays at/before cost — growth self-funds on annual billing.
- Strong unit economics: ~80%+ gross margin, sub-6-month CAC payback (LTV/CAC ~7–23x on shown math).
- Buyer owns the budget and feels chronic per-claim pain — satisfies the "buyer has budget and authority" green flag, avoiding the HR-benefits no-authority trap.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Timeline-to-$2M: ~2,000+ paid seats (5–7% of the active universe) in 24 months via content/community with no inside-sales is the optimistic case, not the base case.
- Seasonal/CAT churn: solo adjusters' income is storm-dependent; a quiet season triggers pause/cancel that craters net revenue retention.
- Bounded market ceiling: niche caps total ARR; clears "lifestyle" but scale-into-large optionality depends on unproven adjacent expansion.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The single most likely financial killer is the collision of the 24-month deadline with the go-to-market motion. Unit economics are fine and the product is buildable cheaply — but $2M of combined founder income demands ~$3.5M ARR = ~1,950–2,430 seats = 5–7% of the entire active independent-adjuster population, acquired through community/content/word-of-mouth with no described sales team. The founder profile flags sales/closing as 6–7/10 "not where their energy sits," and Jon as chronically over-optimistic on growth speed. Vertical-SaaS land-grabs of a fragmented, hard-to-reach, on-the-road buyer typically take 30–48 months to reach that seat count, not 24 — especially because the buyer is living out of a truck during the season they most need the tool and least have time to evaluate software. If penetration lands at a still-excellent 2–3% in 24 months (~600–900 seats, ~$1M ARR), that is a wonderful business but only ~$500–600k combined founder income — half the target — at the 24-month mark. The business probably succeeds; it likely just misses the specific deadline.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- What is the actual ACTIVELY-WORKING independent (non-staff) adjuster population from state licensing rosters and IA-firm directories — not the headline "tens of thousands" license count? The whole case hinges on whether the denominator is 30k or 80k.
- What is realized blended ARPU and annual-vs-monthly mix after a real 50-customer pricing test? At $120/mo you need 2,400 seats; at $200/mo you need 1,460 — that swing is plausible vs heroic.
- What does net revenue retention look like across a full off-season — what fraction of solo adjusters pause/cancel between deployments, and does the per-claim add-on offset it?
- With no inside-sales team and an on-the-road buyer, what is demonstrated CAC and seats/month from a $5–10k pilot — and does that, extrapolated, reach ~2,000 seats in 24 months?
- Is there a credible adjacent expansion (home inspectors, restoration/water-mitigation, public adjusters) that lifts the ceiling, or is this capped at the IA niche?
