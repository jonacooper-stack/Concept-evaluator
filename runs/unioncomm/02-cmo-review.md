CMO REVIEW — Member Engagement & Dues-Collection Overlay for Labor Unions

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ

This is the best kind of market-pull problem: a Supreme Court decision (Janus v. AFSCME, June 2018) turned dues collection from an automatic payroll process into an affirmative, member-by-member fight that unions are losing — SEIU lost roughly 210,000 agency-fee payers in the immediate aftermath, and public-sector unions have spent the years since scrambling to re-sign members and capture personal contact data they never had. That is a painkiller with a revenue gun pointed at the buyer's head, not a vitamin. The trouble is the GTM: there are only a few thousand union bodies in the country, the economic buyer is a slow, politically-charged, consensus-driven institution, and the "free tier rolls downhill" land-and-expand thesis assumes national leadership will mandate a tool across autonomous locals — which is exactly the kind of top-down command that union federalism resists. The wedge is real and the pain is genuine; the question is whether two outsiders with no labor-movement relationships can get past the gatekeepers fast enough, and whether a transaction take-rate on dues survives contact with union finance committees.

POSITIONING DRAFT

"The fastest way for your union to re-sign members and collect dues directly — text, renew, and get paid in days, without ripping out the database you already run." That is a clean, specific, edges-having sentence aimed at a buyer (a union political/membership director post-Janus) who knows exactly what re-signing and direct dues collection mean. It does NOT sound like generic SaaS mush because it names the precise post-Janus job-to-be-done. The one weakness: "engagement platform" language, if the founders default to it, would instantly turn this into undifferentiated nonprofit-CRM soup (think EveryAction / Bonterra). The positioning lives or dies on staying nailed to the dues-collection painkiller, not the engagement vitamin.

THE WEDGE

The first 100 "customers" here are not 100 logos — they are a handful of national or large state affiliates, each of which then rolls down to dozens or hundreds of locals. Realistically the wedge is a single beachhead affiliate. The cheapest, most specific entry vectors: (1) the labor-tech and union-organizing conference circuit — the AFL-CIO Tech Institute, the Labor Notes conference (~2,000+ attendees, registration low hundreds of dollars), and state-federation conventions — where you can directly reach membership and political directors; (2) warm-intro founder-led sales through labor consultants, progressive digital agencies (e.g., Blue State, Trilogy Interactive), and the post-Janus "re-commitment campaign" vendors who already sit in these rooms; (3) targeted outbound to the ~50 largest public-sector affiliates whose dues-loss exposure is public and quantifiable. A single mid-size state affiliate (say 20,000–60,000 members) that mandates the tool downhill could seed hundreds of local "deployments" off one sale. CAC in absolute dollars is low (conference + outbound + founder time), but the sales CYCLE is the real cost: [ASSUMED 6–14 months per affiliate; verify by asking three union digital directors how long their last vendor procurement took]. The wedge exists, but it is narrow and gated by relationships the founders do not yet have.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE

- UnionWare (by NGP VAN / Bonterra lineage): the legacy database-of-record incumbent — strong at membership records, dues history, grievance/compliance tracking. Desktop-heavy, slow, not built for real-time SMS or modern digital money movement. Sleepy and beatable on engagement/collections, but it OWNS the system of record, which is the integration surface this concept depends on.
- UNICAMP / Union Link / ULA-style systems: similar old-line membership/records vendors. Stale, poorly marketed — exactly the kind of incumbent the founders like to out-position.
- ActionNetwork, EveryAction / Bonterra, NationBuilder: progressive/nonprofit engagement and email/SMS CRMs widely used by unions for advocacy blasts. These are the real competitive air problem on the COMMUNICATION half of the product — many locals already pay for these and they are not sleepy.
- Stripe / dues-collection point solutions and labor-specific payment processors (e.g., affiliates rolling their own via Stripe, or vendors like Spruce/various dues-checkoff replacements): the dues-collection take-rate competes with plain Stripe at ~2.9% that unions can adopt without a vendor.
- The post-Janus "re-commitment" campaign specialists and digital agencies: not software, but they own the budget and the relationship for "get members re-signed."

Where is the air? In the SEAM between the records incumbents (UnionWare et al., who don't do money or real-time comms) and the comms CRMs (ActionNetwork et al., who don't do dues collection or sit on the database of record). The genuine wedge is "the layer that does direct dues collection AND re-sign capture AND tiered comms, on top of the legacy DB." That seam is real. But notice: the moment this works, ActionNetwork/Bonterra can bolt on dues-collection (they already do donation processing), and UnionWare can add a modern web layer. The differentiation is a feature configuration, not a structural moat — copyable in well under a year by players who already own the customer.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.0 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS

- A genuine, dated, existential painkiller: Janus v. AFSCME (2018) plus state legislation turned dues collection into an affirmative member-by-member fight unions are demonstrably losing — this is budgeted, urgent, and bought without having to convince the buyer the problem exists.
- A real competitive seam between sleepy records incumbents (UnionWare, Union Link) and comms-only CRMs (ActionNetwork, Bonterra) that neither side currently fills with integrated re-sign + direct dues collection.
- A compounding, referenceable story inside a small, tight-knit, gossip-rich community where one credible "we recovered $Y in dues for [affiliate]" case study propagates across affiliates faster than paid ads ever could.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS

- The defensibility is thin: the differentiator is a feature bundle, and the players who already own the customer's comms budget (Bonterra/ActionNetwork, which already process donations) can extend into dues collection within a year.
- The buyer is a slow, political, consensus-driven institution, and the "national mandates it downhill to autonomous locals" thesis fights union federalism — locals guard their autonomy and budgets, so the free-tier rollout may stall at the top.
- The founders show no evidence of labor-movement relationships or actual customer conversations; in a relationship-gated market of only a few thousand bodies, outsider status is a hard cold-start.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK

The single most likely killer is the take-rate on dues meeting union finance reality. Unions are intensely cost-sensitive, ideologically wary of "skimming" member dues, and run by elected officers accountable to members who will notice a percentage of their paycheck going to a private software vendor. A payment-processing take-rate on every dollar of dues collected — on top of the ~2.9% the card networks already take — invites the exact "why are we paying a Silicon Valley middleman to handle our members' money?" objection that gets a deal killed in a finance committee or a convention floor vote. Worse, the moment dues volume is meaningful, a large affiliate's CFO will run the math and realize they can drop in raw Stripe (or pressure you to a flat fee) and cut the take-rate entirely, collapsing the revenue model precisely on the accounts that matter most. The transaction-take-rate model is elegant for removing procurement friction, but it puts the most defensible, highest-margin revenue stream directly in the path of the most cost-conscious, most politically-scrutinized line item the buyer has. If the model can't survive that scrutiny, the business is left selling SMS markup into a market of a few thousand bodies — too thin for the income ladder.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE

- How many union membership/political directors have you actually interviewed, and what did they say about (a) their current dues-collection stopgap, (b) their willingness to pay a take-rate on dues, and (c) who in the org could mandate a tool downhill?
- Does union federalism actually let a national/state body force a tool onto autonomous locals, or is each local an independent buy? If it's the latter, your CAC and sales-cycle math change by an order of magnitude — what's the evidence?
- When a large affiliate's finance committee asks "why pay you a percentage of our members' dues instead of using Stripe directly," what is the answer, and does the business survive if they win that argument and force you to flat per-transaction or per-member pricing?
- What stops Bonterra/ActionNetwork — who already own the comms budget and already process donation payments — from adding re-sign + dues collection and bundling it into contracts unions already hold?
- Can you name the specific beachhead affiliate (or three) you will target first, and what your actual path-in is — a warm intro, a conference, a consultant relationship — not "outbound to the top 50"?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE

The underlying pain is one of the most legitimate must-haves I've seen — Janus made dues collection an existential, budgeted, urgent problem, and that alone earns this concept serious attention. But three things need to be resolved before it's a GO. First, prove the customer is known, not researched: book ten conversations with union membership directors and come back with what they actually said about paying a take-rate. Second, de-risk the revenue model — pressure-test whether a dues take-rate survives a finance committee, and have a fallback (flat per-member or per-transaction subscription tied to the re-sign workflow) that doesn't collapse on your biggest accounts. Third, sharpen defensibility from "feature bundle" toward "rail of record": the only durable moat here is becoming the system through which the money and the up-to-date member contact data flow, so that ripping you out means re-collecting consent and re-onboarding payment for tens of thousands of members. Nail the beachhead-affiliate-with-a-real-path-in, lock the positioning to dues collection (not "engagement"), and this moves toward GO. Left as a transaction-take-rate engagement overlay sold cold into a relationship-gated, politically-cautious market, it's too thin and too copyable.
