# Business Objectives & Evaluation Rubric

The source of truth. Every expert agent (CFO, CMO, COO, Legal, CTO) and the PM
synthesizer reads from this doc. Where any council doc still references an older
"$2M in 24 months" target, THIS doc's income ladder supersedes it.

## The business we're building

### Primary objective
A bootstrapped business built around a **market-pulled, must-have problem** —
something customers are already paying to solve, not a nice-to-have we have to
convince them to want. Strong, durable, recurring income for two versatile
founders, with optionality to grow into something larger and lasting.

### What "must-have" means (this is the spine of the thesis)
We are tired of selling vitamins. The problem must be a **painkiller**: urgent,
recurring, already budgeted, and bought without heavy internal selling. If the
customer has to be educated into believing they have the problem, it is the
wrong problem.

### Founder edge (how we expect to win)
The founders are **versatile, adaptive generalists** who are excellent at
marketing, positioning, and reaching and persuading people, and who can build
software. The expected winning move is to enter a market whose incumbents are
**stale, sleepy, old-school, and poorly marketed** (legacy consultancies,
generalist MSPs, old-line vendors) and out-position them.

A market **moat is fine — often desirable.** A regulatory, compliance, standard,
or expertise barrier that forces a recurring, budgeted buy AND keeps lazy
competitors out is a feature, not a bug, PROVIDED two things hold: (a) the
incumbents currently behind the moat are sleepy and beatable, and (b) the
expertise barrier is **bridgeable** by versatile founders via advisors, named
practitioners, partners, hires, or reasonable learning. What the founders avoid
is **high-performing, well-funded, strong-tech incumbents** — anywhere the only
winning move is to outspend or out-engineer them.

The founders are NOT a field-service crew and do not want a business whose core
is sustained manual/field operations.

### Archetype — what good looks like
The strongest concept evaluated to date is **managed CMMC Level 2 readiness for
small defense subcontractors**. Reproduce its shape:
- A **mandate/regulation/standard forces a recurring, deadline-driven, budgeted
  buy** — a must-have, not a vitamin.
- Delivered as a **productized, software-leveraged recurring managed service**
  (fixed scope, published pricing, one delivery spine; AI does top-of-funnel).
- **Marketing/positioning-led acquisition + founder-led close** — the founders' edge.
- A **fragmented segment the strong players ignore** (too small for the big
  consultancies, too complex for cheap tools), served only by sleepy incumbents.
- The **expertise moat is real but bridgeable** (named practitioner + advisors +
  adjacent founder background) — it keeps lazy competition out.
- **Capital-light, remote-first, NOT field ops.**

### Financial targets (a ladder, not a single cliff)
- By ~month 12: **~$300K+ in annual income to each founder.**
- By ~month 24: **~$500K+ to each founder, and growing from there.**
- **$2M/founder is upside/optionality, NOT a requirement.** Do not kill an
  otherwise-strong idea for failing to reach $2M in 24 months.
- **Recurring revenue is the spine** — subscription, contract, retainer, take-rate,
  or other repeating models, strongly preferred over one-time transactions.

### Capital posture
Bootstrapped, very low upfront capital, cash-flow positive fast, no large
pre-revenue outlay. No outside raise assumed.

### Competitive posture
No dominant, well-funded incumbent. No deep regulatory or SME/credential moat
protecting incumbents. Fragmented markets full of stale incumbents are IDEAL —
that is exactly where the founders' marketing edge wins.

### Probability of success
Favor high-probability, market-validated demand over moonshots.

## Hard constraints (any "no" = automatic disqualification)
1. Cannot be built with the founders' versatile skills + reasonable learning, OR
   its core requires sustained manual/field operations.
2. Requires more than modest upfront capital.
3. Has no plausible recurring revenue component.
4. No plausible path to even **~$300K/founder by ~24 months** at reasonable scale.
5. Faces **high-performing, well-funded, strong-tech incumbents** in the target
   segment, where the only winning move is to outspend or out-engineer them.
   (Stale/sleepy incumbents and regulatory/expertise moats are NOT disqualifying
   — often the opposite; see the archetype above.)
6. Requires specialized credentials/expertise the founders lack AND **cannot
   scaffold** via advisors, named practitioners, partners, hires, or reasonable
   learning. (A bridgeable expertise barrier is acceptable and often attractive.)
(Note: "must-have" is NOT a hard constraint — it is the most heavily scored
dimension below. A weak-pull idea is not disqualified, just scored low.)

## Evaluation rubric — 1–10 each, weighted to 100

| # | Dimension | Weight | A 10 | A 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Market pull / must-have** | 20 | Urgent, recurring, already-budgeted pain; customers actively buying; bought without internal selling | Nice-to-have; no budget line; customer must be convinced the problem exists |
| 2 | **Recurring revenue strength** | 15 | True subscription/contract; >90% repeating; high retention | One-time transactions; no repeat dynamic |
| 3 | **Competitive landscape** | 15 | Fragmented or regulation-forced market whose incumbents are stale, sleepy, poorly-marketed; any moat is bridgeable and keeps lazy entrants out | Owned/contested by high-performing, well-funded, strong-tech incumbents; winning requires outspending or out-engineering |
| 4 | **Path to founder income (ladder)** | 15 | Conservative math reaches ~$300K+/founder by mo 12 and ~$500K+ by mo 24, with room to grow | No credible path to ~$300K/founder even by mo 24 without heroics |
| 5 | **Probability of success** | 10 | Demand evidenced by existing market signals (current spend, analogous markets, named buyers); clear, reachable customer; low novelty risk | Speculative; requires market creation |
| 6 | **Capital efficiency** | 10 | Launchable for low four/five figures; cash-flow positive fast | Needs significant capital before first revenue |
| 7 | **Operational tractability** | 5 | Lean, remote-first, low-touch; NOT trucks/installs/field visits | Field-service/manual-labor-heavy or large ops team day one |
| 8 | **Scalability optionality** | 5 | Can stay lifestyle OR scale into something larger/passable | Inherently capped, no growth lever |
| 9 | **Skill fit (versatility)** | 5 | Squarely in strategy/finance/marketing/positioning/GTM/software; no field-ops or credential requirement | Needs sustained field ops, credentials, or expertise founders lack |

**Scoring discipline:** 5 = honestly mediocre, 7 = good, 9+ rare and earned.
Don't cluster in 6–8.

## Scorecard template
```
IDEA: <name>
ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION: <what it is>

HARD CONSTRAINT CHECK
- Buildable w/ founder skills, not field-ops core? [Y/N + note]
- Low capital required? [Y/N + note]
- Recurring revenue component? [Y/N + note]
- Plausible ~$300K/founder by ~24mo? [Y/N + note]
- Incumbents stale/sleepy (not strong-tech, well-funded)? [Y/N + note]
- No specialized credentials required? [Y/N + note]
> Any N: STOP. Disqualified.

DIMENSION SCORES (1–10)
1. Market pull / must-have:        [score] — [reason]
2. Recurring revenue strength:     [score] — [reason]
3. Competitive landscape:          [score] — [reason]
4. Path to founder income:         [score] — [reason]
5. Probability of success:         [score] — [reason]
6. Capital efficiency:             [score] — [reason]
7. Operational tractability:       [score] — [reason]
8. Scalability optionality:        [score] — [reason]
9. Skill fit (versatility):        [score] — [reason]

WEIGHTED TOTAL: [sum(score×weight)/10] / 100

TOP 3 STRENGTHS / TOP 3 RISKS / RECOMMENDATION
```

## Thresholds
- Weighted total >= 70 AND no dimension below 5 -> advance to full council.
- 55–69 -> refine and rescore.
- < 55 OR any dimension at 3 or below -> drop.
