# Briefing #8 — PermitPulse
*(Council average: ~6.5 / 10. Verdict: weakest of the eight — great pain, but the price is too low and the work too manual.)*

## The big idea, in plain words
A restaurant has to hold a stack of licenses and permits to stay open: a health permit, food-handler cards, a liquor license, fire/occupancy permit, sign permit, and more. Each one is issued by a different city, county, or state office, and each renews on its own date. If a key one lapses — say the health permit or liquor license — a location can be **shut down** or fined.

For a group that runs many restaurant locations, this becomes a huge web (locations × ~6–15 permits × different government offices × different renewal dates). This business tracks all of it and handles the renewals so no location ever gets shut down for a paperwork slip.

## Who buys it and why they're stuck
Small and mid-size restaurant groups and franchises (3–50 locations). They're stuck because a lapsed permit can close a money-making location, and the web of renewals is unmanageable by hand — especially since the person who "owned" the renewal calendar (a manager) often quits and takes the knowledge with them.

## How it makes money
- A **monthly fee per location**, plus a setup fee — roughly **$40 to $150 per location per month**, so a 20-location group might pay $1,500–$4,000 a month.
- **The problem:** that price is **low**. The founders would need **hundreds of locations** signed up to hit their income goal — a lot of selling.

## Who else is out there (the competition)
Pretty sleepy, which is good:
- **Local "permit expediters"** who do one-time jobs, not ongoing renewals.
- **Office managers with spreadsheets** — the real competitor.
- Some big business-license companies (Avalara, Harbor Compliance) exist, but they handle generic business licenses, **not** the restaurant-specific stack (health, liquor, fire). So no big rival focused on this niche.

## Strengths
- **Forced, recurring, "close-the-location" pain** — about as serious as it gets.
- **Sticky customers** (a lapse closes a location).
- **Cheap to start, runs from a laptop**, and a genuinely open niche.

## Weaknesses
- **The price is too low for the work.** At $40–$150 per location, you need a huge number of locations, and the work per location is real. The money math reaches the lower income goal **late** and struggles to reach the higher one.
- **A constantly-changing, never-finished rulebook.** There's no master list of every city's permit rules, fees, and forms — and they change all the time. Building and maintaining that is endless manual work, and a stale rule that misses a renewal causes the exact harm you sell against.
- **For the most important permits, you legally can't file for them.** Many places — especially for **liquor and health** permits — require the owner to sign or appear in person, so the service can only "prepare and remind," not truly "do it for you." That weakens the whole pitch right where it matters most.
- **Restaurant owners don't shop for this.** They only think about it when something breaks, so they're hard to reach cheaply.

This was the **weakest of the eight**. Four experts said "fix it first"; only the legal expert said "go" (because the founders themselves face few legal rules — but that's a narrow win). Its lowest single scores were **4s** (for the never-ending rulebook and the close-the-location blame risk). The report-card total (64) was the lowest of all.

**What the experts liked most:** Genuinely serious, forced pain and an open niche.

**What worried them most:** (1) The **low price vs. real work** (income goal reached late, if at all). (2) The **endless manual rulebook**. (3) That for the **biggest permits (liquor/health), you can't actually file** — you can only remind. (4) A buyer who only reacts after something breaks.

## How it fits our rules
| Our rule | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Must-have (forced) | 🟢 Strong | A lapse closes a location |
| Repeat money | 🟢 Strong | Always renewing |
| Sleepy rivals | 🟢 Strong | Expediters + spreadsheets |
| Reaches income goal | 🔴 Problem | Price too low; needs huge volume |
| Low startup money | 🟢 Strong | Laptop |
| Laptop, not field work | 🟢 Strong | Fully remote |
| Software-like profits | 🔴 Problem | Endless manual rulebook |
| Safe legal risk | 🟡 Mixed | Close-the-location blame |
| Buyers pay + easy to find | 🔴 Problem | Reactive, scattered buyers |
| Fits founders' strengths | 🟡 Mixed | Marketing fits |

**Bottom line:** The pain is real and the niche is open, but the low price, the never-ending manual rulebook, and the fact that you can't truly file the most important permits make this the weakest of the eight. Would need higher prices and a much narrower (one- or two-city) start to be workable.
