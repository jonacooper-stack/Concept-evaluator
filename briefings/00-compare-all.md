# The 8 Near-Miss Business Ideas — Side by Side

This file lets you compare all eight ideas at a glance. Each idea also has its own
2–3 page briefing in this folder. Plain-language version.

**How to read the scores:**
- Five experts each gave a score out of 10 (CFO = money, CMO = marketing, COO = running it day-to-day, Legal = legal/rules, CTO = the software/tech).
- "Average" is those five scores averaged together. To "pass," an idea needed an average of **8.0 or higher**, with **no single score below 7**, and a separate report-card total of **80 out of 100**.
- **None of the eight passed.** They are all "near-misses" — good ideas with one or two real problems that kept them under the bar. That is a normal, honest result.

## The big lesson (true for all 8)
Every idea had **great demand** — a real problem people are forced to pay to fix, run by sleepy old-fashioned competitors. That part is easy to find. What kept every idea under the bar was the **delivery side**: either the work is secretly a lot of manual labor (so profits look like a staffing agency, not software), or the legal blame if something slips is scary, or it's not yet proven that buyers will actually pay. The "winning" idea would need great demand **and** a clean, mostly-automatic, low-blame way to deliver it. None of the eight had all of that at once — which is exactly what the high bar is designed to catch.

## Best of the bunch
1. **ContractorPrevailingWage** (7.12) and **2. VendorTrust** (7.04) are the two strongest. Both have the cleanest "open lane" (no scary competitor) and a single cheap test that could push them over the bar: measure how much hand-work each customer really takes. If it's low, profits look like software and the scores jump.
