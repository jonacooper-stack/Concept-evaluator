CFO REVIEW — Managed ERISA Compliance & Filing Oversight for Small Retirement-Plan Sponsors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
Attractive on its bones: a federally mandated, penalty-backed, permanent annual obligation against a large fragmented installed base, sold as a recurring per-plan subscription with high gross margin and near-zero working-capital drag. The numbers to the founder-income ladder pencil with non-heroic assumptions — roughly 450–550 plans at a ~$3.6K–$4.8K blended annual contract gets two founders past the month-24 rung. The real fragility is not the model — it's customer acquisition cost and willingness-to-pay in a market where the sponsor often (incorrectly) believes the TPA already "has it covered," plus a specialist-labor cost line on exception-handling I want sized before I call the margin durable.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.9 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Negative working capital + upfront setup fees make every new plan cash-positive in month one — growth self-funds without external capital.
- Mandate-backed, permanent, penalty-driven obligation → structurally high retention and recurring revenue against a base hundreds-of-thousands of plans deep.
- Income ladder pencils on non-heroic assumptions: ~450–550 plans at ~$4K ACV clears the mo24 rung, a fraction of 1% of the serviceable market.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- CAC / willingness-to-pay unproven: the buyer frequently believes the TPA already covers compliance, so the founders may have to spend to educate the buyer that the gap exists — contradicting the "must-have, no internal selling" ideal and inflating CAC.
- Gross-margin erosion from the specialist-labor line: if exception-handling and notice distribution consume more ERISA-admin hours per plan than assumed, 75% GM slides toward the low-60s.
- Liability/E&O cost surprise: positioning as the sponsor's compliance-assurance layer invites a missed-deadline claim; E&O + the cost of one disputed remediation can compress net margin.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Willingness-to-pay colliding with perceived redundancy. The pain is genuine — but latent and intermittent; the sponsor only feels it acutely after a penalty notice arrives. Day to day the mental model is "my TPA files the 5500, my advisor handles the plan" — they believe they're already paying someone. To sell a NEW $3,600–$4,800/year line item, the founders must first convince the buyer a gap exists between what the TPA contractually does and what the sponsor is legally on the hook for. The moment a sale requires educating the customer into believing they have an uncovered problem, CAC rises, cycles lengthen, and the "already-budgeted, bought-without-internal-selling" premise weakens. If blended CAC settles at $3,000+ against a $4,000 ACV, payback stretches and the mo12 rung becomes very hard. The whole model's attractiveness hinges on whether a penalty-driven, fear-based pain converts at low CAC — currently [ASSUMED], not validated.

- E&O coverage and liability cap for the "compliance-assurance" positioning, and its effect on per-plan net margin? (Two E&O quotes on the disclaimed-fiduciary scope.)

The financial architecture matches the 01-objectives archetype almost line-for-line: mandate-forced recurring buy, productized software-leveraged managed service, capital-light, remote-first, no field ops, expertise barrier real but bridgeable via a named ERISA specialist, and a fragmented segment the well-funded bundled players explicitly do not serve.
