# Managed ERISA Compliance & Filing Oversight for Small Retirement-Plan Sponsors

## Customer
Small businesses that sponsor a 401(k) or other ERISA retirement plan — typically 10–200 employees, plan assets ~$1M–$50M — that are NOT on a modern all-in-one bundled provider, i.e., plans running on a traditional/independent recordkeeper + third-party administrator (TPA) arrangement, or that have outgrown a starter provider. The buyer is the business owner / HR or finance lead who is the legal "plan administrator."

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
An ERISA plan sponsor carries a stack of annual, deadline-driven federal obligations, each penalty- and fiduciary-liability-backed: file Form 5500 (plus schedules) with the DOL/IRS by the deadline (late-filing penalties run into the tens of thousands of dollars per plan-year under DOL/IRS amounts), maintain an ERISA fidelity bond, distribute required participant notices on schedule (Summary Annual Report, safe-harbor, QDIA, 404(a)(5) fee disclosure, automatic-enrollment notices, and more), keep the plan document and amendments current (SECURE 2.0 added new mandatory provisions and deadlines), and coordinate annual nondiscrimination testing. The business owner is legally the plan administrator and personally on the hook; missing the 5500 or a required notice triggers DOL penalties, IRS sanctions, and personal fiduciary exposure. In the bundled, modern-provider world this is largely handled inside the platform — but in the large legacy/independent-recordkeeper-and-TPA segment, responsibilities are split across the recordkeeper, the TPA, the advisor, and the sponsor, and items routinely fall through the cracks: a TPA is slow, an advisor disclaims responsibility, a notice never goes out, an amendment deadline is missed, a 5500 is filed late. It is forced (federal mandate), recurring (annual plus event-driven), already budgeted, and penalty-backed.

## Product
A productized managed service that acts as the sponsor's independent ERISA compliance/oversight layer — distinct from the recordkeeper and TPA — making sure every fiduciary, filing, and notice obligation is actually met:
- A software workspace that maps the plan's full annual compliance calendar (5500 + schedules, SAR and all participant notices, fidelity-bond renewal, SECURE 2.0 and other amendment deadlines, testing coordination) with status and owner for each item.
- A managed process that verifies the TPA/recordkeeper actually completed and filed each item, prepares and distributes the required participant notices, tracks the fidelity bond and amendments, and chases the responsible party when something is at risk.
- Remediation support for late or missed filings, including DOL Delinquent Filer Voluntary Compliance Program (DFVCP) submissions.
- A dashboard and audit-ready fiduciary file documenting that every obligation was met on time.
AI/software handles the high-volume calendar tracking, notice generation, and deadline management; a specialist handles exceptions and coordination with the TPA/recordkeeper/advisor. Delivery is fully remote. The required ERISA-administration expertise is bridgeable via a named specialist (e.g., an experienced ERISA/retirement-plan administrator) plus the founders' compliance background; the service does not act as the plan's ERISA fiduciary or replace the actuary/auditor.

## Revenue model
Per-plan annual or monthly subscription (tiered by plan size and complexity), plus a setup/onboarding engagement and per-remediation (e.g., DFVCP) fees. Proposed pricing (the plan, to validate): roughly $150–$600 per plan per month recurring depending on plan size and whether the service also handles notice distribution; setup $1K–$4K; remediation engagements priced separately. Retention is high because the obligation is permanent and penalty-backed for as long as the plan exists.

## Positioning / competition
Today this is split across the plan's TPA and recordkeeper (who handle the 5500 and testing for plans they administer, but vary widely in service quality and proactivity), the financial advisor (who sells investments and typically disclaims administrative/fiduciary filing duties), and the sponsor's own HR/finance staff reacting to deadlines. Modern all-in-one 401(k) providers (Guideline, Human Interest, Vestwell, Betterment at Work) bundle compliance and 5500 filing — but only for plans on their own platform, and they are well-funded, strong-tech players; this concept deliberately does NOT compete for plans on those platforms. It targets the large installed base of small plans on legacy/independent recordkeeper-and-TPA arrangements, where no one owns the sponsor's end-to-end compliance assurance and items fall through the cracks. Incumbent ERISA-compliance help in that segment is sleepy TPAs and hourly ERISA consultants with little marketing or productization. The intended wedge is a sharply positioned, productized, independent sponsor-side compliance-oversight managed service, with content marketing and a founder-led close (and an advisor/TPA referral channel), against split-responsibility confusion and unmarketed consultants.

## Why now
SECURE 2.0 introduced a wave of new mandatory plan provisions, amendment deadlines, and expanded auto-enrollment and notice requirements; DOL enforcement and per-day late-filing penalties have increased; new state auto-IRA mandates are pushing more small employers to start plans; and the legacy recordkeeper-and-TPA segment remains large, fragmented, and under-served on proactive compliance assurance even as the obligations multiply.
