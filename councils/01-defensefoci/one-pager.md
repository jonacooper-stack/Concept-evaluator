# Managed FOCI Mitigation & NISPOM Compliance for Small Cleared Defense Contractors

## Customer
Small and mid-size U.S. defense contractors and suppliers (roughly 5–250 employees) that hold, or are pursuing, a facility security clearance (FCL) under the National Industrial Security Program and that have some form of foreign ownership, control, or influence (FOCI) — foreign investors, a foreign parent, foreign board members, or material foreign revenue — which the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) requires them to formally mitigate.

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
To win and keep classified contracts, a contractor must maintain an FCL. If the company has FOCI, DCSA requires a government-approved mitigation arrangement (board resolution, security control agreement, special security agreement, proxy/voting-trust, etc.) and a set of permanent, recurring obligations: annual self-inspections, security program reviews, recurring reporting, a technology control plan and electronic communications plan, training, and minuted meetings of the company's government security committee. If mitigation lapses or the firm fails DCSA's recurring security review, the clearance can be suspended or revoked — which immediately ends the company's ability to bid or perform classified work. This is not discretionary spend; it is the price of staying in the cleared defense market, and prime contractors increasingly push clearance- and compliance-readiness requirements down onto their subcontractors.

## Product
A productized managed service that runs the contractor's FOCI mitigation and NISPOM (32 CFR Part 117) compliance program on a fixed annual scope:
- A software workspace that maintains required policies, the technology control plan, electronic communications plan, visitor/IT-access controls, training records, self-inspection checklists, and a tamper-evident evidence library.
- An annual self-inspection workflow with findings tracking and remediation.
- Preparation and document packages for DCSA security reviews and for government security committee meetings.
- A compliance calendar that tracks every recurring obligation and deadline.
- AI-assisted document generation and intake to handle the high-volume, repetitive top of the work.
Delivery is fully remote. Specialized expertise (FOCI instrument specifics, NISPOM detail, the statutory roles of a Facility Security Officer and government-approved outside directors/proxy holders) is supplied through a named cleared practitioner/advisor plus the founders' compliance-program and regulated-deal background. The offering is structured so any role that legally requires a cleared individual is filled by a partner/advisor; the founders are not assumed to hold personal clearances at launch.

## Revenue model
Annual subscription per cleared facility, tiered by company size and the complexity of the mitigation instrument, plus a managed self-inspection / security-review-prep retainer. Customers land with a readiness or mitigation-setup engagement and expand into the recurring annual program. Proposed pricing (the plan, to be validated): roughly $20K–$60K per facility per year; setup engagements $10K–$30K. Retention is structurally high because the obligation persists for as long as the clearance and the FOCI condition exist.

## Positioning / competition
The segment is served today by boutique cleared-facility / industrial-security consultants — often retired FSOs — who bill hourly, run on email and spreadsheets, do little to no marketing, and have no productized, fixed-scope offering; and by overstretched in-house FSOs at small firms who want help. Large consultancies treat these accounts as too small to pursue. Horizontal compliance-automation platforms (the SOC 2 / GRC vendors) do not, and effectively cannot, serve a classified, DCSA-specific niche. The intended wedge is sharp positioning, productization, and content marketing aimed at a fragmented base of small cleared contractors, with a founder-led close — competing against incumbents who have never been out-marketed.

## Why now
The NISPOM rule codified as 32 CFR Part 117 (effective 2021), a tightened DCSA enforcement and security-review cadence, rising foreign investment in the U.S. defense industrial base, and primes pushing clearance/compliance requirements onto subcontractors have together increased the forced, recurring compliance burden on exactly this underserved segment.
