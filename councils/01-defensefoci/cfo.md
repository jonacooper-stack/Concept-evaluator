CFO REVIEW — Managed FOCI Mitigation & NISPOM Compliance for Small Cleared Defense Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil well for a bootstrap: this is forced, recurring, budgeted spend tied to keeping a facility clearance — the most painkiller-shaped demand profile I see, since a lapse literally ends the company's ability to bid classified work. At a plausible $25K–$40K blended ACV with software-heavy delivery margins, the founder-income ladder (~$300K/founder by month 12, ~$500K+ by month 24) is reachable at low double-digit then mid-double-digit customer counts. The two structural cautions for me are market thinness (the addressable count of small cleared FOCI firms is finite — this is a niche, not a broad market) and a hard dependency on a named cleared practitioner for the statutory roles, which is a key-person/margin-share risk rather than a capital risk.

Target ladder (relabeled per objectives, NOT $2M): ~$300K/founder by mo 12 = ~$600K combined; ~$500K+/founder by mo 24 = ~$1.0M+ combined.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.6 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Forced, budgeted, structurally sticky revenue: obligation persists for life of clearance+FOCI; renewal non-discretionary, very low churn.
- Excellent cash dynamics: annual upfront + paid setup; near-zero AR, no inventory, self-funding; launchable low five figures.
- Low logo count to hit ladder: ~20 facilities for ~$600K combined, ~30 for ~$1.0M+; fits two-founder marketing-led motion.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Finite niche: addressable count is a narrow subset of an already-small population; real ceiling on upside.
- Practitioner dependency and margin share: statutory cleared roles compress margin and create key-person delivery risk.
- Pricing not yet validated: $20K–$60K is a plan; if incumbents anchor buyers low, ACV and ladder compress.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Market thinness colliding with the speed of the income ladder. The serviceable population — small/mid cleared contractors specifically carrying a DCSA-mitigated FOCI condition, reachable and willing to pay $20K–$60K/yr for a productized service rather than their existing retired-FSO consultant — is plausibly one to a few thousand firms [ASSUMED 1,000–3,000; verify against DCSA NISP enrollment + FOCI-arrangement stats]. The ladder needs only ~30, so this does NOT threaten the $300K–$500K/founder goal; it threatens the rate of year-one accumulation and caps larger optionality. Because retention is near-permanent, even a slower ramp compounds — a financing-of-time risk, not a viability risk.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- How many U.S. firms (5–250 employees) hold an FCL AND carry a DCSA-approved FOCI instrument today, and realistic year-1/year-2 obtainable share?
- All-in cost and contractual commitment of the named cleared practitioner per account/month, and what happens to delivery/margin if they leave — is there a second advisor?
- What ACV do the first 10 pricing-discovery conversations support vs what incumbents bill annually?
- Realistic sales cycle and CAC for a first cohort in a low-inbound market; time to 15–20 recurring accounts?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Among the strongest financial profiles for a two-founder bootstrap: forced/recurring demand, upfront-billed self-funding cash, very low logo count to hit the ladder, high retention, software-leverageable margins, clean transferable asset. Clears the ladder with conservative assumptions. Reservations (finite niche, unvalidated pricing, practitioner key-person/margin dependency) are scope-and-timing risks, cheaply testable before meaningful spend.
