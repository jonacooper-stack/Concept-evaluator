COO REVIEW — Managed FOCI Mitigation & NISPOM Compliance for Small Cleared Defense Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
A remote, software-leveraged, recurring managed-compliance service with a near-ideal operational shape on paper: no trucks, no installs, no inventory, no field crew, and a delivery unit (an annual compliance program per cleared facility) that is documentable and repeatable. The hard catch is not logistics but personnel: the model concedes certain roles "legally require a cleared individual," and FOCI mitigation requires government-approved outside directors, proxy holders, or a cleared FSO who by statute cannot be the founders at launch. Two founders + AI can run the documentation/workflow spine, but the most defensible and regulated layer depends on a scarce pool of cleared practitioners — and whether that practitioner is a named advisor or a per-account staffed role determines whether this scales like software or like a staffing agency.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday in March, ~12-month mark. [ASSUMED] 18 active facility accounts at ~$35K/yr → ~$630K ARR, plus 2–3 setups in flight. Founder A reviews the compliance-calendar dashboard: four items across three accounts — one self-inspection due in 21 days, one GSC quarterly minutes to finalize, one TCP revision (onboarded a foreign-national contractor), and a DCSA security review in six weeks (the big one). Founder A spends the morning on the review-prep account: pulling the evidence library, running the AI gap-check against the 32 CFR Part 117 checklist, assembling the package. AI drafts; Founder A reviews and corrects — the load-bearing human step, because a wrong answer can cost the client its clearance.
Mid-morning the named cleared advisor (fractional retired-FSO type) joins a 45-min call for the review account; because the founders aren't cleared and can't sit as approved proxy holder, the advisor signs off on FOCI-instrument-specific judgments and cleared-person items. Operational pinch point: the advisor's calendar is shared across all 18 accounts; on a heavy week (clustered reviews/GSC meetings) the advisor is the bottleneck, not the founders.
Founder B splits the day between a 90-min onboarding/intake for a new setup client (mapping FOCI condition, mitigation instrument, document state) and marketing (publishing a content piece, fielding two inbound demos, a founder-led sales call). A support ticket: an existing client got a surprise DCSA inquiry and is panicking — high-touch same-day human response. Where it breaks: if three accounts hit reviews in the same two weeks, founders' review-and-correct time + the single advisor's cleared-signoff time collide, and quality (the one thing that cannot slip) is at risk.

CAPACITY MATH
Ladder: ~$300K/founder mo12 = ~$600K combined; ~$500K/founder mo24 = ~$1M combined. At ~55–65% income/revenue [ASSUMED]: mo12 ~$950K–$1.1M ARR; mo24 ~$1.6M–$1.8M ARR.
Unit work/account/year [ASSUMED]: self-inspection 16–24 hrs; DCSA review-year prep 24–40 hrs (~every 12–18 mo); GSC quarterly 4×3=12 hrs; TCP/ECP/training/reporting/tickets 20–30 hrs → ~70–105 hrs, midpoint ~85 hrs/account/yr after AI. Verify by time-tracking first 3 accounts a quarter.
Mo12 at ~$1M ARR / ~$35K = ~29 accounts × 85 = ~2,465 delivery hrs. Two founders at ~1,600 productive hrs each = 3,200, but ~50% goes to sales/marketing/build/setup → ~1,600 delivery hrs available → ~865-hr gap = ~0.5 FTE cleared/qualified practitioner. Feasible; advisor is a real recurring cost.
Mo24 at ~$1.7M / ~$35K = ~49 accounts × 85 = ~4,165 hrs. Two founders can't cover even at 80% (~2,560). Gap ~1,600 hrs = ~1 FTE plus advisor signoff across 49 accounts. Cleared-signoff (4–6 hrs/account/yr → ~200–300 cleared hrs/yr) is the constraint; sourceable from one fractional advisor but high concentration. Operable lean, but NOT two-person-forever at the upper ladder.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.2 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Best-in-class logistics profile: fully remote, zero field ops, no inventory/routing — documents and calls across all 50 states.
- Forced, calendar-driven recurring work: a fixed annual cycle of mandated obligations, predictable/schedulable, AI can pre-draft against it.
- High structural retention: obligation persists as long as clearance + FOCI exist; low churn, multi-year delivery relationships smooth capacity planning.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Cleared-practitioner dependency: statutory roles require individuals the founders cannot be at launch; the defensible layer rides on a scarce single-sourced human input.
- Asymmetric quality risk: an error can cost a client its facility clearance, so every artifact needs expert human review, capping AI leverage and raising the hiring bar.
- Scaling-hire bottleneck: analysts/practitioners to grow past ~30 accounts come from a tiny, expensive, slow-to-clear pool — mo24 throughput is a hiring problem more than a sales problem.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The cleared-practitioner bottleneck colliding with catastrophic quality stakes. You cannot hand a generalist a checklist and absorb the occasional mistake — a single missed FOCI obligation or botched DCSA review package can suspend a client's clearance, ending their classified business and, in a tightly networked community, their referral value. The human review layer can't be cheaply offshored, juniorized, or fully AI-automated; the most senior judgments and statutory attestations route through a small number of cleared specialists — effectively one named advisor at launch, a single point of failure. As accounts climb toward ~49 (mo24), the founders aren't scaling a software product, they're scaling a thin, expensive, slow-to-source pool of cleared experts; if they can't recruit/retain that talent, throughput, not demand, becomes the binding constraint. Two people can run ~25–30 accounts; whether two can responsibly run 49 without real cleared-staff capacity is genuinely uncertain.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Exactly which delivery roles require a cleared individual; is the advisor one fractional person or a bench? Concrete plan/cost to add a second and third cleared practitioner before ~25 accounts?
- Real measured human-hours per account per year, broken out by self-inspection, review-year prep, GSC packages, crises? (Time-track first 3 accounts.)
- Liability/QC posture when an AI- or analyst-prepared package contributes to a failed review/lost FCL — who reviews every outbound artifact; how does review time scale?
- How lumpy is the DCSA review/GSC calendar; can accounts cluster; surge plan when the single advisor is the bottleneck?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Operationally one of the better-shaped concepts — remote, recurring, calendar-driven, no field ops, high retention — and mo12 ladder reachable with 2 founders + modest fractional cleared support (~29 accounts, ~2,465 hrs, ~0.5 FTE gap). REFINE not GO because the defensible layer depends on a scarce single-sourced slow-to-clear human input with asymmetric quality stakes, and cleared-talent sourcing beyond one advisor is unproven. Before GO: (1) build a real cleared-practitioner bench plan with named candidates/partnership pipeline; (2) define a hard QC/sign-off chain bounding the AI leverage; (3) consider capping initial scope to lower-complexity FOCI instruments (board resolutions, SCAs) before taking proxy/voting-trust accounts.
