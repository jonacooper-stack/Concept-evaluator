CFO REVIEW — Managed Supplier-Compliance Document & Attestation Service for SMB Suppliers to Large Buyers

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
Genuinely attractive shape: a recurring, budgeted, retention-heavy managed service with near-zero working-capital drag and software-leveraged margins. The pain is real and forced — losing approved-vendor status cuts off revenue, so suppliers pay to avoid it. Two concerns: (1) ARPU — at $200–$1,000/mo you need a lot of suppliers to hit the ladder, and the low end ($200/mo = $2.4K/yr) is too thin to carry managed-service labor; (2) the manual-exception/portal-idiosyncrasy component, which if not productized fast caps GM and slows scaling. The numbers pencil to the $300K/founder rung comfortably and to the $500K rung plausibly, but only if ARPU lands in the upper half of the band and specialist-labor-per-account stays low.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.3 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Near-ideal cash cycle: recurring billed in advance + setup fee front-loading onboarding cost, no inventory/AR/deposit drag — growth self-funds.
- Diversified, retention-heavy revenue: many small accounts, no concentration; churn suppressed because lapsing compliance blocks the customer's POs and payments.
- Capital-light, high-LTV/CAC profile: launchable for low four figures, LTV/CAC ~4–11x [ASSUMED], setup fees can fully offset CAC.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ARPU/labor mismatch at the low tier: $200/mo accounts may not cover the specialist labor they consume, dragging blended margin.
- Margin compression from manual portal-exception work: the GM ceiling; if it doesn't productize, GM stays ~65–70% and scaling economics stay flat.
- Account-count treadmill to the upper rung: ~$500K/founder needs ~350–415 active managed accounts — a meaningful ops-team build and churn-replacement burden.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The labor-per-account ratio inside the managed service, hiding behind a confident pricing band. The reason this pain exists is that buyer portals are heterogeneous, change requirements, and demand bespoke questionnaire responses — i.e., the work that resists automation is precisely what the customer is paying to offload. If each account consumes ~3–6 specialist-hours/month on portal exceptions, then at a $300–$450/mo account loaded labor alone can eat 40–60% of revenue, collapsing the 70% GM to 40–50% and turning a software-margin business into a staffing business. That would still clear the $300K/founder rung but make the $500K rung and the "scales into something larger" optionality far harder. The thesis depends on exception work being a thin bounded tail rather than the median account's reality — an empirical question, not a given.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Realized specialist-labor cost per account per month, by tier? (Time-and-motion 10 real accounts for 60 days; median + 90th-percentile hours sets the true GM.)
- Where does ARPU actually land — are enough suppliers multi-buyer ($700–$1,000 tier) to pull blended ARPU above ~$500/mo, or does it cluster at single-buyer $200? (Pre-sell at three price points.)
- Monthly logo churn and NRR? (Measure 6-month cohort retention and the rate at which buyers push suppliers onto a buyer-paid network — a key churn vector.)

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
Financially sound — recurring, capital-light, diversified, positive cash cycle, comfortably clearing the $300K/founder rung — and squarely in the bootstrapped software-leveraged managed-service archetype. A REFINE rather than GO only because two load-bearing numbers are assumed: realized blended ARPU and specialist-hours-per-account. To GO: (1) prove via a 60-day time-and-motion on 10 live accounts that median exception labor is ≤~3 hrs/account/month so blended GM holds 70%+; (2) anchor pricing to the multi-buyer supplier, targeting blended ARPU ≥$500/mo, with a labor-floor minimum or per-submission metering so low-tier accounts can't run negative-margin; (3) show one cohort of 6-month retention above ~90%. Land those three and this is a clean GO.
