COO REVIEW — Managed Supplier-Compliance Document & Attestation Service for SMB Suppliers to Large Buyers

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
A fully-remote, software-leveraged managed service with no trucks, installs, or field crew — the unit of work is document collection, portal upload, and questionnaire completion done from a laptop. Two founders plus a small remote specialist team can plausibly operate it, and the shape is genuinely attractive: recurring, deadline-driven, standardizable across customers. The real drag is NOT physical logistics; it is the long tail of buyer-portal idiosyncrasy and per-questionnaire exception work, labor that resists full automation and scales closer to linearly than a pure-SaaS founder would like. The question that decides this business is whether the average supplier's monthly compliance workload fits inside a price point that supports a small team, or whether portal heterogeneity makes each account a custom snowflake.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
Tuesday, month 12. ~120 supplier accounts at ~$450/month [ASSUMED blended ACV], ~$650K ARR. Founder A runs compliance ops; Founder B runs sales/marketing/onboarding; one full-time remote compliance specialist (hired ~month 8) + one part-time contractor for overflow.
The day opens in the dashboard's prioritized queue: every document expiring in the next 30 days across 120 accounts, every open buyer questionnaire with a deadline, every "supplier on hold / action required" alert forwarded from buyer portals. A typical Tuesday: ~8 COIs expiring this month (software already emailed each supplier's broker for the renewal endorsement naming the buyer additional insured), 3 buyer-portal questionnaires due this week (a Walmart-style supplier assessment, a healthcare-system vendor security questionnaire, a UFLPA/forced-labor attestation refresh), and 2 urgent buyer chaser emails where a grocery distributor threatens a payment hold because an SQF cert lapsed.
The specialist works the questionnaire queue: software pre-filled ~70% of each from the supplier's stored profile (entity info, insurance limits, prior attestation answers, certs on file); the specialist reviews, fills buyer-specific blanks, submits via the portal. The two urgent holds go to Founder A: one a genuine fire (the SQF cert truly lapsed, re-audit takes weeks — manage the buyer relationship, document the corrective timeline, keep the supplier from deactivation), the other a false alarm (COI uploaded to the wrong portal field, just needs re-submission). The part-time contractor chases two suppliers who haven't returned the broker authorization needed to pull an updated COI — the chronic bottleneck is supplier-side responsiveness, not internal capacity.
Founder B onboards a new account: a $25M packaging manufacturer selling into 4 large buyers. Onboarding means inventorying all 4 buyer relationships, getting delegated access to each portal, cataloguing every required document and expiration, and collecting current COIs, W-9, certs, and prior questionnaire answers — the heaviest single labor event (~6–10 hours over the first week, which is why the $1K–$5K setup fee exists). Where it breaks on a bad Tuesday: a buyer changes its portal or adds a new attestation overnight and 15 suppliers serving that buyer all need it by Friday — a synchronized fire drill the specialist + contractor must absorb. Nothing requires leaving the desk.

CAPACITY MATH
Per-account recurring workload [ASSUMED — verify with a 10-account time study]: an average supplier serving ~2–3 large buyers generates: COI renewals ~6/year → ~0.5/month × ~20 min ≈ 10 min/month; questionnaires/attestations ~4/year → ~0.33/month × ~45 min (70% pre-fill) ≈ 15 min/month; deadline monitoring/chaser/upkeep ≈ 20 min/month. Steady-state ≈ ~45 min skilled labor/account/month. At 120 accounts: 90 hrs/month ≈ ~5 hrs/working day = comfortably ONE FTE (~160 productive hrs/month) with headroom, plus founders on escalations.
Onboarding: ~8 hrs/account, front-loaded. 0→120 in 12 months ≈ 10 new/month × 8 = 80 hrs/month ≈ another half-to-full FTE during growth (Founder B + contractor).
Throughput ceiling at the ladder: ~$600K+ founder income implies ~$1.2–1.5M+ revenue after team cost. At $450 blended ACV, ~$1.3M ARR ≈ ~240 accounts. Recurring labor at 240: 180 hrs/month ≈ ~1.1 FTE delivery + onboarding churn. So even at mo24, ~2 founders + 2–3 remote specialists/contractors — a genuinely lean, capital-light profile. Stress tests on the [ASSUMED] 45-min figure: at 90 min, 240 accounts = 360 hrs/month ≈ ~2.3 FTEs (margins compress, viable); at 3 hours, 240 = 720 hrs/month ≈ ~4.5 FTEs and $450 ACV looks thin against loaded specialist cost — the model bends toward a staffing-heavy BPO. This single variable is the whole ballgame, which is why the time study is the first thing to run.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.4 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Fully remote, zero physical logistics or field ops — no trucks/installs/routing/seasonality (dims 3 and 9 both 9s), exactly the profile the founders want.
- Lean, validated staffing curve: shown math puts even the mo24 ladder (~240 accounts) on ~2 founders + 2–3 affordable, non-credentialed remote specialists at ~180 delivery hrs/month.
- Recurring, deadline-driven, standardizable work with a built-in retention mechanism — losing approved-vendor status cuts off revenue, so churn is structurally suppressed and the renewal cadence predictable.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- Per-account recurring labor is an [ASSUMED] 45 min/month; if portal heterogeneity pushes it to 2–3 hours, the model drifts from software-leveraged service toward a staffing-heavy BPO and margins compress against a $200–$1,000 ACV.
- Buyer-portal dependency: the founders submit into platforms they don't own; a buyer or its TPRM/SIM network could restrict third-party submitters or change portals overnight, forcing synchronized fire-drills across affected accounts.
- Severe, asymmetric QC failure mode: a single missed deadline can put a supplier on hold and cut their revenue, creating outsized liability/reputation exposure relative to a $450/month fee.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Portal heterogeneity colliding with the price point. The thesis that two founders + a couple of remote specialists can run hundreds of accounts rests on ~45 minutes of recurring labor per account per month [ASSUMED] and on questionnaire pre-fill actually working. But each large buyer runs its own vendor portal (or rides a third-party SIM/TPRM network), each with different fields, formats, attestation language, and many without APIs — so submission is often manual data entry into a web form, and pre-fill mappings break every time a buyer revises its questionnaire. If true labor is 2–3x the assumption, the business does not stop working but transforms: it becomes a compliance-BPO whose headcount scales close to linearly with accounts, where each new buyer a customer adds is a new portal to learn, and where a $450/month fee cannot comfortably carry a fully-loaded specialist's time. That is the difference between a high-margin software-leveraged service and a low-margin labor shop — and the founders cannot know which one they have until they run a real time study across a representative spread of buyers.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Run a 10-account time study across a representative buyer mix and report actual minutes/account/month for recurring work AND hours per onboarding — does the ~45-min recurring / ~8-hr onboard assumption hold, or is it 2–3x worse?
- For the top ~10 buyer portals/networks your target suppliers sell into, do any prohibit or block third-party/delegated submission on the supplier's behalf, and what's your fallback when one does?
- Contractual liability posture and SLA when a deadline IS missed and a supplier is placed on hold or deactivated — who eats the loss, and how do you cap exposure so a $450/month account can't generate a six-figure claim?
- What share of recurring labor is genuinely automatable (deadline tracking, broker COI requests, pre-fill) vs irreducibly human-in-portal, and how does that ratio trend as you add buyers per account?

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with one mandatory de-risking step)
Operationally one of the cleaner managed-service shapes: fully remote, no field ops, no physical supply chain, no seasonality, a national year-round market, an abundant non-credentialed labor pool for the first hires, and capacity math that puts even the 24-month ladder on ~2 founders plus 2–3 remote specialists. It maps almost exactly onto the desired archetype — a forced, recurring, budgeted, deadline-driven buy delivered as a productized software-leveraged service. The single gate: the per-account labor reality — the founders must run a real time study across diverse buyer portals to confirm the work is software-leveraged (high margin, lean team) rather than irreducibly manual (BPO, linear headcount). If that study lands anywhere near ~45 min/account/month, this is a strong, operable, lean business. GO, conditioned on that time study and a clear liability cap, because the downside case is "lower-margin labor shop," not "inoperable" — the operational risk is to margin, not feasibility.
