CFO REVIEW — Managed CLIA Compliance & Quality-Systems Program for Small Clinical Laboratories

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil and pencil well. A forced, recurring, already-budgeted compliance spend (biennial inspection cycle, PT events several times a year; a lapse is existential) sold as a fixed-scope managed subscription — the shape that produces high retention and self-funding cash flow. At $1.5K–$5K/mo plus a $5K–$20K setup, the income ladder (~$300K/founder mo12, ~$500K+ mo24) is reachable with ~100–160 labs at a blended ~$3K/mo (and far fewer to clear the lower rung). The fragility is not demand or margin — it's the cost-to-serve of the human specialist on the messier exception work (inspection prep, accreditor correspondence, CAPA), which decides whether GM lands near software economics (80%+) or staffed-services (55–65%), and the real-world sales cycle/CAC into a small, busy, non-tech-forward buyer.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Forced, permanent, already-budgeted demand with existential downside (certificate suspension halts testing AND billing) — drives both retention and pricing power.
- Prepaid recurring subscription + paid setup → excellent working capital (cash in before cost out), self-funding growth without a raise.
- Low customer count to hit the ladder (~30–36 labs mo12, ~50–61 mo24) against [ASSUMED] 15K–30K addressable — negligible penetration risk.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Cost-to-serve uncertainty: if a specialist carries only 12–15 labs (not 25–40), blended GM falls toward 50–55% and the income math stretches 6–12 months.
- CAC/sales-cycle into a small, busy, non-tech-forward buyer: if CAC runs $15K–$25K and cycles 4–6 months, the mo12 target slips.
- Pricing validation: if true WTP clusters at the low end (~$1.5K/mo = $18K/yr), required customer count roughly doubles.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Gross-margin compression from human exception work, not demand. Demand is forced and real; the question is whether this is a software business with a services wrapper (60–75%+ GM, scalable) or a consulting business with a software brochure (50–60% GM, capacity-capped by how many labs one credentialed specialist can shepherd through a biennial cycle plus PT/competency/CAPA exceptions). The honest answer sits in the middle and is unknown until a real inspection cycle is run end-to-end and time-logged. If the ratio comes in low, the model still works — it just becomes a slower-growing, lower-margin managed-services business that hits ~$300K/founder later than mo12 and needs disciplined hiring ahead of revenue. A fragility, not a fatal flaw: it changes the slope, not the direction. Mitigant: the software can progressively absorb the repeatable spine, pushing the specialist toward only judgment-heavy exceptions.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Real specialist-hour load per lab across a full biennial cycle (steady-state vs inspection-prep spike), and how many labs one credentialed specialist can carry? (Shadow/time-log one consultant through a single real inspection cycle.)
- What do small labs/POLs pay today (hourly consultant + accreditor fees + internal lab-director time), and is $1.5K–$5K/mo additive or substitutive? (15–20 buyer interviews + pricing-anchored landing tests.)
- Actual sales cycle and CAC for a founder-led close, and what triggers the buy (failed PT? deficiency citation? new accreditation? staffing departure)? (20 outbound conversations, log time-to-yes + precipitating event.)
