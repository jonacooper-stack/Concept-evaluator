# Managed CLIA Compliance & Quality-Systems Program for Small Clinical Laboratories

## Customer
Independent small and mid-size clinical laboratories and physician-office laboratories (POLs) — independent diagnostic labs, specialty and molecular labs, physician-office labs, and small reference labs — that hold a CLIA certificate (Certificate of Compliance or Certificate of Accreditation) to perform moderate- and high-complexity testing and bill Medicare/Medicaid/commercial payers.

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
Under the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA, 42 CFR 493), any lab testing human specimens must hold a CLIA certificate and maintain an ongoing quality program: a documented quality-management system and quality control, personnel competency assessments, proficiency-testing (PT) enrollment and successful performance, biennial inspections by CMS or a CMS-approved accreditor (CAP, COLA, The Joint Commission), and corrective-action responses to any deficiencies. Failure — a PT failure, an unsuccessful inspection, lapsed competency records — can trigger sanctions up to suspension or revocation of the CLIA certificate, which halts the lab's ability to test and to bill: an existential event. The burden is continuous (PT events several times a year, ongoing competency assessment, a biennial inspection cycle), forced, and already budgeted as a cost of operating a lab. Many small labs run it on binders, spreadsheets, and an overworked lab director or general supervisor.

## Product
A productized managed service that runs a lab's CLIA quality and compliance program on a fixed recurring scope:
- A software workspace maintaining the QMS document set (policies/SOPs), the proficiency-testing calendar and result tracking, personnel competency-assessment schedules and records, equipment/QC logs, and the deficiency/corrective-action (CAPA) tracker.
- A managed inspection-readiness program: mock inspections, checklist mapping against CMS/CAP/COLA standards, and document packages for the biennial survey.
- Deadline tracking for PT enrollment, competency cycles, and the inspection window.
- AI/software for document generation, competency-record management, and gap analysis; a compliance specialist handles exceptions, accreditor correspondence, and inspection prep.
Delivery is fully remote: the lab performs the physical testing and QC at the bench; the service runs the documentation and quality-program spine, not the bench work. Required expertise (CLIA regulations, CAP/COLA checklists, the duties of the lab director / technical supervisor / general supervisor) is supplied via a named lab-quality specialist/advisor (e.g., a credentialed MT(ASCP) or lab-quality professional) plus the founders' compliance-program background. CLIA requires the lab itself to designate qualified personnel (lab director, technical/general supervisor); those are the lab's own staff — the service supports their program and does not replace the statutory roles.

## Revenue model
Annual or monthly subscription per lab / CLIA certificate (tiered by test complexity and volume), plus a managed inspection-prep retainer and an onboarding/setup engagement; optional per-PT-event or per-competency-cycle add-ons. Proposed pricing (the plan, to validate): roughly $1.5K–$5K per lab per month recurring depending on complexity, a setup engagement of $5K–$20K, and inspection-prep packages. Retention is very high because the obligation is permanent for as long as the lab operates and a lapse is existential.

## Positioning / competition
The segment is served today by sleepy lab-quality consultants (often retired lab directors or MT(ASCP) consultants) who bill hourly and run on documents and email with little marketing or productization; by the accreditors themselves (CAP, COLA, The Joint Commission), which provide checklists and standards but are the inspectors, not a managed-compliance partner; and by laboratory-information-system (LIS) vendors and QC-data tools (e.g., Bio-Rad Unity for QC data) that handle slices of the problem but not the whole compliance program. Many small labs simply DIY on binders. Horizontal compliance-automation platforms (Vanta, Drata) do not speak CLIA/CAP. The intended wedge is a genuinely productized, software-leveraged, done-for-you recurring CLIA quality-and-compliance managed service for the under-served small/independent lab tier that the big consultancies and LIS vendors don't focus on, with sharp positioning, content marketing, and a founder-led close, competing against hourly, sleepy consultants.

## Why now
Growth of independent specialty and molecular labs (post-pandemic testing expansion), heightened CMS and accreditor scrutiny, the ongoing VALID Act / laboratory-developed-test (LDT) FDA-oversight debate raising quality-system expectations, and persistent lab-staffing shortages (fewer in-house quality experts) have together increased the forced, recurring compliance burden on small labs that lack the staff to keep up.
