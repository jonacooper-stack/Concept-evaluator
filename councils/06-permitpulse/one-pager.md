# Managed License & Permit Renewal Compliance for Multi-Location Food-Service Operators

## Customer
Multi-location restaurant, bar, café, and food-service operators and franchise groups — small and mid-size chains running roughly 3–50 locations — plus fast-growing single-unit operators expanding into new sites and jurisdictions.

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
Each location must hold and continually renew a stack of operating licenses and permits — health/food-establishment permit, food-handler and food-manager certifications, liquor/alcohol license, general business license, fire/occupancy permit, sign permit, weights-and-measures, music licensing, and assorted local permits (grease, sidewalk, etc.) — each issued by a different city, county, or state authority on its own staggered renewal cycle and fee schedule. A lapsed health permit or liquor license can shut a location down or trigger fines and failed inspections; missing a renewal is common because the matrix (locations × ~6–15 permits × jurisdictions × renewal dates × fees) is large, decentralized, and "owned" by whoever happens to track it — often a GM, an office manager, or no one. As a group adds locations across jurisdictions, the renewal calendar becomes unmanageable manually. It is a forced, deadline-driven, already-budgeted operating cost tied directly to keeping each location open and serving.

## Product
A productized managed service that runs a multi-location operator's entire license-and-permit renewal lifecycle:
- A software workspace that inventories every location's licenses/permits/certifications, their issuing authority, renewal dates, fees, and required documents.
- A managed renewal process: the service tracks each deadline, prepares and submits renewal applications, surfaces fees due, files renewals where the jurisdiction allows a third-party agent, chases the required documents, and confirms issuance.
- Food-handler / food-manager certification tracking for staff.
- New-location onboarding: building the full permit inventory for a new site.
- A dashboard with alerts and an audit-ready record of every license's status.
AI/software handles the high-volume tracking, reminders, form prep, and jurisdiction-rule lookups; a specialist handles exceptions, authority correspondence, and odd local requirements. Delivery is fully remote — the operator handles physical/inspection compliance; the service runs the renewal and documentation spine, not on-site work. The required expertise is the universe of local permit types and renewal mechanics — administrative knowledge, not a credential — bridgeable via a specialist plus the founders' operations and compliance background.

## Revenue model
Per-location monthly subscription (tiered by permit count and jurisdiction complexity), plus a per-new-location onboarding fee and per-filing/renewal fees, with government fees passed through. Proposed pricing (the plan, to validate): roughly $40–$150 per location per month recurring; a 20-location group lands around $1.5K–$4K per month plus onboarding. Retention is high because the obligation is permanent and a lapse closes a location.

## Positioning / competition
The segment is served today by local permit expediters (project-based, not recurring), in-house office managers on spreadsheets and calendar reminders, and paper diaries; many operators simply react to renewal notices and occasionally miss one. Adjacent business-license-management providers exist (e.g., Avalara's license offering, CSC, Harbor Compliance) but target broad business-license registration, not the food-service operational-permit stack (health, liquor, fire, food-handler) at the location level for small/mid restaurant groups; some restaurant-tech suites bundle weak compliance slices aimed at large chains. The small/mid multi-location operator running on spreadsheets is genuinely under-served, and horizontal compliance platforms do not speak local permit minutiae. The intended wedge is a sharply positioned, productized, done-for-you recurring renewal-management managed service for that under-served tier, with content marketing and a founder-led close, against project-based expediters and DIY spreadsheets.

## Why now
Restaurant-group consolidation and multi-unit franchising have multiplied the cross-jurisdiction permit matrix; jurisdictions have digitized many permit portals (making remote filing feasible) while keeping renewal cycles fragmented; and high turnover at the GM/office-manager level — the people who used to "own" the renewal calendar — means the institutional knowledge keeps walking out the door, raising missed-renewal risk.
