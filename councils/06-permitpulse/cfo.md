CFO REVIEW — Managed License & Permit Renewal Compliance for Multi-Location Food-Service Operators

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The recurring, must-have, already-budgeted nature is attractive — a lapsed health/liquor license closes a location, so churn is low and willingness-to-pay real. The problem is ARPU: at $40–$150/location/month you need ~600–1,000+ locations under contract to clear the ladder, and each customer is only 3–50 locations, so this is a high-volume, mid-velocity SMB sales motion against a service with real per-filing labor (jurisdiction correspondence, document chasing, odd local requirements). The economics pencil to a solid lifestyle-to-mid business, but the path to $300K/founder by mo12 is tight and the per-filing exception labor is the margin risk that decides whether this is software-margin or a thinly-staffed ops shop.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.6 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Structurally low churn + genuine WTP: forced, recurring, deadline-driven, budgeted; a lapse closes a revenue-generating location.
- Capital-light and remote; government fees pass through (clean working capital).
- Diversified, fragmented revenue base; TAM far larger than two founders can service.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- ARPU-vs-labor squeeze: $80/location/month is small against a service that may require real per-filing labor on 6–15 permits/location; low locations-per-specialist collapses GM toward a staffing model.
- Sales velocity to hit the ladder: ~90–110 groups by mo24 via a founder-led SMB close is a grind; the mo12 rung is likely missed and the mo24 rung at risk.
- "Done-for-you" liability/exception tail: when the service files and a renewal is missed/rejected, the operator's location can be shut down; pricing must cover labor + implied risk, and one bad miss can churn a multi-location group.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Locations-per-specialist — how much human exception labor each location consumes per year. The pitch assumes AI handles tracking while a specialist handles exceptions, but in fragmented local permitting the exceptions ARE the work: jurisdictions that won't accept a third-party agent, portals that change, slow operators, idiosyncratic local permits. If a specialist can safely run only 80–100 locations rather than 200+, then at $80/location/month a specialist generating ~$96K of revenue costs ~$70K loaded, leaving thin contribution after software/overhead — a low-margin remote-ops shop, not a software-leveraged service. That ratio decides whether net margin is 35% (ladder reachable in year 2) or ~15% (out of reach without raising ARPU well above $150/location).

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- How many locations can ONE trained specialist realistically service per year given 6–15 permits across jurisdictions on staggered cycles? (Time-and-motion test before scaling.)
- In what fraction of target jurisdictions will the authority actually accept a third-party agent filing a renewal? Where it won't, you're a reminder/prep tool (lower price). Price/retention difference between the two modes?
- Realistic blended ARPU after fee compression — can a 20-location group be landed at $1.5K–$4K/month, or does DIY pressure pull toward the $40 floor, and what do onboarding + per-filing add per group per year?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
The structure is genuinely good — forced, recurring, budgeted, low-capital, diversified, low-churn — and clears the hard constraints. But at $40–$150/location ARPU the math reaches only ~$280K–$350K/founder by mo24, short of the $500K rung, and GM depends on an unproven locations-per-specialist ratio that could make this a staffing shop. To GO: (1) prove or raise effective ARPU — push blended toward $120–$150/location and lean on onboarding + per-filing + premium tiers so a 20-location group is $4K–$6K/month, not $1.5K; (2) prove a specialist can run 150+ locations so GM holds 65%+; (3) confirm third-party-agent filing is accepted in enough jurisdictions to justify "done-for-you" pricing.
