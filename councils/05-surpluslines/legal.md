LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Managed Surplus-Lines Tax, Stamping & Diligence Compliance for E&S Brokers

(Not legal advice; experienced-operator pattern-recognition to brief real counsel.)

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
A compliance-services business operating one layer removed from a heavily regulated activity (surplus-lines insurance placement) rather than performing the regulated act — the single most important structural fact about its risk profile. The broker — not this service — holds the surplus-lines license, owes the tax, signs the diligent-search affidavit, and remits funds from its own trust account; the service prepares filings and runs back-office workflow. That keeps the founders out of the worst landmines (no insurance license, no money transmission, no fiduciary custody of tax dollars). The real exposure is professional-services E&O: if the service miscalculates an allocation, misses a stamping-office deadline, or mis-files a state return, the broker eats penalties and interest and will look to this service to make them whole — a knowable, insurable, contract-allocable risk, not a fatal one, but the spine of the liability story. The big open question is whether any state treats outsourced surplus-lines tax filing as itself a licensable or registrable activity.

REGULATORY MAP
- State surplus-lines law / state DOIs (50 states + DC): the substantive regime the customer lives under — premium tax, transaction reporting, diligent-search/affidavit, home-state determination, multi-state allocation. The regulated party is the licensed broker, NOT the service; the service's burden is operational accuracy, not a license. The load-bearing assumption to confirm with counsel state-by-state.
- Stamping offices (~15 states: FSLSO FL, SLA CA, SLSOT/SLTX TX, SLA IL, etc.): quasi-regulatory bodies that review/"stamp" filings, each with its own portal/format/cadence, interfacing with the filer (broker), typically on the broker's credentials. The service operates these on the broker's behalf — generally an agency/authorized-submitter posture most offices permit on their own terms.
- Nonresident surplus-lines broker licensing (customer's obligation): relevant only because if the service ever signs an affidavit, makes the diligent-search determination, or holds itself out as placing/binding coverage, it could be pulled into needing a license. Mitigation: a hard scope line — service prepares and files; the licensed broker attests and remits. Zero burden if the line holds; potentially fatal if it blurs.
- NRRA (Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act, federal, 2010): home-state-rules-the-tax framework; implemented unevenly. The legal backbone of the home-state-determination + allocation engine; the product's domain knowledge, not a compliance cost.
- Custody-of-funds / money-transmission and insurance-trust-account rules: avoided by design — broker remits tax from its own trust account; the service never takes custody/transmits. Deliberately sidesteps money-transmitter licensing and trust-account fiduciary rules — CONTINGENT on never touching the money.
- GLBA / NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (~25 states; NY DFS 23 NYCRR 500): the service ingests policy/premium data and ties into licensee data-security obligations; likely a covered "third-party service provider" with flowed-down safeguards — a WISP, vendor questionnaires, breach-notice cooperation; standard B2B infosec.
- FTC Act §5 / advertising substantiation: "audit-ready," "never miss a deadline," "fully compliant" are performance claims needing substantiation. B2B, so ROSCA/CA ARL largely do not bite; TCPA/CAN-SPAM apply to outbound only.
- UPL: determining tax obligations and filing returns is bookkeeping/tax-prep territory, not law practice — low risk; "advising" on diligent-search adequacy or interpreting a statute edges toward it.

LICENSING SUMMARY
Pending counsel, the service itself likely needs NO insurance license to prepare and file surplus-lines tax returns/stamping submissions on a licensed broker's behalf — the way a CPA/tax-filing service does not need an insurance license to file an insurer's premium tax. The licensable acts (placing/binding with a non-admitted carrier, conducting the diligent search, signing the affidavit, custody of premium/tax trust funds) all remain with the broker. The service must hold a clean operational line: it computes, populates, submits; the broker reviews, attests, remits. Routine business licensing is the floor. The genuine unknowns counsel must run down before scale: (1) whether any states require registration of "surplus-lines tax filing services"/third-party filers with the DOI or stamping office; (2) whether stamping offices require the submitting party to be a licensed broker/authorized sub-producer rather than a back-office agent — a few may, forcing a power-of-attorney/authorized-submitter arrangement per office; (3) whether the named specialist should hold a surplus-lines broker license to backstop any activity drifting toward attestation. None is plausibly a 50-state-exam wall like the customer faces; the service inherits the broker's license rather than needing its own.

LIABILITY POSTURE
Dominant exposure is professional liability / E&O on the filings the service prepares. Mis-allocate multi-state premium, apply a stale tax rate, miss a stamping-office/state-return deadline, or botch an affidavit record → the broker incurs penalties, interest, audit assessments, worst case license jeopardy — and seeks recovery from the service. Real money, recurring by design. But classic insurable/contractually-allocable: (1) tech-E&O / miscellaneous professional liability sized to penalty/interest exposure (not the broker's whole book); (2) MSA liability cap to fees paid (or a small multiple), consequential-damages waiver, broker contractually responsible for final review and attestation of every filing before submission (true legally and contractually — the broker is the licensed signer); (3) carve out that the service provides no legal/tax advice and does not determine diligent-search adequacy. Data liability is secondary/standard (GLBA-flowed-down + state insurance-data-security notification; cyber insurance + WISP + DPA). Personal exposure: form the entity before the first customer, run all contracts/bank accounts through it. The piece that cannot be fully contracted away is reputational/relationship risk — a broker who loses a license partly on the service's watch is a catastrophic reference even if the MSA caps the dollars — arguing for conservative QA, four-eyes review on deadlines, and over-investing in the deadline engine.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.2 / 10

TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY STRENGTHS
- The service performs none of the licensable acts: the broker holds the license, signs the affidavit, and remits tax from its own trust account — avoiding insurance licensing, money-transmitter licensing, and fund custody entirely.
- Clean B2B posture: selling to licensed brokerages sidesteps the entire consumer-protection thicket; remote all-W-2 staffing keeps employment/contractor risk at baseline.
- The principal liability is a familiar, insurable, contract-allocable professional-services E&O risk that can be capped to fees, backstopped by tech-E&O, and shifted in part to the broker via a mandatory review-and-attest clause.

TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY RISKS
- Scope creep across the licensing line: the moment the service determines diligent-search adequacy, signs/co-signs an affidavit, holds out as placing coverage, or touches tax/premium funds, it risks needing a surplus-lines broker or money-transmitter license.
- Filing-error E&O: a missed stamping-office deadline, stale tax rate, or bad multi-state allocation produces penalties, interest, audit assessments for the broker — recurring by design and the most likely real claim — with reputational fallout no liability cap fully contains.
- Per-state/per-stamping-office authorization unknowns: a subset may require the submitting party to be a licensed broker or registered third-party filer, forcing power-of-attorney/authorized-submitter arrangements office-by-office and complicating the 50-state scale story.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The licensing-line boundary around the diligent search and the affidavit. Surplus-lines law is built on the premise that a licensed broker personally conducted a diligent search, found the admitted market declined, and attests to that under penalty of perjury before placing with a non-admitted carrier. If the service stays strictly clerical — ingesting the broker's data, calculating tax, populating/submitting forms the broker reviews and signs — it is almost certainly an unlicensed back-office vendor, fine in most states. But the product promises to "maintain the diligent-search/affidavit records" and be the broker's compliance brain, and the commercial temptation to drift from "we store and format what you attest" into "we determine whether your search was adequate" or "we author the affidavit" will be enormous. That drift can convert the service into something a DOI views as participating in the regulated transaction, triggering surplus-lines broker licensing in every state where business is written, plus UPL/unlicensed-activity questions. Mitigation is architectural and contractual: hard-code the workflow so the broker affirmatively attests to every diligent-search determination and reviews every return before submission, paper the division in the MSA, and get a written opinion from insurance-regulatory counsel — ideally surveying the largest states by surplus-lines volume (CA, TX, FL, NY, IL) — that the defined scope is non-licensable. Get this right and the whole risk profile is benign; get it wrong and the model needs a licensed broker at its core, changing staffing/cost/scalability substantially.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
1. In CA/TX/FL/NY/IL, does preparing and filing surplus-lines tax returns and stamping submissions on a licensed broker's behalf (broker reviews/attests/remits) require any insurance license, surplus-lines license, or third-party-filer registration for the service or its specialist?
2. Where exactly is the boundary between "maintaining/formatting diligent-search records the broker attests to" (safe) and "determining/authoring the affidavit" (likely licensable/UPL), and how to structure workflow + contracts to stay on the safe side?
3. For the ~15 stamping states, do their rules permit a non-licensed back-office agent to submit on the broker's credentials, or require a licensed broker / authorized submitter / power of attorney, and what is the per-office onboarding instrument?
4. Confirm that, as long as the broker remits from its own trust account and the service never takes custody/initiates transmission, no money-transmitter license and no trust-account fiduciary obligation attaches.
5. Draft the MSA liability cap, consequential-damages waiver, mandatory broker review-and-attest clause, and "no legal/tax advice" disclaimer; confirm enforceability; size tech-E&O to realistic penalty/interest exposure.
6. Determine which states' NAIC Model Law / NY DFS 500 adoption make the service a covered third-party service provider, and the required WISP/breach-notification/vendor terms.

RECOMMENDATION: GO (with one gating diligence item)
The legal/regulatory architecture is fundamentally sound and, unusually for an insurance-adjacent business, deliberately designed to keep the founders out of the licensable acts: the broker holds the license, signs the affidavit, and moves the money, while the service stays in calculation, form-generation, and filing — the difference between a benign B2B compliance-SaaS-plus-service posture and a regulated insurance operation. Residual risks (filing-error E&O, per-state filer/stamping authorization) are knowable and either insurable, contract-allocable, or resolvable with a counsel survey of the top states. GO rather than REFINE because no structural change is required; what is required is one gating action before signing the first customer: a written insurance-regulatory-counsel opinion (covering at least CA, TX, FL, NY, IL) confirming the defined strictly-clerical scope is non-licensable and that the diligent-search/affidavit handling stays on the records-and-formatting side of the line. If that opinion is clean, this is a low-to-moderate regulatory-risk business with a strong durable recurring thesis. If it requires a licensed broker inside the service, the model survives but must add a licensed principal — workable, but a material change to staffing/economics to pressure-test first.
