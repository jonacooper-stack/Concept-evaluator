CFO REVIEW — Managed Surplus-Lines Tax, Stamping & Diligence Compliance for E&S Brokers

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers pencil well on what matters most: a forced, deadline-driven, license-tied recurring obligation with budget already allocated, sold to a buyer (E&S brokers) the founders know from prior insurance-industry deals. Gross margin is software-leveraged-services territory (est. 55–70% once the specialist is loaded, climbing toward 70–75% as software absorbs volume), retention should be exceptional (lapsing risks the surplus-lines license), and the income ladder is reachable with ~120–180 brokerages at midpoint ARPU. The fragility is not demand or churn; it is (a) liability/errors exposure on filings the broker is legally responsible for, which can cap pricing and inflate E&O + compliance-labor cost, and (b) early dependence on one named compliance specialist whose throughput governs onboarding speed — making the year-1 ramp specialist-gated, not capital-gated.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.5 / 10

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Forced, license-tied recurring obligation with budget allocated; retention suppressed by regulation, not vendor effort.
- Excellent cash shape: advance-billed monthly + front-loaded setup, and deliberately no custody of tax dollars (no escrow/float drag or money-movement liability).
- Low customer concentration: fragments across many small brokerages; financeable, diversified, transferable book.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Pricing-vs-liability squeeze: broker stays legally liable, but a productized filer is expected to stand behind accuracy; E&O + exception labor compress the 55–70% GM and cap price.
- Specialist-throughput gating: early ramp governed by one specialist's onboarding/exception capacity; if it bottlenecks, ~5 net-adds/month slips.
- Rule-maintenance cost creep: keeping 50 states' rates/bases/deadlines + ~15 stamping formats current is permanent non-billable overhead scaling with breadth, not customers.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The liability-and-accuracy tail interacting with pricing. The broker is legally responsible, but the moment a productized service prepares and files, it is on the hook commercially (and likely contractually) for a missed deadline, a misallocation, a wrong tax base. One material error triggering a client's penalty/interest/audit creates direct indemnity exposure AND a reputational hit in a tight broker community where references travel. The consequence is twofold: forces real E&O cost and a heavier human review layer (capping GM below the software-leveraged ideal), and limits pricing power because the service can't fully transfer the liability it implicitly assumes. Not disqualifying — incumbents carry the same exposure with worse tooling — but it most directly determines whether GM lands at 65–70% or 50–55%, and therefore whether the ladder is comfortable or tight.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Real per-customer delivery cost in specialist-hours, split onboarding vs steady-state? (90-day hours log on first 5 customers — sets GM at 50% vs 70%.)
- Liability posture + E&O cost: contracts cap to fees? who carries penalty/interest if a filing is wrong? what does broker E&O quote at? Get a real quote before scaling.
- What ARPU will the small/mid tier pay, and how many are genuinely multi-state? Run 10 priced proposals at $500/$1,500/$3,000 tiers; measure close rate and state-count distribution.

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Among the better structures: a must-have, license-tied, advance-billed recurring obligation with low startup capital, no fund-custody float, low customer concentration, a clean transferable book, and a ladder reachable at ~60 brokerages mo12 / ~120–180 mo24 against a serviceable market several times larger. Founders' insurance credibility lowers CAC against sleepy clerk/consultant incumbents (no well-funded modern player owns surplus-lines tax filing; AgentSync/Vertafore sit in the adjacent producer-licensing lane). The two things that move this from a comfortable GO to a tight one — real per-customer specialist-hour cost (GM) and E&O/liability cost (pricing ceiling) — are cheaply measurable early. Proceed and instrument both from customer one.
