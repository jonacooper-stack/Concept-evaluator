# Managed Surplus-Lines Tax, Stamping & Diligence Compliance for Excess & Surplus (E&S) Brokers

## Customer
Small and mid-size surplus-lines (excess & surplus, "E&S") and wholesale insurance brokers and MGAs that place coverage with non-admitted carriers across multiple states — independent surplus-lines brokerages, from solo brokers up to ~50-broker shops, that write business in many states.

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
When a broker places a policy with a non-admitted (surplus-lines) carrier, the broker — not the carrier — is legally responsible for filing surplus-lines premium tax and transaction reports to each state, and to the stamping office in the ~15 stamping states, on monthly/quarterly/annual cadences that vary by state, plus maintaining the "diligent search" / affidavit documentation proving the admitted market declined the risk. The broker must determine the home state, allocate premium across states for multi-state risks, calculate surplus-lines tax (rates and bases vary by state), file each state's return and stamping-office submission by its deadline, and keep the diligence records. Miss a filing, misallocate, or misreport and the broker faces penalties, interest, audit assessments, and license suspension or non-renewal by the state insurance department. The matrix (policies × home-state rules × multi-state allocation × stamping-office formats × deadlines × tax rates) explodes as a brokerage grows and writes in more states — a forced, deadline-driven, already-budgeted back-office obligation tied directly to keeping the surplus-lines license.

## Product
A productized managed service that runs a brokerage's entire surplus-lines compliance lifecycle:
- A software workspace that ingests policy/premium data (CSV or agency-management-system export), determines the home state and multi-state premium allocation, and calculates surplus-lines tax per state.
- Generates and files the required state tax returns and stamping-office submissions on each deadline.
- Maintains the diligent-search/affidavit and transaction records and produces audit-ready reports.
- Tracks every state's filing deadlines and tax-rate/rule changes.
AI/software handles the high-volume calculation, form generation, and deadline tracking; a compliance specialist handles exceptions, state correspondence, and audit response. Delivery is fully remote. Tax dollars owed are remitted by the broker from the broker's own surplus-lines trust account — the service prepares and files the returns but does not take custody of or transmit tax funds. Required expertise (state surplus-lines tax rules, stamping-office mechanics, diligent-search requirements) is supplied via a named surplus-lines compliance specialist plus the founders' insurance-industry and compliance background.

## Revenue model
Per-brokerage monthly subscription (tiered by transaction volume and number of states), plus per-filing/per-transaction fees and an onboarding/setup engagement. Proposed pricing (the plan, to validate): roughly $500–$3,000 per brokerage per month recurring depending on volume and state count, plus a setup engagement of $2K–$10K. Retention is very high because the obligation is permanent for as long as the brokerage writes surplus-lines business and a lapse risks the license.

## Positioning / competition
The segment is served today by sleepy back-office clerks, hourly surplus-lines tax consultants, and stamping-office-adjacent filing services that run on spreadsheets and manual portal submissions with little marketing and no modern productized offering; many small brokerages do it in-house on spreadsheets and live in fear of an audit. The large insurance-tech platforms (AgentSync, Vertafore/Sircon) focus on producer licensing and appointments, not surplus-lines tax filing and stamping — an adjacent but distinct problem that no well-funded modern-tech player owns. The intended wedge is a genuinely productized, software-leveraged, done-for-you recurring surplus-lines tax-and-stamping compliance managed service for the under-served small/mid wholesale-broker tier, with sharp positioning, content marketing, and a founder-led close, leveraging the founders' direct insurance-industry credibility against hourly clerks and sleepy filing services.

## Why now
The E&S/surplus-lines market has grown sharply (a hard property market pushing more risks to non-admitted carriers), pulling more brokers into multi-state surplus-lines placement; states have tightened surplus-lines tax enforcement and modernized e-filing/stamping requirements; and the multi-state matrix has become untenable for small brokerages to manage manually.
