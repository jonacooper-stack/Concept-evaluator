COO REVIEW — Managed Surplus-Lines Tax, Stamping & Diligence Compliance for E&S Brokers

ONE-PARAGRAPH OPERATIONS READ
One of the cleaner managed-compliance shapes: the unit of work is a recurring data-in / filing-out cycle that is software-leverageable, fully remote, and weather/geography-neutral. The hard truth is that "AI/software handles high-volume calculation and form generation" hides a brutal long tail — ~50 states + DC + ~15 stamping offices, each with its own portal, return format, tax base, and deadline cadence, most NOT API-accessible. The real operating question is not "can two founders run a remote SaaS-plus-service" (yes) but "how many distinct state filing surfaces must a human master and babysit, and does that surface area scale faster than headcount." That long-tail integration burden, plus audit-response and exception-handling load, is where this either holds as a productized service or degrades into a bespoke compliance shop billing hours.

THE TUESDAY-IN-MARCH WALKTHROUGH
March is a realistic high-load month: many states run annual surplus-lines tax filings on a calendar-year basis with Q1 deadlines (March 1/15 or quarterly), and the largest stamping states (CA SLA, FL FSLSO, TX SLTX) have monthly/transactional cadences in parallel. At month 12 with ~40 brokerage clients [ASSUMED 30–50].
Morning: the specialist opens a deadline dashboard showing every client's upcoming filings — monthly stamping submissions (CA/FL/TX volume states), quarterly state premium-tax returns, three annual returns this week. First job is ingestion QA: clients drop CSVs/AMS exports (AMS360, EZLynx, Sagitta, raw spreadsheets). Software parses policies, infers home state (NRRA rule), allocates multi-state premium, computes tax per state. A meaningful fraction of rows kick out as exceptions — missing affidavit, ambiguous home state on a multi-state commercial risk, a new sub-broker, a fee that may or may not be taxable, an unrecognized coverage code. The specialist works the queue, emails two clients for missing diligent-search docs, resolves a Texas allocation question against SLTX rules.
Late morning: submission mode — logging into FSLSO (FL), SLTX (TX), SLA (CA), and individual state DOI portals for annual-return clients. Some the software can pre-fill or generate a PDF; many require manual keying/file upload into a portal with its own session, MFA, and validation quirks; each submission's confirmation is captured into the audit record. Afternoon: a state returns a rejection (rounding/stamping-fee discrepancy), and a new client's onboarding requires reconciling six months of back-filings to establish a clean baseline — labor-heavy. One founder is on sales/content; the other co-founder is in product (exception-mapping engine, deadline-rules database) and escalations. Where it breaks: if 40 clients generate 60–120 distinct filings that week at a 10–20% exception rate, two filers hold it; if exceptions are 35% or three clients get audited at once, the queue overflows and a deadline slips — and a slipped deadline is the one failure that harms the client's license.

AVERAGE SCORE: 6.5 / 10

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL STRENGTHS
- Fully remote, zero-physical-logistics delivery (dim 3 = 9): structurally ideal for two founders avoiding field ops.
- Recurring deadline-driven cadence with very high structural retention; work repeats without re-selling, smoothing capacity planning.
- Capacity math closes on the ladder with thin headcount: ~1 specialist mo12 (~40 clients), ~2 mo24 (~72), leaving margin for $500K+/founder against ~$1.3M gross.

TOP 3 OPERATIONAL RISKS
- The ~65-surface long tail (50 states + DC + ~15 stamping offices), mostly manual portals with no APIs, means software leverage may be far lower than pitched and per-filing labor may scale near-linearly with clients.
- Near-zero error tolerance (dim 8 = 5): a single missed deadline harms the client's license — the precise harm they pay to prevent — turning QC into per-filing verification with high reputational/liability stakes.
- Onboarding is labor-heavy: establishing a clean baseline often requires reconciling/correcting months of prior back-filings — a custom forensic engagement that doesn't productize cleanly and front-loads cost.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
The gap between the pitch's "AI/software handles high-volume calculation and form generation" and the reality that surplus-lines filing happens across ~65 distinct state/stamping surfaces, most requiring manual keying/upload into idiosyncratic government portals with no API, each with its own deadlines/formats/validation/unannounced changes. If per-filing human time stays 45–90 minutes and exception rates run high, there is no true software leverage — headcount scales roughly linearly, GM compresses, and the founders run a labor-bound compliance bureau rather than a leveraged service, capping the ladder and concentrating capacity risk in a scarce specialist pool. The reason this is the killer and not merely a drag is the asymmetry: the one operational failure that matters (a missed filing) is catastrophic for the client (license jeopardy), so you cannot run "good enough" automation with a tolerable error rate — every one of those 65 surfaces must be right, on time, every cycle, forcing a verification-heavy human layer that resists the very automation the margin depends on.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Of ~50 states + DC + ~15 stamping offices, how many support API/bulk e-filing vs manual portal keying, and what is the measured human minutes-per-filing for the top 10 states by volume? (Have the specialist time 20 real filings across CA/FL/TX/NY + 4–6 mid-volume states.)
- Realistic exception rate per ingested batch (ambiguous home state, missing diligent-search docs, fee taxability), and how much needs client back-and-forth vs internal rules? (Run 5 real brokerages' month of data through a prototype, count kickouts.)
- How heavy is onboarding back-filing reconciliation; can it be scoped into a fixed-price setup, or is it a variable forensic engagement? (Pull 3 prospects' last 6 months; estimate hours before pricing the $2K–$10K setup.)
- How deep/fast-changing is the deadline/rate/rules database, and who owns keeping it current — one specialist's part-time job, or a continuous research load that grows with state count?
- Where is the named specialist coming from, loaded cost, and bench/coverage plan if they leave?

RECOMMENDATION: REFINE
A fundamentally sound, remote-first, recurring, no-field-ops shape that sits in the founders' lane and whose capacity math closes on the ladder — a real look, not a no-go. Average 6.5 because two load-bearing unknowns are unresolved: the true degree of automatable leverage across the ~65 manual filing surfaces (dims 8, 10), and the labor weight of exception-handling and onboarding back-filing. To GO: (1) instrument and publish real measured minutes-per-filing and exception rates from a prototype run on 5 real brokerages' data; (2) narrow the launch to the highest-volume stamping states (CA, FL, TX + 3–5 adjacent) rather than claiming all-50-state coverage day one, shrinking the integration surface to something two founders + one specialist can master and standardize; (3) productize onboarding into a fixed-scope baseline-reconciliation engagement with a clear hour ceiling. If the prototype shows per-filing time under ~30 minutes and bounded exceptions, this reweights toward a strong GO.
