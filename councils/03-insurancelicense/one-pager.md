# Managed Producer-Licensing, Appointment & CE Compliance for Multi-State Insurance Agencies and MGAs

## Customer
Insurance agencies, brokerages, and managing general agents/underwriters (MGAs/MGUs) that employ or contract licensed producers (agents) selling across multiple states — particularly fast-growing digital agencies, multi-state brokerages, and MGAs, typically running 20–500 producers.

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
Every producer must hold an active resident/non-resident license in each state where they sell, be appointed by each carrier they represent (where the state requires it), and complete continuing-education (CE) credits ahead of periodic renewal deadlines (often biennial, staggered by state and birth month). Lapses are not cosmetic: an unlicensed or unappointed producer cannot legally sell or bind, commissions earned during a lapse can be clawed back or are unpayable, carriers may terminate appointments, and state insurance departments (DOIs) levy fines. As an agency expands into more states and adds producers, the matrix (producers × states × carriers × license types × CE × renewal dates) explodes, and missing a single renewal silently breaks the revenue chain. NIPR (the National Insurance Producer Registry) and state DOIs run the system of record, but tracking it and acting on it is the agency's burden.

## Product
A productized managed service that runs an agency's entire producer-licensing lifecycle:
- A software workspace synced to NIPR/state data that tracks every producer's licenses, appointments, CE status, and renewal deadlines across all states and carriers.
- Automated alerts plus a managed renewal process — the service files renewals, CE confirmations, new-state and new-carrier appointments, and terminations on the agency's behalf.
- Producer onboarding/offboarding workflows.
- Audit-ready reporting for carriers and DOIs.
AI/software does the high-volume tracking, reminders, and form prep; a licensing specialist handles exceptions, carrier/DOI correspondence, and complex multi-state appointment work. Delivery is fully remote. The expertise (state-by-state licensing/appointment rules, NIPR mechanics, CE requirements) is supplied via a named licensing specialist plus the founders' insurance-industry and compliance background.

## Revenue model
Per-producer-per-month subscription (tiered by producer count), plus per-transaction fees for new-license/appointment filings and a base platform fee; optional CE-management and onboarding add-ons. Proposed pricing (the plan, to validate): roughly $15–$40 per producer per month, state filing fees passed through, and ~$25–$75 per appointment/license transaction; a 100-producer multi-state agency lands around $3K–$8K per month recurring plus transaction volume. Revenue recurs for as long as the agency operates, with very high retention because lapses break commission revenue.

## Positioning / competition
The category has established players. NIPR is the government-backed registry and rails (a data source, not a competitor). On the vendor side: Vertafore (which owns Sircon) is a large, entrenched, well-resourced incumbent; AgentSync is a well-funded, venture-backed, modern-tech entrant focused specifically on producer-license compliance; and there is a tail of older licensing tools, agency-management-system add-ons, outsourced paralegal/licensing teams, and many agencies still running it on spreadsheets or a single overworked licensing coordinator. The intended wedge is a genuinely done-for-you managed service — not just software the agency operates itself — aimed at the under-served small/mid multi-state agency and MGA tier that the enterprise tools price or scope past, with sharp positioning and content marketing, a founder-led close, and the founders' direct insurance-industry credibility and relationships.

## Why now
Rapid growth of digital and multi-state insurance distribution, MGAs, and embedded insurance has multiplied the producer × state × carrier matrix; carrier and DOI enforcement of appointment and licensing compliance has tightened; and agencies increasingly operate in many states at once, making manual tracking untenable.
