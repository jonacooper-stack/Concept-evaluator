CMO REVIEW — Managed Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH MARKET READ
A near-textbook market: a penalty-backed, weekly, federally-and-state-mandated obligation (Davis-Bacon / WH-347) contractors already pay to handle, served today by aging point tools (LCPtracker, Points North) and frightened in-house clerks, with essentially no modern productized managed-service player and no funded SaaS aggressor. The pain is forced, recurring, budgeted as a cost of public work; the buyer drops it only at the risk of withheld progress payments — the dream retention dynamic. The real question is not whether it can be sold; it's whether the founders can reach a fragmented, relationship-driven, offline contractor base cheaply enough, and whether "managed service" scales without specialist headcount eating margin. Sellable and positionable with edges; the hard part is channel reach into a low-trust offline buyer and avoiding a services-labor cost trap.

POSITIONING DRAFT
"We run your certified payroll and prevailing-wage compliance for every public job — filed correctly, on time, audit-proof — so the agency never withholds a dollar and you never have to understand Davis-Bacon yourself." Sharp and ownable; the crucial distinction vs incumbents is the verb: LCPtracker/Points North sell software you operate; this sells an outcome someone else operates. To a $15M electrical sub with one overworked clerk, "we do it, not you do it" is categorically different. Risk in the wording: "audit-proof" implies a liability the founders may not want to underwrite literally; honest version is "audit-ready, and we manage the agency response."

THE WEDGE
The first 100 customers come from a knowable, list-buildable population. (1) Public bid-award data is open: federal SAM.gov/FPDS, state DOT bid tabulations, municipal/school-district award notices publish the winning contractor on every covered project, weekly. Build a near-real-time list of contractors who JUST won a covered job — a fresh, acute WH-347 obligation starting next week — and do founder-led outbound (the founders' stated strength); cost is data tooling + time, low four figures/month. The standout, unusually defensible wedge: intent data nobody else operationalizes for this buy. (2) Channel partners: surety/bonding agents, construction CPAs, union/apprenticeship coordinators — one intro yields a cluster. (3) Content/SEO on "WH-347 instructions," "Davis-Bacon fringe credit," "LCPtracker help," "certified payroll [state]" — high-intent, low-competition. (4) Trade associations: AGC, ABC, NECA, MCAA chapter sponsorships and lunch-and-learns.

COMPETITIVE PICTURE
- LCPtracker — category-defining electronic certified-payroll platform, often the agency-mandated submission portal itself; best at being the required system of record, but it is the contractor's burden to operate, widely called clunky — software, not a managed service.
- Points North (eMARS / Certified Payroll Reporting) — solid mid-market certified-payroll software integrated with payroll; again DIY.
- ADP / Paychex / large payroll — prevailing wage is a weak bolt-on; structurally uninterested in the long-tail edge cases (conformance, fringe credit, ratios) that cause penalties.
- eBacon, Foundation Software (FOUNDATION) — construction-payroll-adjacent with certified-payroll features; best for contractors already on their suite.
- Long tail of regional bookkeeping/consulting services doing it manually — sleepy, sub-scale, no marketing.
Where is the air: every one is either software the contractor must still operate, or a non-scaled local service shop. Nobody owns "national, productized, software-leveraged, done-for-you managed prevailing-wage compliance with a named expert." Real gap; incumbents are the stale/poorly-marketed profile the founders beat. Watch: LCPtracker or Foundation could add a managed-service layer, but that's a service motion their software-licensing DNA resists, which buys time.

AVERAGE SCORE: 7.5 / 10

TOP 3 MARKET STRENGTHS
- Forced, penalty-backed, weekly recurring demand: buyer can't stop without risking withheld payments and debarment — about as close to involuntary retention as B2B gets.
- Genuinely ownable wedge: open bid-award data lets you find contractors with a brand-new acute obligation and reach them with founder-led outbound — intent targeting incumbents aren't using.
- Clean white space: the entire competitive set is DIY software, a weak bolt-on, or sleepy regional services; no one owns national productized done-for-you managed compliance.

TOP 3 MARKET RISKS
- Buyer is offline, fragmented, low-digital, slow to trust — content/SEO and outbound work but aren't high-velocity, so growth may be linear and cycle-bound.
- "Managed service" hides labor: exceptions, conformance, agency correspondence consume specialist hours, threatening margin and risking a services-shop slide.
- Willingness-to-pay at $300–$1,200/project/mo is unvalidated; contractors who "muddle through" with a clerk may anchor to "free-ish," especially smaller subs.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Reach velocity into a low-trust offline buyer combined with services-labor drag. Small public-works contractors don't behave like SaaS buyers: they don't self-serve a website, they're skeptical of outsiders touching a perjury-signed federal filing, and they buy on relationships/referral. The early motion is founder-led consultative selling — high-conviction but slow — and each closed account then consumes ongoing specialist labor for exceptions and audit response. If CAC runs high AND each account carries meaningful delivery cost, the business can be real and profitable but grow linearly and stay services-flavored, threatening both the income-ladder timing and the "software-leveraged, scalable" thesis. Mitigant: the bid-award intent wedge is the one lever that can make outbound efficient enough, and how much AI absorbs exception-handling (not just generation) determines whether margin is software-like or agency-like. Both must be proven early.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- Have you interviewed 15–20 actual $2M–$50M public-works contractors on what certified payroll costs them in hours/fear/withheld-payment incidents, and at what monthly price they flinch?
- What fraction of weekly delivery is true automation vs a specialist handling exceptions/conformance/correspondence, and how does specialist-hours-per-account trend at the 10th, 50th, 100th contractor?
- Can you operationalize bid-award data into a clean, current, contactable outbound list, and what's the measured reply→demo→close on a 50-contact test?
- Who is the named specialist, are they signed, and does the model survive (margin + credibility) if you hire a second and third?
- What liability do you carry when a filing you produced is wrong — is "audit-ready" defensible without becoming the legally exposed party under a perjury-backed regime?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Clears the bar that matters most: a must-have, penalty-backed, weekly-recurring, already-budgeted pain, in a fragmented market whose incumbents are stale or structurally disinterested, with sharp ownable positioning and a distinctive intent-data wedge fitting the founders' outbound/persuasion edge. Remote-first, not field ops; expertise bridgeable via a named specialist + founders' compliance background. GO rather than gushing because two things are still assumptions: (1) willingness-to-pay at the proposed price into a thrifty offline buyer, and (2) whether delivery stays software-leveraged or drifts into specialist-labor services. Both answerable with a cheap 50-contractor outbound-and-interview pilot off bid-award data before any build.
