CFO REVIEW — Managed Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

ONE-PARAGRAPH FINANCIAL READ
The numbers work well because this sits on a forced, weekly, penalty-backed obligation contractors already budget as a cost of public work — the strongest demand profile a recurring business can have. ARPU is healthy ($1.5K–$6K/mo/contractor), gross margins on a software-plus-light-review managed service should land 65–75%, and ~$300K/founder by month 12 needs only ~70–90 active mid-size contractors — a tiny slice of a market with tens of thousands of public-works contractors. The fragility is not demand or pricing — it is (a) per-project compliance-specialist labor inside COGS that erodes margin if AI doesn't deflect the review load, and (b) a moderately long enterprise-style sales cycle into a relationship-driven, non-self-serve buyer that raises CAC and slows the ramp. Net: pencils conservatively; watch-item is labor-to-margin coupling at scale.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL STRENGTHS
- Forced, pre-budgeted demand: weekly, perjury-signed, payment-gating obligation — painkiller with low price sensitivity and very low voluntary churn.
- Capital-light, advance-billed, fast-payback: monthly sub in advance, no inventory/AR drag, payback ~2–5 mo [ASSUMED], LTV/CAC ~8–18x if CAC holds.
- Low logo count to hit the ladder: ~35 mo12, ~50–55 mo24 against tens of thousands — huge headroom, diversified revenue.

TOP 3 FINANCIAL RISKS
- Labor-in-COGS coupling: if AI under-deflects exception/audit work, specialist-to-contractor ratio falls below ~25, GM slides from 70% toward 50%, capping flow-through and the ladder.
- CAC/sales-cycle drag: relationship-driven non-self-serve buyer with 60–120 day cycle could push CAC above $4K–$10K and slow the year-one ramp.
- Regulatory/reputation single-points: a Davis-Bacon coverage rollback softens "why now"; one publicized audit miss on a perjury-signed client could chill referrals in a tight community.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
Gross-margin coupling to compliance-specialist labor. The 65–75% margin and ~55% flow-through rest on an unverified assumption: that software deflects the bulk of generation, validation, conformance flagging, and — critically — agency correspondence and audit response, so one specialist carries 25–40 contractors. Prevailing-wage compliance is heterogeneous (per-jurisdiction wage determinations, fringe rules, ratios, portals). If the state-specific long tail and audit responses are high-touch and hard to template, the ratio collapses, COGS balloons, and you run a 45–55%-GM labor-arbitrage consultancy rather than a 70% software-leveraged service — roughly halving founder income at a given count and pushing the mo24 target from ~55 to ~90–100 contractors. Not a demand risk; an "is-this-software-or-headcount" risk. Test first on live volume.

QUESTIONS THE FOUNDERS MUST ANSWER BEFORE I'M COMFORTABLE
- How many contractors can ONE specialist manage end-to-end (review + corrections + agency correspondence + audit) once software generates/validates — 15, 30, or 50? This ratio sets gross margin.
- True sales cycle and fully-loaded CAC for a $3K/mo contractor, and does it hold across more than one state's contractor community, or does each new jurisdiction reset GTM?
- Revenue concentration by jurisdiction/referral channel; impact of (a) a Davis-Bacon coverage rollback or (b) a back-wage finding on a report you signed off — liability/indemnity, priced into the audit retainer?
- Realistic onboarding cost/time per new project (ingest data, map determinations); is the setup fee enough to keep onboarding from being a margin sink during growth?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
Sound financials reaching the ladder on a small, achievable count atop a forced, pre-budgeted obligation, with capital-light advance-billed economics and a clean recurring asset acquirers buy. GO conditioned on validating the specialist-to-contractor ratio early — that ratio (not demand or pricing) is the hinge between a 70%-margin software service and a 50%-margin headcount consultancy. Run 15–20 pricing interviews + measure the named specialist's real per-head capacity and live margin/CAC on the first 10 onboardings; if the ratio holds at ~25+, this is a genuinely strong bet.
