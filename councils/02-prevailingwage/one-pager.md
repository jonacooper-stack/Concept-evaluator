# Managed Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

## Customer
Small and mid-size construction contractors and subcontractors (roughly $2M–$50M revenue, 10–200 field employees) that perform work on federal, state, and municipal publicly-funded projects — general contractors and the electrical, mechanical, site, and specialty-trade subs that bid government and government-funded jobs.

## The problem (forced, recurring, budgeted)
Davis-Bacon (federal) and state/local "little Davis-Bacon" prevailing-wage laws require any contractor on a covered public-works project to pay legally-set prevailing wage + fringe rates and to submit certified payroll reports (federal form WH-347 or state equivalents) every week, for every worker, signed under penalty of perjury, for the life of the project. Miss or mis-file and the awarding agency withholds progress payments; repeat or willful violations bring back-wage and penalty liability and debarment from future public work. The requirements multiply across jurisdictions — different wage determinations, fringe rules, apprenticeship ratios, and electronic-submission portals (DOL, LCPtracker, state systems) — and recent infrastructure spending plus expanded Davis-Bacon coverage have pulled many more contractors into scope. The obligation is weekly, mandatory, penalty-backed, and already budgeted as a cost of doing public work.

## Product
A productized managed service that runs a contractor's prevailing-wage compliance end to end, per project:
- Software ingests the contractor's payroll/timekeeping data, applies the correct wage determinations and fringe calculations, and generates and validates weekly certified-payroll reports (WH-347 and state formats).
- Checks apprenticeship-ratio, EEO, and fringe-benefit-credit requirements; flags conformance needs.
- Files into the required agency portals, tracks deadlines and corrections, and maintains an audit-ready record.
- AI/software handles high-volume report generation and error-checking; a compliance specialist reviews exceptions and manages agency correspondence and audit response.
Delivery is fully remote. The expertise (Davis-Bacon Related Acts, conformance requests, fringe-credit rules, and state-specific regimes) is supplied via a named prevailing-wage specialist/advisor plus the founders' compliance-program and software background. The service onboards by project and runs for the multi-month or multi-year life of each job.

## Revenue model
Per-project monthly subscription that scales with active covered projects and crew size, plus a base platform fee per contractor; optional setup/onboarding fee per new project and an audit-response retainer. Proposed pricing (the plan, to validate): roughly $300–$1,200 per active project per month depending on crew size and jurisdiction count; a typical mid-size contractor running several covered jobs lands around $1.5K–$6K per month. Revenue recurs for as long as the contractor bids public work, with high retention because dropping it risks withheld payment.

## Positioning / competition
Contractors today muddle through with in-house payroll clerks and spreadsheets, generalist bookkeepers/CPAs, or aging certified-payroll point tools (e.g., LCPtracker, Points North / eMARS, and a long tail of regional services) that are dated, narrow, and poorly supported, with little modern marketing; many small subs do it manually and live in fear of an audit. Large payroll platforms (ADP and peers) treat prevailing wage as a weak bolt-on. The intended wedge is sharp positioning plus a genuinely productized managed service — not just software the contractor must operate themselves — with content marketing to a fragmented, relationship-driven contractor base and a founder-led close, against incumbents that are either DIY tools or sleepy regional services.

## Why now
Infrastructure spending and recent DOL Davis-Bacon rule updates (2023) expanded coverage and tightened enforcement; more states have prevailing-wage and electronic-filing mandates; and apprenticeship-utilization and fringe requirements keep growing — all increasing the weekly compliance burden on a fragmented contractor base that lacks the staff to keep up.
