LEGAL / REGULATORY REVIEW — Managed Certified-Payroll & Prevailing-Wage Compliance for Public-Works Contractors

(Not formal legal advice; experienced-operator pattern-recognition to brief real counsel.)

ONE-PARAGRAPH RISK READ
An unusually clean B2B regulatory-services concept, with one large caveat that defines the posture. The founders are not a regulated entity, need no occupational license, collect no consumer PII, and sell to sophisticated businesses on contract — that side is genuinely low-risk. The caveat: the product's value is to file documents (WH-347 certified payroll, signed "under penalty of perjury" per 40 U.S.C. § 3145, 29 C.F.R. § 5.5) on which the contractor, not the service, bears criminal and debarment liability, while the founders touch the worker comp/wage data producing those filings. The risk that matters is not licensing or privacy — it is professional/E&O liability for getting a prevailing-wage determination, fringe-credit, or apprenticeship-ratio calculation wrong and causing a client to under-pay workers, trigger withheld payments, or face False Claims Act exposure. Insurable and contractually limitable, but the spine of the deal — engineer it from day one.

REGULATORY MAP
- DOL Wage and Hour Division — Davis-Bacon Act (40 U.S.C. §§ 3141–3148) and Related Acts; 29 C.F.R. Parts 1, 3, 5. The substantive law the service operates around, not a regime the founders are licensed under. Burden falls on the contractor; founders' burden is competence/accuracy. The Aug 2023 final rule expanded coverage and re-introduced the 30% prevailing-rate methodology — direct tailwind and a source of substantive complexity to master.
- Copeland Anti-Kickback Act (18 U.S.C. § 874; 40 U.S.C. § 3145; 29 C.F.R. Part 3). Governs the weekly statement of compliance + "under penalty of perjury" certification. The founders' software generates the document the contractor's officer signs — founders must NEVER sign/certify; the contractor's authorized officer must.
- State "little Davis-Bacon" regimes + e-filing portals — 25+ states (CA DIR eCPR, NY DOL, IL IDOL, WA L&I), each with own determinations, fringe rules, apprenticeship mandates, portals. The real jurisdictional multiplication; additive complexity, not a licensing cliff. No state licenses the preparer.
- Federal civil False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733) + state analogues; criminal false-statements (18 U.S.C. § 1001). The sleeper: a knowingly false certified payroll on federally funded work can be an FCA "false claim" (treble damages, qui tam). Sits on the contractor, but a preparer could be drawn into discovery/contributor claims. Manageable via accuracy controls, "client certifies" allocation, E&O.
- FTC ROSCA / state auto-renewal laws (CA ARL, NY GBL § 527-a) — applies to the founders' own subscription billing; low burden (B2B), satisfied by clear renewal/cancellation terms.
- State privacy (CCPA/CPRA + ~19 state laws) + employee-data handling — ingests names, addresses, SSNs (last four on WH-347, often full SSNs in source feeds), wage/hours; processor/service-provider relationship → DPAs + reasonable-security; no special-category regime.
- IRS/SSN data handling — identity-theft-sensitive data; no special license, but breach exposure + 50-state breach-notification apply.

LICENSING SUMMARY
No occupational or professional license is required — a real, nameable strength. Certified-payroll preparation is not a licensed profession in any U.S. jurisdiction (unlike CPA attest, payroll-tax fiduciary status, or PEO licensing). The contractor — not the preparer — holds all Davis-Bacon/state obligations. Founders need only standard formation (LLC/corp), a local business license, and tax registration. Two boundaries to police with counsel: (1) do not cross into providing legal advice on whether a project is "covered" or interpreting a wage determination as a legal opinion — UPL exposure in some states; the named specialist frames interpretive calls as compliance advisory, genuine legal questions go to client's counsel; (2) if the platform ever processes payroll funds or remits taxes, it steps into money-transmitter / payroll-fiduciary territory — the model (ingest data, generate, file reports) avoids this and should stay on that side.

LIABILITY POSTURE
Dominant exposure is professional liability / E&O: the founders compute fringe credits, apply wage determinations, validate ratios; an error can cause underpayment, withheld progress payments, back wages/penalties, or — worst case — an FCA claim. Core insurable risk: Technology E&O / Professional Liability with a "compliance services" endorsement + a Cyber/data-breach policy for SSN/wage data. Premiums for a remote software-plus-advisory firm are moderate and affordable at this revenue scale — a known insurance market, not an uninsurable catastrophe. Contractually the MSA must (a) allocate certification/final responsibility to the client (client's officer signs WH-347 under penalty of perjury, not the founders), (b) cap liability at fees paid (or a small multiple), (c) disclaim consequential damages incl. withheld-payment and FCA exposure, (d) require the client to provide and warrant accurate source data, (e) include a DPA. Pre-entity personal exposure is standard — form the entity and execute all contracts in its name before client one. Residual after insurance + contracts: reputational, and the risk of being named in a client's FCA dispute even when allocated — real but second-order and manageable.

TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY STRENGTHS
- No occupational/professional license required anywhere; the regulatory obligation (Davis-Bacon) sits on the contractor-client, not the founders.
- Regulatory trajectory is a documented tailwind — DOL 2023 rule + IIJA/IRA expanded the must-buy compliance burden.
- Pure B2B, no consumer-protection complexity, no field workers, no special-category data — sidesteps ROSCA/ARL, premises/background-check, and HIPAA/biometric regimes.

TOP 3 LEGAL/REGULATORY RISKS
- Professional/E&O liability: a wrong determination, fringe-credit, or ratio calc can cause withheld payments, back-wage liability, or FCA exposure — the central insurable-but-real risk.
- FCA / criminal false-statement contamination (31 U.S.C. § 3729; 18 U.S.C. § 1001): the service prepares perjury-certified documents, so a client's fraud/error could draw the founders into discovery/contributor claims even with clear allocation.
- Unauthorized practice of law: interpreting coverage or a wage determination as a legal conclusion could cross into UPL in some states — the advisory framing must police it.

BIGGEST SINGLE RISK
E&O on the substantive compliance calculations, with FCA contamination as its sharp edge. The whole promise is "we'll get your certified payroll right." It inverts on a bad day: a wrong determination, mis-computed fringe credit (29 C.F.R. Part 5), or mishandled ratio can underpay workers across a multi-month job, withhold progress payments, owe back wages/penalties, and — on federally funded work — expose the client to treble-damages FCA or a qui tam suit, with the founders' prepared filings in discovery. Because the certified payroll is signed under penalty of perjury (40 U.S.C. § 3145), per-error stakes are high relative to ordinary SaaS. Not fatal and not unusual for a compliance-services firm — squarely insurable via Tech E&O with a compliance endorsement and contractually limitable (liability cap, consequential-damages disclaimer, client-certification allocation). But it must be engineered into the contract, insurance, and exception-review workflow from client one, because a single uncapped engagement gone wrong on a large federal job could generate a claim that dwarfs annual revenue.

QUESTIONS FOR REAL COUNSEL BEFORE LAUNCH
1. How to structure the MSA so the client's officer alone certifies the WH-347, our role is "preparation and advisory," liability is capped at fees paid, consequential/withheld-payment/FCA damages disclaimed, and the client warrants source-data accuracy?
2. Where is the line between compliance advisory and UPL when we interpret coverage, determinations, conformance requests, or fringe-credit rules — and how should the specialist frame interpretive calls per state?
3. What is our FCA and 18 U.S.C. § 1001 exposure as a preparer when a client's filing is later alleged false, and what client→us indemnification + E&O limits neutralize it?
4. What data-processing/security obligations attach to handling full SSNs/wage data under CCPA/CPRA + state laws, and what breach-notification/DPA terms per client?
5. Do any portals (LCPtracker, DIR eCPR, state DOL) impose ToS/authorized-agent requirements constraining a third-party service filing on a contractor's behalf?

RECOMMENDATION: GO
From a legal/regulatory standpoint a strong, low-friction, near-ideal shape: the founders sell a recurring, must-buy compliance service into a regime (Davis-Bacon + state analogues) that binds their customers, not themselves; need no license; touch no consumer or special-category data; and enjoy a documented tailwind from the DOL 2023 rule and infrastructure spending. The one material risk — professional/E&O liability on the calculations with an FCA tail — is real and load-bearing but precisely what a standardized MSA (liability cap, client-certification allocation, consequential-damages disclaimer, accuracy warranties) plus Tech E&O and Cyber insurance is built to handle, provided it is engineered in from the first engagement. No structural change to geography/customer/model is needed; retain counsel to paper the contract and confirm the UPL boundary before client one.
