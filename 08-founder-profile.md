# Founder Fit Profile — Jonathan Cooper & Mike Logsdon

> **What this file is.** A reusable lens for evaluating business ideas against *this specific founding team*. Upload it (or paste it) alongside a business idea and ask the AI to score the idea using the rubric at the bottom. It tells you where this team is strong, where it's exposed, who would lead what, and what they'd have to hire or partner for.

> **How to use it.** Paste this at the top of a chat, then add an idea and say:
> *"Evaluate this business idea against my Founder Fit Profile. Apply the hard gates first, then score each rubric dimension with reasoning, assign who leads what, list the top 3 risks, name what we'd need to hire or partner for, and give a go / refine / no-go recommendation."*

## The team in one line

## Founder profiles

**Domain depth:** healthcare, employee benefits, insurance, technology.

### Mike Logsdon — execution, partnerships, marketing, writing
**Excellent at:** relentless execution and powering through ("gets shit done," hardest worker Jon knows); charisma and relationship-building; partnerships and channel sales; writing, creative, and marketing instinct (stronger than Jon here); generates good ideas and thinks critically; FP&A and financial modeling background (IPO/S-1, Series C, pricing models at Elevance); grit (Alaska→Argentina fundraising ride). Loyal and generous.

**Weak at / dislikes:** analytical precision is "okay" — makes small careless errors that need checking; detail attention ~2–3/10 (lowest on the team); can get swept up in excitement and miss red flags; generous to the point of his own expense (boundary-setting). ## Combined team: strengths
- **Trust and durability.** 25-year friendship, a decade co-founding, one exit. Very low founder-conflict risk. Decisions get made through honest debate; Jon can persuade or overrule when needed, with little ego friction.
- **Strategy + finance firepower.** Together they can model, position, assess risk, and fundraise (if they ever chose to).
- **Marketing + positioning + modern product design.** Their signature edge: making a tired industry's offering look fresh and current.
- **Cheap to launch.** Jon's vibe-coding means they can ship a prototype or simple product without hiring engineers on day one.
- **Lean operators** who have scaled before.

## Combined team: capability map (launch vs. scale)
- **Operational rigor — solid enough to launch (~6–7/10), but not their happy place.** They've taken multiple businesses to the first 10–20 clients and can grind through the early operational work to get there. It's *not* a startup-stage shortcoming — it's simply not what they enjoy or where they want to live long-term. Treat it as a **scaling and sustained-execution** risk, not a launch risk; revisit a systems/ops hire or fractional COO once past early traction.
- **Sales & closing — same story (~6–7/10).** Capable of getting the ball rolling and landing early customers; just not where their energy or passion sits. Fine for launch; pressure-test any idea that hinges on a long, high-volume, hard-closing sales motion *at scale*.
- **Engineering — the one true gap (~3–4/10 today).** Vibe-coding gets prototypes and basic working apps out the door, but it's fake-it-grade, not scale-grade. Jon is a quick, eager learner who can likely reach 5–6 — still a long way from a seasoned engineer's depth. Good enough to launch a bootstrap product; **they'll need to hire a real engineer once anything needs to harden or scale.** Upside: both have strong networks to recruit from when that time comes.
- **Correlated domain expertise.** Both are deep only in healthcare / benefits / insurance. Everywhere else they're sharp generalists, not experts.

## Hard gates (auto-disqualify unless there's a clear workaround)
1. Core value is gated by heavy regulation, licensing, or compliance moats (insurance-style).
2. Success depends on breaking into entrenched incumbent relationships, referral capture, or regulatory capture.
3. Requires more than ~$250k to reach revenue.
4. No realistic path to revenue within ~6–12 months (violates the income need).
5. Requires industrial-grade in-house engineering from day one.

## Green flags (favor heavily)
- An **old, stagnant, unsexy industry** with dated, complacent competition.
- **Real urgency** — acute pain, hard deadlines, buyers who *must* act.
- **Fast to cash flow** (ideally paying within 6–12 months).
- **Low ongoing operational effort** to run — sustained grind isn't where they thrive, so lighter-ops businesses play to their strengths.
- Wins on **modern product + sharp marketing/positioning** rather than on regulatory or relationship moats.
- Buyer **has budget and decision authority**, with a short sales cycle. (Avoid the HR-benefits trap: no authority, tight budgets, slow.)
- Recurring revenue and healthy margins; scalable without massive capital.

## Scoring rubric (total = 100)
Weights reflect the team's stated priorities: **1) speed to cash, 2) low ongoing effort, 3) scale, 4) passion/impact.** Score each dimension 0–10, multiply by the weight, sum, and normalize to /100.

| # | Dimension | Weight | Score high when… |
|---|-----------|:------:|------------------|
| 1 | **Speed to cash flow** | 25 | First real revenue is plausible within 6–12 months. |
| 2 | **Low ongoing effort / detail-tolerance** | 15 | The business runs without demanding sustained operational grind — something the team *can* do but doesn't enjoy long-term. |
| 3 | **Founder skill coverage** | 20 | Winnable with strategy + finance + marketing + prototype-coding, *without* needing capabilities they lack. |
| 4 | **Low regulatory & incumbent friction** | 15 | No licensing, no compliance moat, no entrenched-relationship/referral gatekeeping. |
| 5 | **Capital fit** | 10 | Reaches revenue within $100k–$250k. |
| 6 | **Scale potential** | 10 | Meaningful upside without requiring VC. |
| 7 | **Passion / impact / interest** | 5 | Genuinely motivating; bonus if it leverages healthcare/benefits/insurance know-how *without* the regulatory core. |

**Interpretation:** 80–100 = strong fit, pursue. 60–79 = promising, refine the weak dimensions first. 40–59 = real concerns, only if a specific gap can be closed cheaply. <40 = pass. **Any hard-gate hit caps the score and triggers a no-go unless the workaround is explicit.**

## Required output when evaluating an idea
1. **Hard-gate check** — pass/fail on each of the 5 gates.
2. **Rubric table** — score + one-line reason per dimension, plus the weighted total.
3. **Who leads what** — Jon vs. Mike vs. hire/partner, mapped to the idea's core activities.
4. **Top 3 risks**, with the single most likely failure mode called out.
5. **Hire-or-partner-for list** — the capabilities this idea needs that the team lacks.
6. **Recommendation** — go / refine (with the specific fixes) / no-go.
