## Who they are
Two **versatile, adaptive generalist** founders. They learn fast and can operate
across functions. They are NOT specialists clinging to one trade, and they should
not be scored as if a business must match a narrow skill — penalize only genuine
**credential** or **sustained-field-ops** mismatches, not learnable domains.

## Core strengths (where they win)
- **Marketing, positioning, and messaging** — top-tier. This is the edge.
- **Reaching and persuading people** — outbound, partnerships, channel, founder-led
  sales, getting in front of buyers.
- **Business strategy and finance** — they can model, price, and structure.
- **Software development** — they can build product, not just buy it.
- **Compliance-operator instinct** — real program experience running SOC 2 Type II
  cycles, HIPAA/HITECH, and GDPR/CCPA work. This is what makes regulated/compliance
  markets BRIDGEABLE rather than off-limits.
- **Regulated-enterprise deal-closing** — multi-million-dollar enterprise sales into
  regulated industries (insurance).

## Where they win competitively
- Against **stale, sleepy, old-school, poorly-marketed incumbents** — legacy
  consultancies, generalist MSPs, old-line vendors — in fragmented or
  regulation-forced markets where nobody has shown up with modern positioning,
  productization, and a sharp message. This is the sweet spot.
- In **forced-demand markets** where the buy is mandatory, recurring, and budgeted —
  driven by ANY of the forcing functions in 01-objectives.md (a regulatory/compliance
  mandate; a contractual requirement from a prime/insurer/lender/franchisor; a
  **critical input to a customer's production/revenue process**; operational/financial
  continuity; or effectively-mandatory risk mitigation). Regulation is one favorite
  path, not the only one — a raw material a factory line can't run without is just as
  strong. PROVIDED any required expertise can be SCAFFOLDED (advisors, a named
  practitioner, partners, a hire, or the founders' own compliance-operator
  background). A moat here is an asset: it forces the buy and deters lazy competition.
  The CMMC concept is one illustration of the pattern, not the template.
- Where acquisition is **marketing/positioning/relationship-led**, not
  capital-auction-led.

## Where they do NOT win (avoid)
- **High-performing, well-funded, strong-tech incumbents** — modern SaaS leaders,
  aggressive VC-backed players. Anywhere the only move is to outspend or
  out-engineer. (It's the *quality of the competitor*, not the presence of a moat,
  that matters.)
- **Genuinely unbridgeable expertise/credential barriers** — where no advisor,
  partner, hire, or learning curve can close the gap in a reasonable time.
- **Field-ops / manual-labor cores** — see hard preferences below.

## Hard preferences (treat as near-constraints)
- **NOT a field-service crew.** They will not build a business whose core is
  sustained manual or field operations — trucks, installs, on-site inspections as
  the repeated unit of work. Occasional travel is fine; a field-ops *business
  model* is not. Score operational tractability accordingly.
- **Must-have over nice-to-have.** They are done selling vitamins. Strongly favor
  painkiller problems customers already pay to solve.
- **Recurring revenue** is required for durability.
- **Bootstrapped / low capital.**

## How the council should use this
- Skill fit (rubric dim 9, weight 5): score HIGH for strategy/finance/marketing/
  positioning/GTM/software-shaped businesses; score LOW only for genuine
  credential or sustained-field-ops requirements. Do NOT over-penalize a learnable
  new domain — the founders are adaptive.
- Operational tractability (dim 7): score LOW for field-service/manual-labor models.
- Competitive landscape (dim 3): score HIGH for stale, sleepy, beatable incumbents
  (even behind a regulatory/expertise moat, which is often a plus); score LOW only
  when the incumbents are high-performing, well-funded, strong-tech players.
- A regulatory/expertise moat is NOT a reason to score skill-fit or competition low,
  so long as the barrier is bridgeable (advisors/practitioner/partners/learning) —
  the founders are adaptive and have a compliance-operator background.
